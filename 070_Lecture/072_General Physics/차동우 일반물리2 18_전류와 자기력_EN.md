On **April 21, 1820**, during an experiment, Ørsted accidentally observed that a compass needle near a current-carrying wire moved.

**However, Ørsted was unable to formulate a mathematical relationship between current and magnetic field.**

---

## Page 9 (Slide 9) — Cause of Magnetic Phenomena [8]

**It was discovered that the cause of magnetic phenomena is not magnets but moving charges.**

- Stationary charge → Creates an **electric field** in its surroundings.
- Moving charge → Creates both an **electric field** and a **magnetic field** in its surroundings.

- Electric field → Both stationary and moving charges experience **electric force**.
- Magnetic field → Only **moving charges** experience **magnetic force**.

## Page 16 (Slide 16) — Coulomb's Law and Biot–Savart Law [15]

**Coulomb's Law:**
$$d\vec{E} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{dq}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2} \frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$

**Biot–Savart Law:**
$$d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{id\vec{l}}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2} \times \frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$

*(Diagram: Geometry diagrams as in previous slide, with formulas highlighted.)*

## Page 17 (Slide 17) — Coulomb's Law and Biot–Savart Law [16]

**Coulomb's Law (integrated):**
$$d\vec{E} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{dq}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2} \frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$
$$\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \int \frac{dq}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2} \frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$

$$d\vec{B} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{id\vec{l}}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2} \times \frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$
$$\vec{B}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \int \frac{id\vec{l}}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2} \times \frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$

- **Unit of electric field:** N/C or V/m
- **Unit of magnetic field:** T (Tesla)

## Page 19 (Slide 19) — Coulomb's Law and Biot–Savart Law [18]

**Coulomb's Law:**
$$\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \int dv' \frac{\rho(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2} \frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$

**Biot–Savart Law:**
$$\vec{B}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \int dv' \frac{\vec{J}(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2} \times \frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$

Constants:
$$\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} = 8.99 \times 10^9 \text{ Nm}^2/\text{C}^2, \qquad \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} = 10^{-7} \text{ N/A}^2$$

Key result:
$$\frac{1}{\epsilon_0\mu_0} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\cdot\frac{4\pi}{\mu_0} = \frac{8.99\times10^9}{10^{-7}} = (3\times10^8 \text{ m/s})^2$$

$$\Rightarrow \frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_0\mu_0}} = c \quad \text{(speed of light!)}$$

## Page 36 (Slide 36) — Straight Current [36]

A finite straight wire carrying current $i$ along the $x$-axis from $x=-a$ to $x=b$. Finding the magnetic field at point $P = (0, y, 0)$.

Setup:
- $\vec{r} = y\hat{y}$, $\vec{r}' = x'\hat{x}$
- $\vec{r} - \vec{r}' = y\hat{y} - x'\hat{x}$
- $|\vec{r} - \vec{r}'|^2 = x'^2 + y^2$
- $id\vec{l} = idx'\hat{x}$

Biot–Savart Law:
$$d\vec{B}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{id\vec{l}}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2} \times \frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad d\vec{B}(y) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{idx'\hat{x}}{x'^2+y^2} \times \frac{y\hat{y} - x'\hat{x}}{\sqrt{x'^2+y^2}}$$

*(Diagram: Coordinate axes, wire along $x$-axis, point $P$ on the $y$-axis, $d\vec{B}$ pointing out of page.)*

---

## Page 37 (Slide 37) — Straight Current [37]

Direction of $d\vec{B}$:
$$\hat{x} \times (y\hat{y} - x'\hat{x}) = y\hat{z}$$

**Regardless of the position of $dx'$, the direction is always $\hat{z}$!**

$$dB_z(y) = \frac{\mu_0 iy}{4\pi} \frac{dx'}{(x'^2+y^2)^{3/2}}$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad B_z(y) = \frac{\mu_0 iy}{4\pi} \int_{-a}^{b} \frac{dx'}{(x'^2+y^2)^{3/2}}$$

---

## Page 38 (Slide 38) — Straight Current [38]

Using the integral table (Dwight 200.03):
$$\int \frac{dx}{r^3} = \frac{1}{a^2}\frac{x}{r}, \quad r = \sqrt{x^2+a^2}$$

$$I = \int_{-a}^{b} \frac{dx'}{(x'^2+y^2)^{3/2}} = \left[\frac{x'}{y^2\sqrt{x'^2+y^2}}\right]_{-a}^{b} = \frac{b}{y^2\sqrt{b^2+y^2}} - \frac{-a}{y^2\sqrt{a^2+y^2}}$$

$$\boxed{B_z(y) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{i}{y}\left[\frac{b}{\sqrt{b^2+y^2}} + \frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2+y^2}}\right]}$$

---

## Page 39 (Slide 39) — Infinitely Long Straight Current [39]

From Biot–Savart Law for a finite wire:
$$B_z(y) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{i}{y}\left[\frac{b}{\sqrt{b^2+y^2}} + \frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2+y^2}}\right] = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{i}{y}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+y^2/b^2}} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+y^2/a^2}}\right]$$

For an **infinitely long straight current** ($a = b = \infty$):
$$\boxed{B_z(y) = \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi}\frac{i}{y}}$$

---

## Page 40 (Slide 40) — Infinitely Long Straight Current [40]

**Ampère's Law:** $\Gamma_B = \mu_0 i$

$$\Gamma_B = \oint_C \vec{B}(\vec{r})\cdot d\vec{l}, \qquad i = \int_S \vec{J}(\vec{r})\cdot d\vec{S}$$

From **symmetry alone**, for an infinite straight current along the axis of a circle of radius $a$:

① The magnetic field magnitude is the same everywhere on the circle: $B(a) = B_0$

② The direction of the magnetic field is tangential: $\vec{B}(a) = B_0\hat{\phi}$

*(Diagram: Infinite straight current $i$ along axis, with circular Ampère loop of radius $a$.)*

## Page 41 (Slide 41) — Infinitely Long Straight Current [41]

**Circulation:**
$$\Gamma_B = \oint_C \vec{B}(\vec{r})\cdot d\vec{l}$$

With $\vec{B} = B_0\hat{\phi}$ and $d\vec{l} = ad\phi\,\hat{\phi}$:

*(Diagram: Top view of the Ampère loop, current $i$ coming out of page, $\vec{B} = B_0\hat{\phi}$, and $d\vec{l} = ad\phi\,\hat{\phi}$.)*

## Page 42 (Slide 42) — Infinitely Long Straight Current [42]

**Circulation:**
$$\Gamma_B = \int_0^{2\pi} (B_0\hat{\phi})\cdot(ad\phi\,\hat{\phi}) = B_0 a \int_0^{2\pi}d\phi = 2\pi a B_0$$

## Page 43 (Slide 43) — Infinitely Long Straight Current [43]

**Circulation:** $\Gamma_B = \oint_C \vec{B}(\vec{r})\cdot d\vec{l} = 2\pi a B_0$

**Total current:** $i = \int_S \vec{J}(\vec{r})\cdot d\vec{S} = i$

**Ampère's Law:** $\Gamma_B = \mu_0 i \Rightarrow 2\pi a B_0 = \mu_0 i$

$$\boxed{B_0 = \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi}\frac{i}{a}}$$

**This is the same result obtained using the Biot–Savart Law!!**

## Page 46 (Slide 46) — Circular Current [46]

A circular current $i$ of radius $R$ lies in the $xy$-plane. Find the magnetic field $B(z)$ at a point on the $z$-axis.

- By symmetry of the circular current, the magnetic field at $(0,0,z)$ points along the $z$-axis.
- The current element: $id\vec{l} = iRd\phi\,\hat{\phi}$
- Method: Biot–Savart Law (not directly solvable by Ampère's Law due to lack of symmetry)

*(Diagram: 3D view of circular current loop in $xy$-plane, field point $(0,0,z)$ on $z$-axis, with $d\vec{B}$ shown.)*

---

## Page 47 (Slide 47) — Circular Current [47]

Setup:
- $\vec{r} = z\hat{z}$, $\vec{r}' = R\hat{\rho}$
- $\vec{r} - \vec{r}' = z\hat{z} - R\hat{\rho}$
- $|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2 = z^2 + R^2$

Cross product:
$$iRd\phi\,\hat{\phi} \times (z\hat{z} - R\hat{\rho}) = iRd\phi\,(z\hat{\rho} + R\hat{z})$$

The $z$-component of $d\vec{B}$:
$$dB_z = \frac{\mu_0 i}{4\pi}\frac{R^2}{(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}}\,d\phi$$

---

## Page 48 (Slide 48) — Circular Current [48]

Integrating over the full circle:
$$B_z(z) = \frac{\mu_0 i}{4\pi}\frac{R^2}{(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}}\int_0^{2\pi}d\phi = \frac{\mu_0 i}{2}\frac{R^2}{(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}}$$

$$\boxed{B_z(z) = \frac{\mu_0 i}{2}\frac{R^2}{(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}}}$$

**At the center of the loop ($z=0$):**
$$\boxed{B_z(0) = \frac{\mu_0 i}{2R}}$$

## Page 50 (Slide 50) — Infinitely Long Cylindrical Wire of Radius $a$ [50]

An infinite cylindrical conductor of radius $a$ carries total current $I$ uniformly distributed. Current density: $J = \dfrac{I}{\pi a^2}$

**Ampère's Law:** $\Gamma_B = \mu_0 i$

**(1) Magnetic field for $r < a$:** Take a circular Ampère loop of radius $r < a$.

$$\Gamma_B = \oint_C \vec{B}(\vec{r})\cdot d\vec{l} = 2\pi r B(r)$$

$$i = \int_S \vec{J}(\vec{r})\cdot d\vec{S} = J\pi r^2 = I\frac{r^2}{a^2}$$

$$\Gamma_B = \mu_0 i \Rightarrow 2\pi r B(r) = \mu_0 I\frac{r^2}{a^2}$$

$$\boxed{B(r) = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi}\frac{r}{a^2}}$$

---

## Page 51 (Slide 51) — Infinitely Long Cylindrical Wire of Radius $a$ [51]

**(2) Magnetic field for $r > a$:** Take a circular Ampère loop of radius $r > a$.

$$\Gamma_B = \oint_C \vec{B}(\vec{r})\cdot d\vec{l} = 2\pi r B(r), \qquad i = \int_S \vec{J}(\vec{r})\cdot d\vec{S} = I$$

$$\Gamma_B = \mu_0 i \Rightarrow 2\pi r B(r) = \mu_0 I$$

$$\boxed{B(r) = \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi}\frac{I}{r}}$$

**This is the same as the magnetic field of an infinitely long straight current!!**

## Page 52 (Slide 52) — Example [52]

**Problem:** As shown, a current $i_1$ flows in a semicircular wire of radius $a$ extended by two long straight wires. An infinitely long straight current $i_2$ flows into the page through the center of the opposite semicircle. Find the magnetic field at center $C$.

*(Diagram: Semicircular wire with current $i_1$ on left, infinite straight current $i_2$ going into page on right, center $C$ marked.)*

**Strategy:** Use superposition.

**Key fact:** The magnetic field at any point on the extension line of a straight current is zero.

## Page 73 (Slide 73) — What It Means to Understand Electric Phenomena [73]

① Understand the electric field produced by charges in the surroundings.

$$\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_V dv'\,\frac{\rho(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2}\frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|} \quad \text{(Coulomb's Law)}$$

② Understand the electric force on a charge placed in the electric field.

$$\vec{F}_e = q\vec{E}(\vec{r}) \quad \text{(Coulomb force)}$$

③ Unit of electric field: $[E] = [F]/[q]$ → **N/C**

*(Reference: Section 15A)*

---

## Page 74 (Slide 74) — What It Means to Understand Magnetic Phenomena [74]

① Understand the magnetic field produced by currents (moving charges) in the surroundings.

$$\vec{B}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int dv'\,\frac{\vec{J}(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2}\times\frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|} \quad \text{(Biot–Savart Law)}$$

② Understand the magnetic force on currents (moving charges) placed in the magnetic field.

$$\vec{F}_m = i\vec{l}\times\vec{B}(\vec{r}), \qquad \vec{F}_m = q\vec{v}\times\vec{B}(\vec{r}) \quad \text{(Lorentz force)}$$

③ Unit of magnetic field: $[B] = [F]/[qv]$ → **Ns/Cm = T (Tesla)**

---

## Page 75 (Slide 75) — Space Where Both Electric and Magnetic Fields Exist [75]

For a charge $q$ moving with velocity $\vec{v}$ in a region with both $\vec{E}$ and $\vec{B}$:

- Electric force: $\vec{F}_e = q\vec{E}$
- Magnetic force: $\vec{F}_m = q\vec{v}\times\vec{B}$

**Electromagnetic force (Lorentz force):**
$$\boxed{\vec{F} = \vec{F}_e + \vec{F}_m = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v}\times\vec{B})}$$

---

## Page 76 (Slide 76) — Comparison of Electric and Magnetic Forces [76]

For charge $q$ at $\vec{r}$ and test charge $q'$ at $\vec{r}'$:

**Electric field at $\vec{r}'$ due to $q$:**
$$\vec{E}'(\vec{r}') = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q}{|\vec{r}'-\vec{r}|^2}\frac{\vec{r}'-\vec{r}}{|\vec{r}'-\vec{r}|}$$

**Electric force on $q'$:**
$$\vec{F}'_e = q'\vec{E}'(\vec{r}') = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q'q}{|\vec{r}'-\vec{r}|^2}\frac{\vec{r}'-\vec{r}}{|\vec{r}'-\vec{r}|}$$

---

## Page 77 (Slide 77) — Comparison of Electric and Magnetic Forces [77]

**Magnetic field at $\vec{r}'$ due to $q$ moving with $\vec{v}$:**
$$\vec{B}'(\vec{r}') = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{q\vec{v}}{|\vec{r}'-\vec{r}|^2}\times\frac{\vec{r}'-\vec{r}}{|\vec{r}'-\vec{r}|}$$

**Magnetic force on $q'$ moving with $\vec{v}'$:**
$$\vec{F}'_m = q'\vec{v}'\times\vec{B}'(\vec{r}') = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{q'q}{|\vec{r}'-\vec{r}|^2}\vec{v}'\times\left(\vec{v}\times\frac{\vec{r}'-\vec{r}}{|\vec{r}'-\vec{r}|}\right)$$

---

## Page 78 (Slide 78) — Comparison of Electric and Magnetic Forces [78]

**Magnitude of electric force on $q'$:**
$$F'_e = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q'q}{|\vec{r}'-\vec{r}|^2}$$

**Maximum magnitude of magnetic force on $q'$:**
$$F'_m = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{q'qv'v}{|\vec{r}'-\vec{r}|^2}$$

---

## Page 79 (Slide 79) — Comparison of Electric and Magnetic Forces [79]

**Ratio of magnetic to electric force:**
$$\frac{F'_m}{F'_e} = \frac{\dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\dfrac{q'qv'v}{|\vec{r}'-\vec{r}|^2}}{\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q'q}{|\vec{r}'-\vec{r}|^2}} = \epsilon_0\mu_0 v'v = \frac{v'v}{c^2} \ll 1$$

The magnetic force is much smaller than the electric force for non-relativistic velocities.

## Page 103 (Slide 103) — From Scalar Potential to Electric Field [103]

**Scalar potential:**
$$\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int dv'\frac{\rho(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$

Using the identity:
$$\nabla\left(\frac{1}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}\right) = -\frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^3} = -\frac{1}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2}\frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$

**Scalar potential → Electric field:**
$$\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = -\nabla\phi(\vec{r}) = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\nabla\int dv'\frac{\rho(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$
$$= -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int dv'\rho(\vec{r}')\nabla\left(\frac{1}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}\right) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int dv'\frac{\rho(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2}\frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|} = \vec{E}(\vec{r})\ \checkmark$$

## Page 104 (Slide 104) — Product Rules [104]

$$\nabla(fg) = g\nabla f + f\nabla g$$

$$\nabla(\vec{A}\cdot\vec{B}) = \vec{A}\times(\nabla\times\vec{B}) + \vec{B}\times(\nabla\times\vec{A}) + (\vec{A}\cdot\nabla)\vec{B} + (\vec{B}\cdot\nabla)\vec{A}$$

$$\nabla\cdot(f\vec{A}) = \vec{A}\cdot\nabla f + f\nabla\cdot\vec{A}$$

$$\nabla\cdot(\vec{A}\times\vec{B}) = \vec{B}\cdot(\nabla\times\vec{A}) - \vec{A}\cdot(\nabla\times\vec{B})$$

$$\nabla\times(f\vec{A}) = (\nabla f)\times\vec{A} + f\nabla\times\vec{A}$$

$$\nabla\times(\vec{A}\times\vec{B}) = \vec{A}(\nabla\cdot\vec{B}) - \vec{B}(\nabla\cdot\vec{A}) + (\vec{B}\cdot\nabla)\vec{A} - (\vec{A}\cdot\nabla)\vec{B}$$

---

## Page 105 (Slide 105) — From Vector Potential to Magnetic Field [105]

**Vector potential:**
$$\vec{A}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int dv'\frac{\vec{J}(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$

**Vector potential → Magnetic field:**
$$\vec{B}(\vec{r}) = \nabla\times\vec{A}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\nabla\times\int dv'\frac{\vec{J}(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int dv'\,\nabla\times\left(\frac{\vec{J}(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}\right)$$

---

## Page 106 (Slide 106) — From Vector Potential to Magnetic Field [106]

Using product rule $\nabla\times(f\vec{A}) = (\nabla f)\times\vec{A} + f\nabla\times\vec{A}$:

$$\nabla\times\left(\frac{\vec{J}(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}\right) = \left(\nabla\frac{1}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}\right)\times\vec{J}(\vec{r}') + \frac{1}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}\underbrace{\nabla\times\vec{J}(\vec{r}')}_{=0} = -\vec{J}(\vec{r}')\times\left(\nabla\frac{1}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}\right)$$

Since $\nabla\left(\dfrac{1}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}\right) = -\dfrac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^3} = -\dfrac{1}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2}\dfrac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$:

$$\nabla\times\left(\frac{\vec{J}(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}\right) = \frac{\vec{J}(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2}\times\frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$

## Page 107 (Slide 107) — From Vector Potential to Magnetic Field [107]

$$\vec{B}(\vec{r}) = \nabla\times\vec{A}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int dv'\,\nabla\times\left(\frac{\vec{J}(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}\right) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int dv'\frac{\vec{J}(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2}\times\frac{\vec{r}-\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|} = \vec{B}(\vec{r})\ \checkmark$$

This confirms the vector potential formulation is consistent with the Biot–Savart Law.

## Page 110 (Slide 110) — Scalar Potential of a Uniformly Charged Straight Line [110]

A straight line charge with uniform linear density $\lambda$ along the $z$-axis from $-a$ to $+a$.

Setup:
- $\vec{r} = x\hat{x} + z\hat{z}$, $\vec{r}' = z'\hat{z}$
- $\vec{r}-\vec{r}' = x\hat{x} + (z-z')\hat{z}$
- $|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'| = \sqrt{x^2+(z-z')^2}$
- $dq = \lambda\,dz'$

$$d\phi(x,z) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\lambda\,dz'}{\sqrt{x^2+(z-z')^2}}$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \phi(x,z) = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_{-a}^{a}\frac{dz'}{\sqrt{x^2+(z-z')^2}}$$

---

## Page 111 (Slide 111) — Integration [111]

**Indefinite integral** (Dwight 200.01):
$$\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} = \log\!\left(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}\right)$$

**Verification:**
$$\frac{d}{dx}\log\!\left(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}\right) = \frac{1}{x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}}\left(1+\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}}\right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}}\ \checkmark$$

---

## Page 112 (Slide 112) — Scalar Potential of a Uniformly Charged Line [112]

The integral $I = \int_{-a}^{a}\dfrac{dz'}{\sqrt{x^2+(z-z')^2}} = \int_{-a}^{a}\dfrac{dz'}{\sqrt{x^2+(z'-z)^2}}$

Substitution: $t = z'-z$, $dt = dz'$

When $z'=-a$: $t=-a-z$; when $z'=a$: $t=a-z$.

$$I = \int_{-a-z}^{a-z}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{x^2+t^2}}$$

---

## Page 113 (Slide 113) — Scalar Potential of a Uniformly Charged Line [113]

$$I = \int_{-a-z}^{a-z}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{x^2+t^2}} = \left[\log\!\left(t+\sqrt{t^2+x^2}\right)\right]_{-a-z}^{a-z}$$

$$= \log\!\left(a-z+\sqrt{(a-z)^2+x^2}\right) - \log\!\left(-a-z+\sqrt{(a+z)^2+x^2}\right)$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \phi(x,z) = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\log\!\left(a-z+\sqrt{(a-z)^2+x^2}\right) - \log\!\left(-a-z+\sqrt{(a+z)^2+x^2}\right)\right]$$

---

## Page 114 (Slide 114) — Scalar Potential (3D Extension) [114]

For a point in the $xz$-plane ($y=0$):
$$\phi(x,0,z) = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\log\!\left(a-z+\sqrt{(a-z)^2+x^2}\right) - \log\!\left(-a-z+\sqrt{(a+z)^2+x^2}\right)\right]$$

Extending to a general point $(x,y,z)$ (replacing $x^2 \to x^2+y^2$):
$$\phi(x,y,z) = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\log\!\left(a-z+\sqrt{(a-z)^2+x^2+y^2}\right) - \log\!\left(-a-z+\sqrt{(a+z)^2+x^2+y^2}\right)\right]$$

---

## Page 115 (Slide 115) — Scalar Potential → Electric Field [115]

Let $I_a(x,y,z) = \log\!\left(a-z+\sqrt{(a-z)^2+x^2+y^2}\right) - \log\!\left(-a-z+\sqrt{(a+z)^2+x^2+y^2}\right)$

Then $\phi(x,y,z) = \dfrac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0}I_a(x,y,z)$

$$\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = -\nabla\phi(\vec{r}) = -\frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\nabla I_a(\vec{r}) = -\frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\hat{x}\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial x} + \hat{y}\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial y} + \hat{z}\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial z}\right)$$

---

## Page 116 (Slide 116) — Scalar Potential → Electric Field [116]

$$\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial x} = \frac{\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{(a-z)^2+x^2+y^2}}}{a-z+\sqrt{(a-z)^2+x^2+y^2}} - \frac{\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{(a+z)^2+x^2+y^2}}}{-a-z+\sqrt{(a+z)^2+x^2+y^2}}$$

$$\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial y} = \frac{\dfrac{y}{\sqrt{(a-z)^2+x^2+y^2}}}{a-z+\sqrt{(a-z)^2+x^2+y^2}} - \frac{\dfrac{y}{\sqrt{(a+z)^2+x^2+y^2}}}{-a-z+\sqrt{(a+z)^2+x^2+y^2}}$$

---

## Page 117 (Slide 117) — Scalar Potential → Electric Field [117]

$$\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial z} = \frac{-1-\dfrac{a-z}{\sqrt{(a-z)^2+x^2+y^2}}}{a-z+\sqrt{(a-z)^2+x^2+y^2}} - \frac{-1+\dfrac{a+z}{\sqrt{(a+z)^2+x^2+y^2}}}{-a-z+\sqrt{(a+z)^2+x^2+y^2}}$$

$$= -\frac{1}{\sqrt{(a-z)^2+x^2+y^2}} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{(a+z)^2+x^2+y^2}}$$

---

## Page 118 (Slide 118) — Scalar Potential → Electric Field [118]

Summary of partial derivatives and finding the electric field at $z=0$.

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0}I_a(\vec{r}), \qquad \vec{E}(\vec{r}) = -\frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\hat{x}\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial x} + \hat{y}\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial y} + \hat{z}\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial z}\right)$$

**Goal: find the electric field at $z=0$.**

---

## Page 119 (Slide 119) — Scalar Potential → Electric Field [119]

At $z=0$:

$$\left.\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial x}\right|_{z=0} = \frac{x}{\sqrt{a^2+x^2+y^2}}\cdot\frac{1}{a+\sqrt{a^2+x^2+y^2}} - \frac{x}{\sqrt{a^2+x^2+y^2}}\cdot\frac{1}{-a+\sqrt{a^2+x^2+y^2}}$$

$$= \frac{x}{\sqrt{a^2+x^2+y^2}}\cdot\frac{-2a}{(x^2+y^2)} = -\frac{2x}{(x^2+y^2)\sqrt{1+\dfrac{x^2+y^2}{a^2}}}$$

$$\left.\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial y}\right|_{z=0} = -\frac{2y}{(x^2+y^2)\sqrt{1+\dfrac{x^2+y^2}{a^2}}}$$

---

## Page 120 (Slide 120) — Scalar Potential → Electric Field [120]

At $z=0$:
$$\left.\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial z}\right|_{z=0} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2+x^2+y^2}} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2+x^2+y^2}} = 0$$

---

## Page 121 (Slide 121) — Scalar Potential → Electric Field [121]

$$\vec{E}(x,y,0) = -\frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\hat{x}\left.\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial x}\right|_{z=0} + \hat{y}\left.\frac{\partial I_a}{\partial y}\right|_{z=0} + \hat{z}\cdot 0\right)$$

$$= \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{1}{(x^2+y^2)\sqrt{1+\dfrac{x^2+y^2}{a^2}}}(x\hat{x}+y\hat{y}) = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\hat{\rho}}{\rho}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\dfrac{\rho^2}{a^2}}}$$

For an **infinitely long** line charge ($a\to\infty$):
$$\boxed{\vec{E}(\rho,\theta) = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\hat{\rho}}{\rho}}$$

---

## Page 122 (Slide 122) — Unit Vector Transformation [122]

In cylindrical coordinates:
$$x = \rho\cos\theta, \quad y = \rho\sin\theta, \quad \rho = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}$$

$$\hat{\rho} = \cos\theta\,\hat{x} + \sin\theta\,\hat{y} = \frac{x\hat{x}+y\hat{y}}{\rho}$$

$$\hat{\theta} = -\sin\theta\,\hat{x} + \cos\theta\,\hat{y} = \frac{-y\hat{x}+x\hat{y}}{\rho}$$

*(Reference: Section 03G)*

## Page 123 (Slide 123) — Scalar Potential → Electric Field [123]

Compact form of the electric field:
$$\vec{E}(x,y,0) = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\hat{\rho}}{\rho}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\dfrac{\rho^2}{a^2}}}$$

For **infinitely long** line charge ($a\to\infty$):
$$\boxed{\vec{E}(\rho,\theta) = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\hat{\rho}}{\rho}} \quad \checkmark$$

## Page 127 (Slide 127) — Vector Potential → Magnetic Field [127]

$$\vec{A}(x,y,z) = \frac{\mu_0 i\hat{z}}{4\pi}I_a(x,y,z) \Rightarrow A_x = A_y = 0, \quad A_z = \frac{\mu_0 i}{4\pi}I_a$$

## Page 134 (Slide 134) — Multipole Expansion of Scalar Potential [134]

Using $(1+\alpha)^n \approx 1+n\alpha$ for $|\alpha|\ll 1$:

$$|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|^2 = r^2\left[1+\left(-\frac{2\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}'}{r^2}+\frac{r'^2}{r^2}\right)\right]$$

$$\frac{1}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|} \approx \frac{1}{r}+\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}'}{r^3}+\cdots$$

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_V dv'\,\rho(\vec{r}')\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}'}{r^3}+\cdots\right) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac{q}{r}+\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3}+\cdots\right)$$

## Page 135 (Slide 135) — Multipole Expansion of Scalar Potential [135]

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac{q}{r}+\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3}+\cdots\right)$$

- **Monopole moment:** $q = \int_V dv'\,\rho(\vec{r}')$ → $\phi_\text{mono}(\vec{r}) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{r}$

- **Dipole moment:** $\vec{p} = \int_V dv'\,\rho(\vec{r}')\vec{r}'$ → $\phi_\text{dipole}(\vec{r}) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3}$

## Page 136 (Slide 136) — Multipole Expansion of Vector Potential [136]

**Conditions:**
1. Current distribution $\vec{J}(\vec{r}')$ is confined to a finite region.
2. Find the vector potential far from the source ($r \gg r'$).

**Vector potential:**
$$\vec{A}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_V\frac{id\vec{r}'}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int_V dv'\frac{\vec{J}(\vec{r}')}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}'|}$$

## Page 137 (Slide 137) — Multipole Expansion of Vector Potential [137]

Using the same approximation:

$$\vec{A}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\left[\underbrace{\left(\int_V dv'\,\vec{J}(\vec{r}')\right)}_{\text{monopole}}\frac{1}{r} + \underbrace{\left(\int_V dv'\,\vec{J}(\vec{r}')\vec{r}'\right)}_{\text{dipole}}\cdot\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}+\cdots\right]$$

Compare with scalar potential:
$$\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\underbrace{\left(\int_V dv'\,\rho(\vec{r}')\right)}_{\text{monopole}}\frac{1}{r} + \underbrace{\left(\int_V dv'\,\rho(\vec{r}')\vec{r}'\right)}_{\text{dipole}}\cdot\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}+\cdots\right]$$

## Page 138 (Slide 138) — Magnetic Monopole Moment [138]

**Magnetic monopole moment:**
$$\int_V dv'\,\vec{J}(\vec{r}') = \oint_C i\,d\vec{r}' = 0$$

because $\oint_C d\vec{r}' = \hat{x}\int_{x_0}^{x_0}dx' + \hat{y}\int_{y_0}^{y_0}dy + \hat{z}\int_{z_0}^{z_0}dz' = 0$

**There is no magnetic monopole!**

## Page 139 (Slide 139) — Magnetic Dipole Moment [139]

**Magnetic dipole term:**
$$\left(\int_V dv'\,\vec{J}(\vec{r}')\vec{r}'\right)\cdot\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3} = \left(\oint_C id\vec{r}'\,\vec{r}'\right)\cdot\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}$$

---

## Page 140 (Slide 140) — Integration [140]

Using the **vector triple product**: $(\vec{A}\times\vec{B})\times\vec{C} = -\vec{A}(\vec{B}\cdot\vec{C}) + \vec{B}(\vec{A}\cdot\vec{C})$:

$$(\vec{r}'\times d\vec{r}')\times\vec{r} = -\vec{r}'(d\vec{r}'\cdot\vec{r}) + d\vec{r}'(\vec{r}'\cdot\vec{r}) \quad \text{...(1)}$$

$$d[\vec{r}'(\vec{r}'\cdot\vec{r})] = d\vec{r}'(\vec{r}'\cdot\vec{r}) + \vec{r}'(d\vec{r}'\cdot\vec{r}) \quad \text{...(2)}$$

Adding (1) and (2):
$$d\vec{r}'(\vec{r}'\cdot\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{2}(\vec{r}'\times d\vec{r}')\times\vec{r} + \frac{1}{2}d[\vec{r}'(\vec{r}'\cdot\vec{r})]$$

Integrating over closed loop $C$ (the total differential integrates to zero):
$$\frac{i}{r^3}\oint_C d\vec{r}'(\vec{r}'\cdot\vec{r}) = \left(\frac{i}{2}\oint_C \vec{r}'\times d\vec{r}'\right)\times\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}$$

---

## Page 141 (Slide 141) — Magnetic Dipole Moment [141]

$$\vec{A}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\left[\cdots + \left(\int_V dv'\,\vec{J}(\vec{r}')\vec{r}'\right)\cdot\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}+\cdots\right]$$

The dipole term:
$$\left(\int_V dv'\,\vec{J}(\vec{r}')\vec{r}'\right)\cdot\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3} = \left(\oint_C id\vec{r}'\,\vec{r}'\right)\cdot\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3} = \left(\frac{i}{2}\oint_C \vec{r}'\times d\vec{r}'\right)\times\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3} = \frac{\vec{\mu}\times\vec{r}}{r^3}$$

where the **magnetic dipole moment** is:
$$\boxed{\vec{\mu} = \frac{i}{2}\oint_C \vec{r}'\times d\vec{r}'}$$

**Vector potential of a magnetic dipole:**
$$\boxed{\vec{A}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{\vec{\mu}\times\vec{r}}{r^3}}$$

For a circular current loop $i$ of radius $a$ in the $xy$-plane:

$$\vec{\mu} = i\vec{S}$$

*(Diagram: Circular loop with area vector $\vec{S}$ pointing upward and current $i$ flowing counterclockwise.)*

## Page 143 (Slide 143) — Integration [143]

$$\oint_C \vec{r}\times d\vec{r} = \int_0^{2\pi}a^2d\theta\,\hat{z} = a^2\hat{z}\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta = 2\pi a^2\hat{z}$$

## Page 149 (Slide 149) — Magnetic Field of a Magnetic Dipole [149]

**Vector potential** of magnetic dipole $\vec{\mu}$ at the origin:
$$\vec{A}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{\vec{\mu}\times\vec{r}}{r^3}$$

**Magnetic field:** $\vec{B}(\vec{r}) = \nabla\times\vec{A}(\vec{r})$

Using the identity $\nabla\times(\vec{A}\times\vec{B}) = \vec{A}(\nabla\cdot\vec{B}) - \vec{B}(\nabla\cdot\vec{A}) + (\vec{B}\cdot\nabla)\vec{A} - (\vec{A}\cdot\nabla)\vec{B}$:

$$\nabla\times\left(\vec{\mu}\times\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\right) = \vec{\mu}\left(\nabla\cdot\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\right) - \frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}(\nabla\cdot\vec{\mu}) + \left(\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\cdot\nabla\right)\vec{\mu} - (\vec{\mu}\cdot\nabla)\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3} = -(\vec{\mu}\cdot\nabla)\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}$$

Computing each term:
$$\mu_x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\right) = \mu_x\left(\frac{\hat{x}}{r^3} - 3\vec{r}\frac{x}{r^5}\right)$$

---

## Page 150 (Slide 150) — Magnetic Field of a Magnetic Dipole [150]

$$\mu_x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\right) = \mu_x\left(\frac{\hat{x}}{r^3} - 3\vec{r}\frac{x}{r^5}\right), \quad \mu_y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\left(\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\right) = \mu_y\left(\frac{\hat{y}}{r^3} - 3\vec{r}\frac{y}{r^5}\right), \quad \mu_z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\right) = \mu_z\left(\frac{\hat{z}}{r^3} - 3\vec{r}\frac{z}{r^5}\right)$$

$$\nabla\times\left(\vec{\mu}\times\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\right) = -(\vec{\mu}\cdot\nabla)\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3} = \frac{3(\vec{\mu}\cdot\hat{r})\hat{r}}{r^3} - \frac{\vec{\mu}}{r^3} = \frac{3(\vec{\mu}\cdot\hat{r})\hat{r}-\vec{\mu}}{r^3}$$

$$\boxed{\vec{B}(\vec{r}) = \nabla\times\vec{A}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{3(\vec{\mu}\cdot\hat{r})\hat{r}-\vec{\mu}}{r^3}}$$

## Page 151 (Slide 151) — Electric Dipole in a Uniform Electric Field [151]

**Net force on electric dipole:**
$$\vec{F} = \vec{F}_q + \vec{F}_{-q} = q\vec{E} + (-q)\vec{E} = 0$$

**Torque on electric dipole:**
$$\vec{\tau} = \vec{r}_1\times\vec{F}_{-q} + \vec{r}_2\times\vec{F}_q = \vec{r}_1\times(-q\vec{E}) + \vec{r}_2\times(q\vec{E})$$

$$= q(\vec{r}_2-\vec{r}_1)\times\vec{E} = q\vec{d}\times\vec{E}$$

$$\boxed{\vec{\tau} = \vec{p}\times\vec{E}}$$

*(Reference: Section 15F)*

## Page 152 (Slide 152) — Magnetic Dipole in a Uniform Magnetic Field [152]

A rectangular current loop of sides $a$ and $b$ in a uniform field $\vec{B} = B\hat{y}$, tilted at angle $\theta$.

Forces on each segment:
$$\vec{F}_1 = i\vec{a}\times\vec{B} = iaB\sin(90°-\theta)\hat{x} = iaB\cos\theta\,\hat{x}$$
$$\vec{F}_2 = -iaB\cos\theta\,\hat{x}$$
$$\vec{F}_3 = ibB\hat{z}$$
$$\vec{F}_4 = -ibB\hat{z}$$

**Net force on magnetic dipole:**
$$\vec{F} = \vec{F}_1+\vec{F}_2+\vec{F}_3+\vec{F}_4 = 0$$

---

## Page 153 (Slide 153) — Magnetic Dipole in a Uniform Magnetic Field [153]

**Torques:**
$$\vec{\tau}_1 = 0, \quad \vec{\tau}_2 = 0$$

$$\vec{\tau}_3 = \vec{r}_3\times\vec{F}_3 = (-x\sin\theta\,\hat{y}+x\cos\theta\,\hat{z})\times(ibB\hat{z}) = -ibBx\sin\theta\,\hat{x}$$

$$\vec{\tau}_4 = \vec{r}_4\times\vec{F}_4 = ((a-x)\sin\theta\,\hat{y}-(a-x)\cos\theta\,\hat{z})\times(-ibB\hat{z}) = -ibB(a-x)\sin\theta\,\hat{x}$$

**Total torque on magnetic dipole:**
$$\vec{\tau} = \vec{\tau}_1+\vec{\tau}_2+\vec{\tau}_3+\vec{\tau}_4 = -iabB\sin\theta\,\hat{x}$$

---

## Page 154 (Slide 154) — Magnetic Dipole in a Uniform Magnetic Field [154]

With $\vec{B} = B\hat{y}$ and $\vec{\mu} = i\vec{S} = iab\hat{n} = iab(\cos\theta\,\hat{y}+\sin\theta\,\hat{z})$:

$$\vec{\mu}\times\vec{B} = iab(\cos\theta\,\hat{y}+\sin\theta\,\hat{z})\times B\hat{y} = -iabB\sin\theta\,\hat{x}$$

$$\boxed{\vec{\tau} = \vec{\mu}\times\vec{B}}$$
