**MATERIAL SYNTHESIS FOR TUNABLE RADIATIVE COOLING AND HEATING**

Passive radiative cooling has emerged as a compelling strategy for sustainable thermal management, enabling heat dissipation into outer space through the atmospheric transparency window (8–13 µm) without any energy input. Despite significant progress, practical deployment remains constrained by the inability of most coolers to dynamically adapt to changing environmental conditions. This research addresses these limitations by developing electrochemically tunable radiative cooling and heating materials. Key enabling technologies include atomic layer deposition (ALD), CO₂ laser processing, and electrochromic switching.

The material platform is based on carbon substrates, including carbon cloth and laser-induced graphene, which serve as flexible, conductive, and thermally stable scaffolds. CO₂ laser irradiation will be applied to carbonize precursor films or anneal deposited layers, enabling precise tuning of crystallinity, porosity, and infrared optical response. Building upon these engineered substrates, ALD will be employed as the primary thin-film growth technique to deposit conformal oxide layers, including TiO₂, HfO₂, and WO₃, as well as ultrathin metal film such as platinum with atomic-level thickness control to engineer spectral selectivity within the atmospheric window.

A defining innovation of this research lies in the integration of electrochromic oxide films, particularly WO₃, NiO, and V₂O₅, which undergo reversible optical changes upon electrochemical stimulation. This enables dynamic switching of mid-infrared emissivity between a low-emissivity heating mode and a high-emissivity cooling mode by applying a small voltage, opening new possibilities for smart buildings and wearable thermal management. The ultimate targets include solar reflectance exceeding 0.93, tunable mid-infrared emissivity from below 0.30 to above 0.95, and a net cooling power of over 100 W/m².[[SL1]](#_msocom_1)
