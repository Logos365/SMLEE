**Proposal for Study**

1\. Please describe in detail your motivations for seeking further learning at this institution. (0/1600)

I wanted to pursue the Integrative program at UST-KIMM to apply my background in solid-state physics to functional materials and devices. Holding a Bachelor’s degree in Physics Education from the High-Quality Program at Hanoi National University of Education (HNUE), I completed specialized courses and my graduation thesis in Solid State Physics,

gaining hands-on experience in thin-film fabrication, interface engineering, and characterization with XRD, VSM, and four-point probe systems. During my undergraduate period, I studied magnetic anisotropy in multilayered spin valves. This allowed me to co-author a paper in Thin Solid Films, which encouraged me to be curious about how atomic-level interface tailoring controls macroscopic device performance.

UST’s research environment is the perfect fit to connect fundamental materials science with engineering. By training students inside national research institutes, UST links theory directly with practice. Korea Institute of Machinery and Materials (KIMM) is a leader in precision micro/nano-manufacturing. I am particularly motivated to join Professor Seung-Mo Lee’s laboratory. His pioneering work on atomic-layer-deposited (ALD) coatings, functional thin films, and interfacial engineering for advanced energy and composite materials perfectly aligns with my background and research ambition. Under Professor Lee’s guidance, I hope to use my background in physics to contribute effectively to his lab and develop my research skills at KIMM.

2\. Among the courses you have completed (or academic fields you have experienced) at the undergraduate /master’s level, which was the one that interested you the most and what are your accomplishments in the field? (0/1600)

When I look back at the time I spent in college, the area of study that interested me was Solid State Physics, Solid State Optics and Semiconductor‑Magnetic Materials. These subjects helped me understand how quantum confinement and spin dynamics shape behavior on a small scale, inspiring me to pursue nanoscale materials research.

The biggest achievement I had in this area was investigating anisotropy in spin valves. In my thesis I studied how the thickness of the Cu spacer changes the interlayer exchange coupling in Co/Pd multilayered films. By altering these layers my team understood the spin coupling mechanisms and this work led to a paper that I co‑authored in Thin Solid Films. That moment was very special for me. During this time I gained a lot of experience with equipment such as the Bruker D8 Advance XRD, VersaLab VSM and four‑point probe systems. I also used MATLAB and Origin to process data. I co‑wrote another paper for the TNU Journal of Science and Technology. With this experience, in films and semiconductors I feel completely ready to begin nanoscale device research at KIMM.

3\. Please describe your desired career path upon graduation in detail. (0/1600)

When looking ahead to my professional future, my desired career path upon graduating from the UST–KIMM program is to establish myself as a dedicated research scientist specializing in functional thin films, atomic-scale coatings, and nanoscale interface engineering.

In the short term, during the first few years after completing my degree, my primary focus will be on deepening my expertise through intensive R&D work and advanced projects. I plan to focus on improving my skills through lots of research and development work. I want to write high-impact papers about what happens at the nanoscale, learn how to make thin films and use what I learn to solve real problems in engineering that connect solid-state physics with real semiconductor uses.

Over the long term, I aspire to take on a senior R&D role at a leading global research institute or within the high-tech materials industry. Building closely on the research experience gained under Professor Seung-Mo Lee and leveraging KIMM's extensive network, I want to lead innovative projects that successfully integrate atomic coatings into energy-efficient nano-devices. Ultimately, my vision is to act as an active bridge for international scientific collaborations, helping to drive meaningful technological progress in nanomaterials fabrication and materials physics.
