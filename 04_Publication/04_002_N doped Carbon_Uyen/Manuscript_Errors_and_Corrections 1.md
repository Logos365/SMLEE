## I. Critical Errors (Must Fix)

### 🔴 C-1 | Missing word in critical sentence — p. 17, Section 3.3

**Location:** Section 3.3, DFT Calculations paragraph  
**Original:**
> "these results suggest that pyridinic nitrogen is particularly favorable **the key requirements** for pseudocapacitive redox activity"

**Problem:** The word "for" (or a verb phrase) is missing between "favorable" and "the key requirements." The sentence is syntactically broken.

**Correction options:**
> **Option A:** "…is particularly favorable **for satisfying** the key requirements for pseudocapacitive redox activity"  
> **Option B:** "…is particularly **well-suited to meet** the key requirements for pseudocapacitive redox activity"  
> **Option C (recommended):** "…**uniquely satisfies** the key requirements for pseudocapacitive redox activity in acidic electrolyte"

## II. Major Grammatical Errors

### 🟠 G-1 | Tense inconsistency (past → present) — pp. 11–12, Section 3.1

The manuscript consistently uses **present tense** throughout the Results & Discussion section, but two sentences revert to past tense without reason:

**Instance 1 — p. 11:**
> "The slight narrowing of both bands at 900 °C **suggested** increased ordering within the disordered carbon network [31]."

→ **Correction:** "…**suggests** increased ordering…"

**Instance 2 — p. 12:**
> "Collectively, these observations **suggested** that g-C₃N₄-assisted pyrolysis enables precise control over the type and spatial distribution of nitrogen functionalities."

→ **Correction:** "…these observations **suggest** that g-C₃N₄-assisted pyrolysis enables…"

---

### 🟠 G-2 | Capitalization error mid-sentence — p. 17, Section 3.3

**Original:**
> "**Also, Proton** adsorption at carbon sites neighboring each nitrogen site was evaluated…"

**Problems:** (1) "Proton" should not be capitalized mid-sentence; (2) "Also," is too informal for this context.

**Correction:**
> "**Additionally, proton** adsorption at carbon sites neighboring each nitrogen site was evaluated…"

---

### 🟠 G-3 | Subject-verb agreement — p. 17, Section 3.3

**Original:**
> "The weaker adsorption at adjacent carbon sites, compared to direct adsorption at pyridinic-N, further **support** the site-specific preference for proton binding at pyridinic nitrogen (Figure S10)."

**Problem:** The singular subject "The weaker adsorption" requires the singular verb "supports."

**Correction:**
> "…further **supports** the site-specific preference for proton binding at pyridinic nitrogen (Figure S10)."

---

### 🟠 G-4 | Possible duplicate reference — [38] and [47]

**Concern:** Both [38] and [47] appear to cite a paper by Qiao et al. (2016) from *Journal of Materials Chemistry A*, volume 4, pages 12658–12666 — the same journal, volume, and page numbers — but with different author lists:

### 🟡 F-4 | Inconsistent spacing around % sign

**Issue:** The manuscript uses both "89 %" (with space) and "89%" (without space) in different locations.

**Examples of inconsistency:**
- Abstract: "89 % capacitance retention" (with space)
- p. 13: "89.2 % of its initial capacitance" (with space)
- Table S2: mixed styles

**Recommendation:** Choose one style and apply consistently throughout. *Applied Surface Science* typically follows SI convention with a space before the unit symbol (i.e., "89 %" is technically correct per SI), but the most important thing is internal consistency.

## IV. Stylistic and Clarity Suggestions

### 🔵 S-1 | Slightly awkward phrasing — p. 3, Introduction

**Original:**
> "systematic studies that quantitatively link each nitrogen configuration to electrochemical performance appear to be still lacking"

**Suggestion (more direct and confident):**
> "systematic studies that quantitatively link each nitrogen configuration to electrochemical performance **are still largely lacking**"

---

### 🔵 S-2 | Incorrect preposition — p. 4, Introduction

**Original:**
> "Solid-state doping with g-C₃N₄ allows more precise control **of** the crystalline framework and morphology."

**Correction:** "control **of** something" means examining or managing it; "control **over** something" means having authority or influence over it.

> "…allows more precise control **over** the crystalline framework and morphology."

---

### 🔵 S-3 | Redundant phrasing in Introduction — p. 3-4

**Original:**
> "Solid-state doping with g-C₃N₄ allows more precise control of the crystalline framework and morphology. As a result, nitrogen can be introduced in a more controllable manner…"

**Issue:** Both sentences make essentially the same point (controllable nitrogen introduction). Consider consolidating:

> "Solid-state doping with g-C₃N₄ enables more precise control over nitrogen incorporation, reducing undesirable side reactions associated with gaseous precursors while better preserving the structural integrity of the carbon framework."

---

### 🔵 S-4 | Non-standard terminology — p. 12

**Original:**
> "**over-graphitized** NCF-1000 suffered from partial pore collapse"

**Suggestion:** "Over-graphitized" is not a standard term in the field. Consider:
> "**excessively graphitized** NCF-1000 suffered from partial pore collapse"

---

### 🔵 S-5 | Flow issue — paragraph placement in Section 3.2 (p. 13)

**Issue:** The paragraph beginning:
> "As illustrated in Figure 4c, the development of mesopores appears to be closely associated with the type and spatial distribution of nitrogen functionalities. Pyridinic and pyrrolic nitrogen species, which are often located at edge or defect sites, likely induce local lattice distortion during carbonization…"

…appears in the middle of the Section 3.2 electrochemical performance discussion (between EIS and CV analysis). However, this paragraph discusses the **mechanism of mesopore formation**, which is a structural topic that belongs more naturally at the **end of Section 3.1** (structural characterization) or immediately before the electrochemical discussion opens, with a clear bridging sentence.

**Recommended action:** Either (a) move this paragraph to the end of Section 3.1 with a transition such as "This pore-formation mechanism provides a structural basis for the superior electrochemical performance discussed in the following section," or (b) add a stronger bridging sentence at the start of this paragraph to reconnect it to the electrochemical context.

---

### 🔵 S-6 | Cycling stability in H₂SO₄ — potential reviewer concern (p. 13 + Table S2)

**Observation:** NCF-900 retains **69.6%** of its initial capacitance after 5,000 cycles in 1 M H₂SO₄ (three-electrode, 100 mV s⁻¹). In the comparison table (Table S2), most competitor materials report **87–96.6%** retention. This disparity is not discussed in the main text.

**Recommendation:** Add a sentence in Section 3.2 or the Conclusions to acknowledge and contextualize this difference. Suggested text:

> "We note that the capacitance retention in the acidic electrolyte (69.6% after 5,000 cycles) is lower than values reported for materials evaluated in alkaline electrolytes. This difference is attributed to the more demanding electrochemical conditions in the acidic system, where reversible proton-coupled redox reactions at nitrogen sites involve greater structural perturbation per cycle. The superior retention of 89% after 10,000 cycles in 6 M KOH demonstrates the inherent durability of the NCF-900 electrode under less aggressive conditions."
