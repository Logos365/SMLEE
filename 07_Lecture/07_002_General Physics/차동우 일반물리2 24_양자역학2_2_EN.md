## Page 1 (Slide 61) — 1D Infinite Square Well: SE and Boundary Conditions

**Setting up the equation:**

Inside the well ($0 \leq x \leq a$): $-\dfrac{\hbar^2}{2m}\dfrac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} = E\psi \implies \dfrac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} + k^2\psi = 0, \quad k = \dfrac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}$

Using free-particle solutions: $\psi(x) = e^{+ikx}$ or $\psi(x) = e^{-ikx}$

**General solution:** $\psi(x) = Ae^{+ikx} + Be^{-ikx}$ (constants $A$, $B$ determined from boundary conditions)

**Boundary conditions** (wave function must vanish at walls):

- At $x = 0$: $\psi(0) = A + B = \psi_{<}(0) = 0 \quad (1)$
- At $x = a$: $\psi(a) = Ae^{+ika} + Be^{-ika} = \psi_{>}(a) = 0 \quad (2)$

---

## Page 2 (Slide 62) — 1D Infinite Square Well: Energy Quantization

**Applying the boundary conditions:**

From (1): $B = -A$

Substituting into (2): $A(e^{+ika} - e^{-ika}) = 2iA\sin(ka) = 0$

Using $\sin\theta = \dfrac{e^{i\theta} - e^{-i\theta}}{2i}$

Since $A \neq 0$: $\quad ka = n\pi \quad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

**Allowed wave numbers:** $k_n = \dfrac{n\pi}{a}$

**Energy eigenvalues — quantized!**

$$E_n = \frac{\hbar^2 k_n^2}{2m} = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{a}\right)^2$$

> The energy of a particle confined in an infinite well is **quantized**.

---

## Page 3 (Slide 63) — Free Particle vs. Particle in Infinite Well: Energy Comparison

**Free particle** (wave number $k$ unrestricted):

$$E_k = \frac{\hbar^2 k^2}{2m} \quad \text{(continuous — no restriction on }k\text{, energy takes all positive values)}$$

**Particle in 1D infinite well** (wave number $k_n = n\pi/a$):

$$E_n = \frac{\hbar^2 k_n^2}{2m} = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\!\left(\frac{n\pi}{a}\right)^2 \quad \text{(discrete — energy is quantized)}$$

> **When a particle's motion is confined within a finite region, its energy is quantized.**

---

## Page 4 (Slide 64) — 1D Infinite Square Well: Eigenfunctions

**Schrödinger equation with energy eigenvalue $E_n$:**

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi_n}{dx^2} = E_n\psi_n$$

- The energy eigenvalue $E_n$ is the value for which the SE has physical solutions
- Only one solution exists for each energy eigenvalue $E_n$

**Energy eigenvalue:** $E_n = \dfrac{\hbar^2 k_n^2}{2m}$

**Eigenfunction (unnormalized):**

$$\psi_n(x) = A\sin k_n x = A\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}$$

---

## Page 5 (Slide 65) — 1D Infinite Square Well: Normalization Constant

$$\psi_n(x) = A\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}$$

Although $A$ can be arbitrary, we determine $A$ so that the orthonormal property $\langle n|n'\rangle = \delta_{ii'}$ is satisfied — this is called **normalization**.

**Computing the normalization:**

$$\langle n|n\rangle = \int_0^a dx\,|A|^2\sin^2\frac{n\pi x}{a} = |A|^2\frac{a}{2} = 1 \implies A = \sqrt{\frac{2}{a}}$$

**Normalized eigenfunction:**

$$\boxed{\psi_n(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{a}}\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}}$$

---

## Page 8 (Slide 68) — Potential Barrier: Classical World

**Classical world:** kinetic energy $K = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}$, potential energy $V = mgy$

*Diagram: A hill (potential barrier) of height $V$; three scenarios with a sled approaching from the left:*

- If $K < V$: the sled **cannot** cross the hill
- If $K > V$: the sled **easily crosses** the hill

This energy barrier is called a **potential barrier**.

---

## Page 9 (Slide 69) — Potential Barrier: Quantum World (Tunnel Effect)

**Quantum world:** particle with energy $E$ approaching a rectangular barrier of height $V_0$ and width $a$

*Diagram: Three cases — free particle (no barrier), $E > V_0$, and $E < V_0$*

- If $E > V_0$: **some is reflected, some is transmitted** (even classically, reflection occurs due to wave nature)
- If $E < V_0$: **still some is reflected and some is transmitted** ← this is the **Tunnel Effect**!

> Even when $E < V_0$, partial transmission through the barrier is the **tunnel effect**.

---

## Page 10 (Slide 70) — 1D Potential Barrier Problem

**Problem:** A particle of mass $m$ with energy $E < V_0$ approaching a 1D barrier from the left.

**Potential:**

$$V(x) = V_0 \;\text{ where }\; 0 \leq x \leq a; \qquad V(x) = 0 \;\text{ where }\; x < 0 \;\text{ or }\; x > a$$

**Set up Schrödinger equations in three regions:**

- $x < 0$ ($\psi_1$): $-\dfrac{\hbar^2}{2m}\dfrac{d^2\psi_1}{dx^2} = E\psi_1$
- $0 \leq x \leq a$ ($\psi_2$): $-\dfrac{\hbar^2}{2m}\dfrac{d^2\psi_2}{dx^2} + V_0\psi_2 = E\psi_2$
- $x > a$ ($\psi_3$): $-\dfrac{\hbar^2}{2m}\dfrac{d^2\psi_3}{dx^2} = E\psi_3$

*Diagram: Potential $V(x)$ with rectangular barrier; wave functions $\psi_1$, $\psi_2$, $\psi_3$ sketched in each region*

---

## Page 11 (Slide 71) — Schrödinger Equations and Solutions (Three Regions)

| Region | Equation | $k$ or $\kappa$ | Solution |
|--------|----------|-----------------|----------|
| $x < 0$ | $\dfrac{d^2\psi_1}{dx^2} + k^2\psi_1 = 0$ | $k = \dfrac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}$ | $\psi_1(x) = Ae^{ikx} + Be^{-ikx}$ |
| $0 \leq x \leq a$ | $\dfrac{d^2\psi_2}{dx^2} - \kappa^2\psi_2 = 0$ | $\kappa = \dfrac{\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}}{\hbar}$ | $\psi_2(x) = Ce^{\kappa x} + De^{-\kappa x}$ |
| $x > a$ | $\dfrac{d^2\psi_3}{dx^2} + k^2\psi_3 = 0$ | $k = \dfrac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}$ | $\psi_3(x) = Fe^{ikx}$ (transmitted only) |

- $Ae^{ikx}$: incident wave; $Be^{-ikx}$: reflected wave; $Fe^{ikx}$: transmitted wave
- No left-moving wave in region 3 (no source from the right)

---

## Page 12 (Slide 72) — Boundary Conditions

**Boundary conditions for a 2nd-order DE in position:**

1. The dependent variable (wave function) is **continuous** at each boundary
2. The first derivative of the dependent variable is **continuous** at each boundary

**Applied boundary conditions:**

At $x = 0$:
$$\psi_1(0) = \psi_2(0), \qquad \psi_1'(0) = \psi_2'(0)$$

At $x = a$:
$$\psi_2(a) = \psi_3(a), \qquad \psi_2'(a) = \psi_3'(a)$$

---

## Page 13 (Slide 73) — Applying the Boundary Conditions

**Wave functions:** $\psi_1 = Ae^{ikx} + Be^{-ikx}$, $\quad\psi_2 = Ce^{\kappa x} + De^{-\kappa x}$, $\quad\psi_3 = Fe^{ikx}$

From the four boundary conditions:

$$\psi_1(0) = \psi_2(0): \quad A + B = C + D \qquad (1)$$
$$\psi_1'(0) = \psi_2'(0): \quad ikA - ikB = \kappa C - \kappa D \qquad (2)$$
$$\psi_2(a) = \psi_3(a): \quad e^{\kappa a}C + e^{-\kappa a}D = e^{ika}F \qquad (3)$$
$$\psi_2'(a) = \psi_3'(a): \quad \kappa e^{\kappa a}C - \kappa e^{-\kappa a}D = ike^{ika}F \qquad (4)$$

---

## Page 14 (Slide 74) — Finding All Constants

**Rearranging the boundary equations:**

- (1): $C + D = A + B$
- (2): $C - D = i\dfrac{k}{\kappa}A - i\dfrac{k}{\kappa}B$
- (3): $C + e^{-2\kappa a}D = e^{(ik-\kappa)a}F$
- (4): $C - e^{-2\kappa a}D = \dfrac{ik}{\kappa}e^{(ik-\kappa)a}F$

**Solving the system:**

$$(1)+(2): \quad C = \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(1 + i\tfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)A + \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(1 - i\tfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)B \quad (5)$$

$$(1)-(2): \quad D = \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(1 - i\tfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)A + \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(1 + i\tfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)B \quad (6)$$

$$(3)+(4): \quad C = \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(1 + i\tfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)e^{(ik-\kappa)a}F \quad (7)$$

$$(3)-(4): \quad D = \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(1 - i\tfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)e^{(ik+\kappa)a}F \quad (8)$$

---

## Page 15 (Slide 75) — Finding $A/F$

**From (5)=(7) and (6)=(8), eliminating $C$, $D$, $B$:**

$\left(1 + i\dfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)A + \left(1 - i\dfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)B = \left(1 + i\dfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)e^{(ik-\kappa)a}F \quad (9)$

$\left(1 - i\dfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)A + \left(1 + i\dfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)B = \left(1 - i\dfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)e^{(ik+\kappa)a}F \quad (10)$

Computing $\left(1+i\dfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)(9) - \left(1-i\dfrac{k}{\kappa}\right)(10)$:

$$\frac{A}{F} = \left[\frac{1}{2} - \frac{i}{4}\!\left(\frac{\kappa}{k} - \frac{k}{\kappa}\right)\right]e^{(ik-\kappa)a} + \left[\frac{1}{2} + \frac{i}{4}\!\left(\frac{\kappa}{k} - \frac{k}{\kappa}\right)\right]e^{i(ik+\kappa)a}$$

---

## Page 16 (Slide 76) — Reflection and Transmission Probabilities

**Wave functions:** $\psi_1 = \underbrace{Ae^{ikx}}_\text{incident} + \underbrace{Be^{-ikx}}_\text{reflected}$, $\quad\psi_2 = Ce^{\kappa x} + De^{-\kappa x}$, $\quad\psi_3 = \underbrace{Fe^{ikx}}_\text{transmitted}$

**Reflection coefficient (probability):**

$$R = \frac{|\psi_1^-(x)|^2\,v_1}{|\psi_1^+(x)|^2\,v_1} = \left|\frac{B}{A}\right|^2$$

**Transmission coefficient (probability):**

$$T = \frac{|\psi_3^+(x)|^2\,v_3}{|\psi_1^+(x)|^2\,v_1} = \left|\frac{F}{A}\right|^2$$

Note: $R + T = 1$ (probability is conserved).

## Page 18 (Slide 78) — Potential Barrier Transmission Coefficient (Formula)

$$\frac{A}{F} = \left[\frac{1}{2} - \frac{i}{4}\!\left(\frac{\kappa}{k}-\frac{k}{\kappa}\right)\right]e^{(ik-\kappa)a} + \left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{4}\!\left(\frac{\kappa}{k}-\frac{k}{\kappa}\right)\right]e^{(ik+\kappa)a}$$

where $k = \dfrac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}$ and $\kappa = \dfrac{\sqrt{2m(V_0-E)}}{\hbar}$.

**When $E \ll V_0$:** $k \ll \kappa$, so

$$\frac{A}{F} \approx \left(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{i\kappa}{4k}\right)e^{(ik+\kappa)a}$$

$$\left|\frac{A}{F}\right|^2 = \left(\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{16}\frac{\kappa^2}{k^2}\right)e^{2\kappa a}$$

**Barrier transmission coefficient:**

$$\boxed{T = \left|\frac{F}{A}\right|^2 = \frac{16}{4 + \dfrac{\kappa^2}{k^2}}\,e^{-2\kappa a}}$$

## Page 20 (Slide 80) — Schrödinger Equation for the Hydrogen Atom

**3D time-dependent SE:**

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi(\vec{r},t) + V(\vec{r})\psi(\vec{r},t) = i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(\vec{r},t) \quad \longleftrightarrow \quad \vec{F}(\vec{r}) = m\frac{d^2\vec{r}}{dt^2}$$

**Potential energy of hydrogen atom (Coulomb potential):**

$$V(r) = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{e^2}{r}$$

It is convenient to use **spherical coordinates** $(r, \theta, \phi)$.

Since $V(r)$ does not depend on time, time and space can be separated:

$$\psi(\vec{r},t) = \varphi(\vec{r})f(t)$$

*Diagram: Spherical coordinate system with axes $x$, $y$, $z$; polar angle $\theta$; azimuthal angle $\phi$; radial distance $r$*

---

## Page 21 (Slide 81) — Hydrogen Atom: Time-Independent SE

**Separation $\psi(\vec{r},t) = \varphi(\vec{r})f(t)$ gives:**

- **Spatial equation:** $-\dfrac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\varphi(\vec{r}) + V(\vec{r})\varphi(\vec{r}) = E\varphi(\vec{r})$
- **Time equation:** $i\hbar\dfrac{d}{dt}f(t) = Ef(t) \implies f(t) = f_0\,e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}Et}$

**Laplacian operators:**

- Cartesian: $\nabla^2 = \dfrac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2} + \dfrac{\partial^2}{\partial y^2} + \dfrac{\partial^2}{\partial z^2}$

- Spherical: $\nabla^2 = \dfrac{1}{r^2}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r^2\dfrac{\partial}{\partial r}\right) + \dfrac{1}{r^2\sin\theta}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\!\left(\sin\theta\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right) + \dfrac{1}{r^2\sin^2\theta}\dfrac{\partial^2}{\partial\phi^2}$

> The hydrogen atom problem uses **spherical coordinates**.

---

## Page 22 (Slide 82) — Separation of Variables in Spherical Coordinates

**Full spatial SE in spherical coordinates:**

$$\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r^2\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial r}\right) + \frac{1}{r^2\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\!\left(\sin\theta\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\theta}\right) + \frac{1}{r^2\sin^2\theta}\frac{\partial^2\varphi}{\partial\phi^2} + \frac{2m}{\hbar^2}(E-V)\varphi = 0$$

**Assume** $\varphi(\vec{r}) = R(r)\Theta(\theta)\Phi(\phi)$; substitute and multiply by $\dfrac{r^2\sin^2\theta}{R\,\Theta\,\Phi}$:

$$\frac{\sin^2\theta}{R}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r^2\frac{dR}{dr}\right) + \frac{\sin\theta}{\Theta}\frac{d}{d\theta}\!\left(\sin\theta\frac{d\Theta}{d\theta}\right) + \underbrace{\frac{1}{\Phi}\frac{d^2\Phi}{d\phi^2}}_{\displaystyle= -m_l^2} + \frac{2mr^2\sin^2\theta}{\hbar^2}(E-V) = 0$$

**$\phi$-equation:**

$$\frac{d^2\Phi}{d\phi^2} + m_l^2\Phi = 0 \qquad (1)$$

---

## Page 23 (Slide 83) — Separation of Variables (Continued): $\theta$ and $r$ Equations

After extracting the $\phi$-equation, the remaining terms separate:

$$\underbrace{\frac{1}{R}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r^2\frac{dR}{dr}\right) + \frac{2mr^2}{\hbar^2}(E-V)}_{\text{depends only on }r} - \underbrace{\frac{m_l^2}{\sin^2\theta} + \frac{-1}{\Theta\sin\theta}\frac{d}{d\theta}\!\left(\sin\theta\frac{d\Theta}{d\theta}\right)}_{\text{depends only on }\theta} = l(l+1)$$

**$\theta$-equation:**

$$\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{d}{d\theta}\!\left(\sin\theta\frac{d\Theta}{d\theta}\right) + \left[l(l+1) - \frac{m_l^2}{\sin^2\theta}\right]\Theta = 0 \qquad (2)$$

**$r$-equation:**

$$\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r^2\frac{dR}{dr}\right) + \left[\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}(E-V) - \frac{l(l+1)}{r^2}\right]R = 0 \qquad (3)$$

---

## Page 24 (Slide 84) — Quantum Numbers: Magnetic Quantum Number

**$\phi$-equation (1):** $\dfrac{d^2\Phi}{d\phi^2} + m_l^2\Phi = 0$

**Solution:** $\Phi(\phi) = Ae^{im_l\phi}$

**Periodicity condition:** $\phi$ and $\phi + 2\pi$ represent the same direction, so $\Phi(\phi) = \Phi(\phi + 2\pi)$:

$$e^{im_l\phi} = e^{im_l(\phi+2\pi)} \implies e^{2\pi im_l} = 1 \implies m_l = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \ldots$$

**Magnetic quantum number** (azimuthal quantum number): $m_l = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \ldots$

**Solution of (1):**

$$\Phi_{m_l}(\phi) = Ae^{im_l\phi}$$

*Diagram: Azimuthal angle $\phi$ and $\phi + 2\pi$ point in the same direction*

---

## Page 25 (Slide 85) — Quantum Numbers: Orbital Quantum Number

**$\theta$-equation (2):**

$$\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{d}{d\theta}\!\left(\sin\theta\frac{d\Theta}{d\theta}\right) + \left[l(l+1) - \frac{m_l^2}{\sin^2\theta}\right]\Theta = 0$$

This equation has **physical (finite, non-diverging) solutions only when $l$ is an integer $\geq |m_l|$**.

**Orbital (azimuthal) quantum number:** $l = |m_l|, |m_l|+1, \ldots$

Therefore the magnetic quantum number is limited: $m_l = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \ldots, \pm l$

**Solution of (2):** (Associated Legendre Polynomial)

$$\Theta_{l,m_l}(\theta) = P_l^{m_l}(\cos\theta)$$

---

## Page 26 (Slide 86) — Quantum Numbers: Principal Quantum Number

**$r$-equation (3) with $V(r) = -\dfrac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}$:**

$$\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r^2\frac{dR}{dr}\right) + \left[\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}(E-V) - \frac{l(l+1)}{r^2}\right]R = 0$$

For bound states (energy $< 0$ but $> $ total energy), physical solutions exist only for:

**Principal quantum number:** $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ ← **primary quantum number**

$$E_n = -\frac{me^4}{32\pi^2\epsilon_0^2\hbar^2}\cdot\frac{1}{n^2}$$

The orbital quantum number is further limited: $l = 0, 1, 2, \ldots, (n-1)$

**Solution of (3):** (Associated Laguerre Polynomial)

$$R_{n,l}(r) = L_n^l(r)$$

> This result exactly reproduces the Rydberg formula derived from Bohr's model!!!

**Full wave function:**

**Three quantum numbers:**

## Page 33 (Slide 93) — Conservation of Angular Momentum: Classical vs. Quantum

**Classical angular momentum conservation:**
- Both the **magnitude** and **direction** of angular momentum are conserved
- Object moves only in the plane perpendicular to the angular momentum vector

**Quantum mechanical angular momentum conservation:**
- Only the **magnitude** $L$ and **one component** $L_z$ are conserved (not all three components simultaneously)
- Why? If angular momentum direction were completely fixed, the particle would be confined to a definite plane
  - Then $\Delta z = 0$, and by the uncertainty principle $\Delta p_z = \infty$ → **impossible!**
- Therefore only the $z$-component $L_z$ is fixed, and the direction of angular momentum itself is **spatially quantized**

## Page 35 (Slide 95) — Hydrogen Atom: Electron Probability Distribution

**Wave function:** $\varphi(r,\theta,\phi) = R(r)\Theta(\theta)\Phi(\phi)$

**Probability density:** $|\varphi(r,\theta,\phi)|^2 = |R(r)|^2|\Theta(\theta)|^2|\Phi(\phi)|^2$

**Probability of finding electron in volume element $dV$:** $|\varphi|^2\,dV$

**Volume elements:**
- Cartesian: $dV = dx\,dy\,dz$
- Spherical: $dV = r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi$

**Radial probability (finding electron in shell between $r$ and $r+dr$):**

$$P(r)\,dr = r^2|R(r)|^2\,dr\int_0^\pi\sin\theta\,|\Theta|^2\,d\theta\int_0^{2\pi}|\Phi|^2\,d\phi = r^2|R(r)|^2\,dr = |rR(r)|^2\,dr$$

## Page 37 (Slide 97) — Bohr's Hydrogen Atom Model (1913) Revisited

Bohr assumed the following **three conditions**, applied Newton's laws, and derived the Rydberg formula:

① Electrons can exist stably in pre-defined energy orbits

② When an electron transitions between orbits, it emits or absorbs light with energy $\Delta E = E' - E = hf$

③ Only orbits with quantized angular momentum $L = n\hbar$ are permitted

*Diagram: Bohr circular orbits with de Broglie waves fitted around each orbit*

> **Bohr's assumption ③ is equivalent to requiring de Broglie standing waves to fit around the orbit!** (cf. Section 23D)

## Page 38 (Slide 98) — Time-Dependent State Vector of an Electron

**State vector for energy eigenstate $E_m$:** $|\psi_m\rangle = |\varphi_m\rangle e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\mathcal{E}_m t}$

**Superposition state (electron simultaneously in $E_n$ and $E_m$):** $|\psi\rangle = a|\psi_n\rangle + b|\psi_m\rangle$

**Expected position when state is $|\psi\rangle$:**

$$\langle x\rangle = \langle\psi|x|\psi\rangle = |a|^2\langle\psi_n|x|\psi_n\rangle + a^*b\langle\psi_n|x|\psi_m\rangle + b^*a\langle\psi_m|x|\psi_n\rangle + |b|^2\langle\psi_m|x|\psi_m\rangle$$

**Using:**

$$\langle\psi_n|x|\psi_n\rangle = \langle\varphi_n|x|\varphi_n\rangle, \quad \langle\psi_m|x|\psi_m\rangle = \langle\varphi_m|x|\varphi_m\rangle$$

$$\langle\psi_n|x|\psi_m\rangle = \langle\varphi_n|x|\varphi_m\rangle\,e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}(\mathcal{E}_m-\mathcal{E}_n)t}, \quad \langle\psi_m|x|\psi_n\rangle = \langle\varphi_m|x|\varphi_n\rangle\,e^{+\frac{i}{\hbar}(\mathcal{E}_m-\mathcal{E}_n)t}$$

## Page 39 (Slide 99) — Time-Dependent State Vector → Photon Emission

$$\langle x\rangle = |a|^2\langle\varphi_n|x|\varphi_n\rangle + |b|^2\langle\varphi_m|x|\varphi_m\rangle$$

$$+ \cos\!\left(\frac{E_m-E_n}{\hbar}t\right)\!\left[a^*b\langle\varphi_n|x|\varphi_m\rangle + b^*a\langle\varphi_m|x|\varphi_n\rangle\right]$$

$$- i\sin\!\left(\frac{E_m-E_n}{\hbar}t\right)\!\left[a^*b\langle\varphi_n|x|\varphi_m\rangle - b^*a\langle\varphi_m|x|\varphi_n\rangle\right]$$

**Electron oscillation frequency:**

$$\omega = \frac{E_m - E_n}{\hbar} \implies f = \frac{E_m - E_n}{h} \quad \text{(radiation frequency)}$$

> **When an electron transitions from state $|\psi_m\rangle$ to state $|\psi_n\rangle$, it emits light at frequency $f = \dfrac{E_m - E_n}{h}$**

## Page 41 (Slide 101) — Electric and Magnetic Dipoles

**Electric dipole:**
- Charges $+q$ and $-q$ separated by $\vec{d}$; dipole moment $\vec{p} = q\vec{d}$
- Torque in uniform field: $\vec{\tau} = \vec{p}\times\vec{E}$
- Potential energy: $U_e = -\vec{p}\cdot\vec{E} = -pE\cos\theta$

**Magnetic dipole:**
- Current loop with area $\vec{S}$; magnetic moment $\vec{\mu} = i\vec{S}$
- Torque in uniform field: $\vec{\tau} = \vec{\mu}\times\vec{B}$
- Potential energy: $U_m = -\vec{\mu}\cdot\vec{B} = -\mu B\cos\theta$

*(Reference: Section 18L)*

---

## Page 42 (Slide 102) — Magnetic Dipole Moment from Orbital Angular Momentum $\vec{L}$

**Electron of charge $e$ orbiting at radius $r$ with speed $v$ (circular orbit):**

Current: $i = \dfrac{\Delta q}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{e}{T}$

Angular momentum: $L = mvr = m\omega r^2 = \dfrac{2m}{T}S \implies \dfrac{S}{T} = \dfrac{L}{2m}$

Magnetic moment magnitude: $\mu = iS = \dfrac{e}{T}S = \dfrac{e}{2m}L$

**Magnetic moment vector** (negative sign because electron charge is $-e$):

$$\vec{\mu} = -\frac{e}{2m}\vec{L}$$

**Potential energy in external field $\vec{B} = B_0\hat{z}$:**

$$U_m = -\vec{\mu}\cdot\vec{B} = \frac{e}{2m}L_z B_0 = \frac{e}{2m}m_l\hbar B_0 = \frac{e\hbar}{2m}m_l B_0$$

**Bohr magneton:** $\displaystyle\mu_B = \frac{e\hbar}{2m}$

## Page 43 (Slide 103) — Zeeman Effect

**Without external magnetic field:** electron state energy is determined only by principal quantum number $n$

- All states with the same $n$ (e.g., $1s$; or $2s$, $2p$; or $3s$, $3p$, $3d$) have the same energy $E_n$ (degenerate)

**With external magnetic field $B_0$:** energy shifts by $m_l$-dependent amount

$$E_{n,m_l} = E_n + \frac{e\hbar}{2m}m_l B_0 = E_n + \mu_B m_l B_0$$

> **Each energy level splits into $2l+1$ sub-levels** — this is the **Zeeman effect**.

---

## Page 44 (Slide 104) — Zeeman Effect: Energy Level Diagram

*Diagram: Splitting of $3d$ ($E_3$, $l=2$) into 5 sub-levels ($m_l = -2,-1,0,+1,+2$) and $2p$ ($E_2$, $l=1$) into 3 sub-levels ($m_l = -1,0,+1$) when magnetic field $B$ is applied.*

**Selection rule:** $\Delta m_l = 0, \pm 1$ and $\Delta l = 1$

**Allowed transitions** from $3d$ ($5$ levels) to $2p$ ($3$ levels) give spectral lines at:

$$f_0 - \frac{eB}{4\pi m}, \quad f_0, \quad f_0 + \frac{eB}{4\pi m}$$

> **Applying a magnetic field splits every spectral line into exactly three lines.**

**Pieter Zeeman** (Netherlands, 1865–1943)

- **1896:** Discovered the Zeeman effect — splitting of spectral lines in a magnetic field
  - One year **before** Thomson's discovery of the electron (1897)!
- His supervisor **Hendrik Lorentz** provided the theoretical explanation for the Zeeman effect
- Together, Zeeman and Lorentz shared the **2nd Nobel Prize in Physics (1902)**

## Page 46 (Slide 106) — Anomalous Zeeman Effect

**Normal Zeeman effect:** when a magnetic field is applied, spectral lines split into an **odd** number of lines

**Anomalous Zeeman effect:** when a magnetic field is applied, spectral lines split into an **even** number of lines — observed experimentally!

*Image: Side-by-side comparison of:*
- *Left: Hydrogen atom — Normal Zeeman Effect (without B: 1 line; with B: splits into 3)*
- *Right: Sodium doublet (D line) — Anomalous Zeeman Effect (without B: doublet; with B: splits into many, even-numbered)*

The anomalous Zeeman effect **cannot be explained** by orbital angular momentum alone → requires **intrinsic spin**.

## Page 47 (Slide 107) — Intrinsic Electron Spin: Proposal

**Samuel Goudsmit** (Netherlands, 1902–1978) and **George Uhlenbeck** (Netherlands, 1900–1988)

- Both were graduate students at the University of Leiden in 1925
- Proposed that electrons possess **intrinsic spin** in order to explain the anomalous Zeeman effect

**Intrinsic spin:** angular momentum an electron possesses **without orbital motion**

**Intrinsic spin quantum numbers:**

$$s = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad m_s = \pm\frac{1}{2}$$

---

## Page 48 (Slide 108) — Stern–Gerlach Experiment

**Otto Stern** (Germany, 1888–1969) and **Walther Gerlach** (Germany, 1889–1979)

- In 1922, proved experimentally that electrons have intrinsic spin — using **silver atoms**
- Silver's outermost electron is in the $5s$ state ($l = 0$, $m_l = 0$, so no orbital magnetic moment)
- Silver atoms passing through a **non-uniform magnetic field** split into **exactly two beams**
  - Evidence that an angular momentum of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ exists (giving $2s+1 = 2$ values: $m_s = +\frac{1}{2}$ and $-\frac{1}{2}$)
- Stern received the **Nobel Prize in Physics 1943**

*Image: Experimental diagram (oven, collimator, magnet, detector); portraits of Stern and Gerlach; silver atom diagram showing $5s$ outer electron*

## Page 49 (Slide 109) — Pauli's Exclusion Principle

**Wolfgang Pauli** (Austria, 1900–1958) — Nobel Prize 1945

**Four quantum numbers defining an electron state in an atom:**

| Quantum number | Symbol | Allowed values |
|---|---|---|
| Principal | $n$ | $1, 2, 3, \ldots$ |
| Orbital | $l$ | $0, 1, 2, \ldots, (n-1)$ |
| Magnetic | $m_l$ | $0, \pm 1, \ldots, \pm l$ |
| Spin | $m_s$ | $\pm\dfrac{1}{2}$ |

**Pauli's Exclusion Principle:**

> **No two electrons in an atom can have all four quantum numbers the same.**

Pauli analyzed atomic emission spectra and arrived at the exclusion principle, which explains the periodic table of elements.

## Page 50 (Slide 110) — Non-Interacting Identical Particles

**Two-particle Schrödinger equation** (no interaction between particles):

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\!\left(\frac{d^2}{dx_1^2} + \frac{d^2}{dx_2^2}\right)\psi(x_1,x_2) + [V_1(x_1) + V_2(x_2)]\psi(x_1,x_2) = E\psi(x_1,x_2)$$

**Separable form:** $\psi(x_1,x_2) = \varphi_a(x_1)\varphi_b(x_2)$

**Identical particle condition:** exchanging two particles cannot change probability density:

$$|\psi(x_1,x_2)|^2 = |\psi(x_2,x_1)|^2 \implies \psi(x_1,x_2) = \pm\psi(x_2,x_1)$$

**Two types of wave functions:**

- **Symmetric** (exchange → same sign): $\psi(x_1,x_2) = +\psi(x_2,x_1)$
- **Antisymmetric** (exchange → sign flip): $\psi(x_1,x_2) = -\psi(x_2,x_1)$

---

## Page 51 (Slide 111) — Symmetric and Antisymmetric Wave Functions

**Symmetric wave function:**

$$\psi_S(x_1,x_2) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl[\varphi_a(x_1)\varphi_b(x_2) + \varphi_a(x_2)\varphi_b(x_1)\bigr]$$

**Antisymmetric wave function:**

$$\psi_A(x_1,x_2) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl[\varphi_a(x_1)\varphi_b(x_2) - \varphi_a(x_2)\varphi_b(x_1)\bigr]$$

**If $a = b$ (both particles in the same quantum state):**

$$\psi_S(x_1,x_2) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl[\varphi_a(x_1)\varphi_a(x_2) + \varphi_a(x_2)\varphi_a(x_1)\bigr] \neq 0 \quad \text{(allowed)}$$

$$\psi_A(x_1,x_2) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl[\varphi_a(x_1)\varphi_a(x_2) - \varphi_a(x_2)\varphi_a(x_1)\bigr] = 0 \quad \longrightarrow \; \textbf{Pauli's Exclusion Principle!}$$

- **Electrons** (spin $\frac{1}{2}$, fermions): antisymmetric wave function → obey Pauli exclusion
- **Photons** (spin 1, bosons): symmetric wave function → can occupy same state
