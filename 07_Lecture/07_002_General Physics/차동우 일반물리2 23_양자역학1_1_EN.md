## Page 6 (Slide 5) — Cathode Ray Experiment and Discovery of the Electron (1897)

*(Photos: Crookes tube with glowing cathode ray, hair dryer-shaped cathode ray tube)*

By applying electric and magnetic fields to the path of the cathode ray in a Crookes tube, the path of the cathode ray was observed to deflect, leading to the following conclusion:

- **The cathode ray is a flow of negatively charged particles**
- **These particles come from atoms**
- **Only these particles exist inside atoms**

*(Diagram: sphere with uniformly distributed positive charge (+) and electrons (–) embedded throughout — compared to a cross-section of a watermelon)*

**Positive charge is distributed throughout the atom like a sphere, with electrons embedded in between**

→ **Thomson's Plum Pudding Atomic Model**

## Page 22 (Slide 21) — Balmer Formula

**Johann Jakob Balmer**
*(Switzerland, 1825–1898)*
*Mathematician, physicist; discoverer of the Balmer formula*

While working as a mathematics teacher, he discovered the Balmer formula (1885) for the spectral lines in the visible region of hydrogen:

| Observed | Calculated |
|---|---|
| 656.45377 nm | **656.22276 nm** |
| 486.13615 nm | **486.00909 nm** |
| 434.0462 nm | **434.00976 nm** |
| 410.174 nm | **410.13923 nm** |

$$\lambda = B\frac{n^2}{n^2 - 2^2}, \quad n = 3, 4, 5, 6$$

**If B = 364.50682 nm, all wavelengths can be obtained**

**Why this formula works was unknown.**

---

## Page 23 (Slide 22) — Rydberg Constant

**Johannes Rydberg**
*(Sweden, 1854–1919)*
*Researcher on the theoretical basis of spectral lines; discoverer of the Rydberg constant*

He generalized Balmer's formula for hydrogen spectral lines to the Rydberg formula (1888):

$$\lambda = B\frac{n^2}{n^2-2^2} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \frac{1}{\lambda} = R\left(\frac{1}{2^2} - \frac{1}{n^2}\right)$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{1}{\lambda} = R_H\left(\frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2}\right)$$

**Rydberg constant** $R_H = 1.09678 \times 10^{-2}\ \text{nm}^{-1}$

With the Rydberg formula, all wavelengths of spectral lines from light emitted by hot gases can be obtained.

**Why this formula works was unknown.**

## Page 27 (Slide 26) — The Blackbody

## Page 29 (Slide 28) — Blackbody Radiation Curve

The intensity curve of light emitted from a hot object as a function of frequency depends only on the **temperature** of the object.

*(Graph: B_λ (W/m²/nm) vs. λ (nm) for T = 3500 K, 4000 K, 4500 K, 5000 K, 5500 K — each curve has a peak that shifts to shorter wavelengths at higher temperatures)*

**By the end of the 19th century, no single formula could explain the blackbody radiation curve.**

*(Graph on right: Wien's formula and Rayleigh-Jeans formula vs. experimental data — each fits only part of the curve)*

---

## Page 30 (Slide 29) — Wien's Formula and the Rayleigh-Jeans Formula

*(Graph: Wien's formula fits short wavelengths; Rayleigh-Jeans fits long wavelengths; neither fits the full curve)*

**Short wavelength region:**

$$B_\lambda(T) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\,e^{-\frac{hc}{\lambda kT}}$$

**Wien's Formula (1896)**

**Long wavelength region:**

$$B_\lambda(T) = \frac{2ckT}{\lambda^4}$$

**Rayleigh-Jeans Formula (1900)**

---

## Page 31 (Slide 30) — The Person Who Derived the Correct Blackbody Radiation Formula

**Max Planck**
*(Germany, 1858–1947)*
*Father of quantum mechanics*

- Born in a family of theologians and lawyers
- Showed outstanding talent in music but was captivated by Newton's mechanics and became a physicist
- To explain the blackbody, he introduced the concept of energy quanta, but believed at the time it was merely a mathematical technique

**Planck's energy quanta were successfully applied to explain the photoelectric effect (Einstein), and to explain the light and electricity of atoms (Bohr)**

As Bohr's hydrogen model revealed that atomic interiors require Planck's energy quantization condition, it became clear that $E = hf$ is not merely a mathematical trick but a fundamental principle of the microscopic world.

---

## Page 32 (Slide 31) — Planck's Formula for Blackbody Radiation

**Planck succeeded in expressing the blackbody radiation curve with a single formula:**

**Planck Formula (1900):**

$$B_\lambda(T) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\cdot\frac{1}{e^{\frac{hc}{\lambda kT}}-1}$$

When λ is very small ($\frac{hc}{\lambda kT} \gg 1$):

$$B_\lambda(T) \approx \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\,e^{-\frac{hc}{\lambda kT}} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{Wien's Formula (1896)}$$

When λ is very large ($e^{\frac{hc}{\lambda kT}} \approx 1 + \frac{hc}{\lambda kT}$):

$$B_\lambda(T) \approx \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\cdot\frac{\lambda kT}{hc} = \frac{2ckT}{\lambda^4} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{Rayleigh-Jeans Formula (1900)}$$

---

## Page 33 (Slide 32) — How Planck Derived the Blackbody Radiation Formula

Planck used a cavity with a small hole as a model for a blackbody.

*(Diagram: cavity model on left; bar chart of energy levels on right)*

To calculate the probability that light of frequency $f$ is contained in the cavity, he divided the continuously existing light energy into intervals of $hf$ for calculation.

→ As $h \to 0$, the discretely distributed energies become continuous again.

---

## Page 34 (Slide 33) — How Planck Derived the Blackbody Radiation Formula (continued)

Using the fact that the probability that an electromagnetic wave of energy $E_n = n(hf)$ exists is proportional to $P_n = e^{-E_n/kT}$:

*(Bar chart: energy levels at multiples of hf, with Boltzmann probability weighting)*

- Energy of light in the n-th interval: $E_n = n(hf)$
- Probability of this energy existing: $P_n \propto e^{-nhf/kT}$

**Average energy of the electromagnetic wave:**

$$\langle E \rangle = \frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} E_n P_n}{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P_n}$$

---

## Page 35 (Slide 34) — Derivation of the Planck Formula

**Average energy of the electromagnetic wave:**

$$\langle E \rangle = \frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} E_n P_n}{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P_n} = \frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(nhf)\,e^{-nhf/kT}}{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} e^{-nhf/kT}}$$

$$\propto \frac{1}{\dfrac{hf}{e^{kT}}-1} = \frac{1}{e^{hc/\lambda kT}-1}$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad B_\lambda(T) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\cdot\frac{1}{e^{\frac{hc}{\lambda kT}}-1}$$

where $c = \lambda f$

---

## Page 36 (Slide 35) — Energy Quantization Formula and Planck's Constant

**Planck Formula:**

$$B_\lambda(T) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\cdot\frac{1}{e^{\frac{hc}{\lambda kT}}-1}$$

*(Graph: blackbody curves at T = 3500 K to 5500 K matching experimental data)*

Planck found that the above formula's curve matches the blackbody radiation curve when $h = 6.63\times10^{-34}\ \text{J·s}$

$$\boxed{E = hf} \qquad \boxed{h}$$

**Energy Quantization Condition** $\quad$ **Planck's Constant**

---

## Page 37 (Slide 36) — E = hf

*(Bar chart: discrete energy levels at multiples of hf)*

The energy quantization condition $E = hf$ means that the energy of light of frequency $f$ cannot change continuously but only in multiples of $hf$.

**The fact that energy is discontinuous is impossible according to Newtonian mechanics.**

Planck originally believed that the energy quantization $E = hf$ was merely a temporary approximation method for explaining the blackbody radiation curve.

However, contrary to Planck's expectation, $E = hf$ turned out to be the most fundamental principle describing the atomic interior of the microscopic world.

*(Film clapperboard icon)*

---

## Page 38 (Slide 37) — Summary (23C)

**Characteristic of the blackbody radiation curve:**

→ Depends only on temperature

While deriving the formula explaining the blackbody radiation curve, Planck accidentally discovered that the energy of light of frequency $f$ cannot change continuously but only in multiples of $hf$.

→ **Energy quantization condition: $E = hf$**

The constant $h$ in the energy quantization condition was revealed to be a fundamental constant of the microscopic world.

→ **Planck's constant $h = 6.63\times10^{-34}\ \text{J·s}$** ▶

## Page 40 (Slide 39) — 3rd Century BC, 17th Century, 20th Century

**3rd Century BC:**
This world is divided into the heavenly world and the earthly world.
The heavenly world is a perfect world governed by the laws of heaven;
the earthly world is an imperfect world where humans live, governed by the laws of the earth.

**17th Century:**
This world is governed everywhere by the fundamental law called Newton's law of motion: $F = ma$.

**20th Century:**
$F = ma$ cannot be applied to the interior of atoms. The world where $F = ma$ applies is called the **macroscopic world**; the world where it does not apply began to be called the **microscopic world**.

---

## Page 41 (Slide 40) — Questions That Arose in the Early 20th Century

- The electron and nucleus were discovered (1911), so the constituents of the atom were known, but the Rutherford atomic model is incomplete.

- The wavelengths of solar spectral lines were published (1868), and it became known that those lines match the spectral lines from hot gases.

- Many solar spectral lines satisfy the simple Rydberg formula (1888).

- In the process of deriving the blackbody radiation formula, it was revealed that the energy of light of frequency $f$ must be an integer multiple of $hf$ (1900).

---

**Niels Bohr**
*(Denmark, 1885–1962)*

## Page 43 (Slide 42) — Bohr's Hydrogen Atomic Model (1913)

**Assuming three conditions hold → applying Newton's law of motion → Rydberg formula emerges**

*(Diagram: electron orbiting around nucleus in allowed circular orbits, with a photon emitted during a transition)*

① **In pre-determined energy orbits, electrons can exist stably**

② **When an electron transitions from one orbit to another, $\Delta E = E' - E = hf$ light is emitted or absorbed**

③ **The allowed angular momentum of electrons in stable orbits: $L = n\hbar$ only**

$\Delta E = E' - E = hf$

---

## Page 44 (Slide 43) — Bohr's Hydrogen Atomic Model → Rydberg Formula

For an atom with atomic number $Z$, the Coulomb force by which the nucleus attracts the electron (Coulomb's law):

$$F = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2}{r^2}$$

When an electron undergoes uniform circular motion with speed $v$ in a circle of radius $r$, the centripetal acceleration:

$$a = -\frac{v^2}{r}$$

---

## Page 45 (Slide 44) — Bohr's Hydrogen Atomic Model → Rydberg Formula (continued)

$$F = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2}{r^2} \qquad a = -\frac{v^2}{r}$$

**Newton's equation of motion:** $F = m_e a$

$$-\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2}{r^2} = -m_e\frac{v^2}{r}$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad v = \sqrt{\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2}{m_e r}}$$

---

## Page 46 (Slide 45) — Bohr's Hydrogen Atomic Model → Rydberg Formula (continued)

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2}{m_e r}}$$

**Angular momentum of the electron:**

$$L = m_e vr = \sqrt{\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}m_e rZe^2}$$

**Bohr's assumption ③:** $L = n\hbar$

$$\sqrt{\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}m_e rZe^2} = n\hbar$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad r_n = \frac{4\pi\epsilon_0}{m_e Ze^2}(n\hbar)^2 \qquad \text{(radius of the n-th orbit)}$$

---

## Page 47 (Slide 46) — Bohr's Hydrogen Atomic Model → Rydberg Formula (continued)

Newton's equation of motion in the n-th orbit:

$$-\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2}{r_n^2} = -m_e\frac{v_n^2}{r_n} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \frac{1}{2}m_e v_n^2 = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2}{r_n}$$

**Energy at the orbit of radius $r_n$ in Bohr's atomic model:**

$$E_n = E_k + E_p = \frac{1}{2}m_e v_n^2 - \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2}{r_n} = -\frac{1}{8\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2}{r_n}$$

---

## Page 48 (Slide 47) — Bohr's Hydrogen Atomic Model → Rydberg Formula (continued)

$$E_n = -\frac{1}{8\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2}{r_n} \qquad r_n = \frac{4\pi\epsilon_0}{m_e Ze^2}(n\hbar)^2$$

**Energy at the orbit of radius $r_n$:**

$$E_n = -\frac{Ze^2}{8\pi\epsilon_0}\cdot\frac{m_e Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\cdot\frac{1}{(n\hbar)^2} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c}\right)^2\frac{Z^2 m_e c^2}{n^2}$$

**Using Bohr's assumption ②:**

$$\Delta E = E' - E = hf = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \qquad (c = \lambda f)$$

---

## Page 49 (Slide 48) — Bohr's Hydrogen Atomic Model → Rydberg Formula (continued)

$$E_n = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c}\right)^2\frac{Z^2 m_e c^2}{n^2} \qquad \frac{hc}{\lambda} = E_{n'} - E_n$$

$$\frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{1}{hc}(E_{n'}-E_n) = \left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c}\right)^2 Z^2 m_e c^2\right]\left(\frac{1}{n'^2}-\frac{1}{n^2}\right)$$

$$= \frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c}\right)^2\frac{Z^2 m_e c^2}{\hbar c}\left(\frac{1}{n'^2}-\frac{1}{n^2}\right)$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \boxed{\frac{1}{\lambda} = R_H\left(\frac{1}{n'^2}-\frac{1}{n^2}\right)} \qquad \textbf{Rydberg Formula}$$

---

## Page 50 (Slide 49) — Rydberg Constant from Bohr's Hydrogen Atomic Model

$$R_H = \frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c}\right)^2\frac{Z^2 m_e c^2}{\hbar c}$$

**Rydberg constant for hydrogen ($Z = 1$):**

$$R_H = \frac{1}{4\pi}(1.44\ \text{MeV·fm})^2\frac{(0.511\ \text{MeV})}{(197.3\ \text{MeV·fm})^3}$$

$$= 1.098\times10^{-8}\ \text{fm}^{-1} = 1.098\times10^{-2}\ \text{nm}^{-1}$$

$$\boxed{R_H = 1.09678\times10^{-2}\ \text{nm}^{-1}}$$

→ **The Rydberg formula can be reproduced from Bohr's hydrogen atomic model.**

## Page 58 (Slide 58) — The Microscopic World

**Constituents of the microscopic world: light, electrons, positrons, neutrons, atomic nuclei…**

**Is light a particle or a wave?**

- Newton (1704): Published optics showing that the color of light does not change by any method → light behaves as a particle
- Thomas Young (1803): Confirmed interference fringes using the double-slit experiment → believed light is a wave
- Maxwell (1865): Derived from Maxwell's equations the wave equation for electromagnetic waves, predicting the existence of electromagnetic waves, and proposed that light is a type of electromagnetic wave
- Hertz (1886): Experimentally verified the existence of electromagnetic waves predicted by Maxwell

---

## Page 59 (Slide 59) — The Microscopic World (continued)

**Is light a particle or a wave?**

- Planck (1900): While deriving the formula for the blackbody radiation curve, discovered that the energy of light must be quantized → light behaves as particles
- Einstein (1905): To explain the photoelectric effect, light must be treated as a particle
- Compton (1923): Confirmed that momentum is conserved before and after collision between an electron and an X-ray → **Compton scattering**

**Light is both a particle and a wave, but this is impossible in the macroscopic world.**

## Page 61 (Slide 61) — The Puzzle of the Photoelectric Effect at the End of the 19th Century

*(Diagram: photoelectric effect apparatus — metal plate, light source, variable voltage supply, ammeter)*

After it was confirmed that light is an electromagnetic wave, the following phenomena difficult to explain if light is a wave were observed:

**Experimental observations:**

① **When light is shone, current flows immediately**

② **For the same color (frequency) of light, the more light there is, the more current flows, but the maximum kinetic energy of electrons is the same regardless of brightness**

③ **When the frequency of light increases, the maximum kinetic energy of electrons increases; below a certain threshold frequency, no current flows no matter how bright the light is**

## Page 63 (Slide 63) — Energy Quantization Condition and the Photoelectric Effect

**Energy quantization condition $E = hf$:**

- Planck: used while deriving the formula for the blackbody radiation curve (1900)
- Einstein: used to solve the puzzle of the photoelectric effect (1905)
- Bohr: used when constructing the hydrogen atomic model (1913)

*(Diagram: metal surface with electrons; photons striking and ejecting electrons)*

**Einstein's assumption to explain the photoelectric effect:**

Light of frequency $f$ consists of **photons** (light quanta), each carrying energy $E = hf$.

→ When light is shone, electrons come out immediately — naturally explained!

## Page 64 (Slide 64) — Explanation of the Photoelectric Effect

**Threshold frequency $f_0$:**
→ The minimum frequency at which electrons are emitted

**Work function $\phi = hf_0$:**
→ The minimum energy needed to release an electron from the metal

**Maximum kinetic energy of the emitted electron:**

$$K_{\max} = hf - hf_0$$

*(Diagram: metal plate with electrons; left — photon energy E₀ = hf₀ gives K = 0; right — photon energy E = hf gives K_max = hf − hf₀)*

→ The maximum kinetic energy of electrons emitted when light of frequency $f$ is shone

## Page 65 (Slide 65) — Example

Violet light of wavelength $\lambda_0 = 400\ \text{nm}$ is shone on the surface of a metal and electrons just begin to come out. Find (a) the work function of the metal and (b) the maximum kinetic energy of electrons emitted when UV light of wavelength $\lambda = 320\ \text{nm}$ is shone on the metal surface.

$$\text{hint: } \hbar c = 197.3\ \text{eV·nm}$$

**(a) Work function:**

$$\phi = hf_0 = \frac{hc}{\lambda_0} = 2\pi\times\frac{197.3}{400} = 3.10\ \text{eV}$$

**(b) Maximum kinetic energy:**

$$K_{\max} = hf - \phi = 2\pi\times\frac{197.3}{320} - 3.10\ \text{eV} = 0.77\ \text{eV}$$
