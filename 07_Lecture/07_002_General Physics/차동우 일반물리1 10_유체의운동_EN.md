## Page 3 — From Rigid-Body Mass to Fluid Density

**Rigid body**

- The motion state of a rigid body changes as a whole; the **mass** of the body represents its motion.

**Fluid**

- When describing the motion state of a fluid, **mass plays little role**.

$$\text{Rigid body: mass} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{Fluid: density}$$

---

## Page 4 — Density

**Density** — mass contained per unit volume

$$\rho = \frac{M}{V} \qquad \xrightarrow{\text{non-uniform}} \qquad \rho(\vec{r}) = \frac{dm}{dv}$$

- When mass is **uniformly distributed** → applicable to fluids (use $\rho = M/V$)
- When mass is **not uniformly distributed** → applicable to rigid bodies (use $\rho(\vec{r}) = dm/dv$)

## Page 6 — From Rigid-Body Force to Fluid Pressure

**Rigid body**

- When a force is applied to a rigid body, the motion state of the **entire rigid body** changes.

**Fluid**

- When a force is applied to a fluid, only the **part that receives the force** moves first.

$$\text{Rigid body: force} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{Fluid: pressure}$$

## Page 9 — Pressure

**Pressure** — force acting per unit area

$$P = \frac{F}{S} \qquad \xrightarrow{\text{non-uniform}} \qquad P(\vec{r}) = \frac{dF}{ds}$$

- When force acts **uniformly**: $\vec{F} = P\vec{S}$
- When force does **not** act uniformly: $d\vec{F} = P\,d\vec{S}$

> Force is a **vector** quantity, whereas pressure is a **scalar** quantity.

---

## Page 10 — Units of Pressure

**SI unit** → **Pascal (Pa)**

$$1\ \text{Pa} = 1\ \text{N/m}^2 = 1 \times \frac{10^5\ \text{dyne}}{(100\ \text{cm})^2} = 10\ \text{dyne/cm}^2$$

$$1\ \text{bar} = 10^6\ \frac{\text{dyne}}{\text{cm}^2} = 10^5\ \text{Pa}$$

$$1\ \text{mb} = 10^{-3}\ \text{bar} = 100\ \text{Pa} = 1\ \text{hPa}$$

## Page 17 — Deriving Pascal's Principle

*(Diagram: cylindrical fluid column of cross-sectional area $dS$ and depth $h$ inside a fluid-filled container)*

Consider a cylindrical element in the fluid with cross-sectional area $dS$ and depth $h$ measured from the surface.

- Density of the fluid: $\rho$
- Pressure at the upper surface (atmospheric pressure): $P_0$
- Pressure at depth $h$: $P(h)$

Using the equilibrium condition for the cylindrical element, find the pressure $P(h)$ at depth $h$ below the surface.

---

## Page 18 — Pascal's Principle: Forces on the Cylinder

*(Diagram: vertical cylindrical fluid column with $F_1$ downward at top, $mg$ downward (gravity), and $F_2$ upward at bottom)*

- Force pushing the top of the cylinder **downward** (atmosphere):
$$F_1 = P_0\,dS$$

- Gravity pulling the mass of the cylinder **downward**:
$$mg = \rho h\,dS\,g$$

- Force pushing the bottom of the cylinder **upward** (fluid below):
$$F_2 = P(h)\,dS$$

---

## Page 19 — Pascal's Principle: Result

*(Diagram: same cylinder with three forces)*

Equilibrium condition (net force = 0):

$$F_1 + mg - F_2 = P_0\,dS + \rho g h\,dS - P(h)\,dS = 0$$

$$\boxed{P(h) = P_0 + \rho g h}$$

> The pressure inside a fluid depends only on the **depth $h$** below the surface.

## Page 21 — Pascal's Discovery of Pascal's Principle (Application)

*(Diagram: hydraulic lift — small piston on the left with force $F_1$ pressing down, large piston on the right lifting a car with force $F_2$)*

$$F_1 = P_1 S_1 \qquad F_2 = P_2 S_2$$

$$P_1 = P_2 = P$$

$$\boxed{F_2 = \frac{S_2}{S_1} F_1 \gg F_1}$$

## Page 22 — Pascal's Discovery of Pascal's Principle (Hydraulic Lift)

*(Diagram: same hydraulic lift as above, shown again)*

$$F_1 = P_1 S_1 \qquad F_2 = P_2 S_2$$

Pascal's discovery: $P_1 = P_2 = P$

$$\Rightarrow F_2 = \frac{S_2}{S_1} F_1 \gg F_1$$

---

## Page 23 — Work and Energy in a Hydraulic Lift

*(Diagram: hydraulic lift; left piston moves down by $h_1$, right piston moves up by $h_2$)*

$$F_2 = \frac{S_2}{S_1} F_1 \gg F_1$$

Work done on the left (input):
$$W_1 = F_1 h_1 = P S_1 h_1$$

Work done on the right (output):
$$W_2 = F_2 h_2 = P S_2 h_2$$

Volume conservation:
$$S_1 h_1 = S_2 h_2 = \Delta V$$

$$\Rightarrow W_1 = W_2$$

---

## Page 24 — Summary: Pascal's Principle

**Pressure inside a stationary fluid:**

$$\boxed{P(h) = P_0 + \rho g h} \quad \text{(Pascal's principle)}$$

- The pressure inside a fluid depends only on the **depth $h$** below the surface.

- Pascal's principle can be derived simply from Newton's equations of motion, but Pascal discovered it experimentally.

## Page 26 — What Is Buoyancy?

*(Diagram: hot-air balloon with buoyant force $B$ upward and gravity $mg$ downward)*

Forces acting on the balloon:

- **Gravity** $mg$: Earth's gravitational attraction pulling the balloon downward.
- **Buoyancy** $B$: The resultant of all pressure forces exerted by air on the surface of the balloon.

---

## Page 27 — Buoyancy: Direction of Action

*(Diagram: hot-air balloon with air pressure arrows on all sides; higher pressure below, lower pressure above)*

**Buoyancy $B$** — resultant of all pressure forces exerted by air on the surface of the balloon.

- As we go downward, pressure **increases** → buoyancy acts **upward**.
- As we go upward, pressure **decreases** → buoyancy acts **downward** (net result is upward force).

## Page 30 — Deriving Archimedes' Principle from Pascal's Principle

*(Diagram: cylindrical object submerged in a cylindrical fluid container, with $h_1$ above the object top and $h_2$ the height of the object)*

- Fluid density: $\rho_0$
- Atmospheric pressure: $P_0$
- Object density: $\rho$

Pressure at the **top face** of the object:
$$P_1 = P_0 + \rho_0 g h_1$$

Pressure at the **bottom face** of the object:
$$P_2 = P_0 + \rho_0 g(h_1 + h_2)$$

---

## Page 31 — Archimedes' Principle: Derivation

*(Diagram: same submerged cylinder with cross-sectional area $S$)*

- Cross-sectional area of the object: $S$

Force of fluid pushing the **top face downward**:
$$F_1 = P_1 S = (P_0 + \rho_0 g h_1)S$$

Force of fluid pushing the **bottom face upward**:
$$F_2 = P_2 S = (P_0 + \rho_0 g(h_1 + h_2))S$$

**Buoyant force:**
$$\boxed{B = F_2 - F_1 = \rho_0 g h_2 S = \rho_0 V g}$$

---

## Page 32 — True Weight vs. Apparent Weight

*(Diagram: cube with buoyant force $B$ upward and gravity $mg$ downward)*

- True weight: $W = mg$
- Apparent weight: $W' = mg - B$

**Question:** The apparent weight of an object in air is what percentage of its true weight?

- Water density: $\rho = 1{,}000\ \text{kg/m}^3$
- Air density: $\rho' = 1.2\ \text{kg/m}^3$

$$W = mg = \rho V g \qquad B = m'g = \rho' V g$$

$$W' = \rho V g - \rho' V g = \rho V g\!\left(1 - \frac{\rho'}{\rho}\right) = W\!\left(1 - \frac{\rho'}{\rho}\right)$$

$$\left(1 - \frac{\rho'}{\rho}\right) = 1 - \frac{1.2}{1{,}000} = 0.9988 \;\Rightarrow\; \mathbf{99.88\%}$$

---

## Page 33 — Application: Iceberg

*(Diagram: iceberg floating in the ocean, most of it below the waterline)*

What percentage of a floating iceberg is exposed above the water?

- Seawater density: $\rho = 1{,}024\ \text{kg/m}^3$
- Ice density: $\rho' = 917\ \text{kg/m}^3$

Let:
- $V$ = total volume of iceberg
- $V'$ = submerged volume

Weight of iceberg: $mg = \rho' V g$

Buoyant force: $B = \rho V' g$

---

## Page 34 — Iceberg: Result

*(Diagram: iceberg floating in seawater)*

Seawater density: $\rho = 1{,}024\ \text{kg/m}^3$, Ice density: $\rho' = 917\ \text{kg/m}^3$

Condition for floating: $mg = B \;\Rightarrow\; \rho' V = \rho V'$

Fraction exposed above water:
$$\frac{V - V'}{V} = 1 - \frac{V'}{V} = 1 - \frac{\rho'}{\rho} = 1 - \frac{917}{1{,}024} = 0.10 \;\Rightarrow\; \mathbf{10\%}$$

---

## Page 35 — Summary: Archimedes' Principle

**Archimedes' Principle:**

> The magnitude of the buoyant force acting on an object submerged in a fluid equals the weight of the fluid displaced by the object.

- Archimedes' principle can be **proved** using Pascal's principle.
- Pascal's principle is **derived** by applying Newton's equations of motion to a fluid at rest.
