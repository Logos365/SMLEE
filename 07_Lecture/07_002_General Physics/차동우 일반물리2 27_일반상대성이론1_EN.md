### Slide 2 — Reference Frame vs. Coordinate System (기준계와 좌표계)

**Reference frame (기준계)**:
- Provides a standard for representing an object's motion numerically
- Defines an **origin** and **coordinate axes**

Types of reference frames:
- **Inertial frame (관성계)**: Newton's first law holds
- **Non-inertial frame (비관성계)**: Newton's first law does NOT hold

**Coordinate system (좌표계)**:
- Provides coordinates (numbers) to specify the position of an object
- Defines the **type** of coordinates

Common coordinate systems:
- Rectangular (Cartesian): $(x, y, z)$
- Cylindrical: $(\rho, \phi, z)$
- Spherical: $(r, \theta, \phi)$

*Diagram: Split panel — left shows "Reference frame" concept (origin + axes), right shows "Coordinate system" concept (position numbers).*

### Slide 3 — Inertial vs. Non-Inertial Frames (관성계와 비관성계)

| | Inertial Frame (관성계) | Non-Inertial Frame (비관성계) |
|--|------------------------|------------------------------|
| Definition | Frame where Newton's first law holds | Frame where Newton's first law does NOT hold |
| Newton's 2nd law | $F = ma$ applies | $F = ma$ does NOT apply |
| Motion relative to inertial frame | Constant velocity | Accelerating |

**Rules:**
- Any frame moving at **constant velocity** relative to an inertial frame → also inertial
- Any frame **accelerating** relative to an inertial frame → non-inertial

*Diagram: Left panel (Inertial frame) — person in constant-velocity train; ball on table stays put. Right panel (Non-inertial frame) — person in accelerating train; ball slides backward.*

---

### Slide 4 — Physical Quantities and Reference Frames (물리량과 기준계)

**Frame-independent quantities** (same value in all frames):
- Force, mass, time, length, volume

**Frame-dependent quantities** (value changes with frame):
- Position, velocity, acceleration, momentum

**Key point**: $F = ma$

- If this equation holds → **inertial frame**
- If this equation does NOT hold → **non-inertial frame**

Quantities like position, velocity, and momentum can be measured differently in different frames — but the laws of physics in their correct form only hold in inertial frames (in Newtonian mechanics).

---

### Slide 5 — Galilean Addition Laws (위치, 속도, 가속도 덧셈법칙)

Three frames: **A** (reference), **B** (another frame), **C** (object being tracked).

**Position addition**:
$$x_{CA} = x_{CB} + x_{BA}$$
If $x_{BA} = 0$ (origins coincide) → $x_{CA} = x_{CB}$

**Velocity addition**:
$$v_{CA} = v_{CB} + v_{BA}$$
If $v_{BA} = 0$ (B at rest relative to A) → $v_{CA} = v_{CB}$

**Acceleration addition**:
$$a_{CA} = a_{CB} + a_{BA}$$
If $a_{BA} = 0$ (B moves at constant velocity relative to A) → $a_{CA} = a_{CB}$

**This last result means**: If B moves at **constant velocity** relative to A (an inertial frame), then the acceleration of any object C is the same in both A and B → B is also inertial.

---

### Slide 6 — Two Different Inertial Frames (서로 다른 두 관성계)

**Inertial frame A** $\neq$ **Inertial frame B**

- Frame B moves at constant velocity relative to inertial frame A
- **All inertial frames are equivalent for measuring accelerations**:

$$a_{CA} = a_{CB}$$

Because $a_{BA} = 0$, so $a_{CA} = a_{CB} + 0 = a_{CB}$

Therefore Newton's law $F = ma_{CA} = ma_{CB}$ holds the same way in both A and B.

*Diagram: Two boxes A and B, B moving at constant velocity v relative to A. Same object C inside. The acceleration of C measured in both frames is identical.*

---

### Slide 7 — Non-Inertial Frame and Newton's Law (비관성계와 뉴턴의 운동방정식)

**In inertial frame A**:
$$F = ma_{CA} \quad \cdots (1)$$

**In non-inertial frame B** (accelerating):
$$F \neq ma_{CB} \quad \cdots (2)$$

Because $a_{CA} \neq a_{CB}$ when $a_{BA} \neq 0$.

**Question**: Can we write a Newton-like equation that IS valid in the non-inertial frame B?

**YES WE CAN.**

*Diagram: Two panels — A (blue, inertial) with $F = ma_{CA}$, B (orange, non-inertial) with $F \neq ma_{CB}$. Yellow starburst: $a_{CA} \neq a_{CB}$.*

---

### Slide 8 — Newton-Like Equation in Non-Inertial Frame (비관성계에서 뉴턴 형태의 식)

**Derivation:**

From frame A (inertial): $F = ma_{CA}$

Using acceleration addition: $a_{CA} = a_{CB} + a_{BA}$

Substituting:

$$F = m(a_{CB} + a_{BA}) = ma_{CB} + ma_{BA}$$

$$F - ma_{BA} = ma_{CB}$$

$$\boxed{F' = ma_{CB}}$$

where $F' = F - ma_{BA}$.

This has the **same form** as Newton's second law, but in the non-inertial frame!

The extra term $-ma_{BA}$ must be added to the real force $F$ — this term is called the **inertial force (관성력)**.

---

### Slide 9 — Inertial Force (관성력)

$$F' = F - ma_{BA}$$

| Term | Physical meaning |
|------|-----------------|
| $F'$ | Effective force that object appears to experience in non-inertial frame B |
| $F$ | Real force (the actual force acting on the object) |
| $-ma_{BA}$ | **Inertial force** (관성력) — extra term added to make Newton's law hold |

**Critical point**: The inertial force is **NOT a real force!**

> 관성력은 힘이 아님 !!

It is simply a mathematical term we add to correct for the non-inertial nature of the frame.

*Diagram: $F' = F - ma_{BA}$: left bubble (yellow) labels $F'$ "the force the object actually receives," middle bubble (yellow) labels $F$ "just an added term," red arrow from $ma_{BA}$ labels it "called inertial force — NOT a real force!"*

---

### Slide 10 — Why Inertial Force Is NOT a Force (관성력을 힘이 아니라고 말하는 이유)

**What makes something a "force"?**

A force requires **two objects** interacting — force measures the degree of interaction between them.

$$\text{If A exerts force on B} \Rightarrow \text{B exerts equal and opposite force on A (Newton's 3rd law)}$$

**Every real force has a reaction force.**

If you can find the reaction force → it is a real force.
If you cannot find the reaction force → it is NOT a force.

*Diagram: Two rocks A and B with arrows pointing toward each other — each exerts force on the other. "Force = how much two objects interact."*

---

### Slide 11 — Centripetal vs. Centrifugal "Force" (구심력 vs. 원심력)

**Centripetal force (구심력)**: The rope pulls the ball inward → this IS a real force.
- Reaction: the ball pulls the rope outward ✓

**Centrifugal force (원심력)**: An apparent outward "force" felt in the rotating frame.
- Reaction: ??? → **No reaction force can be found** ✗

**Conclusion**: Centrifugal force is **NOT a real force** — it is an inertial force (artifact of the rotating non-inertial frame).

| Force | Real force? | Reaction force |
|-------|------------|---------------|
| Centripetal (구심력) | YES | Ball pulls rope outward ✓ |
| Centrifugal (원심력) | NO | Cannot be found ✗ |

*Diagram: Person swinging a ball on a rope. Centripetal force arrow (rope→ball, inward). Centrifugal force arrow (ball→outward). "No reaction → not a force."*

---

### Slide 12 — Centrifugal Force Has No Reaction (관성력은 반작용을 찾을 수 없음)

Continuing the centrifugal force analysis:

The centripetal force (rope pulling ball inward) has a clear reaction: the ball pulls the rope outward.

But the centrifugal "force" (apparently pushing the ball outward) — what is pulling it? There is no second object exerting this "force."

> 원심력은 힘이 아님 (Centrifugal force is not a force)

The same logic applies to ALL inertial forces — they arise from the acceleration of the reference frame, not from any physical interaction.

*Diagram: Same ball-rope system. Centripetal force and its reaction (rope-ball pair) clearly labeled. Centrifugal force labeled with "reaction: cannot be found."*

---

### Slide 13 — Summary: 27A

**Non-inertial frame**: A frame where Newton's $F = ma$ does not hold.

**Modified equation for non-inertial frame B**:

$$F' = ma_{CB}$$

$$F' = F - ma_{BA}$$

where $a_{BA}$ = acceleration of non-inertial frame B relative to inertial frame A.

**Inertial force** $= -ma_{BA}$:
- Allows Newton-like form in non-inertial frame
- Is NOT a real force (no reaction force exists)

## Section 27B: Equivalence Principle (동등 원리)

---

### Slide 14 — Section Title: 27B 동등 원리

**27B: The Equivalence Principle**

*Einstein's key insight: gravity and inertial force are indistinguishable.*

---

### Slide 15 — What Is Relativity Theory? (상대성이론이란?)

**Relativity theory**: Studies the **relationship** between how the same physical phenomenon is described in two different reference frames A and B.

*Diagram: Dancer performing a leap. The same motion photographed from two different positions — observers A and B see the same event from different reference frames and describe it differently using different coordinates.*

---

### Slide 16 — What Is Special Relativity? (특수 상대성이론이란?)

**Special relativity**: The theory of relativity when frames A and B move at **constant velocity** relative to each other — i.e., both are inertial frames.

$$\text{Special relativity: deals with two inertial frames}$$

*Diagram: Same dancer, frames A and B both with horizontal arrows showing constant relative velocity (equal speed, no acceleration).*

---

### Slide 17 — What Is General Relativity? (일반 상대성이론이란?)

**General relativity**: The theory of relativity when frames A and B can include **accelerating** (non-inertial) frames.

$$\text{General relativity: deals with inertial AND non-inertial frames}$$

*Diagram: Same dancer, frame A at rest, frame B accelerating (arrow growing longer) — the more general case.*

---

### Slide 18 — Einstein and General Relativity (아인슈타인과 일반 상대성이론)

Einstein, having achieved great success with Special Relativity, tried to extend it to include non-inertial frames — but found it extremely difficult.

He was living in Bern, Switzerland. One day he read in a Berlin newspaper about a young man who had fallen from a tall building and survived.

When interviewed, the young man said:

> "During the fall, I felt no force at all."

This statement inspired Einstein profoundly.

*Photo: Tall building exterior, suggesting the Sherlock Holmes story also mentioned. Einstein's famous thought-experiment moment.*

---

### Slide 19 — Equivalence Principle: The Thought Experiment (동등원리)

**Einstein's thought experiment:**

Two scenarios involving an observer in a rocket with a ball:

**Frame A** (Inertial frame, on Earth):
- Rocket stationary on Earth surface
- Ball accelerates downward at $a = -g$ due to gravity

**Frame B** (Non-inertial frame, in deep space):
- Rocket accelerating upward at $a = -g$ (relative to an inertial frame)
- Ball appears to accelerate downward at $a = -g$ due to inertial force

**Einstein's question**: If you can only watch objects fall inside the rocket, can you tell whether you're in frame A or frame B?

*Diagram: Two identical rockets side by side. Left (A): sitting on Earth, ball falls due to gravity $g$ downward. Right (B): rocket thrusting in space, ball appears to fall due to inertial force, $a=-g$.*

---

### Slide 20 — Equivalence: The Two Equations (동등원리)

**In inertial frame A** (on Earth):

The real force acting on the ball: $F = mg$ (gravity, downward)

**In non-inertial frame B** (accelerating rocket):

The inertial force acting on the ball: $F' = -ma_{BA}$ (downward, with $a_{BA} = +g$)

$$F = mg \text{ (gravity)} \quad \longleftrightarrow \quad F' = -ma_{BA} \text{ (inertial force)}$$

Both produce the same downward acceleration $-g$ on the ball.

*Diagram: Same two rockets. Under A: "Object experiences gravity $F = mg$." Under B: "Object experiences inertial force $F' = -ma_{BA}$."*

---

### Slide 21 — Equivalence: Cannot Distinguish (동등원리)

**If** the two situations (gravity in A, inertial force in B) are **completely indistinguishable** from the inside of the rocket...

$$\Downarrow$$

**Then**: Gravity and inertial force must be the **same thing!**

Einstein's quote (paraphrased): "If watching objects fall cannot distinguish whether gravity or inertial force is present, then perhaps they are identical in nature."

*Same two-rocket diagram. Einstein's portrait added. Bubble: "중력과 관성력이 구분할 수 없을 정도로 똑같은 것이 아닐까?" (Could gravity and inertial force be identical to an indistinguishable degree?)*

---

### Slide 22 — Equivalence Leads to a Radical Idea (동등원리에서 혁명적 아이디어로)

**If gravity and inertial force are identical** (equivalent)...

$$\Downarrow$$

**Then**: What we call "gravity" might actually be the inertial force — which is **not a real force**.

Einstein's radical conclusion:

> *"What we think of as gravity might actually not be a real force at all — it could be an inertial force."*

*Same diagram. New bubble: "중력이라고 생각한 것이 관성력처럼 힘이 아닌 것은 아닐까?" (Could gravity, like inertial force, not be a real force?)*

---

### Slide 23 — Can This Be Tested on Earth? (동등원리 — 지상에서 확인?)

**Einstein's next thought**: If gravity is not fundamentally a force, this would be a profound insight — but is it just a thought experiment, or can it be confirmed on Earth?

The key question: **Is there genuine weightlessness achievable on the surface of the Earth?**

If yes → equivalence principle is correct → non-inertial frame physics = gravitational physics → General Relativity.

*Same two-rocket diagram. New bubble: "우주선 사고실험이 아니라 지상에서 실제로 그것을 확인할 수 있을까?" (Not just a spaceship thought experiment — can we actually confirm this on Earth?)*

---

### Slide 24 — Summary: 27B

Einstein tried to generalize Special Relativity to include non-inertial frames.

The spaceship thought experiment showed:
- **Gravity** (in inertial frame on Earth) and **inertial force** (in non-inertial accelerating rocket) produce identical effects
- They are **indistinguishable** from inside the rocket

**Equivalence principle (동등원리)**: Gravity and inertial force are the same thing.

**Implication**: Gravity (like inertial force) might not be a real force — it might be a geometric effect of curved spacetime.

## Section 27C: Weightlessness (무중력 상태)

---

### Slide 25 — Section Title: 27C 무중력 상태

**27C: Weightlessness**

*The equivalence principle confirmed on Earth — free fall produces genuine weightlessness.*

---

### Slide 26 — Free Fall Motion (자유낙하 운동)

**Free fall (자유낙하)**: Motion where an object is only affected by gravity (inertial force / 만유인력).

**True free fall** (real physical examples):
- Artificial satellites
- The Moon orbiting Earth
- Planets orbiting the Sun
- Stars in the galaxy

**On Earth**: Can approximate as free fall when:
- Gravity is much larger than other forces (e.g., a heavy ball dropped in air — air resistance negligible)

**Cannot approximate as free fall on Earth**:
- When other forces are comparable to gravity (e.g., parachutist — air drag $\approx mg$; rocket with thrust)

---

### Slide 27 — Equivalence: Free Fall = No-Gravity (동등원리)

Both of the following are equivalent:

**Left scenario**: Inertial frame A (on Earth) + Non-inertial frame B (rocket accelerating upward at $a=+g$)
→ Observer in B feels same as observer on Earth; ball falls at $a=-g$

**Right scenario**: Non-inertial frame A (free-falling rocket on Earth, $a=-g$) + Inertial frame B (in space, no gravity)
→ Observer in freely falling rocket feels same as observer in deep space with no gravity

*Diagram: Four-panel comparison showing both equivalences. Key equation: $a = -g$ appears in all panels, demonstrating the complete symmetry.*

---

### Slide 28 — Weightlessness in Free Fall (동등원리 — 무중력)

**In inertial frame B** (floating in deep space):
- Gravity acting on object: **zero**
- Object is in **weightless state** (무중력 상태)

**In non-inertial frame A** (freely falling rocket on Earth):
- Gravity $mg$ acts downward
- Inertial force $ma_{BA} = m\cdot g$ acts upward (since rocket falls at $a = -g$)
- Net effective force: $mg - m\cdot g = 0$ → **weightless state**

**This means**: Gravity and inertial force **cancel** in a freely falling frame.

The freely falling rocket on Earth IS equivalent to floating in deep space with no gravity. Genuine weightlessness can be achieved on Earth — inside a freely falling object!

---

> Remarkable foresight: Verne predicted weightlessness based on gravitational balance, 40 years before Einstein formulated the equivalence principle.

### Slide 31 — Accelerating Elevator with Scale (가속 운동하는 승강기)

**Setup**: A person stands on a scale in an elevator. The elevator accelerates upward at acceleration $a$.

**Free body diagram** (FBD) of person:
- Normal force $N$ (scale reading) acts upward
- Weight $mg$ acts downward

**Newton's second law** (in inertial frame):
$$N - mg = ma \implies N = mg + ma = m(g+a)$$

**Special case**: If $a = -g$ (elevator in free fall):
$$N = m(g + (-g)) = m \times 0 = 0$$

**Scale reads zero!** — Weightlessness inside the freely falling elevator.

*Diagram: Elevator with person on scale. FBD showing $N$ (up) and $mg$ (down). Scale display showing "0 kg" in free-fall case.*

### Slide 32 — Pop Quiz: Forces During a Fall (돌발 퀴즈)

**Question**: A person jumps from the roof of a 63-story building. During the fall (ignoring air resistance), what forces do they feel?

1. Gravity acting downward
2. Gravity downward AND inertial force upward
3. **No forces at all**
4. They're unconscious so feel nothing

**Answer: ③ No forces at all.**

By the equivalence principle: the person is in a freely falling reference frame. Gravity and inertial force cancel. The effective force is zero.

They are in a temporary state of **perfect weightlessness** — just like an astronaut in orbit.

(Air resistance would exist in reality, but the question specifies to ignore it.)

### Slide 33 — Summary: 27C

**Equivalence principle in action:**

By the equivalence principle, a freely falling reference frame on Earth experiences the same physics as an inertial frame in deep space with no gravity.

This means: **weightlessness is achievable on Earth** — inside any freely falling object.

**Evidence for General Relativity**: If genuine weightlessness exists on Earth (demonstrable by experiment), then the equivalence principle is confirmed, supporting General Relativity.

## Section 27D: Einstein's Field Equation (아인슈타인의 장방정식)

---

### Slide 34 — Section Title: 27D 아인슈타인의 장방정식

**27D: Einstein's Field Equation**

*The mathematical heart of General Relativity.*

---

### Slide 35 — From Special to General Relativity (특수 상대성이론에서 일반 상대성이론으로)

**1905**: Special Relativity — Lorentz transformation:

$$x' = \gamma(x - \beta ct), \quad ct' = \gamma(ct - \beta x)$$

↓ Extended to non-inertial frames (10 years of effort)

**1915**: General Relativity — **Einstein's field equation**:

$$\boxed{R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}}$$

**Three tensors:**

| Symbol | Name | Physical meaning |
|--------|------|-----------------|
| $g_{\mu\nu}$ | **Metric tensor** | Describes the geometry (curvature) of spacetime |
| $R_{\mu\nu}$ | **Ricci tensor** | Complex differential operator applied to $g_{\mu\nu}$ (measures curvature) |
| $T_{\mu\nu}$ | **Energy-momentum tensor** | Distribution of mass, energy, and momentum |

---

### Slide 36 — Einstein's Field Equation: The Solution (아인슈타인의 장 방정식)

$$R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}$$

This is a system of **10 nonlinear partial differential equations** (symmetric $4\times4$ tensor equations).

**Variables:**
- Dependent variable: $g_{\mu\nu}$ (the 10 independent components of the metric tensor)
- Independent variables: $x, y, z, t$ (spacetime coordinates)

**The solution**: $g_{\mu\nu}(x, y, z, t)$ — the metric tensor field throughout all spacetime.

**Interpretation**: The metric tensor tells you how spacetime is curved at every point. Once you know $g_{\mu\nu}$, you know the geometry, and from that you know how matter moves.

*Image: Curved spacetime grid depressed by a massive body (like a bowling ball on a rubber sheet). Illustrates the concept of spacetime curvature.*

---

### Slide 37 — Newton vs. Einstein: Theory of Gravity (뉴턴과 아인슈타인의 중력 이론)

| Aspect | Newton's Gravity Theory | Einstein's Gravity Theory (GR) |
|--------|------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Core idea | Mass creates a gravitational field; other masses feel the force | Mass curves spacetime; objects follow curved spacetime |
| Motion law | Force law $\vec{F} = G\frac{Mm}{r^2}\hat{r}$ | Geodesic equation (shortest path in curved spacetime) |
| Free fall | Object accelerates under gravity | Object follows geodesic (straight line in curved spacetime) |
| Accuracy | Works perfectly at everyday speeds and weak gravity | Works at all speeds and all gravitational strengths |
| Difficulty | Simple to compute | Extremely complex (10 coupled nonlinear PDEs) |

**Key quote**: *"Gravity is the curved geometry."*

Both theories give the same results for everyday situations (solar system, etc.) — GR corrections are tiny but measurable.

### Slide 38 — Eddington's Eclipse Experiment (에딩턴의 개기 일식 실험)

**Arthur Eddington** (England, 1882–1944)

**1919**: Used a **total solar eclipse** to test General Relativity.

**Idea**: During a solar eclipse, stars near the Sun's edge are visible. If GR is correct, the Sun's gravity should bend light from these stars by a specific angle.

- **Newton's prediction**: Light deflection = $0.875''$
- **Einstein's GR prediction**: Light deflection = $1.75''$ (exactly twice Newton's)

**Results (May 29, 1919 eclipse)**:
- Measured angle = $1.61''$ to $1.98''$ (consistent with GR, not Newton)

**Conclusion**: **Newton's gravity theory is WRONG; Einstein's GR is correct.**

This was the first experimental confirmation of General Relativity, making Einstein world-famous overnight.

*Portrait: Arthur Eddington. Diagram: Star positions during eclipse (apparent position vs. true position displaced by solar gravity).*

### Slide 39 — Planetary Orbits Explained by GR (행성들의 운동)

**Kepler's laws** (elliptical orbits) can be derived from Newtonian gravity under the one-body approximation:
- Planet orbits Sun → exact Kepler ellipse

**Two-body corrections** (planet-planet gravitational interactions):
- Orbital axes **precess** (rotate slowly) → departures from perfect Kepler ellipses

**Examples:**
- Discovery of Uranus (1781) led to Neptune's discovery (1846)
- **Mercury's anomalous precession** → required GR to explain

*Diagrams: Perfect ellipse (one-body); precessing ellipse (two-body; orbital axis slowly rotates, tracing a rosette pattern).*

### Slide 42 — Newton's Two Puzzles: Inertial vs. Gravitational Mass (뉴턴 역학의 두 퍼즐)

**Puzzle 1: Why should $m_g = m_i$?**

Newton's mechanics defines mass in two completely different ways:

**Inertial mass** $m_i$ (from $F = m_i a$):
- Mass as **resistance to acceleration**
- Defined by Newton's second law (motion law)

**Gravitational mass** $m_g$ (from $F = G\frac{Mm_g}{R^2}$):
- Mass as **source of gravity**
- Defined by Newton's universal gravitation law

**Question**: Why are these two separately-defined quantities always equal? $m_i = m_g$?

In Newton's framework, this equality is **unexplained** — it's just an observed fact.

---

### Slide 43 — Newton's Two Puzzles: Same Trajectory (같은 궤적)

**Puzzle 2: Why do objects of different masses follow the same trajectory?**

If a basketball and a ping-pong ball start at the same position with the same velocity, they follow the **same path** under gravity — regardless of their mass difference.

This seems obvious from everyday experience, but theoretically:

$$\text{Trajectory in Newton's gravity: } a = \frac{F}{m_i} = \frac{Gm_g M}{R^2 m_i} = \frac{GM}{R^2} \cdot \frac{m_g}{m_i}$$

If $m_g/m_i$ were different for different objects, they would follow different trajectories — but they don't.

**Explanation**: Because $m_g = m_i$ always → $a = GM/R^2$, independent of mass.

But again: **why** should $m_g = m_i$? Newton could not explain this.

*Diagram: Two projectiles (basketball and ping-pong ball) launched with same initial conditions; both follow identical parabolic trajectory.*

---

### Slide 44 — GR Resolves Puzzle 1: Equivalence Principle (뉴턴 역학 → 일반 상대성이론 ①)

**Resolution of the $m_g = m_i$ puzzle by GR:**

**Equivalence principle**: An object freely falling on Earth is in a weightless state.

In the freely falling frame, the net effective force is zero. This requires:

$$m_g \cdot g = m_i \cdot g$$

i.e., the gravitational force ($m_g g$, from gravitational mass) must equal the inertial force ($m_i g$, from inertial mass) for them to cancel.

$$\boxed{m_g = m_i}$$

**The equivalence principle demands that gravitational and inertial mass be equal** — it is no longer a mysterious coincidence but a consequence of a fundamental principle.

*Diagram: Rocket A (on Earth) with inertial frame; ball in weightless state. Equations: $m_g g$ (gravity) = $m_i g$ (inertial force) → puzzle resolved!*

---

### Slide 45 — GR Resolves Puzzle 2: Curved Spacetime (뉴턴 역학 → 일반 상대성이론 ②)

**Resolution of the same-trajectory puzzle by Einstein's field equation:**

Einstein's key insight: **"Gravity IS the curved geometry."**

**How**: Mass curves spacetime. Objects (regardless of their mass) simply follow the shortest path (geodesic) through that curved spacetime.

Since all objects travel along the same geodesics in the same spacetime — they all follow the **same trajectory**, independent of mass.

$$\text{Different masses, same path} \Leftrightarrow \text{All objects follow the same geodesic}$$

**Puzzle solved**: There is no need for $m_g/m_i$ to be the same for all objects — the trajectory is determined by spacetime geometry, not by any ratio of masses.

*Image: Curved spacetime funnel. Planets of different sizes all follow the same curved paths around the Sun.*

**27E: Non-Euclidean Geometry**

### Slide 48 — Euclid and Flat Space (유클리드 기하학 → 평평한 공간)

**Euclid** (Greece, 4th–3rd century BC)

- Studied mathematics in Alexandria, the intellectual center of the ancient world
- Taught mathematics to King Ptolemy
- Famous for saying: "There is no royal road to geometry" (when Ptolemy asked for a shorter path to mathematics)
- Wrote *Elements* (~300 BC) — one of the most influential textbooks ever written

Euclid's geometry was so complete and successful that for nearly 2000 years, mathematicians assumed it described the **only possible geometry**.

All of Euclidean geometry is based on **five axioms (postulates)** that Euclid took as self-evidently true.

*Portrait: Euclid (artist's impression).*

---

### Slide 49 — Five Postulates of Euclidean Geometry (유클리드 기하학의 다섯 가지 공리)

| # | Postulate | Statement |
|---|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | Two-point postulate | Given any two points, exactly one straight line can be drawn through them |
| 2 | Extension postulate | Any finite straight line can be extended indefinitely |
| 3 | Circle postulate | A circle can be drawn with any center and any radius |
| 4 | Right angle postulate | All right angles are equal |
| **5** | **Parallel postulate** | Through a point not on a given line, **exactly one** line parallel to the given line exists |

The first four postulates seemed obviously true. The fifth (**parallel postulate**) was always slightly suspect — more complex than the others.

Euclid himself noted he couldn't prove it from the first four, which is why he listed it as an axiom. Was this truly obvious?

---

### Slide 50 — 19th Century Mathematicians Challenge Euclid (19세기 기하학자들이 제기한 가능성)

**The parallel postulate**: Through point $P$ not on line $a$, how many lines exist that are parallel to $a$ (never meet $a$)?

Three possibilities:

| Answer | Geometry | Proponents |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| Exactly **1** parallel line | Flat / Euclidean Geometry | Euclid |
| **0** parallel lines (all lines through P meet $a$) | Elliptic / Riemannian Geometry | Gauss, Riemann |
| **Infinitely many** parallel lines through P | Hyperbolic / Bolyai Geometry | Bolyai, Lobachevsky |

**Key discovery** (19th century): Each of these alternatives is internally **self-consistent** — they are all valid geometries, just describing different kinds of curved spaces.

Non-Euclidean geometries are mathematically legitimate!

---

### Slide 51 — Three Types of Geometry (유클리드 기하학 vs. 비-유클리드 기하학)

| Property | Flat Geometry | Elliptic Geometry | Hyperbolic Geometry |
|----------|--------------|------------------|---------------------|
| Parallel postulate | 1 parallel | 0 parallels | ∞ parallels |
| Also called | Euclidean Geometry | Riemannian Geometry | Bolyai Geometry |
| Curvature | Zero ($\kappa = 0$) | Positive ($\kappa > 0$) | Negative ($\kappa < 0$) |
| Physical realization | Flat plane | Surface of a sphere | Saddle surface |

*Diagram: Three panels showing a right-angle figure on: flat surface (parallel lines stay parallel), spherical surface (lines converge), saddle surface (lines diverge).*

### Slide 52 — Curvature: 1D (곡률)

**Curvature (곡률)** $\kappa$: A quantity that measures **how much a space is bent** (compared to flat).

**1D curvature**: For a curve in 2D space:

$$\kappa = \frac{1}{r}$$

where $r$ = radius of the **osculating circle** (the circle that best fits the curve at that point).

- A straight line: $\kappa = 0$ at every point ($r \to \infty$)
- A tightly curved arc: large $\kappa$ (small $r$)
- A gently curved arc: small $\kappa$ (large $r$)

*Graph: A wavy curve in the xy-plane. At two points on the curve, osculating circles of radius $r$ are drawn. The local curvature is $1/r$.*

---

### Slide 53 — Curvature: 2D (곡률)

**2D curvature**: For a surface in 3D space:

$$\kappa = \frac{1}{r}$$

where $r$ = radius of the **osculating sphere** (the sphere that best fits the surface at that point).

- A flat plane: $\kappa = 0$ at every point
- A spherical surface: $\kappa = 1/R$ (constant, where $R$ = sphere radius)
- A saddle surface: $\kappa < 0$

**Key difference from 1D**: In 2D, curvature can be **intrinsic** — measurable by a 2D being living ON the surface without reference to a 3D embedding.

A sphere has intrinsic positive curvature; a saddle has intrinsic negative curvature; a flat plane has zero intrinsic curvature.

---

### Slide 54 — Properties of Different Geometries: Angles (서로 다른 geometry의 성질)

How do triangles behave in each geometry?

| Geometry | Curvature $\kappa$ | Sum of triangle angles $\Delta$ |
|----------|--------------------|--------------------------------|
| Flat (Euclidean) | $\kappa = 0$ | $\Delta = \pi$ (= 180°) |
| Elliptic (Riemann) | $\kappa = 1/r > 0$ | $\Delta > \pi$ (> 180°) |
| Hyperbolic (Bolyai) | $\kappa = 1/r < 0$ | $\Delta < \pi$ (< 180°) |

**Example**: A triangle drawn on a sphere — connect three points at the North Pole and two points on the equator. Each angle is 90°, so the sum = 270° > 180°. ✓

*Diagram: Three images — flat surface with triangle ($\Delta = 2\pi$, normal angles), sphere with triangle ($\Delta > 2\pi$, angles add to more than 180°), saddle with triangle ($\Delta < 2\pi$).*

---

### Slide 55 — Properties of Different Geometries: Circles (서로 다른 geometry의 성질)

How do circles and areas behave in each geometry?

| Geometry | Circle circumference $C$ | Circle area $A$ |
|----------|--------------------------|----------------|
| Flat (Euclidean) | $C = 2\pi r$ | $A = \pi r^2$ |
| Elliptic (Riemann, positive curvature) | $C < 2\pi r$ | $A > \pi r^2$ |
| Hyperbolic (Bolyai, negative curvature) | $C > 2\pi r$ | $A < \pi r^2$ |

**Intuition**: On a sphere (positive curvature), a circle of radius $r$ (measured along the surface) encloses a smaller circumference than a flat circle of the same radius — the surface "curves in," making the boundary smaller.

*Diagram: Three surfaces (flat plane, sphere, saddle) each with a circle drawn. Circumference and area relationships labeled.*

---

### Slide 56 — What Geometry Is Our Universe? (우리 우주는 어떤 geometry에 속할까?)

**Karl Friedrich Gauss** (Germany, 1777–1855) — often called "the greatest mathematician of all time."

- First to use the term "non-Euclidean geometry"
- Wondered: Is our actual physical space flat, elliptic, or hyperbolic?

**Gauss's experiment**: Climbed three mountain peaks and measured the angles of the triangle formed. Found the sum was $\pi$ (180°) within measurement error — suggesting flat (Euclidean) geometry.

But: his triangle was too small to detect any curvature of space.

> "Using only logic, you can't disprove the existence of spaces that are flat — a great mathematician who imagined spaces beyond what can be drawn."

*Portrait: Gauss. Einstein's portrait also shown with comment about Gauss as a mathematician Gauss deserves respect.*
