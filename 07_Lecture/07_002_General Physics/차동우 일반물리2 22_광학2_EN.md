## Page 2 (Slide 59) — Optical Instruments

**What is an optical instrument?**

- A device that allows us to observe objects more clearly by controlling the path of light
- Made by appropriately combining mirrors and lenses

The operating principle of optical instruments is explained by the law of reflection and Snell's law.

*(Photos: telescope, microscope, magnifying glass on stand)*

---

## Page 3 (Slide 60) — The Mirror Problem

*(Diagram: eye on left, mirror (vertical), object (candle) on right of mirror, image on left of mirror)*

- **o** → Distance from mirror to object
- **i** → Distance from mirror to image

**Goal of the mirror problem:**

Given the distance o from the mirror to the object, find the distance i from the mirror to the image and the size (magnification) of the image.

---

## Page 4 (Slide 61) — The Mirror Problem (continued)

*(Same diagram with highlighted ray paths)*

Since the mirror changes the path of light, a person's eye does not know that the path of light entering the eye was changed midway.

The eye perceives the object as being located at the position where the incoming rays, extended as straight lines, intersect.

→ **This is called an image.**

---

## Page 5 (Slide 62) — Mirror Formula

All mirror problems are solved using the following formula:

$$\frac{1}{o} + \frac{1}{i} = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{2}{R}$$

| Symbol | Meaning | Sign Convention |
|---|---|---|
| **o** | Distance from mirror to object | Always positive: o > 0 |
| **i** | Distance from mirror to image | Same side as object: i > 0; opposite side: i < 0 |
| **f** | Distance from mirror to focal point | Same side as object: f > 0; opposite side: f < 0 |
| **R** | Radius of curvature of the mirror | R = 2f |
| **m** | Magnification of mirror | $m = -\dfrac{i}{o}$ ; upright: m > 0; inverted: m < 0 |

---

## Page 6 (Slide 63) — Plane Mirror

**Focal length of a plane mirror** → f = ∞

**Mirror formula:**

$$\frac{1}{o} + \frac{1}{i} = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{\infty} = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad i = -o$$

The image is formed at the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front.

**Magnification:**

$$m = -\frac{i}{o} = +1 > 0$$

The image is the same size as the object — **upright virtual image**.

*(Diagram: object in front of mirror, image behind mirror at equal distance, with o and i labeled)*

---

## Page 7 (Slide 64) — How to Draw the Image for a Plane Mirror

1. Draw a ray from a point on the object according to the law of reflection.
2. Draw another ray from the same point in a different direction, also by the law of reflection.
3. Extend the two rays entering the eye until they intersect — this gives the image point.
4. The intersection point is where the image forms.
5. From the same point on the object, any ray starting from the image travels in the same direction as the ray from the image.
6. **Since light does not actually travel from the image, this image is a virtual image.**

*(Diagram: ray construction showing object, mirror, rays, and virtual image behind mirror)*

---

## Page 8 (Slide 65) — Image Formed by a Concave Mirror

1. Draw two rays starting from a point on the object according to the law of reflection.
2. The image forms at the point where the two reflected rays meet.
3. From the same point on the object, any ray travels so as to meet at the image point.
4. **Since light actually converges at the image, this image is a real image.**

*(Diagram: concave mirror with object in front, two rays converging to form a real image)*

---

## Page 9 (Slide 66) — Easy Way to Find the Image in a Concave Mirror

1. Drawing two rays from a point on the object according to the law of reflection gives the image.
2. There are rays that can be easily drawn even without applying the law of reflection.
3. **These rays are called the principal rays of the concave mirror.**

*(Diagram: same concave mirror ray construction)*

---

## Page 10 (Slide 67) — Terms Used for Concave Mirrors

*(Diagram: concave mirror with labeled points C (center of curvature), F (focal point), A (vertex), and distances f and R)*

**Terms:**

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Principal axis** | The line connecting the center of the mirror A and the center of curvature C |
| **Focal point F** | The point through which all reflected rays pass when light parallel to the principal axis is incident on the concave mirror |
| **Focal length f** | Distance from the center of the mirror A to the focal point F |
| **Radius of curvature R** | Distance from the center A to the center of curvature C; R = 2f |

---

## Page 11 (Slide 68) — Principal Rays of a Concave Mirror

*(Diagram: concave mirror with 4 numbered principal rays)*

**Principal rays** — rays that are easy to draw from the mirror:

1. A ray parallel to the principal axis is reflected and passes through the focal point.
2. A ray directed toward the focal point is reflected parallel to the principal axis.
3. A ray directed toward the center of curvature is reflected back on the same path.
4. A ray directed toward the center of the mirror is reflected at the same angle as the angle of incidence.

---

## Page 12 (Slide 69) — Summary (22F)

Mirror problems are solved using the mirror formula:

$$\frac{1}{o} + \frac{1}{i} = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{2}{R}$$

The image forms where two rays from an object intersect.

Using principal rays, the image can be found easily.

**Principal rays of a concave mirror:**
1. A ray parallel to the principal axis is reflected and passes through the focal point.
2. A ray directed toward the focal point is reflected parallel to the principal axis.
3. A ray directed toward the center of curvature is reflected back on the same path.
4. A ray directed toward the center of the mirror is reflected at the same angle as the angle of incidence.

## Page 15 (Slide 72) — Image in a Concave Mirror (o > R)

**Case (1): o > R**

- **Principal ray 1** → parallel to axis → reflected, passes beyond focal point
- **Principal ray 2** → directed toward focal point → reflected, travels parallel to axis

The image forms where the two reflected rays meet → **inverted real image**

*(Diagram: object outside center of curvature, real inverted image between F and C)*

---

## Page 16 (Slide 73) — Image in a Concave Mirror (o > R) — Analysis

$$o > 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{always}$$

$$i > 0, \quad f > 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{image and focal point are on the same side as object}$$

$$0 < f < i < o$$

$$\frac{1}{o} + \frac{1}{i} = \frac{1}{f}$$

**Magnification:**

$$m = -\frac{i}{o} < 0 \qquad |m| < 1$$

**→ Reduced, inverted real image**

---

## Page 17 (Slide 74) — Image in a Concave Mirror (f < o < R)

**Case (2): f < o < R**

- **Principal ray 1** → parallel to axis → reflected, passes beyond focal point
- **Principal ray 2** → directed toward center of mirror → reflected at equal angles

The image forms where the two reflected rays meet → **inverted real image**

*(Diagram: object between F and C, enlarged real inverted image beyond C)*

---

## Page 18 (Slide 75) — Image in a Concave Mirror (f < o < R) — Analysis

$$o > 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{always}$$

$$i > 0, \quad f > 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{image and focal point on same side as object}$$

$$0 < f < o < i$$

$$\frac{1}{o} + \frac{1}{i} = \frac{1}{f}$$

**Magnification:**

$$m = -\frac{i}{o} < 0 \qquad |m| > 1$$

**→ Enlarged, inverted real image**

---

## Page 19 (Slide 76) — Image in a Concave Mirror (o < f)

**Case (3): o < f**

- **Principal ray 1** → parallel to axis → reflected, passes focal point
- **Principal ray 2** → directed toward center of mirror → reflected at equal angles

The two reflected rays diverge; extending them backward (dashed) gives their intersection → **upright virtual image**

*(Diagram: object inside F, virtual enlarged upright image behind mirror)*

---

## Page 20 (Slide 77) — Image in a Concave Mirror (o < f) — Analysis

$$o > 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{always}$$

$$i < 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{image is on opposite side from object}$$

$$f > 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{focal point is on same side as object}$$

$$o < |i|$$

$$\frac{1}{o} + \frac{1}{i} = \frac{1}{f}$$

**Magnification:**

$$m = -\frac{i}{o} > 1$$

**→ Enlarged, upright virtual image**

---

## Page 22 (Slide 79) — Principal Rays of a Convex Mirror

*(Diagram: convex mirror with 4 principal rays shown)*

**Principal rays:**

1. A ray parallel to the principal axis is reflected and travels along the extension of the line through the focal point.
2. A ray directed toward the focal point is reflected parallel to the principal axis.
3. A ray directed toward the center of curvature is reflected back on the same path.
4. A ray directed toward the center of the mirror is reflected at the same angle as the angle of incidence.

---

## Page 23 (Slide 80) — Image Formed by a Convex Mirror

There is no constraint on the object distance o for a convex mirror.

- **Principal ray 1** → parallel to axis → reflected, extends backward through focal point
- **Principal ray 2** → directed toward center of curvature → reflected back on same path

The intersection of the backward extensions of the two reflected rays gives the image → **upright virtual image**

*(Diagram: convex mirror with object in front; dashed extensions behind mirror converge to virtual image)*

---

## Page 24 (Slide 81) — Image in a Convex Mirror — Analysis

Regardless of the object distance o:

$$|i| < o, \quad |i| < |f|$$

$$\frac{1}{o} + \frac{1}{i} = \frac{1}{f}$$

$$o > 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{always}$$

$$i < 0, \quad f < 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{image and focal point on opposite side from object}$$

**Magnification:**

$$m = -\frac{i}{o} > 0 \qquad |m| < 1$$

**→ Reduced, upright virtual image (always)**

|---|---|

## Page 27 (Slide 84) — Lens Formula

All lens problems are solved using the following formula:

$$\frac{1}{o} + \frac{1}{i} = \frac{1}{f}$$

## Page 41 (Slide 98) — Superposition of Waves

When two waves arrive at the same point in a medium simultaneously, how does the medium vibrate?

→ It vibrates as the **vector sum of the displacements** of each wave.

→ **Superposition of two waves**

*(Diagrams: two triangular pulses passing through each other)*

---

## Page 42 (Slide 99) — Superposition of Waves (continued)

When two waves arrive at the same point in a medium simultaneously, the medium vibrates as the **vector sum of the displacements** of each wave — **Superposition**.

*(Diagram: two sinusoidal waves passing through each other and restoring to original shapes)*

→ After the superposed waves separate, each wave **returns to its original form**.
→ This superposition principle applies equally to **electromagnetic waves**.

---

## Page 43 (Slide 100) — Constructive and Destructive Interference

**Wave Interference** → The phenomenon in which the effects of two waves add together when they meet.

| **Constructive Interference** | **Destructive Interference** |
|---|---|
| Wave 1 and Wave 2 are in phase → amplitude doubles | Wave 1 and Wave 2 are out of phase (180°) → amplitude cancels |

*(Diagrams: constructive — same-phase waves and their sum; destructive — opposite-phase waves and their sum)*

---

## Page 44 (Slide 101) — Double-Slit Interference Fringes

**Double-slit interference fringe** → Fringes formed by light passing through two slits and interfering.

*(Diagram: plane wave → double slit → interference pattern on screen with alternating bright/dark bands)*

- **Bright fringes** → Constructive interference
- **Dark fringes** → Destructive interference

## Page 47 (Slide 104) — Young's Double-Slit Interference Pattern

*(Diagram: two slits a (top) and b (bottom) separated by distance d; screen at distance D; point c on screen; path lengths ac and bc)*

The **path difference δ** between the two paths ac and bc:

$$\delta = \overline{bc} - \overline{ac} \approx d\sin\theta' \approx d\sin\theta$$

$$\boxed{\delta \approx d\sin\theta}$$

---

## Page 48 (Slide 105) — Young's Double-Slit Interference Conditions

*(Diagram: same geometry — slits, distance D, angle θ)*

**Constructive and destructive interference conditions for light of wavelength λ:**

| Condition | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Constructive interference** | $\delta = d\sin\theta = m\lambda$ |
| **Destructive interference** | $\delta = d\sin\theta = \left(m + \dfrac{1}{2}\right)\lambda$ |

where $m = 0, \pm1, \pm2, \ldots$

---

## Page 49 (Slide 106) — Young's Double-Slit Interference Pattern (Position)

$$d\sin\theta = m\lambda \quad \text{(constructive)} \qquad d\sin\theta = \left(m+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\lambda \quad \text{(destructive)}$$

The position of the **m-th bright fringe** on the screen, y_m (where the angle θ has value θ_m):

$$y_m = D\tan\theta_m \approx D\sin\theta_m = D\frac{m\lambda}{d}$$

**Spacing between bright fringes Δ:**

$$\Delta = y_{m+1} - y_m = D\frac{\lambda}{d}$$

---

## Page 50 (Slide 107) — Summary (22I)

Interference of light occurs only between **coherent** (same-phase) light waves.

Two beams that were originally the same light, split and sent along different paths, recombine as coherent light.

**Young's double-slit interference fringes:**

| Condition | Formula |
|---|---|
| Constructive interference | $d\sin\theta = m\lambda$ |
| Destructive interference | $d\sin\theta = \left(m+\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\lambda$ |

**Spacing between bright fringes:**

$$\Delta = D\frac{\lambda}{d}$$

## Page 52 (Slide 109) — Diffraction of Light

$$\delta_m = \frac{a}{2m}\sin\theta_m = \frac{\lambda}{2} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad a\sin\theta_m = m\lambda$$
