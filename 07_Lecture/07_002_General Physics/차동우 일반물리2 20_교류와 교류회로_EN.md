## Page 3 (Slide 3) — AC Generator: Principle

**AC Generator**

**Principle**

When a closed loop is forcibly rotated inside a magnetic field produced by a permanent magnet, an induced EMF is generated in the loop.

Using this EMF as a time-varying source of alternating current is what an AC generator does.

*(Diagram: 3D view of a permanent magnet (N–S poles) with a rectangular coil rotating between the poles, connected via slip rings to an external resistor R and voltmeter; a sinusoidal voltage waveform U(t) is displayed above.)*

---

## Page 4 (Slide 4) — Magnetic Flux Through the Rotating Loop

**Magnetic flux through the closed loop**

For a closed loop with area vector $\vec{S}$ placed in a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$, the magnetic flux $\Phi_B$ is:

$$\Rightarrow \quad \Phi_B(t) = \vec{B} \cdot \vec{S} = BS\cos\theta(t)$$

*(Diagram: Side view of the coil rotating between N and S poles of a magnet. The angle $\theta$ between $\vec{B}$ (horizontal, pointing from N to S) and the area vector $\vec{S}$ changes as the coil rotates with angular velocity $\omega$.)*

---

## Page 5 (Slide 5) — Induced EMF of the AC Generator

**If the closed loop rotates at constant angular velocity $\omega$:**

$$\Rightarrow \quad \theta(t) = \omega t$$

**Faraday's Law:**

$$\Rightarrow \quad \mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi_B(t)}{dt} = -\frac{d}{dt}(BS\cos\omega t)$$

$$= \omega BS\sin\omega t$$

$$= \mathcal{E}_0 \sin\omega t \qquad \text{where } \mathcal{E}_0 = \omega BS$$

**Expression for AC EMF:**

$$\Rightarrow \quad \mathcal{E}(t) = \mathcal{E}_0 \sin\omega t$$

*(Diagram: Simple circuit with an AC source (symbol) connected to a resistor R.)*

## Page 7 (Slide 7) — AC EMF (Graph and Parameters)

**Expression for AC EMF:**

$$\Rightarrow \quad \mathcal{E}(t) = \mathcal{E}_0 \sin\omega t$$

*(Diagram: Sinusoidal graph of $\mathcal{E}(t)$ vs. $t$, showing amplitude $\mathcal{E}_0$ and $-\mathcal{E}_0$, with period $T$ marked.)*

- $\mathcal{E}_0$ $\Rightarrow$ **Amplitude (peak value)** of the AC EMF
- $T$ $\Rightarrow$ **Period** of the AC EMF
- $\omega$ $\Rightarrow$ **Angular frequency** of the AC EMF

$$\Rightarrow \quad \omega = 2\pi f = \frac{2\pi}{T}$$

---

## Page 8 (Slide 8) — Average Value of AC EMF

**What is the EMF of an AC source?**

The AC EMF value varies between maximum $\mathcal{E}_0$ and minimum $-\mathcal{E}_0$ over one period $T$.

**Average value?** Time average of the EMF over one period $T$:

$$\Rightarrow \quad \langle \mathcal{E} \rangle = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T \mathcal{E}_0 \sin\omega t\, dt = -\frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{T\omega}\cos\frac{2\pi}{T}t\Bigg|_0^T$$

$$= -\frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{T\omega}(\cos 2\pi - \cos 0) = 0$$

---

## Page 9 (Slide 9) — RMS Value of AC EMF → Effective EMF

**What is the RMS (root mean square) value?**

For a periodic function whose average is zero, the RMS value is defined as the square root of the time-averaged square — this is called the **effective value**.

**Mean-square average of EMF over one period $T$:**

$$\Rightarrow \quad \langle \mathcal{E}^2 \rangle = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T (\mathcal{E}_0 \sin\omega t)^2\, dt = \frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}_0^2$$

**Effective (RMS) EMF:**

$$\Rightarrow \quad \mathcal{E}_\text{rms} = \sqrt{\langle \mathcal{E}^2 \rangle} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathcal{E}_0 \approx 0.707\,\mathcal{E}_0$$

---

## Page 10 (Slide 10) — Trigonometric Integration

**Useful integral identities:**

$$\int \sin\theta\, d\theta = -\cos\theta \qquad \int \cos\theta\, d\theta = \sin\theta$$

$$\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1 \qquad \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = \cos 2\theta$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \cos^2\theta = \frac{1+\cos 2\theta}{2} \qquad \sin^2\theta = \frac{1-\cos 2\theta}{2}$$

**Derivation of the key integral:**

$$\int_0^T \sin^2\omega t\, dt = \int_0^T \sin^2\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}t\right)dt = \frac{T}{2\pi}\int_0^T \sin^2\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}t\right)d\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}t\right)$$

$$= \frac{T}{2\pi}\int_0^T \frac{1-\cos 2\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}t\right)}{2}\,d\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}t\right) = \frac{T}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2\pi}{T}\cdot T = \frac{T}{2\pi}[\pi] = \frac{T}{2}$$

**Goal:** Find the current $i(t)$ flowing in the circuit.

**Method:** Kirchhoff's voltage law holds instantaneously in AC circuits as well.

## Page 14 (Slide 14) — Current in the AC Resistor Circuit

**AC EMF:** $\mathcal{E}(t) = \mathcal{E}_0 \sin\omega t$

**Voltage law:** $\mathcal{E}(t) - i(t)R = 0$

**Current in AC resistor circuit:**

$$\Rightarrow \quad i(t) = \frac{\mathcal{E}(t)}{R} = \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{R}\sin\omega t = i_0 \sin\omega t$$

**Relationship between peak current and peak EMF:**

$$\Rightarrow \quad i_0 = \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{R} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \mathcal{E}_0 = i_0 R \qquad \text{(same as DC circuit)}$$

## Page 27 (Slide 27) — Relationship Between Resistance, Current, and Potential Difference

**Potential difference:** $V = \phi_a - \phi_b$

**DC circuit:** $V = iR$

**AC circuit:** $V(t) = V_0 \sin\omega t$, $i(t) = i_0 \sin\omega t$ $\Rightarrow$ $V(t) = i(t)R$

**Peak value relation:** $V_0\sin\omega t = i_0 R\sin\omega t$ $\Rightarrow$ $V_0 = i_0 R$

**RMS relation:**

$$V_\text{rms} = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}} \qquad i_\text{rms} = \frac{i_0}{\sqrt{2}} \qquad \Rightarrow \quad V_\text{rms} = i_\text{rms} R$$

Both the peak-value and RMS relations in the AC circuit are identical to those in the DC circuit.

## Page 28 (Slide 28) — Power Rate and Electric Power

**Work $dW$ done on an object when force $\vec{F}$ moves it by displacement $d\vec{r}$:**

$$\Rightarrow \quad dW = \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{r}$$

**Power $P$ (work per unit time) when work $dW$ is done in time $dt$:**

$$\Rightarrow \quad P = \frac{dW}{dt} = \vec{F}\cdot\frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} = \vec{F}\cdot\vec{v}$$

**Electric power:** power (work rate) done by electrical force on charges.

When charge $dq$ moves through a potential difference $V$, the work done is:

$$\Rightarrow \quad dW = V\,dq$$

When charge $dq$ moves in time $dt$, the electric power $P$ is:

$$\Rightarrow \quad P = \frac{dW}{dt} = V\frac{dq}{dt} = Vi$$

**Unit of power:**

$$P = \frac{dW}{dt} \quad \Rightarrow \quad [\text{power}] = \frac{[\text{work}]}{[\text{time}]} = \text{J/s} = \text{W (watts)}$$

## Page 29 (Slide 29) — Power Consumed in AC Resistor Circuit

**Current in circuit:** $i(t) = i_0 \sin\omega t$

**Potential difference across resistor:** $V(t) = V_0 \sin\omega t$

**Power consumed in resistor:**

$$P(t) = V(t)\,i(t) = V_0 i_0 \sin^2\omega t = P_0 \sin^2\omega t$$

*(Diagram: Graphs of $i(t)$ (red), $V(t)$ (blue), and $P(t)$ (green) vs. $t$, showing $P(t)$ is always non-negative, oscillating at twice the frequency.)*

## Page 30 (Slide 30) — Power Dissipated in a Resistor

**DC circuit:**

When current $i$ flows through resistor $R$ with potential difference $V$: $V = Ri$

Power dissipated: $P = Vi = Ri^2 = \dfrac{V^2}{R}$

**AC circuit:** $P(t) = V(t)\,i(t) = V_0 i_0 \sin^2\omega t = P_0 \sin^2\omega t$

$$\Rightarrow \quad P_0 = V_0 i_0 = Ri_0^2 = \frac{V_0^2}{R} \qquad \text{(same as DC circuit)}$$

---

## Page 31 (Slide 31) — Average Power Dissipated in AC Resistor Circuit

**Current** $i(t) = i_0 \sin\omega t$ &emsp; **Power** $P(t) = P_0 \sin^2\omega t$

**Time average over one period $T$:**

$$\langle i \rangle = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T i_0 \sin\omega t\, dt = 0$$

$$\langle P \rangle = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T P_0 \sin^2\omega t\, dt = \frac{1}{2}P_0 = \frac{1}{2}V_0 i_0$$

Using $V_\text{rms} = \dfrac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}}$ and $i_\text{rms} = \dfrac{i_0}{\sqrt{2}}$:

$$\Rightarrow \quad \langle P \rangle = \frac{1}{2}V_0 i_0 = \frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{2}\,V_\text{rms})(\sqrt{2}\,i_\text{rms}) = V_\text{rms}\,i_\text{rms} \qquad \text{(same as DC circuit)}$$

---

## Page 32 (Slide 32) — Summary: 20C AC Circuit / Current, Resistance, Potential Difference, Power

**AC circuit: current, resistance, potential difference**

$$i(t) = i_0 \sin\omega t$$

$$V(t) = i(t)R = i_0 R\sin\omega t = V_0 \sin\omega t \quad \Rightarrow \quad V_0 = i_0 R$$

$$i_\text{rms} = \sqrt{\langle i^2 \rangle} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}i_0 \qquad V_\text{rms} = \sqrt{\langle V^2 \rangle} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}V_0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad V_\text{rms} = i_\text{rms} R$$

**Average power dissipated in AC resistor:**

$$\langle P \rangle = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T P_0 \sin^2\omega t\, dt = \frac{1}{2}P_0 = \frac{1}{2}V_0 i_0 = V_\text{rms}\,i_\text{rms}$$

## Page 41 (Slide 41) — Relationship Between Capacitance, Charge, Current, and Potential Difference

**Potential difference:** $V = \phi_a - \phi_b$

**DC circuit:** $V = \dfrac{q}{C}$

**AC circuit:** $V(t) = V_0\sin\omega t$, $q(t) = q_0\sin\omega t$, $i(t) = i_0\cos\omega t$

$$\Rightarrow \quad V(t) = \frac{q(t)}{C}$$

**Peak value relation:**

$$V_0\sin\omega t = \frac{q_0}{C}\sin\omega t \quad \Rightarrow \quad V_0 = \frac{q_0}{C} = \frac{i_0}{\omega C}$$

**RMS relation:**

$$V_\text{rms} = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}} \qquad i_\text{rms} = \frac{i_0}{\sqrt{2}} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \frac{V_\text{rms}}{i_\text{rms}} = \frac{V_0}{i_0} = \frac{1}{\omega C} \quad \Rightarrow \quad V_\text{rms} = \frac{i_\text{rms}}{\omega C}$$

## Page 42 (Slide 42) — Capacitive Reactance

**DC circuit:** $V = iR$ &emsp; $V = \dfrac{q}{C}$

**AC circuit:**

$$V_0 = i_0 R \qquad V_0 = \frac{q_0}{C} = \frac{i_0}{\omega C}$$

$$V_\text{rms} = i_\text{rms} R \qquad V_\text{rms} = \frac{q_\text{rms}}{C} = \frac{i_\text{rms}}{\omega C} = i_\text{rms} X_C$$

**Capacitive reactance:**

$$X_C = \frac{1}{\omega C} \qquad \text{(acts like resistance for capacitor in AC circuit; units: }\Omega\text{)}$$

| Resistor circuit | Capacitor circuit |

$$\Rightarrow \quad X_C = \frac{1}{\omega C} \qquad \text{(Capacitive reactance)}$$

## Page 71 (Slide 71) — Solution of the Voltage Law

**Voltage law:** $L\dfrac{d^2i}{dt^2} + R\dfrac{di}{dt} + \dfrac{i}{C} = \omega\mathcal{E}_0\cos\omega t$

**Solution:** $i(t) = i_0\sin(\omega t - \phi)$

$$\Rightarrow \quad i_0 = \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z} = \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{\sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2}}$$

**Current in AC RLC circuit:**

$$i(t) = i_0\sin(\omega t-\phi) = i_0\cos\phi\sin\omega t - i_0\sin\phi\cos\omega t$$

$$= \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\!\left(\frac{R}{Z}\sin\omega t - \frac{X_L - X_C}{Z}\cos\omega t\right)$$

where $\cos\phi = \dfrac{R}{Z}$ and $\sin\phi = \dfrac{X_L - X_C}{Z}$.

## Page 72 (Slide 72) — Verification of the Solution

**Voltage law:** $L\dfrac{d^2i}{dt^2} + R\dfrac{di}{dt} + \dfrac{i}{C} = \omega\mathcal{E}_0\cos\omega t$

**Current:** $i(t) = \dfrac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\!\left(\dfrac{R}{Z}\sin\omega t - \dfrac{X_L - X_C}{Z}\cos\omega t\right)$

$$\frac{di}{dt} = \omega\frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\!\left(\frac{R}{Z}\cos\omega t + \frac{X_L - X_C}{Z}\sin\omega t\right)$$

$$\frac{d^2i}{dt^2} = \omega^2\frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\!\left(-\frac{R}{Z}\sin\omega t + \frac{X_L - X_C}{Z}\cos\omega t\right)$$

Substituting into the LHS:

$$L\frac{d^2i}{dt^2} + R\frac{di}{dt} + \frac{i}{C} = \cos\omega t\!\left(L\omega^2\frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\frac{X_L-X_C}{Z} + R\omega\frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\frac{R}{Z} - \frac{1}{C}\frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\frac{X_L-X_C}{Z}\right) + \cdots = \omega\mathcal{E}_0\cos\omega t \; ?$$

## Page 73 (Slide 73) — Verification of the Solution (Continued)

**cos $\omega t$ coefficient:**

$$L\omega^2\frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\frac{X_L-X_C}{Z} + R\omega\frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\frac{R}{Z} - \frac{1}{C}\frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\frac{X_L-X_C}{Z}$$

$$= \frac{\omega\mathcal{E}_0}{Z^2}\bigl[X_L(X_L-X_C) + R^2 - X_C(X_L-X_C)\bigr] = \frac{\omega\mathcal{E}_0}{Z^2}\bigl[(X_L-X_C)^2 + R^2\bigr] = \omega\mathcal{E}_0 \checkmark$$

**sin $\omega t$ coefficient:**

$$-L\omega^2\frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\frac{R}{Z} + R\omega\frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\frac{X_L-X_C}{Z} + \frac{1}{C}\frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\frac{R}{Z}$$

$$= \frac{\omega\mathcal{E}_0}{Z^2}\bigl[-X_L R + R(X_L-X_C) + X_C R\bigr] = \frac{\omega\mathcal{E}_0}{Z^2}[0] = 0 \checkmark$$

## Page 76 (Slide 76) — Solving the Non-Homogeneous Linear ODE

$$L\frac{d^2i}{dt^2} + R\frac{di}{dt} + \frac{i}{C} = \omega\mathcal{E}_0\cos\omega t \qquad (1)$$

**Method:** assume $i(t) = A\sin\omega t + B\cos\omega t$ (2)

Substitute (2) into the LHS of (1) and find conditions so it equals the RHS for all $t$:

$$\frac{di}{dt} = \omega A\cos\omega t - \omega B\sin\omega t$$

$$\frac{d^2i}{dt^2} = -\omega^2 A\sin\omega t - \omega^2 B\cos\omega t$$

---

## Page 77 (Slide 77) — Solving the ODE (Continued)

Substituting into LHS:

$$L\frac{d^2i}{dt^2} + R\frac{di}{dt} + \frac{i}{C} = \sin\omega t\!\left(-L\omega^2 A - R\omega B + \frac{A}{C}\right) + \cos\omega t\!\left(-L\omega^2 B + R\omega A + \frac{B}{C}\right) = \omega\mathcal{E}_0\cos\omega t$$

Equating coefficients of $\sin\omega t$ and $\cos\omega t$:

$$\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)A + RB = 0 \qquad (1)$$

$$RA - \left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)B = \mathcal{E}_0 \qquad (2)$$

---

## Page 78 (Slide 78) — Solving the ODE (Result)

From equations (1) and (2):

$(1)\times\!\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right) + (2)\times R$:

$$\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2 A + R^2 A = R\mathcal{E}_0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad A = \frac{R\mathcal{E}_0}{R^2 + \left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2} \qquad (3) \checkmark$$

Substituting (3) into (1):

$$B = -\frac{\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}}{R}A = -\frac{\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)\mathcal{E}_0}{R^2 + \left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2} \checkmark$$

**These results are identical to those obtained by the phasor method!!**

## Page 79 (Slide 79) — RMS Current in AC RLC Circuit → Effective Current

$$\langle i \rangle = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T i_0\sin(\omega t - \phi)\, dt = 0$$

## Page 82 (Slide 82) — Average Power in AC RLC Circuit

**Current:** $i(t) = i_0\sin(\omega t - \phi)$ &emsp; **Voltage:** $V(t) = V_0\sin\omega t$

**Power:** $P(t) = V(t)\,i(t) = V_0 i_0\sin\omega t\sin(\omega t - \phi) = P_0\sin\omega t\sin(\omega t - \phi)$

$$= \frac{V_0 i_0}{2}\cos\phi = V_\text{rms}\,i_\text{rms}\cos\phi = i_\text{rms}^2 Z\cos\phi$$

$$= i_\text{rms}^2 R \qquad \text{(same as DC circuit)}$$

- AC EMF: $\mathcal{E}(t) = \mathcal{E}_0\sin\omega t$
- Current: $i(t) = i_0\sin(\omega t - \phi)$

**Voltage law:** $L\dfrac{d^2i}{dt^2} + R\dfrac{di}{dt} + \dfrac{i}{C} = \omega\mathcal{E}_0\cos\omega t$

**RMS relation:** $V_\text{rms} = i_\text{rms} Z$

**Power:** $P(t) = V(t)\,i(t) = P_0\sin\omega t\sin(\omega t - \phi)$

**Average power:**

$$\langle P \rangle = \frac{P_0}{2}\cos\phi = V_\text{rms}\,i_\text{rms}\cos\phi = i_\text{rms}^2 Z\cos\phi = i_\text{rms}^2 R$$

**Power factor:** $\cos\phi = \dfrac{R}{Z}$

## Page 86 (Slide 86) — Mechanical Forced Oscillation

*(Reference: 11H)*

**Forces on the object:**

- Spring force: $F_k = -kx$
- Friction force: $f = -bv = -b\dfrac{dx}{dt}$
- Driving force: $F_d = F_0\cos\omega t$

**Equation of motion:** $F = m\dfrac{d^2x}{dt^2}$

**Net force:** $F = F_k + f + F_d = -kx - b\dfrac{dx}{dt} + F_0\cos\omega t$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{dx}{dt} + \omega_0^2 x = \frac{F_0}{m}\cos\omega t \qquad \left(\gamma = \frac{b}{2m},\; \omega_0^2 = \frac{k}{m}\right)$$

---

## Page 87 (Slide 87) — Properties of Damped Oscillation

*(Reference: 11H)*

*(Diagram: Graph showing three damping cases — underdamped (small damping), critically damped, and overdamped — alongside photographs of a pendulum clock and a swing.)*

- Critically damped oscillation
- Underdamped (small damping) oscillation
- Overdamped oscillation

$\Rightarrow$ Damped oscillation always eventually stops. $\Rightarrow$ **Forced oscillation** is needed to sustain it.

## Page 88 (Slide 88) — Electromagnetic Forced Oscillation → AC RLC Circuit

**Kirchhoff's voltage law:**

$$L\frac{d^2i}{dt^2} + R\frac{di}{dt} + \frac{i}{C} = \omega\mathcal{E}_0\cos\omega t$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{d^2i}{dt^2} + \frac{R}{L}\frac{di}{dt} + \frac{i}{LC} = \frac{\omega\mathcal{E}_0}{L}\cos\omega t$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{d^2i}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{di}{dt} + \omega_0^2 i = \frac{\omega\mathcal{E}_0}{L}\cos\omega t \qquad \left(\gamma = \frac{R}{2L},\; \omega_0^2 = \frac{1}{LC}\right)$$

**Comparison with mechanical forced oscillation:**

$$\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{dx}{dt} + \omega_0^2 x = \frac{F_0}{m}\cos\omega t \qquad \left(\gamma = \frac{b}{2m},\; \omega_0^2 = \frac{k}{m}\right)$$

The two equations are structurally identical with the correspondence $\gamma = \dfrac{R}{2L}$, $\omega_0^2 = \dfrac{1}{LC}$.

---

## Page 89 (Slide 89) — Method for Finding the ODE Solution

$$\frac{d^2i}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{di}{dt} + \omega_0^2 i = \frac{\omega\mathcal{E}_0}{L}\cos\omega t$$

*(Reference: 11H)*

Write the solution as: $i(t) = i_h(t) + i_p(t)$

**Homogeneous solution $i_h(t)$:**

Satisfies $\dfrac{d^2i_h}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\dfrac{di_h}{dt} + \omega_0^2 i_h = 0$

**Particular solution $i_p(t)$:**

Satisfies $\dfrac{d^2i_p}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\dfrac{di_p}{dt} + \omega_0^2 i_p = \dfrac{\omega\mathcal{E}_0}{L}\cos\omega t$

---

## Page 90 (Slide 90) — Homogeneous Solution

$$\frac{d^2i_h}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{di_h}{dt} + \omega_0^2 i_h = 0$$

**Case $\omega_0 > \gamma$ (underdamped):**

$$i_h(t) = e^{-\gamma t}\!\left(Ae^{+i\omega_1 t} + Be^{-i\omega_1 t}\right) \qquad \omega_1 = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \gamma^2}$$

**Case $\omega_0 = \gamma$ (critically damped):**

$$i_h(t) = (A + Bt)e^{-\gamma t}$$

**Case $\omega_0 < \gamma$ (overdamped):**

$$i_h(t) = e^{-\gamma t}\!\left(Ae^{+\omega_2 t} + Be^{-\omega_2 t}\right) \qquad \omega_2 = \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega_0^2}$$

The homogeneous solution is needed to match initial conditions; it decays and vanishes over time.

---

## Page 91 (Slide 91) — Particular Solution

**Mechanical analogue (from 11H):**

$$\frac{d^2x_p}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{dx_p}{dt} + \omega_0^2 x_p = \frac{F_0}{m}\cos\omega t \qquad x_p(t) = A\sin\omega t + B\cos\omega t$$

$$A = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{2\gamma\omega}{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + (2\gamma\omega)^2} \qquad B = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{\omega_0^2 - \omega^2}{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + (2\gamma\omega)^2}$$

**AC RLC circuit:**

$$L\frac{d^2i}{dt^2} + R\frac{di}{dt} + \frac{i}{C} = \omega\mathcal{E}_0\cos\omega t \qquad \left(\gamma = \frac{R}{2L},\; \omega_0^2 = \frac{1}{LC}\right)$$

$$i_p(t) = A\sin\omega t + B\cos\omega t$$

$$A = \frac{\omega\mathcal{E}_0}{L}\frac{2\gamma\omega}{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega)^2} \qquad B = \frac{\omega\mathcal{E}_0}{L}\frac{\omega_0^2-\omega^2}{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega)^2}$$

---

## Page 92 (Slide 92) — Particular Solution (Equivalence with Phasor Result)

**Particular solution (from ODE):**

$$i_p(t) = A\sin\omega t + B\cos\omega t$$

$$A = \frac{\omega\mathcal{E}_0}{L}\frac{2\gamma\omega}{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega)^2} \qquad B = \frac{\omega\mathcal{E}_0}{L}\frac{\omega_0^2-\omega^2}{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega)^2} \qquad (1)$$

**Phasor method result (from 20H):**

$$A = \frac{R\mathcal{E}_0}{R^2+\!\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2} \qquad B = -\frac{\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)\mathcal{E}_0}{R^2+\!\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2} \qquad (2)$$

Substituting $\gamma = \dfrac{R}{2L}$ and $\omega_0^2 = \dfrac{1}{LC}$ into (1) yields (2). $\checkmark$

---

## Page 93 (Slide 93) — Resonance in Mechanical Forced Oscillation

*(Reference: 11I)*

**Equation of motion:**

$$\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{dx}{dt} + \omega_0^2 x = \frac{F_0}{m}\cos\omega t \qquad \omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \text{ (natural frequency)}, \quad \omega \text{ (driving frequency)}$$

**Particular solution of forced oscillation:**

$$x_p(t) = A\sin\omega t + B\cos\omega t = \frac{F_0}{m}\left(\frac{2\gamma\omega}{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega)^2}\sin\omega t + \frac{\omega_0^2-\omega^2}{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega)^2}\cos\omega t\right)$$

---

## Page 94 (Slide 94) — Resonance in Mechanical Forced Oscillation (Amplitude)

*(Reference: 11I)*

$$x_p(t) = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega)^2}}\left(\frac{2\gamma\omega}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega)^2}}\sin\omega t + \frac{\omega_0^2-\omega^2}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega)^2}}\cos\omega t\right)$$

The bracketed factors equal $\sin\phi$ and $\cos\phi$ respectively (from the phasor triangle).

$$\Rightarrow \quad x_p(t) = C(\omega)(\sin\omega t\sin\phi + \cos\omega t\cos\phi) = C(\omega)\cos(\omega t - \phi)$$

**Amplitude:**

$$C(\omega) = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega)^2}}$$

---

## Page 95 (Slide 95) — Resonance in Mechanical Forced Oscillation (Condition)

*(Reference: 11I)*

**Particular solution of forced oscillation:**

$$x_p(t) = C(\omega)\cos(\omega t - \phi)$$

$$C(\omega) = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega)^2}}$$

**Resonance of forced oscillation:** The amplitude $C(\omega)$ varies with the driving frequency $\omega$. When $\omega = \omega_R$ gives the maximum amplitude, $\omega_R$ is called the **resonance frequency**.

**Resonance condition:**

$$\frac{d}{d\omega}C(\omega) = 0$$

---

## Page 96 (Slide 96) — Resonance Frequency of Mechanical Forced Oscillation

*(Reference: 11I)*

$$\frac{d}{d\omega}C(\omega) = \frac{F_0}{m}\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{-4\omega(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)+8\gamma^2\omega}{\bigl[(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega)^2\bigr]^{3/2}} = 0$$

**Resonance frequency:**

$$\omega_0^2 - \omega^2 - 2\gamma^2 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \omega_R = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2} < \omega_0$$

The resonance frequency is slightly **less than** the natural frequency.

---

## Page 97 (Slide 97) — Resonance in AC RLC Circuit

**Kirchhoff's voltage law:**

$$L\frac{d^2i}{dt^2} + R\frac{di}{dt} + \frac{i}{C} = \omega\mathcal{E}_0\cos\omega t$$

**Current in AC RLC circuit:**

$$i(t) = i_0\sin(\omega t - \phi) = \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{Z}\sin(\omega t - \phi)$$

$$= \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{\sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}}\sin(\omega t-\phi) = \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{\sqrt{R^2+\!\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2}}\sin(\omega t-\phi)$$

**Amplitude:**

$$C(\omega) = \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{\sqrt{R^2+\!\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2}}$$

---

## Page 98 (Slide 98) — Resonance Frequency of AC RLC Circuit

**Resonance condition:**

$$\frac{d}{d\omega}C(\omega) = \mathcal{E}_0\frac{d}{d\omega}\frac{1}{\sqrt{R^2+\!\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2}} = \mathcal{E}_0\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{2\!\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)\!\left(L + \frac{1}{\omega^2 C}\right)}{\left[R^2+\!\left(\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2\right]^{3/2}} = 0$$

**Resonance frequency:**

$$\omega L - \frac{1}{\omega C} = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \boxed{\omega_R = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}}$$

In the AC RLC circuit, the resonance frequency equals the **natural frequency** $\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}$ exactly (unlike the mechanical case, where it is slightly less).
