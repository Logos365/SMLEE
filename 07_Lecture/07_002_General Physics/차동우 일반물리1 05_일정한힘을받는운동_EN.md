## Page 3 — Review of Differentiation

**Review of Differentiation** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **2**

Two meanings of $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$:

**1** The ratio of two differentials $dy$ and $dx$

$\Rightarrow$ Multiplying both sides of $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = f(x)$ by $dx$, we can write:

$$dy = f(x)\,dx$$

**2** The derivative of $y(x)$: $\quad y'(x) = \dfrac{d}{dx}y(x)$

$$\Rightarrow \frac{d}{dx}x^n = nx^{n-1}$$

## Page 14 — Summary: Differentiation and Integration Rules

**Summary** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **13**

**Differentiation** | **Integration**
---|---
$\dfrac{d}{dx}x = 1$ | $\displaystyle\int_a^x dx\,(1) = x - a$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}x^2 = 2x$ | $\displaystyle\int_a^x dx\, x = \dfrac{x^2}{2} - \dfrac{a^2}{2}$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}x^3 = 3x^2$ | $\displaystyle\int_a^x dx\, x^2 = \dfrac{x^3}{3} - \dfrac{a^3}{3}$
$\dfrac{d}{dx}x^n = nx^{n-1}$ | $\displaystyle\int_a^x dx\, x^n = \dfrac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} - \dfrac{a^{n+1}}{n+1}$

General rules:

$$x^n \Rightarrow nx^{n-1} \qquad x^n \Rightarrow \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}$$

## Page 18 — Linear/Nonlinear, Homogeneous/Inhomogeneous

**Differential Equations** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **17**

**Differential equation**: an equation that contains derivatives.

**General form of a 2nd-order linear ODE:**

$$a(x)\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + b(x)\frac{dy}{dx} + c(x)y = f(x)$$

- **Independent variable** $\Rightarrow x$
- **Dependent variable** $\Rightarrow y$
- **Order**: highest derivative order $\Rightarrow$ 2nd order
- **Linear or nonlinear**: if the power of the dependent variable in each term on the left side is 1 → linear; otherwise → nonlinear
- **Homogeneous or inhomogeneous**: if the right-hand side is 0 → homogeneous; if not → inhomogeneous

## Page 19 — Solving 1st-Order ODEs by Integration

**1st-Order Differential Equations** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **18**

**General form of a 1st-order ODE:** $\quad \dfrac{dy}{dx} = f(x,y)$

**First-order ODEs can often be solved by integration.**

Procedure for solving a 1st-order ODE by integration:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x,y) \;\Rightarrow\; dy = f(x,y)\,dx \;\Rightarrow\; \boxed{\int dy = \int f(x,y)\,dx}$$

Check whether it is in integrable form!

## Page 20 — Integrable Form (Separation of Variables)

**Integrable Form** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **19**

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x,y)$$

$$\Downarrow$$

$$\int f_1(x)\,dx = \int f_2(y)\,dy$$

- Left side: a function of $x$ only (integration variable $x$)
- Right side: a function of $y$ only (integration variable $y$)

## Page 21 — Quiz: Which ODE Cannot Be Solved by Integration?

**Which of the following 1st-order ODEs cannot be solved by integration?** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **20**

1. $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 \;\Rightarrow\; \displaystyle\int dy = 3\int x^2\,dx$

2. $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2y^2 \;\Rightarrow\; \displaystyle\int \frac{dy}{y^2} = 2\int dx$

3. $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{y^2}{x} \;\Rightarrow\; \displaystyle\int \frac{dy}{y^2} = \int \frac{dx}{x}$

4. $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x - y$

(Answer: equation 4 cannot be separated into a function of $x$ only on one side and $y$ only on the other.)

## Page 22 — From Indefinite to Definite Integral

**Indefinite Integral $\Rightarrow$ Definite Integral** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **22**

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x,y) \;\Rightarrow\; \text{solution } y(x)$$

$$\downarrow$$

$$\int f_1(x)\,dx = \int f_2(y)\,dy \;\Rightarrow\; \text{the equality does not hold (indefinite)}$$

$$\downarrow$$

$$\int_{x_1}^{x_2} f_1(x)\,dx = \int_{y(x_1)}^{y(x_2)} f_2(y)\,dy$$

## Page 23 — Example: Solving a 1st-Order ODE (Uniform Motion)

**Example of Solving a 1st-Order ODE by Integration** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **23**

*(Diagram: Car moving at constant speed $v$.)*

**Problem:** A car moves at constant speed $v$. Find the distance traveled after time $t$.

$$v = \frac{dx}{dt} = \text{constant} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{integrable}$$

$$\int dx = \int v\,dt \;\Rightarrow\; \int_{x(0)}^{x(t)} dx = \int_0^t v\,dt$$

Left-side integral:
$$\int_{x(0)}^{x(t)} dx = x\Big|_{x(0)}^{x(t)} = x(t) - x(0)$$

Right-side integral:
$$\int_0^t v\,dt = vt\Big|_0^t = vt - 0 = vt$$

$$x(t) - x(0) = vt \;\Rightarrow\; \boxed{x(t) = vt + x_0}$$

---

## Page 24 — Summary: Solving 1st-Order ODEs

**Summary** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **24**

A first-order differential equation can sometimes be solved simply by integration.

However, one must first verify that the equation is in an integrable (separable) form.

## Page 26 — Types of Uniform Motion

**Uniform ___ Motion** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **26**

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Uniform speed motion** (등속 운동) | Motion maintaining constant speed. Includes: uniform linear motion, uniform circular motion. |
| **Uniform velocity motion** (등속도 운동) | Motion maintaining constant velocity. Same as uniform linear motion. |
| **Uniform acceleration motion** (등가속도 운동) | Motion maintaining constant acceleration. Motion under a constant force. |
| **Uniformly accelerated linear motion** (등가속도 직선 운동) | Motion along a straight line with constant acceleration magnitude. |

---

## Page 27 — 1D Motion Kinematics

**Uniformly Accelerated Linear Motion $\Rightarrow$ 1D Motion** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **27**

**1D motion**: motion in which the position of an object is determined by a single coordinate.

- Position: $x(t)$
- Velocity: $v(t) = \dfrac{dx(t)}{dt}$
- Acceleration: $a(t) = \dfrac{dv(t)}{dt} = \dfrac{d^2x(t)}{dt^2}$

**Uniform acceleration motion:**

$$a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = \text{constant}$$

---

## Page 28 — Velocity in Uniformly Accelerated Linear Motion

**Velocity in Uniformly Accelerated Linear Motion** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **28**

**Acceleration of uniform acceleration motion:**

$$a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \text{constant} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{integrable}$$

$$\int_{v_0}^{v(t)} dv = \int_0^t a\,dt$$

Left-side integral:
$$\int_{v_0}^{v(t)} dv = v\Big|_{v_0}^{v(t)} = v(t) - v_0$$

Right-side integral:
$$\int_0^t a\,dt = at\Big|_0^t = at - 0 = at$$

$$\therefore\; \boxed{v(t) = at + v_0}$$

---

## Page 29 — Position in Uniformly Accelerated Linear Motion

**Position in Uniformly Accelerated Linear Motion** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **29**

**Velocity of uniform acceleration motion:**

$$v(t) = \frac{dx}{dt} = at + v_0 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{integrable}$$

$$\int_{x_0}^{x(t)} dx = \int_0^t (at + v_0)\,dt$$

Left-side integral:
$$\int_{x_0}^{x(t)} dx = x\Big|_{x_0}^{x(t)} = x(t) - x_0$$

Right-side integral:
$$\int_0^t (at + v_0)\,dt = \left[\frac{1}{2}at^2 + v_0 t\right]_0^t = \frac{1}{2}at^2 + v_0 t$$

$$\therefore\; \boxed{x(t) = \frac{1}{2}at^2 + v_0 t + x_0}$$

---

## Page 30 — Time-Independent Formula

**Time-Independent Formula for Uniformly Accelerated Linear Motion** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **30**

**Uniformly accelerated linear motion equations:**

| No. | Equation |
|-----|---------|
| (1) | $a = \text{constant}$ |
| (2) | $v = at + v_0$ |
| (3) | $x = \dfrac{1}{2}at^2 + v_0 t + x_0$ |

Eliminating time from equations (2) and (3):

$$x = \frac{1}{2}a\left(\frac{v-v_0}{a}\right)^2 + v_0\left(\frac{v-v_0}{a}\right) + x_0$$

$$\therefore\; \boxed{2a(x - x_0) = v^2 - v_0^2}$$

---

## Page 31 — Summary: Uniform Velocity vs. Uniform Acceleration

**Summary** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **31**

| Uniform Velocity Motion | Uniformly Accelerated Linear Motion |
|------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| $a = 0$ &nbsp;&nbsp; (1) | $a = \text{constant}$ &nbsp;&nbsp; (1) |
| $v = \text{constant}$ &nbsp;&nbsp; (2) | $v = at + v_0$ &nbsp;&nbsp; (2) |
| $x = vt + x_0$ &nbsp;&nbsp; (3) | $x = \dfrac{1}{2}at^2 + v_0 t + x_0$ &nbsp;&nbsp; (3) |
| | $2a(x - x_0) = v^2 - v_0^2$ &nbsp;&nbsp; (4) |

Substituting $a = 0$ into the uniformly accelerated linear motion formulas gives the uniform velocity formulas.

## Page 32 — Example: Ball Thrown Upward (Part a)

**Uniformly Accelerated Linear Motion — Example** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **32**

*(Diagram: Ball thrown vertically upward with initial speed 4 m/s; trajectory shown with dots.)*

**Problem:** A ball is thrown vertically upward with initial speed $v = 4$ m/s. Find:
(a) The time to reach the highest point
(b) The height of the highest point
(c) The speed just before hitting the ground

Define upward as the $+y$ direction:
$a = -g = -9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$. Uniformly accelerated motion with $v_0 = 4\ \text{m/s}$.

**(a) Using kinematic equation (2):**

Condition at highest point: $v(t) = at + v_0 = 0$

$$\therefore\; t = -\frac{v_0}{a} = -\frac{4}{(-9.8)} \approx 0.41\ \text{s}$$

---

## Page 33 — Example: Ball Thrown Upward (Part b, upward = +y)

**Uniformly Accelerated Linear Motion — Example** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **33**

*(Diagram: Same ball trajectory.)*

**(b) Using kinematic equation (4):**

$$2a(y - y_0) = v^2 - v_0^2$$

Condition at highest point: $v = 0$; height to highest point $= y - y_0$

$$\therefore\; y - y_0 = -\frac{v_0^2}{2a} = -\frac{4^2}{2 \times (-9.8)} \approx 0.82\ \text{m}$$

---

## Page 34 — Example: Ball Thrown Upward (Part c, upward = +y)

**Uniformly Accelerated Linear Motion — Example** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **34**

*(Diagram: Same ball trajectory.)*

**(c) Using kinematic equation (4):**

$$2a(y - y_0) = v^2 - v_0^2$$

Condition just before hitting the ground: $y = y_0$

Substituting: $\;0 = v^2 - v_0^2$

$$\therefore\; v = \pm v_0 \;\Rightarrow\; v = -v_0 = -4\ \text{m/s}$$

(Negative sign means downward direction.)

## Page 38 — Key Check: Setting Up a Reference Frame

**Check!** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **38**

First, establish a reference frame (coordinate system).

$\Rightarrow$ Define the origin, the coordinate axes, and the $+$ direction of each axis.

Express all physical quantities consistently with the chosen reference frame.

$\Rightarrow$ Determine the signs of the force and the initial conditions (position and velocity).

## Page 40 — Newton's Equation of Motion

**Newton's Equation of Motion** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **40**

$$\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$$

This is a vector equation $\Rightarrow$ it cannot be solved as written.
The coordinate axes of the reference frame must be defined first.

After defining the coordinate axes:

$$F_x = ma_x$$
$$F_y = ma_y$$
$$F_z = ma_z$$
