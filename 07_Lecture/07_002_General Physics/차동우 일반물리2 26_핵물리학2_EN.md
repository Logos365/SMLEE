### Slide 57 — Alpha Ray and Alpha Particle (알파선과 알파입자)

**Alpha ray (알파선)**: A stream of alpha particles.

**Alpha particle (알파입자)**: A helium-4 nucleus — 2 protons + 2 neutrons bound together.

$${}^4_2\text{He nucleus}$$

**Effect on parent nucleus when one alpha particle is emitted:**

$${}^A_Z X \to {}^{A-4}_{Z-2} Y + {}^4_2\text{He}$$

- Mass number decreases by **4**
- Atomic number decreases by **2**

**Penetrating power**: Of the three types of nuclear radiation (alpha, beta, gamma), alpha particles have the **least** penetrating power — a sheet of paper can stop them.

*Diagram: Heavy nucleus emitting alpha particle (red-orange sphere with 2p+2n). Paper sheet stops alpha, thin metal stops beta, thick lead stops gamma.*

---

### Slide 58 — Q-Value of a Nuclear Reaction (핵반응의 Q값)

**Nuclear reaction**: Two nuclei $a$ and $b$ collide and produce nuclei $c$ and $d$:

$$a + b \to c + d$$

**Q-value**: The energy released by the reaction:

$$Q(a+b \to c+d) = (m_a + m_b)c^2 - (m_c + m_d)c^2$$

**Condition for spontaneous reaction**:

$$Q > 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \text{mass of products} < \text{mass of reactants}$$

If $Q > 0$: mass converts to kinetic energy of products — reaction is **exothermic**.
If $Q < 0$: energy must be supplied — reaction is **endothermic** (does not proceed spontaneously).

*Diagram: Circles labeled a and b on left (reactants), arrow pointing right, circles c and d on right (products).*

---

### Slide 59 — Q-Value and Binding Energy (Q값과 결합에너지)

**Relating Q to binding energies:**

Define binding energy per nuclide: $B_a = A_a m_0 c^2 - m_a c^2$ (using $m_p \approx m_n = m_0$).

The mass of nuclide $a$: $m_a c^2 = A_a m_0 c^2 - B_a$

**Q-value becomes:**

$$Q = (m_a + m_b)c^2 - (m_c + m_d)c^2$$

$$= \left[(A_a m_0 c^2 - B_a) + (A_b m_0 c^2 - B_b)\right] - \left[(A_c m_0 c^2 - B_c) + (A_d m_0 c^2 - B_d)\right]$$

Since $A_a + A_b = A_c + A_d$ (conservation of mass number):

$$\boxed{Q = [B_c + B_d] - [B_a + B_b]}$$

**Q-value = total binding energy of products minus total binding energy of reactants.**

> The reaction releases energy when the products are **more tightly bound** than the reactants.

### Slide 60 — Ground State and Excited State of an Atom (원자의 바닥상태와 들뜬 상태)

**Ground state (바닥상태)** of an atom:
- Electrons fill the lowest available energy shells in order
- The lowest total energy configuration

**Excited state (들뜬 상태)** of an atom:
- One or more electrons have moved to higher-energy shells compared to the ground state
- Higher total energy

**Behavior:**
- Ground state atom: does not spontaneously emit radiation; stays as is unless disturbed
- Excited state atom: spontaneously emits electromagnetic radiation (photons) to return to the ground state

*Diagram: Copper atom (Cu) with electron shells labeled. Left: ground state — all shells filled in lowest configuration. Right: excited state — one electron in a higher shell (green highlight).*

### Slide 61 — Ground State and Excited State of a Nucleus (원자핵의 바닥상태와 들뜬 상태)

**Ground state (바닥상태)** of a nucleus:
- Protons and neutrons fill the lowest available energy shells (nuclear shell model)
- Lowest total energy configuration

**Excited state (들뜬 상태)** of a nucleus:
- Some protons or neutrons occupy higher energy shells than in the ground state

**Behavior:**
- Stable ground-state nucleus: stays as is; does not undergo alpha or beta decay
- Unstable excited-state nucleus: undergoes **alpha or beta decay** to become a different nuclide

The parallel between atomic and nuclear shell structure explains why alpha/beta decay occur: they are the nuclear analogue of atomic emission, but the energy changes involved (MeV scale) are far too large to emit photons — instead, the nucleus transmutes.

*Diagram: Proton energy levels (red) and neutron energy levels (blue) shown as horizontal bars in a well. Ground state: levels filled from bottom. Excited state: a particle (green highlight) at higher level.*

### Slide 64 — Why Alpha Particles Form Inside Nuclei (알파입자)

**Evidence that alpha particles pre-form inside nuclei:**

Inside a nucleus, nucleons cluster into alpha particles (${}^4_2$He = 2p + 2n) before being emitted.

**Two reasons 2p + 2n can exist together:**

1. **Force maximized when nucleons overlap**: nuclear force between two nucleons is strongest at close range → alpha cluster is energetically favorable

2. **Pauli exclusion principle**: two identical fermions cannot occupy the same quantum state.

   - A proton and a neutron are **different** particles → they **can** overlap (occupy the same spatial state with opposite spins)
   - Two protons with opposite spin ($m_s = +1/2$ and $m_s = -1/2$) **can** occupy the same spatial state
   - Two neutrons similarly

Thus in an alpha cluster: 2 protons (↑↓) + 2 neutrons (↑↓) — all four have different quantum numbers — can all occupy the same small spatial region.

*Diagram: Two spheres labeled with $m_s = +1/2$ and $m_s = -1/2$ showing allowed pairing. Proton (red) + neutron (blue) pairing shown.*

### Slide 65 — Why Alpha Decay is Possible: Quantum Tunneling (알파 붕괴가 가능한 이유)

**Alpha decay involves tunneling through the Coulomb barrier.**

Outside the nucleus: the alpha particle sees a Coulomb repulsion potential from the daughter nucleus (positive charge $Z-2$).
Inside the nucleus: the alpha is bound by the nuclear force (attractive well).

The alpha particle does **not** have enough classical energy to overcome the Coulomb barrier — but quantum mechanics allows it to **tunnel through** with nonzero probability.

**History:**
- **1926**: Gamow-Teller equation published (wave function for tunneling)
- **1928**: Russian physicist **George Gamow** applied the Schrödinger equation to explain alpha decay via quantum tunneling

*Graph: Potential energy $V(x)$ vs. position $x$ showing deep attractive well inside nucleus (left), Coulomb barrier $V_0$ at nuclear surface, and classically forbidden region from $0$ to $a$. Inside region: $\psi^+_1(x) = Ae^{ikx}$. Barrier: $\psi_2(x) = Ce^{\kappa x} + De^{-\kappa x}$. Outside: $\psi^+_3(x) = Fe^{ikx}$ (transmitted wave).*

*Inset spectrum: Alpha decay of Polonium-212 (${}^{212}$Po) energy spectrum showing discrete peaks — confirms that alpha particles emerge with well-defined kinetic energies (as expected from fixed Q-value).*

### Slide 68 — Does the Nucleus Contain Electrons? (원자핵에 전자가 존재할까?)

**Beta ray (베타선)**: A stream of electrons emitted from radioactive nuclei.

**Question**: Since electrons come out of the nucleus, does the nucleus contain electrons?

**Answer: NO.**

**Reasons that electrons cannot exist inside the nucleus:**
1. Heisenberg's uncertainty principle (1925 Nobel, Heisenberg)
2. Pauli exclusion principle / Fermi-Dirac statistics (1945 Nobel, Fermi)

*Portraits: Werner Heisenberg (Nobel 1925), Enrico Fermi (Nobel 1938).*

---

### Slide 69 — Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle Rules Out Nuclear Electrons (하이젠베르크의 불확정성 원리)

**Argument**: If an electron were confined inside a nucleus, how much momentum would it need?

Nuclear size: $\Delta x \approx 10$ fm $= 10\times10^{-15}$ m

By the uncertainty principle: $\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \sim \hbar$

$$\Delta p \approx \frac{\hbar}{\Delta x} = \frac{\hbar c}{\Delta x \cdot c} = \frac{197.3\text{ MeV}\cdot\text{fm}}{10\text{ fm}\cdot c} \approx 20\text{ MeV}/c$$

**Energy of an electron with $p = 20$ MeV/$c$:**

$$E = \sqrt{(\Delta p)^2 c^2 + m_e^2 c^4} = \sqrt{(20\text{ MeV})^2 + (0.511\text{ MeV})^2} \approx 20\text{ MeV}$$

**But**: The maximum kinetic energy of observed beta particles is only **~1 MeV**.

If electrons existed inside the nucleus, they would fly out with ~20 MeV — not 1 MeV.

**Conclusion**: Electrons cannot be confined inside atomic nuclei.

---

### Slide 70 — Pauli Exclusion Principle Also Rules Out Nuclear Electrons (파울리의 베타원리)

**Classification of particles by statistics:**

- **Fermions**: obey Pauli exclusion principle (cannot share quantum states)
- **Bosons**: do NOT obey Pauli exclusion (can share quantum states)

**Rule**: A composite particle made of an **even** number of fermions behaves as a **boson**; an **odd** number → **fermion**.

**Example: Nitrogen-14** (${}^{14}_7$N nucleus)

If the nucleus contained protons AND electrons (old hypothesis):
- Option ①: 14 protons + 7 electrons = 21 fermions → fermion ✓ (spin = 1)
  - But then neutral ${}^{14}$N atom has 14p + 7n + 7e = 28 particles (even) → **boson** ✗

Actually the slide shows:
- ①: 14 protons + 7 electrons (in nucleus) + 7 electrons (outside) → nucleus is fermion
- ②: 14 protons + 7 neutrons (modern view) = 21 nucleons (odd) → fermion ✓ AND spin=1 ✓

The statistical argument with ${}^{14}$N shows the nucleus-contains-electrons model is internally inconsistent with observed spin and statistics.

### Slide 71 — The Beta Energy Spectrum Puzzle (베타선 에너지의 퍼즐)

**Observation**: The energy spectrum of beta electrons from ${}^{210}_{83}$Bi is **continuous** — electrons emerge with a range of energies from 0 up to a maximum $E_\text{max}$.

**Contrast with alpha decay**: Alpha particles emerge with **discrete** energies (sharp peaks at specific energies).

**Why is this a puzzle?**

For a two-body decay $A \to B + e^-$:
- Conservation of energy and momentum → both $B$ and $e^-$ should have **fixed** kinetic energies
- This would give a discrete (single) energy for the electron

But the beta spectrum is continuous — implying a **third, undetected particle** also carries away energy.

**Historical note**: James Chadwick (discoverer of the neutron) found in 1930 that beta energy was continuous — some physicists proposed energy conservation might be violated.

*Graph: Beta electron energy spectrum showing smooth continuous curve peaking well below $E_\text{max}$, with $E_\text{max}$ marked at right edge.*

---

### Slide 72 — Resolution: Pauli and Fermi (베타선 에너지의 퍼즐 해결: 파울리와 페르미)

**Wolfgang Pauli** (Austria, Nobel 1945):
- 1930: Proposed that a second undetected neutral particle is emitted alongside the electron in beta decay
- He called it a 'neutron' (before the real neutron was discovered by Chadwick in 1932)
- This new particle carries away the missing energy → explains the continuous spectrum

**Enrico Fermi** (Italy, Nobel 1938):
- Accepted Pauli's proposal and developed the full **theory of beta decay** (Fermi's beta theory)
- Renamed Pauli's neutral particle the **neutrino** (Italian: "little neutral one") in 1933
- Beta decay becomes: $n \to p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$

The neutrino was experimentally confirmed by Cowan and Reines in 1956.

*Portraits: Wolfgang Pauli (Nobel 1945), Enrico Fermi (Nobel 1938).*

### Slide 73 — Beta Decay and the Weak Force (베타 붕괴와 약력)

**Two fundamental forces relevant here:**

| Property | Strong Force (강력) | Weak Force (약력) |
|----------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Acts on | Quarks (via color charge) | Quarks and leptons (via flavor) |
| Effect | Quarks combine to form baryons and mesons | Changes quark flavor → changes baryon/meson type |
| Range | Short | Extremely short |
| Mediator | Gluons (글루온) | Z and W bosons |

### Slide 74 — Quark-Level Beta Decay (쿼크의 베타 붕괴와 핵자의 베타 붕괴)

**Nucleon quark content:**

| Nucleon | Quarks |
|---------|--------|
| Neutron (중성자) | $u + d + d$ |
| Proton (양성자) | $u + u + d$ |

**The weak force changes quark flavor:**

**$\beta^-$ decay** (neutron → proton):
$$n \to p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$$
$$d \to u + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e \quad \text{(quark level)}$$

**$\beta^+$ decay** (proton → neutron):
$$p \to n + e^+ + \nu_e$$
$$u \to d + e^+ + \nu_e \quad \text{(quark level)}$$

### Slide 75 — Nuclear Ground State and Fermi Energy (원자핵의 바닥상태와 들뜬 상태)

**Fermi energy $\varepsilon_F$**: The energy of the highest occupied energy level in the nuclear ground state.

In a nucleus at ground state:
- All proton energy levels up to $\varepsilon_F$ are filled
- All neutron energy levels up to $\varepsilon_F$ are filled

*Diagram: Two potential wells side by side — protons (left, red dots) and neutrons (right, blue dots). Both filled from bottom up to the Fermi energy $\varepsilon_F$ (dashed horizontal line). The Fermi energy acts as the "surface" of the nuclear Fermi sea.*

### Slide 76 — Beta Decay Conditions (원자핵의 베타붕괴)

**$\beta^-$ decay occurs when:**

Neutron energy levels are **lower** than proton levels at the same shell → there are **vacant proton states below** the neutron Fermi energy.

A neutron can lower its energy by converting to a proton + $e^-$ + $\bar{\nu}_e$.

**$\beta^+$ decay occurs when:**

Proton energy levels are **lower** than neutron levels → vacant neutron states exist below the proton Fermi energy.

A proton converts to a neutron + $e^+$ + $\nu_e$.

*Diagram: Two-panel illustration. Left ($\beta^-$): proton well lower than neutron well; neutron (blue) converts to proton (red) by falling to a lower proton state. Right ($\beta^+$): proton well higher than neutron well; proton converts to neutron.*

### Slide 78 — Natural Radioactive Decay: All Three Types (방사성 원소의 자연 붕괴)

**Summary of all three natural decay modes:**

**Alpha decay (알파붕괴)**:
- Cause: nucleus has too many protons and neutrons in the ground state → reduce size by ejecting alpha particle
- Mechanism: quantum tunneling through Coulomb barrier

**Beta decay (베타붕괴)**:
- Cause: proton/neutron imbalance — too many of one type
- Mechanism: weak force converts proton↔neutron; ejects electron or positron + neutrino

**Gamma decay (감마붕괴)**:
- Cause: nucleus in an **excited state** (after alpha or beta decay leaves daughter in excited state)
- Mechanism: nucleus releases energy as a **gamma-ray photon** to return to the ground state
- **Same physical principle as atomic emission** — but at MeV energy scales (not eV)

*Diagram: Three decay pathways shown with nucleus illustrations. Alpha: large nucleus ejects alpha cluster. Beta: neutron converts to proton with ejected electron. Gamma: excited nucleus emits gamma photon.*

### Slide 81 — When Gamma Rays Are Produced (감마선의 발생)

**Atoms:**

| State | Radiation emitted |
|-------|------------------|
| Excited atom | Emits photons (IR to X-ray) to reach ground state |
| Ground state atom | No radiation emitted |

**Nuclei:**

| State | Radiation emitted |
|-------|------------------|
| Excited nucleus | Can emit alpha, beta, **and gamma** to reach lower states |
| Ground state nucleus | Can still emit alpha or beta (if energetically favorable), but **no gamma** |

**Key insight**: Gamma emission is the **only** decay mode that does not change $Z$ or $N$ — it only lowers the nuclear energy state without transmutation.

### Slide 83 — Pair Annihilation and Pair Production (쌍소멸과 감마 생성)

**Pair annihilation (쌍소멸)**:

$$e^+ + e^- \to \gamma + \gamma$$

An electron and positron meet, annihilate, and produce **two** gamma-ray photons (each $E = m_e c^2 = 0.511$ MeV, emitted in opposite directions to conserve momentum).

**Pair production (쌍 생성)**:

$$\gamma + \gamma \to e^+ + e^-$$

Two sufficiently energetic gamma photons can collide and produce an electron-positron pair.

Minimum energy: $E_\gamma \geq m_e c^2 = 0.511$ MeV per photon.

*Diagram: Left — two opposing arrows ($e^+$ and $e^-$) converging to a point, two wavy arrows (gammas) diverging. Right — bubble chamber photograph showing spiral tracks (electron and positron curving in opposite directions in magnetic field).*

### Slide 90 — Mass Deficit in Fission (핵분열의 질량 결손)

**Why does fission release energy?**

For nuclei with $A > 60$: the $B/A$ curve slopes downward with increasing $A$. Splitting a heavy nucleus produces fragments with **higher $B/A$** → more tightly bound → lower mass → energy released.

**Example**: ${}^{235}_{92}$U → ${}^{130}_{50}$Sn + ${}^{105}_{42}$Mo (approximate, using $m_p \approx m_n = m$)

$$Mc^2 = m({}^{235}_{92}\text{U})c^2 = 235mc^2 - B({}^{235}_{92}\text{U})$$

$$m_1c^2 = m({}^{130}_{50}\text{Sn})c^2 = 130mc^2 - B({}^{130}_{50}\text{Sn})$$

$$m_2c^2 = m({}^{105}_{42}\text{Mo})c^2 = 105mc^2 - B({}^{105}_{42}\text{Mo})$$

**Mass deficit** (energy released):

$$(M - m_1 - m_2)c^2 = B({}^{130}_{50}\text{Sn}) + B({}^{105}_{42}\text{Mo}) - B({}^{235}_{92}\text{U}) > 0$$

This is positive because the fragments have higher binding energy per nucleon than uranium.

---

### Slide 91 — Spontaneous vs Induced Fission (자발적 핵분열과 유도 핵분열)

**Fission (핵분열)**: A large nucleus splits into two nuclei of comparable size.

**Spontaneous fission (자발적 핵분열)**: An unstable heavy nucleus splits on its own (without external trigger).
- Occurs in the heaviest nuclides (Cf-252, Fm-256, etc.)
- Analogous to alpha decay — happens spontaneously via quantum tunneling

**Induced fission (유도 핵분열)**: A neutron is absorbed by a heavy nucleus, which then splits.
- The absorbed neutron tips the nucleus into an unstable excited state
- Much more likely than spontaneous fission for ${}^{235}$U, ${}^{238}$U, ${}^{239}$Pu

> **Nuclear reactors and atomic bombs use only induced fission.**

*Diagram: Left — spontaneous fission: unstable nucleus alone splits. Right — induced fission: neutron approaches ${}^{235}$U, absorbed, then ${}^{236}$U* splits into ${}^{139}_{56}$Ba + ${}^{94}_{36}$Kr + 3 neutrons.*

---

### Slide 92 — Induced Fission Step by Step (유도 핵분열이 일어나는 과정)

**Four steps of induced fission in ${}^{235}$U:**

① A **slow neutron** is absorbed by ${}^{235}_{92}$U:
$${}^{235}_{92}\text{U} + n \to {}^{236}_{92}\text{U}^* \text{ (excited)}$$

② The resulting ${}^{236}_{92}$U$^*$ is in a highly excited, **unstable** state.

③ The excited nucleus **vibrates and elongates** like a liquid drop — Coulomb repulsion of the two "halves" drives them apart.

④ The nucleus **splits** into two fragments and ejects **2–3 neutrons + gamma rays**:
$${}^{236}_{92}\text{U}^* \to {}^{139}_{56}\text{Ba} + {}^{94}_{36}\text{Kr} + 3n + \gamma$$

(The actual fragment pair varies each fission event.)

*Diagram: Four-panel sequence: ① neutron (yellow) approaching U-235 (large green); ② U-236* formed; ③ elongated peanut shape; ④ two fragments (Ba, Kr) flying apart with three neutrons and gammas.*

### Slide 93 — Chain Reaction (연쇄 반응)

**Leo Szilard** (Hungary, 1898–1964) — Hungarian-born American physicist.

- 1913: First became aware of chain reactions in chemistry
- 1932: Successfully realized ${}^7$Li + p → 2${}^4$He (first verified nuclear reaction releasing more energy than input)
- **1933**: Realizing that if a nuclear reaction could release two or more neutrons per reaction, a **self-sustaining chain reaction** would be possible
- Filed a patent for the neutron chain reaction as an energy source in 1933 (before fission was discovered!)
- After hearing of Hahn's 1938 fission results, immediately recognized the chain reaction was feasible
- Convinced Einstein to sign the famous Einstein-Szilard letter to President Roosevelt (1939), leading to the Manhattan Project

*Portrait: Leo Szilard.*

### Slide 94 — Fission Chain Reaction (핵분열의 연쇄 반응)

**Chain reaction principle:**

1. Each induced fission of ${}^{235}$U releases **2–3 neutrons**
2. Those neutrons can induce further fissions
3. Each generation produces more neutrons → exponential growth

*Diagram: Tree-structure showing first generation (1 fission → 3 neutrons), second generation (3 fissions → 9 neutrons), third generation (9 fissions → 27 neutrons) — chain branching.*

**Two outcomes:**

| Controlled chain reaction (제어) | Uncontrolled chain reaction (비제어) |
|----------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Nuclear power plant (원자력 발전) | Atomic bomb (원자폭탄) |

Szilard and Fermi confirmed in 1935 that ${}^{235}$U undergoes a real chain reaction. The key was controlling neutron flux.

---

### Slide 95 — Atomic Bomb Construction (원자폭탄의 제조)

**Critical mass (임계질량)**: The minimum mass of fissile material needed for a self-sustaining chain reaction.

**Steps in atomic bomb construction:**

① Prepare fissile material (${}^{235}$U or ${}^{239}$Pu) in pieces each **below critical mass** (so no runaway chain reaction in storage)

② In a very short time, **rapidly assemble** the pieces into a supercritical mass (above critical mass)

③ **Initiate** the chain reaction (with a neutron initiator)

④ **Hold the mass together** long enough for sufficient fissions (with surrounding conventional explosives — implosion design)

*Diagram: Cross-section of a gun-type bomb (Little Boy style): TNT conventional explosive + U-235 sub-critical pieces → rapid assembly → nuclear explosion. Three stages shown: before explosion, detonator firing, nuclear explosion.*

---

### Slide 96 — Nuclear Power Plant Principles (원자로의 원리)

**How a nuclear power plant works:**

① In the **reactor core (노심)**, fission chain reaction releases heat
② Heat boils water in the **1st cooling loop (1차 회로)** → generates steam
③ Steam drives a **steam turbine** connected to a **generator** → electricity produced
④ Steam from the turbine is condensed in the **2nd cooling loop (2차 회로)** using a separate water supply
⑤ Condensed water returns to the steam generator; 2nd loop water goes to a cooling tower

**Key safety feature**: The 1st and 2nd cooling loops are **separate** — radioactive water in the 1st loop never contacts the outside environment.

*Diagram: Full nuclear power plant schematic: reactor vessel → steam generator → turbine → generator → condenser. 1st loop (red, radioactive) and 2nd loop (blue, clean) clearly separated.*

**26J: Nuclear Fusion**

*The energy source of stars — and humanity's future energy goal.*

### Slide 99 — Mass Deficit in Fusion (핵융합의 질량 결손)

**Why does fusion release energy?**

For nuclei with $A < 60$: the $B/A$ curve rises steeply with $A$. Combining light nuclei produces heavier products with **higher $B/A$** → energy released.

**Example**: ${}^2_1$H + ${}^2_1$H → ${}^4_2$He

Using $m_p \approx m_n = m$:

$$Mc^2 = m({}^4_2\text{He})c^2 = 4mc^2 - b({}^4_2\text{He})$$

$$m_1c^2 = m_2c^2 = m({}^2_1\text{H})c^2 = 2mc^2 - b({}^2_1\text{H})$$

**Energy released**:

$$(m_1 + m_2 - M)c^2 = b({}^4_2\text{He}) - b({}^2_1\text{H}) - b({}^2_1\text{H})$$

$$= 4B/A({}^4\text{He}) - 4B/A({}^2\text{H}) > 0$$

Since $B/A({}^4$He$) \approx 7.07$ MeV $> B/A({}^2$H$) \approx 1.11$ MeV per nucleon, the fusion is strongly exothermic.

*Graph: $B/A$ curve redrawn; fusion arrow pointing up-right (light nuclei moving toward iron); fission arrow pointing up-left (heavy nuclei moving toward iron) — both directions release energy.*

### Slide 101 — Solar Fusion Reactions (태양의 핵융합 반응)

**The Sun generates energy via two fusion pathways:**

**① PP Chain (양성자-양성자 연쇄반응)** — 98.5% of solar energy:
- Converts hydrogen to helium starting from protons
- Three sub-chains: PP I (85%), PP II (15%), PP III (<0.01%)

**② CNO Cycle** — 1.5% of solar energy:
- Uses carbon (${}^{12}$C) as a **catalyst**
- ${}^{12}$C cyclically transforms through N and O isotopes, but is ultimately regenerated
- Net result: same as PP chain — 4 protons → ${}^4$He

Both pathways produce the same net reaction:

$$4\,{}^1_1\text{H} \to {}^4_2\text{He} + 2e^+ + 2\nu_e + 26.7\text{ MeV}$$

---

### Slide 102 — Three Types of PP Chain (세 종류의 PP Chain)

**PP I** (~85% of PP reactions):

$${}^1\text{H} + {}^1\text{H} \to {}^2\text{H} + e^+ + \nu_e$$
$${}^2\text{H} + {}^1\text{H} \to {}^3\text{He} + \gamma$$
$${}^3\text{He} + {}^3\text{He} \to {}^4\text{He} + 2\,{}^1\text{H} + \gamma$$

Net: $4\,{}^1\text{H} \to {}^4\text{He} + 2e^+ + 2\nu_e + 26.7$ MeV

**PP II** (~15%):

$${}^1\text{H}+{}^1\text{H}\to{}^2\text{H}+e^++\nu_e, \quad {}^2\text{H}+{}^1\text{H}\to{}^3\text{He}+\gamma, \quad {}^3\text{He}+{}^4\text{He}\to{}^7\text{Be}+\gamma$$
$${}^7\text{Be}+e^-\to{}^7\text{Li}+\nu_e, \quad {}^7\text{Li}+{}^1\text{H}\to 2\,{}^4\text{He}$$

**PP III** (~0.01%):

$${}^7\text{Be}+{}^1\text{H}\to{}^8\text{B}+\gamma, \quad {}^8\text{B}\to{}^8\text{Be}^*+e^++\nu_e, \quad {}^8\text{Be}^*\to 2\,{}^4\text{He}$$

---

### Slide 103 — CNO Cycle (CNO 사이클)

**The CNO Cycle** — Carbon-Nitrogen-Oxygen catalytic cycle:

$${}^{12}\text{C} + {}^1\text{H} \to {}^{13}\text{N} + \gamma$$
$${}^{13}\text{N} \to {}^{13}\text{C} + e^+ + \nu_e$$
$${}^{13}\text{C} + {}^1\text{H} \to {}^{14}\text{N} + \gamma$$
$${}^{14}\text{N} + {}^1\text{H} \to {}^{15}\text{O} + \gamma$$
$${}^{15}\text{O} \to {}^{15}\text{N} + e^+ + \nu_e$$
$${}^{15}\text{N} + {}^1\text{H} \to {}^{12}\text{C} + {}^4\text{He}$$

Net: $4\,{}^1\text{H} \to {}^4_2\text{He} + 2e^+ + 2\nu_e + 26.7$ MeV

${}^{12}$C acts as a **catalyst** — it is consumed at the first step and regenerated at the last step.

The CNO cycle dominates in stars more massive than the Sun (higher core temperatures).

*Diagram: Circular CNO cycle diagram showing the six reactions, with protons entering and He/neutrinos/gamma exiting the cycle.*

### Slide 105 — Fusion Reactions in the Hydrogen Bomb (수소 폭탄에 이용되는 핵융합 반응)

**Hydrogen bomb fuel**: Deuterium (${}^2$H = D) and Tritium (${}^3$H = T).

**Main DT reaction**:

$$\boxed{{}^2_1\text{H} + {}^3_1\text{H} \to {}^4_2\text{He} + n + 17.6\text{ MeV}}$$

**Comparison of fusion reactions:**

| Reaction | Energy released |
|----------|----------------|
| ${}^2\text{H} + {}^2\text{H} \to {}^2\text{H} + {}^1\text{H}$ (DD-p) | 0.4 MeV |
| ${}^2\text{H} + {}^2\text{H} \to {}^3\text{He} + n$ (DD-n) | 3.2 MeV |
| ${}^2\text{H} + {}^3\text{H} \to {}^4\text{He} + n$ (DT) | **17.6 MeV** |

The DT reaction releases by far the most energy per reaction.

*Diagram: D (blue) + T (red) → He-4 (large sphere) + neutron; energy 17.6 MeV labeled.*

### Slide 106 — DT Fusion Cross Section (DT 핵융합 반응의 단면적)

**Reaction cross section**: The probability of a nuclear reaction occurring — larger cross section = more likely.

*Graph: Cross section (mb, millibarns) on y-axis vs. proton or deuteron energy (keV to MeV) on x-axis. Curves for: DT (highest, by far), D-He3, DD, p-B11. The DT curve has a prominent resonance peak around 100 keV.*

**Key result**: The DT fusion cross section is **more than 100 times larger** than other fusion reactions at energies achievable in a practical device.

This is why DT is the preferred fuel for both hydrogen bombs and fusion reactor experiments (ITER, NIF).

---

### Slide 107 — Fission vs. Fusion Power: Fission (핵분열 발전 vs. 핵융합 발전 — 핵분열)

**Fission power plant** uses **induced chain reaction**:

- ${}^{235}$U or ${}^{239}$Pu absorbs a neutron → induced fission
- Fission releases 2–3 neutrons per event
- Released neutrons induce further fissions → **self-sustaining chain reaction**
- **Control rods** absorb excess neutrons to regulate reaction rate

**Risk**: If neutron control fails → runaway chain reaction → meltdown (Chernobyl, Fukushima).

The chain reaction is "positive feedback" — if something goes wrong, the reaction tends to accelerate rather than stop.

---

### Slide 108 — Fission vs. Fusion Power: Fusion (핵분열 발전 vs. 핵융합 발전 — 핵융합)

**Fusion power** uses **self-sustaining fusion** (자발적 핵융합 반응):

- Two nuclei must **approach closely enough** for the nuclear force to overcome Coulomb repulsion → requires very high temperature ($\sim10^8$ K)
- Heat D/T externally (laser, magnetic confinement) → once fusion begins, released energy heats more fuel → self-sustaining

**Key safety advantage over fission**:

> If anything goes wrong in a fusion reactor, the plasma cools and the reaction **simply stops** — there is no chain reaction to run away.

An accidental failure in a fusion reactor causes the reactor to shut down on its own — not an explosion or meltdown.

This is fundamentally different from fission reactors, where loss of control leads to runaway heating.

*Diagram: Comparison of fission (chain reaction tree, can branch uncontrollably) vs. fusion (self-regulating: if $T$ drops, reaction stops).*
