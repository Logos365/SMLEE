## Page 21 — Differential of the Dependent Variable (slide 21)

**Differential of the Dependent Variable — slide 21**

For $y = f(x)$:

- Differential $dx$ of the independent variable $\Rightarrow$ chosen freely
- Differential $dy$ of the dependent variable $\Rightarrow$ depends on $dx$

$$dx = x' - x \quad \therefore \quad x' = x + dx$$

$$dy = y' - y = f(x') - f(x) = f(x+dx) - f(x)$$

If $y = x$, then:

$$dy = (x+dx) - x = dx$$

---

## Page 22 — Differential of Dependent Variable: Power Functions (slide 22)

**Differential of the Dependent Variable — slide 22**

If $y = x^2$:
$$dy = (x+dx)^2 - x^2 = (x^2 + 2x\,dx + (dx)^2) - x^2 = 2x\,dx$$

If $y = x^3$:
$$dy = (x+dx)^3 - x^3 = (x^3 + 3x^2 dx + 3x(dx)^2 + (dx)^3) - x^3 = 3x^2 dx$$

If $y = x^n$:
$$dy = (x+dx)^n - x^n = (x^n + nx^{n-1}(dx) + \cdots) - x^n = nx^{n-1}dx$$

---

## Page 23 — Differentials, Derivatives, and Notation (slide 23)

**Differentials and Derivatives — slide 23**

For $y = f(x)$:

$$f'(x) \equiv \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}y = \frac{d}{dx}f(x)$$

- $f'(x)$ denotes the derivative of $f(x)$ at $x$, and equals the ratio $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ of the two differentials.
- $\dfrac{d}{dx}$ is the differentiation operator: $f'(x) = \dfrac{d}{dx}f(x)$.

$\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ can mean either the ratio of the differential $dy$ to the differential $dx$, or the derivative $\dfrac{d}{dx}y$ (applying the operator $\dfrac{d}{dx}$ to $y$).

---

## Page 24 — Differential of Dependent Variable via Derivative (slide 24)

**Differential of the Dependent Variable — slide 24**

For $y = f(x)$:

Since $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = f'(x)$,

$$dy = f'(x)\,dx \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad dy = \frac{dy}{dx}\,dx$$

If $y = x^n$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = nx^{n-1}$$

## Page 25 — First and Second Derivatives (slide 25)

**First and Second Derivatives — slide 25**

**First derivative:**

$$\frac{dy}{dx} \Rightarrow \begin{cases} dy \div dx & \checkmark \\ \dfrac{d}{dx}\,y & \checkmark \end{cases}$$

**Second derivative:**

$$\frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} \Rightarrow \begin{cases} d^2 y \div dx^2 & \times \\ \dfrac{d}{dx}\dfrac{d}{dx}y = \left(\dfrac{d}{dx}\right)^2 y & \checkmark \end{cases}$$

**Review: Differences and Differentials — slide 28**

$$dy = f'(x)\,dx = \frac{dy}{dx}\,dx$$

## Page 29 — Differences and Differentials of Vectors (slide 29)

**Differences and Differentials of Vectors — slide 29**

**Difference $\Delta\vec{a}$** — Difference: $\Delta\vec{a} = \vec{a}' - \vec{a}$

**Differential $d\vec{a}$** — Differential: $d\vec{a} = \vec{a}' - \vec{a}$, but with $d\vec{a} \cdot d\vec{a} \to 0$ or $d\vec{a} \times d\vec{a} \to 0$

**Derivative** — for $\vec{a} = \vec{F}(x)$:

$$\vec{F}'(x) = \frac{d\vec{a}}{dx} = \frac{\vec{F}(x+dx) - \vec{F}(x)}{dx}$$

$$d\vec{a} = \vec{F}'(x)\,dx = \frac{d\vec{a}}{dx}\,dx$$

---

## Page 30 — Differential of the Sum of Two Scalars (slide 30)

**Differential of the Sum of Two Scalars — slide 30**

Differential of $y$: $\quad dy = y' - y$

Differential of $y_1$: $\quad dy_1 = y_1' - y_1$

Differential of $y_2$: $\quad dy_2 = y_2' - y_2$

Differential of $y = y_1 + y_2$:

$$dy = y' - y = (y_1' + y_2') - (y_1 + y_2) = (y_1' - y_1) + (y_2' - y_2) = dy_1 + dy_2$$

$$\boxed{y = y_1 + y_2 \implies dy = dy_1 + dy_2}$$

---

## Page 31 — Differential of the Product of Two Scalars (slide 31)

**Differential of the Product of Two Scalars — slide 31**

Differential of $y_1$: $dy_1 = y_1' - y_1 \Rightarrow y_1' = y_1 + dy_1$

Differential of $y_2$: $dy_2 = y_2' - y_2 \Rightarrow y_2' = y_2 + dy_2$

Differential of $y = y_1 y_2$:

$$dy = y' - y = y_1'y_2' - y_1 y_2 = (y_1 + dy_1)(y_2 + dy_2) - y_1 y_2 = (dy_1)y_2 + y_1(dy_2)$$

$$\boxed{y = y_1 y_2 \implies dy = dy_1\, y_2 + y_1\, dy_2}$$

---

## Page 32 — Differential of the Sum of Two Vectors (slide 32)

**Differential of the Sum of Two Vectors — slide 32**

Differential of $\vec{a}$: $\quad d\vec{a} = \vec{a}' - \vec{a}$

Differential of $\vec{a}_1$: $\quad d\vec{a}_1 = \vec{a}_1' - \vec{a}_1$

Differential of $\vec{a}_2$: $\quad d\vec{a}_2 = \vec{a}_2' - \vec{a}_2$

Differential of $\vec{a} = \vec{a}_1 + \vec{a}_2$:

$$d\vec{a} = \vec{a}' - \vec{a} = (\vec{a}_1' + \vec{a}_2') - (\vec{a}_1 + \vec{a}_2) = (\vec{a}_1' - \vec{a}_1) + (\vec{a}_2' - \vec{a}_2) = d\vec{a}_1 + d\vec{a}_2$$

$$\boxed{\vec{a} = \vec{a}_1 + \vec{a}_2 \implies d\vec{a} = d\vec{a}_1 + d\vec{a}_2}$$

---

## Page 33 — Differential of Scalar Times Vector (slide 33)

**Differential of Scalar Times Vector — slide 33**

Differential of $y$: $dy = y' - y \Rightarrow y' = y + dy$

Differential of $\vec{a}$: $d\vec{a} = \vec{a}' - \vec{a} \Rightarrow \vec{a}' = \vec{a} + d\vec{a}$

Differential of $\vec{A} = y\vec{a}$:

$$d\vec{A} = \vec{A}' - \vec{A} = y'\vec{a}' - y\vec{a} = (y+dy)(\vec{a}+d\vec{a}) - y\vec{a} = dy\,\vec{a} + y\,d\vec{a}$$

$$\boxed{\vec{A} = y\vec{a} \implies d\vec{A} = dy\,\vec{a} + y\,d\vec{a}}$$

---

## Page 34 — Differential of Scalar (Dot) Product of Two Vectors (slide 34)

**Differential of the Scalar (Dot) Product of Two Vectors — slide 34**

Differential of $\vec{a}$: $d\vec{a} = \vec{a}' - \vec{a} \Rightarrow \vec{a}' = \vec{a} + d\vec{a}$

Differential of $\vec{b}$: $d\vec{b} = \vec{b}' - \vec{b} \Rightarrow \vec{b}' = \vec{b} + d\vec{b}$

Differential of $y = \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}$:

$$dy = y' - y = \vec{a}' \cdot \vec{b}' - \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = (\vec{a}+d\vec{a}) \cdot (\vec{b}+d\vec{b}) - \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = d\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + \vec{a} \cdot d\vec{b}$$

$$\boxed{y = \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} \implies dy = d\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + \vec{a} \cdot d\vec{b}}$$

---

## Page 35 — Differential of Vector (Cross) Product of Two Vectors (slide 35)

**Differential of the Vector (Cross) Product of Two Vectors — slide 35**

Differential of $\vec{a}$: $d\vec{a} = \vec{a}' - \vec{a} \Rightarrow \vec{a}' = \vec{a} + d\vec{a}$

Differential of $\vec{b}$: $d\vec{b} = \vec{b}' - \vec{b} \Rightarrow \vec{b}' = \vec{b} + d\vec{b}$

Differential of $\vec{A} = \vec{a} \times \vec{b}$:

$$d\vec{A} = \vec{A}' - \vec{A} = \vec{a}' \times \vec{b}' - \vec{a} \times \vec{b} = (\vec{a}+d\vec{a}) \times (\vec{b}+d\vec{b}) - \vec{a} \times \vec{b} = d\vec{a} \times \vec{b} + \vec{a} \times d\vec{b}$$

$$\boxed{\vec{A} = \vec{a} \times \vec{b} \implies d\vec{A} = d\vec{a} \times \vec{b} + \vec{a} \times d\vec{b}}$$

---

## Page 36 — Summary: Differential of a Sum (slide 36)

**Summary — Differential of a Sum**

$$y = y_1 + y_2 \implies dy = dy_1 + dy_2 \implies d(y_1 + y_2) = dy_1 + dy_2$$

$$\vec{a} = \vec{a}_1 + \vec{a}_2 \implies d\vec{a} = d\vec{a}_1 + d\vec{a}_2 \implies d(\vec{a}_1 + \vec{a}_2) = d\vec{a}_1 + d\vec{a}_2$$

---

## Page 37 — Summary: Differential of a Product (slide 37)

**Summary — Differential of a Product**

$$y = y_1 y_2 \implies dy = dy_1\, y_2 + y_1\, dy_2 \implies d(y_1 y_2) = dy_1\, y_2 + y_1\, dy_2$$

$$\vec{A} = y\vec{a} \implies d\vec{A} = dy\,\vec{a} + y\,d\vec{a} \implies d(y\vec{a}) = dy\,\vec{a} + y\,d\vec{a}$$

---

## Page 38 — Summary: Differential of a Product (dot and cross) (slide 38)

**Summary — Differential of a Product (continued)**

$$y = \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} \implies dy = d\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + \vec{a} \cdot d\vec{b} \implies d(\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}) = d\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} + \vec{a} \cdot d\vec{b}$$

$$\vec{A} = \vec{a} \times \vec{b} \implies d\vec{A} = d\vec{a} \times \vec{b} + \vec{a} \times d\vec{b} \implies d(\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) = d\vec{a} \times \vec{b} + \vec{a} \times d\vec{b}$$

## Page 43 — Finding $d(\sin\theta)$ — the Differential of $\sin\theta$ (slide 43)

**Finding the Differential $d(\sin\theta)$**

*(Diagram: Quarter of the unit circle; two radii at angles $\theta$ and $\theta + d\theta$ are drawn in red; the small right triangle formed by the arc increment $d\theta$ shows the angle $\theta$ at the tip, with vertical and horizontal projections corresponding to $d(\sin\theta)$ and $d(\cos\theta)$)*

$$\sin\theta \quad \longrightarrow \quad \sin(\theta + d\theta)$$
$$d\theta$$

$$\cos\theta = \frac{\sin(\theta + d\theta) - \sin\theta}{d\theta}$$

$$\boxed{d(\sin\theta) = \cos\theta\, d\theta}$$

---

## Page 44 — Finding $d(\cos\theta)$ — the Differential of $\cos\theta$ (slide 44)

**Finding the Differential $d(\cos\theta)$**

*(Diagram: Same quarter-circle construction as page 43; now the horizontal projection shows $\cos\theta - \cos(\theta+d\theta)$, giving a negative change)*

$$\cos\theta \quad \longrightarrow \quad \cos(\theta + d\theta)$$
$$d\theta$$

$$\sin\theta = \frac{\cos\theta - \cos(\theta + d\theta)}{d\theta}$$

$$\boxed{d(\cos\theta) = -\sin\theta\, d\theta}$$

---

## Page 45 — Finding $\cos(d\theta)$ (slide 45)

**Finding the value of $\cos(d\theta)$**

For $y = f(x)$: $\quad dy = f'(x)\,dx = \dfrac{dy}{dx}\,dx$

*(Note: $f'(x)$ and $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$ are the slope of $f(x)$ at $x$)*

For $y = \cos\theta$: the slope of $\cos\theta$ at $\theta = 0$ is $\dfrac{dy}{d\theta} = 0$

$$\left.\frac{dy}{d\theta}\right|_{\theta=0} = \frac{\cos(0+d\theta) - \cos 0}{d\theta} = \frac{\cos(d\theta) - 1}{d\theta} = 0$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \cos(d\theta) = 1$$

---

## Page 46 — Finding $\sin(d\theta)$ (slide 46)

**Finding the value of $\sin(d\theta)$**

For $y = f(x)$: $\quad dy = f'(x)\,dx = \dfrac{dy}{dx}\,dx$

For $y = \sin\theta$: the slope of $\sin\theta$ at $\theta = 0$ is $\dfrac{dy}{d\theta} = 1$

$$\left.\frac{dy}{d\theta}\right|_{\theta=0} = \frac{\sin(0+d\theta) - \sin 0}{d\theta} = \frac{\sin(d\theta) - 0}{d\theta} = 1$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \sin(d\theta) = d\theta$$

---

## Page 47 — Deriving $d(\sin\theta)$ Using the Sine Addition Formula (slide 47)

**Deriving $d(\sin\theta)$ Using the Sine Addition Formula**

**Sine addition formula:**
$$\sin(\alpha + \beta) = \sin\alpha\cos\beta + \cos\alpha\sin\beta$$

Substitute $\alpha \to \theta$, $\beta \to d\theta$ and use the conditions $\sin(d\theta) = d\theta$, $\cos(d\theta) = 1$:

$$\sin(\theta + d\theta) = \sin\theta\cos(d\theta) + \cos\theta\sin(d\theta) = \sin\theta + \cos\theta\, d\theta$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad d(\sin\theta) = \sin(\theta + d\theta) - \sin\theta = \cos\theta\, d\theta$$

---

## Page 48 — Deriving $d(\cos\theta)$ Using the Cosine Addition Formula (slide 48)

**Deriving $d(\cos\theta)$ Using the Cosine Addition Formula**

**Cosine addition formula:**
$$\cos(\alpha + \beta) = \cos\alpha\cos\beta - \sin\alpha\sin\beta$$

Substitute $\alpha \to \theta$, $\beta \to d\theta$ and use $\sin(d\theta) = d\theta$, $\cos(d\theta) = 1$:

$$\cos(\theta + d\theta) = \cos\theta\cos(d\theta) - \sin\theta\sin(d\theta) = \cos\theta - \sin\theta\, d\theta$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad d(\cos\theta) = \cos(\theta + d\theta) - \cos\theta = -\sin\theta\, d\theta$$

---

## Page 49 — Summary 03D (slide 49)

**Summary**

Differential of $\sin\theta$:

$$d(\sin\theta) = \cos\theta\, d\theta$$

Differential of $\cos\theta$:

$$d(\cos\theta) = -\sin\theta\, d\theta$$

## Page 57 — Displacement and Distance (slide 57/58)

**Displacement and Distance**

*(Diagram: A winding dashed-green path from a house to a building, labeled "Distance $\Delta s$"; a straight black arrow from the house to the building, labeled "Displacement $\Delta\vec{r}$")*

$$\Delta s \geq |\Delta\vec{r}|$$

The equality holds only when the direction of motion does not change.

For an infinitesimal displacement, the equality always holds:
$$ds = |d\vec{r}|$$

**Distance $\Delta s$**: the total length of the path traveled from the house to the building along the shown route.

**Displacement $\Delta\vec{r}$**: the vector drawn from the house to the building.

## Page 58 — Displacement and Distance: Car on a Straight Road (slide 58/59)

**Displacement and Distance — Car on a Straight Road**

*(Diagram: Two cars on a straight road; the car on the left is the initial position, the car on the right is the final position)*

When a car moves along a straight road:

- If the car reverses direction at some point and comes back forward:

$$\Delta s > |\Delta\vec{r}|$$

- If the car does not change direction:

$$\Delta s = |\Delta\vec{r}|$$

## Page 59 — Differential Displacement and Differential Distance (slide 59/60)

**Differential Displacement and Differential Distance**

$$\Delta\vec{r} \;\Rightarrow\; d\vec{r}$$

$$\Delta s \;\Rightarrow\; ds$$

When a difference becomes a differential, there is no time to change direction!!!

$$\Delta s \geq |\Delta\vec{r}| \quad \Rightarrow \quad ds = |d\vec{r}|$$

## Page 60 — Summary 03E (slide 60/61)

**Summary**

**Position and Displacement**

The position vector changes when the origin of the reference frame changes, but the displacement vector does not change.

**Displacement and Distance**

If the object does not change its direction of motion, the magnitude of displacement equals the distance; if the direction changes, the distance is greater than the magnitude of displacement:
$$\Delta s > |\Delta\vec{r}|$$

**Differential Displacement**

For differential displacement, the magnitude of displacement equals the distance:
$$ds = |d\vec{r}|$$

## Page 62 — Velocity (slide 62/63)

**Velocity**

When an object moves with displacement $\Delta\vec{r} = \vec{r}' - \vec{r}$ during time $\Delta t = t' - t$:

**Average velocity:**
$$\vec{v} = \frac{\Delta\vec{r}}{\Delta t}$$

**Instantaneous velocity (or simply velocity):**
$$\vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt}$$

---

## Page 63 — Speed (slide 63/64)

**Speed**

When an object travels a distance $\Delta s = s' - s$ during time $\Delta t = t' - t$:

**Average speed:**
$$v = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$$

**Instantaneous speed (or simply speed):**
$$v = \frac{ds}{dt}$$

---

## Page 64 — Velocity and Speed (slide 64/65)

**Velocity and Speed**

*(Left panel — Distance and Displacement)*

*(Diagram: Winding path from house to building, showing $\Delta s$ (distance) and $\Delta\vec{r}$ (displacement))*

$$\Delta s \geq |\Delta\vec{r}|$$

$$ds = |d\vec{r}|$$

*(Right panel — Speed and Velocity)*

$$v = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}, \qquad \vec{v} = \frac{\Delta\vec{r}}{\Delta t}$$

$$\frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t} \geq \left|\frac{\Delta\vec{r}}{\Delta t}\right|$$

The equality holds only when the object does not change its direction of motion.

$$\frac{ds}{dt} = \left|\frac{d\vec{r}}{dt}\right| \quad \Rightarrow \quad v = |\vec{v}|$$

## Page 65 — Acceleration (slide 65/66)

**Acceleration**

When the velocity of an object changes by $\Delta\vec{v} = \vec{v}' - \vec{v}$ during time $\Delta t = t' - t$:

**Average acceleration:**
$$\vec{a} = \frac{\Delta\vec{v}}{\Delta t}$$

**Instantaneous acceleration (or simply acceleration):**
$$\vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt}$$

---

## Page 66 — Direction of the Position Vector (slide 66/67)

**Direction of the Position Vector $\vec{r}$**

The direction of $\vec{r}$ indicates which direction the object is from the origin.

*(Diagram: 3D coordinate axes with a green arrow $\vec{r}$ pointing from the origin toward a walking figure)*

The direction of the position vector has nothing to do with the direction in which the object is moving.
