## Page 3 — Laws of Motion and Force Laws

**Laws of Motion** — Laws that determine the motion of an object

$$\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$$

The law of motion only tells us the magnitude and direction of the net force acting on the object.

**Force Laws** — Laws that determine how the interaction (force) between two objects is determined.

For each type of force, there exists a force law that determines it.

## Page 4 — Single-Body Force vs. Two-Body Force

| | External Force | Internal Force |

| **Definition** | External forces depend only on the coordinates of the body receiving the force. | Internal forces depend on the coordinates of both interacting bodies. |

## Page 5 — Conservative Forces and Potential Energy

**Force $\vec{F}(\vec{r})$** — the force acting on a body when it is at position vector $\vec{r}$

If force $\vec{F}(\vec{r})$ is conservative, one can define potential energy $U(\vec{r})$:

$$U(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}} \vec{F}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$$

- The negative work done by $\vec{F}(\vec{r})$ while the body moves from the reference point $\vec{r}_s$ to $\vec{r}$

- For $U(\vec{r})$ to depend only on $\vec{r}$ (not on the path), the line integral $\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}} \vec{F}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$ must be path-independent.

---

## Page 6 — Finding the Conservative Force from Potential Energy

**Conservation force $\vec{F}(\vec{r})$** $\Rightarrow$ **Potential energy $U(\vec{r})$**

$$U(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}} \vec{F}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$$

**Potential energy $U(\vec{r})$** $\Rightarrow$ **Conservative force $\vec{F}(\vec{r})$**

$$\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = -\nabla U(\vec{r})$$

The direction of $-\nabla U(\vec{r})$ is the direction in which $U(\vec{r})$ decreases most rapidly.

The form of $\vec{F}(\vec{r})$ expresses the force law. $\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = -\nabla U(\vec{r})$ — the same as the force law for potential energy.

---

## Page 7 — Reference Point of Potential Energy

**Definition of potential energy $U(\vec{r})$ for conservative force $\vec{F}(\vec{r})$:**

$$U(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}} \vec{F}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$$

**Reference point of potential energy:** the position at which potential energy is defined to be zero.

**Confirmation:**

$$U(\vec{r}_s) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}_s} \vec{F}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,' = 0 \checkmark$$

The reference point can be chosen freely — choose it conveniently.

---

## Page 8 — Gravitational Potential Energy

$$U(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}} \vec{F}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$$

$$\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = -mg\hat{y}, \qquad d\vec{r} = dx\,\hat{x} + dy\,\hat{y} + dz\,\hat{z}$$

$$\Rightarrow \vec{F}(\vec{r}) \cdot d\vec{r} = -mg\,dy$$

$$U(y) = -\int_{y_s}^{y}(-mg\,dy) = mgy - mgy_s$$

*(Diagram: coordinate axes with $+y$ upward, $+x$ to the right; mass $m$ at origin with gravitational force $mg$ pointing downward.)*

$\Rightarrow$ $y_s = 0$ is the most convenient choice.

**Gravitational potential energy:**

$$U(y) = mgy$$

---

## Page 9 — Elastic (Spring) Potential Energy

$$U(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}} \vec{F}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$$

$$\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = -kx\,\hat{x}, \qquad d\vec{r} = dx\,\hat{x} + dy\,\hat{y} + dz\,\hat{z}$$

$$\Rightarrow \vec{F}(\vec{r}) \cdot d\vec{r} = -kx\,dx$$

$$U(x) = -\int_{x_s}^{x}(-kx\,dx) = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 - \frac{1}{2}kx_s^2$$

*(Diagram: horizontal spring attached to a wall, mass at displacement $x$, spring force $-kx$ shown.)*

$\Rightarrow$ $x_s = 0$ is the most convenient choice.

**Elastic potential energy:**

$$U(x) = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$$

---

## Page 10 — Gravitational Potential Energy (Universal)

$$U(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}} \vec{F}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$$

$$\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = -G\frac{Mm}{r^2}\hat{r}, \qquad d\vec{r} = \hat{r}\,dr + \hat{\theta}\,r\,d\theta + \hat{\phi}\,r\sin\theta\,d\phi$$

$$\Rightarrow \vec{F}(\vec{r}) \cdot d\vec{r} = -G\frac{Mm}{r^2}\,dr$$

$$U(r) = -\int_{r_s}^{r}\!\left(-G\frac{Mm}{r^2}\,dr\right) = -G\frac{Mm}{r} + G\frac{Mm}{r_s}$$

*(Diagram: large mass $M$ at origin, small mass $m$ at position $\vec{r}$, gravitational force $F$ shown.)*

$\Rightarrow$ $r_s = \infty$ is the most convenient choice.

**Universal gravitational potential energy:**

$$U(r) = -G\frac{Mm}{r}$$

---

## Page 11 — Electric Potential Energy (Point Charge)

$$U(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}} \vec{F}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$$

$$\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{r^2}\hat{r}, \qquad d\vec{r} = \hat{r}\,dr + \hat{\theta}\,r\,d\theta + \hat{\phi}\,r\sin\theta\,d\phi$$

$$\Rightarrow \vec{F}(\vec{r}) \cdot d\vec{r} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{r^2}\,dr$$

$$U(r) = -\int_{r_s}^{r}\!\left(\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{r^2}\,dr\right) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{r} - \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{r_s}$$

*(Diagram: source charge $Q$ at origin, test charge $q$ at position $\vec{r}$.)*

$\Rightarrow$ $r_s = \infty$ is the most convenient choice.

**Electric potential energy:**

$$U(r) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{r}$$

---

## Page 12 — Electric Potential Energy (General Positions)

*(Diagram left: charge $Q$ at origin, charge $q$ at position $\vec{r}$. Diagram right: charge $Q$ at position $\vec{r}\,'$, charge $q$ at position $\vec{r}$.)*

**Case: $Q$ at origin, $q$ at $\vec{r}$**

$$U(r) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{r} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{|\vec{r}|}$$

**Case: $Q$ at $\vec{r}\,'$, $q$ at $\vec{r}$**

$$U(\vec{r}, \vec{r}\,') = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|}$$

---

## Page 13 — Potential Energy and Conservative Force

**Remember!**

$$\nabla\!\left(\frac{1}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|}\right) = -\frac{\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|^3} = -\frac{1}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|^2}\frac{\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|}$$

$$U(\vec{r}, \vec{r}\,') = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|}$$

**Electric force on test charge $q$:**

$$\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = -\nabla U(\vec{r}) = -\frac{Qq}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\nabla\!\left(\frac{1}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|}\right) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|^2}\frac{\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|} \checkmark$$

---

## Page 14 — Electric Force and Potential Energy Between Two Point Charges

*(Diagram: two charges $q_1$ at $\vec{r}_1$ and $q_2$ at $\vec{r}_2$.)*

**Electric force between two point charges $q_1$ and $q_2$:**

$$\vec{F}_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|^2}\frac{\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|}$$

$$\vec{F}_{21} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q_2 q_1}{|\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1|^2}\frac{\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1}{|\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1|}$$

**Electric potential energy between two point charges $q_1$ and $q_2$:**

$$U = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|}$$

---

## Page 15 — Deriving Forces from the Potential Energy of Two Point Charges

**Electric potential energy between $q_1$ and $q_2$:**

$$U(\vec{r}_1, \vec{r}_2) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|}$$

**Electric force on charge $q_1$:**

$$\vec{F}_{12} = -\nabla_1 U(\vec{r}_1, \vec{r}_2) = -\frac{q_1 q_2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\nabla_1\!\left(\frac{1}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|}\right) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|^2}\frac{\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|}$$

**Electric force on charge $q_2$:**

$$\vec{F}_{21} = -\nabla_2 U(\vec{r}_1, \vec{r}_2) = -\frac{q_1 q_2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\nabla_2\!\left(\frac{1}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|}\right) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q_2 q_1}{|\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1|^2}\frac{\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1}{|\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1|}$$

## Page 17 — Electric Force, Electric Field, and Electric Potential Energy

*(Diagram: source charge $Q$ at origin, test charge $q$ at position $\vec{r}$.)*

**Electric force on test charge $q$ at position $\vec{r}$ due to source charge $Q$ at the origin:**

$$\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{r^2}\hat{r} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq\,\vec{r}}{r^2\,r}$$

**Electric field created by $Q$ at position $\vec{r}$ (force on unit positive charge):**

$$\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\vec{F}(\vec{r})}{q} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r^2}\frac{\vec{r}}{r}$$

**Electric potential energy of test charge $q$ at position $\vec{r}$ due to $Q$:**

$$U(r) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{r}$$

---

## Page 18 — Electric Potential Energy and Electric Potential

*(Diagram: source charge $Q$ at origin, test charge $q$ at position $\vec{r}$.)*

**Electric force:**  $\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{Qq}{r^2}\dfrac{\vec{r}}{r}$

**Electric field:**  $\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \dfrac{\vec{F}(\vec{r})}{q} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{Q}{r^2}\dfrac{\vec{r}}{r}$

**Electric potential energy:**  $U(r) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{Qq}{r}$

**Electric potential (scalar potential):**

$$\phi(r) = \frac{U(r)}{q} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r}$$

$\Rightarrow$ The electric potential energy per unit positive charge.

## Page 19 — Properties of the Electric Field in Space

**Electric field** (vector field)

$$\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{r^2}\frac{\vec{r}}{r}$$

The force that acts on the test charge $q$ placed at position $\vec{r}$: depends on $q$ (where $Q$ creates the field).

$$\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{\vec{F}(\vec{r})}{q} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r^2}\frac{\vec{r}}{r}$$

The force on a unit positive charge placed at position $\vec{r}$: **electric field** — a property of space.

$$\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = q\vec{E}(\vec{r}) \Rightarrow \text{Force on charge } q \text{ placed at position where the electric field is } \vec{E}(\vec{r})$$

**Unit of electric field: N/C**

---

## Page 20 — Properties of the Electric Potential in Space

$$U(r) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Qq}{r}$$

The electric potential energy of test charge $q$ at position $\vec{r}$: depends on $q$.

$$\phi(r) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r}$$

The electric potential energy of a unit positive charge at position $\vec{r}$: **electric potential** — a property of space.

$$U(r) = q\phi(r) \Rightarrow \text{Electric potential energy of charge } q \text{ placed where the scalar potential is } \phi(r)$$

**Unit of electric potential: J/C = V (volt)**

## Page 29 — Vector Field and Scalar Field

## Page 30 — Reference Point of the Scalar Potential

**Electric potential (scalar potential) $\phi(\vec{r})$:**

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}} \vec{E}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$$

**Reference point $\vec{r}_s$** — the position at which the scalar potential is defined to be zero.

- Can be chosen freely.
- Can be set conveniently depending on the charge distribution.

## Page 31 — Two Methods to Find the Scalar Potential

**(1)** First find the electric field, then derive the scalar potential from the electric field.

- Advantageous when Gauss's law can be applied.

**(2)** Use the scalar potential of point charges and the superposition principle to find the scalar potential.

- Advantageous when Gauss's law cannot be applied.

---

## Page 32 — Scalar Potential of a Point Charge

*(Diagram: point charge $q$ at origin, field point $P$ at position $\vec{r}$.)*

**Scalar potential at point $P$ created by point charge $q$ at the origin:**

$$\phi(r) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q}{r} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q}{|\vec{r}|} \qquad \text{(reference point: } r_s = \infty\text{)}$$

**Electric field (from Gauss's law):**

$$\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2}\frac{\vec{r}}{r}$$

**Verification:**

$$\phi(r) = -\int_\infty^r \vec{E}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,' = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_\infty^r \frac{q}{r^2}\frac{\vec{r}}{r} \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$$

$$= -\frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_\infty^r \frac{dr}{r^2} = \frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\frac{1}{r}\right]_\infty^r = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q}{r} \checkmark$$

---

## Page 33 — Scalar Potential of an Infinite Uniform Line Charge

*(Diagram: infinite uniform line charge $\lambda$ along the $z$-axis; field point $P$ at position $\vec{r}$, perpendicular distance $r$.)*

**Scalar potential at field point $P$ due to infinite uniform line charge $\lambda$ along the $z$-axis:**

**Electric field (from Gauss's law):**

$$\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\lambda}{r}\frac{\vec{r}}{r}$$

$$\phi(r) = -\int_{r_s}^r \vec{E}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,' = -\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\int_{r_s}^r \frac{\lambda}{r}\frac{\vec{r}}{r} \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$$

$$= -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\int_{r_s}^r \frac{dr}{r} = -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\ln r\Big|_{r_s}^r = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\ln\frac{r_s}{r}$$

**Reference point $r_s$:** Cannot be set to 0 or $\infty$!!

---

## Page 34 — Scalar Potential of an Infinite Uniform Surface Charge

*(Diagram: infinite uniform surface charge $\sigma$ in the $xy$-plane; field point $P$ at height $z$.)*

**Scalar potential at field point $P$ due to infinite uniform surface charge $\sigma$ in the $xy$-plane:**

**Electric field (from Gauss's law):**

$$\vec{E}(z) = \frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0}\hat{z}$$

$$\phi(z) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}} \vec{E}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,' = -\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0}\int_{z_s}^z \hat{z} \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$$

$$= -\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0}\int_{z_s}^z dz = -\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0}\Big[z\Big]_{z_s}^z = -\frac{\sigma}{2\epsilon_0}(z - z_s)$$

Reference point $z_s$: setting $z_s = 0$ is convenient!!

## Page 35 — Relationship Between the Scalar Potential and the Electric Field

**Remember!**

$$d\phi(\vec{r}) = d\phi(x, y, z) = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}dx + \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}dy + \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}dz = \nabla\phi \cdot d\vec{r} = -\vec{E}(\vec{r}) \cdot d\vec{r}$$

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}} \vec{E}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,' \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad d\phi(\vec{r}) = -\vec{E}(\vec{r}) \cdot d\vec{r}$$

## Page 36 — Potential Difference Between Two Points in a Uniform Electric Field

$$d\phi(\vec{r}) = -\vec{E}(\vec{r}) \cdot d\vec{r}$$

**Uniform electric field:**

$$\Delta\phi = -\vec{E} \cdot \Delta\vec{r}$$

$$\phi(Q) - \phi(P) = \Delta\phi = -\vec{E} \cdot \Delta\vec{r} = -E\,\Delta r\cos\theta$$

$$\phi(Q) < \phi(P)$$

*(Diagram: uniform electric field $\vec{E}$ pointing in the $+x$ direction; points $P$ and $Q$ with displacement $\Delta\vec{r}$ at angle $\theta$ to the field.)*

**Note:**

- Unit of electric field: $\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = q\vec{E}(\vec{r})$ → **N/C**
- Unit of electric field from $d\phi(\vec{r}) = -\vec{E}(\vec{r}) \cdot d\vec{r}$ → **V/m**

## Page 37 — Scalar Potential of a Uniformly Charged Sphere (Inside)

*(Diagram: sphere of radius $R$ with uniform charge $Q$ in the interior; field point $P$ at position $\vec{r}$.)*

**Electric field (from Gauss's law) for uniformly charged sphere of radius $R$ with total charge $Q$:**

$$r \leq R: \quad \vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{r}{R^3}\hat{r}$$

$$r > R: \quad \vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r^2}\hat{r}$$

**(1) For $r > R$:**

$$\phi(r) = -\int_\infty^r \vec{E}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,' = -\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_\infty^r \frac{dr}{r^2} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r}$$

**(2) For $r \leq R$:**

$$\phi(r) = -\int_\infty^r \vec{E}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,' = -\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_\infty^R \frac{dr}{r^2} - \int_R^r \frac{r}{R^3}\,dr$$

$$= \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{R} - \frac{Q}{8\pi\epsilon_0 R^3}\Big[r^2\Big]_R^r + \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{R} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{8\pi\epsilon_0 R}\!\left(3 - \frac{r^2}{R^2}\right)$$

## Page 38 — Summary: Finding the Potential Difference from the Electric Field

**How to find the potential difference knowing the electric field:**

$$d\phi(\vec{r}) = -\vec{E}(\vec{r}) \cdot d\vec{r}$$

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}} \vec{E}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$$

$$\phi(Q) - \phi(P) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^Q \vec{E}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,' + \int_{\vec{r}_s}^P \vec{E}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,' = \int_Q^P \vec{E}(\vec{r}\,') \cdot d\vec{r}\,'$$

**For a uniform electric field:**

$$\Delta\phi = -\vec{E} \cdot \Delta\vec{r} = -E\,\Delta r\cos\theta$$

## Page 40 — Scalar Potential of a Single Point Charge

*(Diagram: point charge $q_1$ at the origin, field point $P$ at position $\vec{r}$.)*

## Page 42 — Multiple Point Charges (Superposition)

*(Diagram left: single charge $q_1$ at $\vec{r}_1$, field point $P$ at $\vec{r}$. Diagram right: generalized to charge $q_i$ at $\vec{r}_i$.)*

**Electric field (superposition):**

$$\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q_1}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}_1|^2}\frac{\vec{r} - \vec{r}_1}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}_1|} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\sum_{i=1}^N \frac{q_i}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}_i|^2}\frac{\vec{r} - \vec{r}_i}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}_i|}$$

## Page 43 — Continuous Charge Distribution

*(Diagram left: discrete charges $q_i$ at $\vec{r}_i$. Diagram right: continuous charge distribution $dq$ at $\vec{r}\,'$.)*

**Electric field (Coulomb's law for discrete charges → continuous):**

**Scalar potential (Coulomb's law for discrete charges → continuous):**

## Page 45 — Scalar Potential of a Uniformly Charged Ring

*(Diagram: circular ring of radius $R$ in the $xy$-plane with uniform linear charge density $\lambda$; field point $P$ at $(0,0,z)$ on the $z$-axis.)*

$$d\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{dq}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|}$$

$$dq = \lambda\,dl = \lambda\,R\,d\theta$$

$$|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'| = \sqrt{R^2 + z^2}$$

$$d\phi(z) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\lambda\,R\,d\theta}{\sqrt{R^2 + z^2}}$$

$$\phi(z) = \frac{\lambda R}{4\pi\epsilon_0\sqrt{R^2 + z^2}}\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta = \frac{2\pi\lambda R}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{R^2 + z^2}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{\sqrt{R^2 + z^2}}$$

---

## Page 46 — Electric Field of a Uniformly Charged Ring from Scalar Potential

**Scalar potential:**

$$\phi(z) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{\sqrt{R^2 + z^2}}$$

**Electric field (from scalar potential):**

$$\vec{E}(z) = -\nabla\phi(z) = -\hat{z}\frac{d\phi(z)}{dz}$$

$$E(z) = -\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{d}{dz}\frac{1}{\sqrt{R^2 + z^2}}$$

$$= -\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{2z}{(R^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}$$

$$= \frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{z}{(R^2 + z^2)^{3/2}}$$

This is identical to the result obtained by directly applying Coulomb's law to find the electric field!!

## Page 47 — Scalar Potential of a Uniform Finite Line Charge

*(Diagram: finite line charge of length $2l$ along the $x$-axis from $-l$ to $+l$; field point $P$ at position $y$ on the $y$-axis.)*

$$\vec{r} = y\hat{y}, \quad \vec{r}\,' = x'\hat{x}$$

$$\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,' = y\hat{y} - x'\hat{x}$$

$$|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'| = \sqrt{x'^2 + y^2}$$

$$dq = \lambda\,dx'$$

$$d\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{dq}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|} \quad \Rightarrow \quad d\phi(y) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\lambda\,dx'}{\sqrt{x'^2 + y^2}}$$

$$\phi(y) = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_{-l}^{l}\frac{dx'}{\sqrt{x'^2 + y^2}}$$

## Page 48 — Integration / Dwight, Table of Integrals

**Dwight, Tables of Integrals 200.01:**

$$\int\frac{dx}{r} = \int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} = \log(x + r), \quad \text{where } r = \sqrt{x^2 + a^2}$$

**Verification:**

$$\frac{d}{dx}\log(x + r) = \frac{1}{x + \sqrt{x^2 + a^2}}\left(1 + \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}}\right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}}$$

$$I = \int_{-l}^{l}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}} = 2\int_0^l\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}$$

$$= 2\Big[\log\!\left(l + \sqrt{l^2 + y^2}\right) - \log|y|\Big]$$

---

## Page 49 — Scalar Potential of a Uniform Finite Line Charge (Result)

**Scalar potential:**

$$\phi(y) = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_{-l}^{l}\frac{dx'}{\sqrt{x'^2 + y^2}} = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\Big[\log\!\left(l + \sqrt{l^2 + y^2}\right) - \log|y|\Big]$$

**Note:** For an infinitely long line charge ($l \to \infty$), the scalar potential diverges ($\phi(y) \to \infty$)!!

$\Rightarrow$ For an infinite line charge, the reference point of the scalar potential cannot be set at infinity ($r \to \infty$).

## Page 50 — Electric Field from the Scalar Potential of a Uniform Line Charge

**Scalar potential:**

$$\phi(y) = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\Big[\log\!\left(l + \sqrt{l^2 + y^2}\right) - \log|y|\Big]$$

**Electric field:**

$$\vec{E}(y) = -\nabla\phi(y) = -\hat{y}\frac{d\phi(y)}{dy}$$

$$E(y) = -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{d}{dy}\Big[\log\!\left(l + \sqrt{l^2 + y^2}\right) - \log|y|\Big]$$

$$= -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac{1}{l + \sqrt{l^2 + y^2}}\cdot\frac{y}{\sqrt{l^2 + y^2}} - \frac{y}{|y|^2}\right)$$

**For an infinite line charge** ($l \to \infty$):

$$E_y(y) = \frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\lambda}{y} \checkmark$$

This is identical to the result obtained by directly applying Coulomb's law!!

## Page 53 — Scalar Potential of an Electric Dipole (1)

*(Diagram: dipole with charge $-q$ at $x = -d/2$ and $+q$ at $x = +d/2$ on the $x$-axis; field point $P$ at position $x \gg d$.)*

**Scalar potential at point $P$ on the $x$-axis due to two charges $\pm q$ at $x = \pm d/2$ (for $x \gg d$):**

$$\phi(x) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\frac{+q}{\left(x - \dfrac{d}{2}\right)} + \frac{-q}{\left(x + \dfrac{d}{2}\right)}\right] = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q}{x}\left[\frac{1}{\left(1 - \dfrac{d}{2x}\right)} - \frac{1}{\left(1 + \dfrac{d}{2x}\right)}\right]$$

**Well-known approximation:** For $|\alpha|^2 \ll 1$, $(1 + \alpha)^n \approx 1 + n\alpha$:

$$\frac{1}{\left(1 \pm \dfrac{d}{2x}\right)} = \left[1 + \left(\pm\frac{d}{2x}\right)\right]^{-1} \approx 1 \mp \frac{d}{2x}$$

---

## Page 54 — Scalar Potential of an Electric Dipole (1) — Result

*(Diagram: dipole with $-q$ at $x = -d/2$, $+q$ at $x = +d/2$; field point $P$ at $x \gg d$.)*

$$\phi(x) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q}{x}\left[\left(1 + \frac{d}{2x}\right) - \left(1 - \frac{d}{2x}\right)\right] = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{qd}{x^2} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p}{x^2}$$

**Electric field:**

$$\vec{E}(x) = -\nabla\phi(x) = -\hat{x}\frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p}{x^2}\right) = \hat{x}\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p}{x^3} \checkmark$$

This is identical to the result obtained by directly applying Coulomb's law!!

---

## Page 55 — Scalar Potential of an Electric Dipole (2)

*(Diagram: dipole with $+q$ at $y = +d/2$ and $-q$ at $y = -d/2$; field point $P$ at $x \gg d$ on the $x$-axis.)*

**Scalar potential at point $P$ on the $x$-axis due to two charges $\pm q$ at $y = \pm d/2$ (for $x \gg d$):**

$$\phi(x) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\frac{+q}{\sqrt{x^2 + \left(\dfrac{d}{2}\right)^2}} + \frac{-q}{\sqrt{x^2 + \left(\dfrac{d}{2}\right)^2}}\right] = 0$$

**Warning:** In this case, the scalar potential is zero on the $x$-axis (in the $xz$-plane).

However, the electric field $\vec{E}(x) = -\nabla\phi(x)$ cannot be obtained from this alone!!

## Page 56 — Multipole Expansion of the Scalar Potential

*(Diagram: charge distribution $\rho(\vec{r}\,')$ in volume $V$; field point $P$ at $\vec{r}$ far from the distribution.)*

**Conditions:**
1. The charge density $\rho(\vec{r}\,')$ is distributed in a finite region.
2. Find the scalar potential far from the charge distribution ($r \gg r'$).

**Scalar potential:**

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_V \frac{dq}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_V dv'\,\frac{\rho(\vec{r}\,')}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|}$$

---

## Page 57 — Multipole Expansion of the Scalar Potential (Derivation)

$$|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|^2 = (\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,') \cdot (\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,') = r^2 - 2\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}\,' + r'^2 = r^2\left[1 + \left(-\frac{2\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}\,'}{r^2} + \frac{r'^2}{r^2}\right)\right]$$

$$\Rightarrow \frac{1}{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}\,'|} = \frac{1}{r}\left[1 + \left(-\frac{2\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}\,'}{r^2} + \frac{r'^2}{r^2}\right)\right]^{-1/2}$$

Using $(1 + \alpha)^n \approx 1 + n\alpha$ for $|\alpha|^2 \ll 1$:

$$\approx \frac{1}{r}\left[1 - \frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{2\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}\,'}{r^2} + \frac{r'^2}{r^2}\right)\right] = \frac{1}{r} + \frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}\,'}{r^3} + \cdots$$

$$\Rightarrow \phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_V dv'\,\rho(\vec{r}\,')\left(\frac{1}{r} + \frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}\,'}{r^3} + \cdots\right) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac{q}{r} + \frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3} + \cdots\right)$$

---

## Page 58 — Multipole Expansion: Monopole and Dipole Moments

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int_V dv'\,\rho(\vec{r}\,')\left(\frac{1}{r} + \frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}\,'}{r^3} + \cdots\right) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\frac{q}{r} + \frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3} + \cdots\right)$$

**Monopole moment:**

$$\int_V dv'\,\rho(\vec{r}\,')\frac{1}{r} = \frac{q}{r}, \qquad q = \int_V dv'\,\rho(\vec{r}\,')$$

**Dipole moment:**

$$\int_V dv'\,\rho(\vec{r}\,')\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{r}\,'}{r^3} = \frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3}, \qquad \vec{p} = \int_V dv'\,\rho(\vec{r}\,')\vec{r}\,'$$

**Monopole scalar potential:**

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q}{r}$$

**Dipole scalar potential:**

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3}$$

## Page 59 — Scalar Potential of an Electric Dipole

**Scalar potential of an electric dipole at the origin (dipole moment $\vec{p}$):**

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3}$$

**Case 1:** $\vec{r} = x\hat{x}$, $\vec{p} = p\hat{x}$ (dipole along $x$-axis, field point on $x$-axis)

$$\phi(x) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{x\hat{x}\cdot p\hat{x}}{x^3} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p}{x^2} \checkmark$$

**Case 2:** $\vec{r} = x\hat{x}$, $\vec{p} = p\hat{y}$ (dipole along $y$-axis, field point on $x$-axis)

$$\phi(x) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{x\hat{x}\cdot p\hat{y}}{x^3} = 0 \checkmark$$

---

## Page 60 — Electric Field of an Electric Dipole

**Scalar potential of an electric dipole at the origin:**

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3}$$

**Gradient identity:**

$$\nabla\!\left(\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3}\right) = \left(\nabla\frac{1}{r^3}\right)\hat{r}\cdot\vec{p} + \frac{1}{r^3}(\nabla\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}) = -3\frac{\hat{r}}{r^4}\cdot p$$

$$\nabla\!\left(\frac{1}{r^3}\right) = -\frac{3\hat{r}}{r^4}, \qquad \nabla\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p} = \left(\hat{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x} + \hat{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y} + \hat{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)(xp_x + yp_y + zp_z) = \vec{p}$$

$$\Rightarrow \nabla\!\left(\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3}\right) = -\frac{3(\hat{r}\cdot\vec{p})\hat{r}}{r^3} + \frac{\vec{p}}{r^3} = \frac{-3(\hat{r}\cdot\vec{p})\hat{r} + \vec{p}}{r^3}$$

---

## Page 61 — Electric Field of an Electric Dipole (Result)

**Scalar potential of an electric dipole at the origin:**

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3}$$

**Gradient:**

$$\nabla\!\left(\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3}\right) = \frac{-3(\hat{r}\cdot\vec{p})\hat{r} + \vec{p}}{r^3}$$

**Electric field of the dipole:**

$$\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = -\nabla\phi(\vec{r}) = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\nabla\!\left(\frac{\vec{r}\cdot\vec{p}}{r^3}\right) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{3(\vec{p}\cdot\hat{r})\hat{r} - \vec{p}}{r^3}$$

This is the same expression used without proof in Lecture 15!!

**Verification:** For $\vec{r} = x\hat{x}$, $\vec{p} = p\hat{y}$:

$$\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{(x\hat{x})\cdot(p\hat{y})}{r^3} = 0, \qquad \vec{E}(\vec{r}) = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{p}{x^3}\hat{y} \checkmark$$

## Page 62 — Electric Dipole from the Charge Density Perspective

**Charge density expressed using Dirac delta functions:**

The charge density $\rho(\vec{r})$ of a point charge $q$ at position $\vec{r}_1$:

$$\rho(\vec{r}) = q\delta^3(\vec{r} - \vec{r}_1)$$

**Electric dipole:**

$$\rho(\vec{r}) = -q\delta^3(\vec{r} - \vec{r}_1) + q\delta^3(\vec{r} - \vec{r}_2)$$

**Electric dipole moment:**

$$\vec{p} = \int_V dv\,\rho(\vec{r})\vec{r} = \int_V dv\Big[-q\delta^3(\vec{r} - \vec{r}_1) + q\delta^3(\vec{r} - \vec{r}_2)\Big]\vec{r}$$

$$= -q\vec{r}_1 + q\vec{r}_2 = q(\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1) = q\vec{d} \checkmark$$

(Using $\displaystyle\int dv\,f(\vec{r})\delta^3(\vec{r} - \vec{a}) = f(\vec{a})$.)

## Page 63 — Summary: Multipole Expansion

**When viewing a finite charge density distribution $\rho(\vec{r})$ from far away:**

**Monopole moment:**

$$q = \int_V dv\,\rho(\vec{r})$$

**Dipole moment:**

$$\vec{p} = \int_V dv\,\rho(\vec{r})\vec{r}$$

$$\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = -\nabla\phi(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{3(\vec{p}\cdot\hat{r})\hat{r} - \vec{p}}{r^3}$$

## Page 70 — Electric Field Magnitude Just Outside a Charged Conductor

**Electric field magnitude $E$ just outside a conductor surface with surface charge density $\sigma$:**

*(Diagram: conductor surface with surface charge $\sigma$, Gaussian pillbox of area $S$, $E_\text{in} = 0$ inside.)*

$$\text{Gauss's law:} \quad \Phi_E = \frac{q}{\epsilon_0}$$

$$\text{Electric flux:} \quad \Phi_E = SE$$

$$\text{Surface charge:} \quad q = S\sigma$$

$$\Rightarrow SE = \frac{S\sigma}{\epsilon_0}$$

$$\boxed{E = \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}}$$

## Page 75 — Example: Scalar Potential of a Spherical Conductor Shell

Find the scalar potential for (a) $r > b$, (b) $a < r < b$, and (c) $r < a$.

## Page 76 — Scalar Potential: Region $r > b$

**(a) $r > b$: Scalar potential**

$$\phi(r) = -\int_\infty^r \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r^2}\,d\vec{r}\,' = -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[-\frac{Q}{r}\right]_\infty^r = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r}$$

## Page 77 — Scalar Potential: Region $a < r < b$

**(b) $a < r < b$: Scalar potential**

$$\phi(r) = -\int_\infty^b \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r^2}\,d\vec{r}\,' - \int_b^r 0\,d\vec{r}\,'$$

$$= -\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[-\frac{Q}{r}\right]_\infty^b - 0 = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{b}$$

(The scalar potential is constant throughout the conductor.)

## Page 78 — Scalar Potential: Region $r < a$

**(c) $r < a$: Scalar potential**

$$\phi(r) = -\int_\infty^b \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r^2}\,d\vec{r}\,' - \int_b^a 0\,d\vec{r}\,' - \int_a^r \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r^2}\,d\vec{r}\,'$$

$$= \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{b} + \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r} - \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q}{a}$$

## Page 80 — Summary: Properties of Conductors

**Properties of conductors:**

- The electric field inside a conductor is zero.
- Net positive charges inside a conductor exist only on the conductor surface.
- The electric field just outside the conductor surface is perpendicular to the surface.
- The magnitude of the electric field just outside a conductor surface with surface charge density $\sigma$ is:

$$E = \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0}$$

## Page 83 — Electric Energy of Two Point Charges (Derivation)

*(Diagram: charges $q_1$ at $\vec{r}_1$ and $q_2$ at $\vec{r}_2$.)*

**Electric energy of two point charges $q_1$ and $q_2$:**

$$U = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|}$$

Work required to bring $q_1$ to $\vec{r}_1$ and $q_2$ to $\vec{r}_2$.

**Notation:** $|\vec{r}_i - \vec{r}_j| \equiv r_{ij}$ $\Rightarrow$ $r_{12} = r_{21}$

(1) Work required to bring $q_1$ to $\vec{r}_1$ in empty space: $W_1 = 0$

(3) Electric energy of two point charges $q_1$ and $q_2$:

$$U = W_1 + W_2 = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r_{21}}$$

**Work to bring $q_i$ to $\vec{r}_i$:**
