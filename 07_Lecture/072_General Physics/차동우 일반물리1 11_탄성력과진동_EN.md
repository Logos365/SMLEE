## Page 17 — Deformation of a Solid (Strain)

**Deformation (Strain)**

Consider the case where only the shape changes when a force is applied.

The magnitude of change depends on the original size of the object even under the same applied force.

*(Diagram: rod of length $L$ fixed to wall, pulled by force $\vec{F}$ extending by $\Delta L$; rod of length $2L$ extends by $2\Delta L$ under same force)*

$$\text{Deformation (Strain)} \equiv \frac{\Delta L}{L} \implies \textbf{Strain}$$

---

## Page 18 — Stress

**Stress**

When the same force is applied, the magnitude of change also depends on the cross-sectional area over which the force acts.

*(Diagram: same force $\vec{F}$ applied to rods of different cross-sectional areas — thicker rod deforms less)*

$$\text{Stress} \equiv \frac{F}{S} \implies \textbf{Stress}$$

---

## Page 19 — Strain $\propto$ Stress

**Strain $\propto$ Stress**

> In a solid, if the stress is not too large, the strain is proportional to the stress.

$$\text{Strain} \propto \text{Stress}$$

$$\frac{F}{S} = Y\frac{\Delta L}{L}$$

where $Y$ is the **Young's modulus** — an intrinsic property of the material composing the object.

## Page 25 — Hooke's Law

**Hooke's Law**

> The force applied to a solid and the length by which it stretches or compresses are proportional to each other.

*(Diagram: rod fixed to wall; extension $x$ under force $F$; extension $2x$ under force $2F$)*

$$F = kx \quad \text{(elasticity constant)} \implies \text{spring constant}$$

---

## Page 26 — Equivalent Spring Constant: Two Springs in Series (Setup)

**Equivalent Spring Constant of Two Springs in Series**

When two springs with spring constants $k_1$ and $k_2$ are connected in series, what is the equivalent spring constant $k$?

*(Diagram: two separate springs in series, each stretching by $x_1$ and $x_2$ under force $F$; combined spring with total extension $x = x_1 + x_2$)*

$$x_1 = \frac{F}{k_1}, \qquad x_2 = \frac{F}{k_2}, \qquad x = x_1 + x_2 = \frac{F}{k}$$

---

## Page 27 — Two Springs in Series: Derivation

**Equivalent Spring Constant: Two Springs in Series**

$$x_1 = \frac{F}{k_1}, \qquad x_2 = \frac{F}{k_2}, \qquad x = x_1 + x_2 = \frac{F}{k}$$

$$x = \frac{F}{k_1} + \frac{F}{k_2} = \frac{F}{k}$$

$$\boxed{\frac{1}{k} = \frac{1}{k_1} + \frac{1}{k_2}}$$

> The equivalent spring constant is **smaller** than either individual spring constant.

---

## Page 28 — Two Springs in Parallel

**Equivalent Spring Constant of Two Springs in Parallel**

When two springs with spring constants $k_1$ and $k_2$ are connected in parallel, what is the equivalent spring constant $k$?

*(Diagram: two springs side by side, both displaced by same $x$, each exerting force $F_1 = k_1 x$ and $F_2 = k_2 x$ on the block)*

$$F = F_1 + F_2 = k_1 x + k_2 x = kx$$

$$\boxed{k = k_1 + k_2}$$

> The equivalent spring constant is **larger** than either individual spring constant.

---

## Page 29 — Young's Modulus $\implies$ Spring Constant

**Young's Modulus $\implies$ Spring Constant (Elasticity Constant)**

*(Diagram: cylindrical rod of length $L$, cross-section $S$, displaced by $\Delta L = x$ under force $F$)*

From $\dfrac{F}{S} = Y\dfrac{\Delta L}{L}$:

$$F = Y\frac{S}{L}\Delta L = kx$$

$$\boxed{k = Y\frac{S}{L}}$$

## Page 45 — Equation of Motion for an Object Under Elastic Force

**Equation of Motion for an Object Subject to Elastic Force**

Elastic force on an object connected to a spring:

$$F = -kx$$

Newton's second law:

$$F = m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$$

Substituting the elastic force:

$$-kx = m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$$

**Differential equation to solve:**

$$\boxed{\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + \omega^2 x = 0} \qquad \omega^2 = \frac{k}{m}$$

---

## Page 46 — Simple Harmonic Oscillation Satisfies the Equation of Motion

**Simple Harmonic Oscillation and the Equation of Motion for an Object Under Elastic Force**

$$\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + \omega^2 x = 0 \qquad \frac{d^2y}{dt^2} + \omega^2 y = 0$$

For $x(t) = r\cos\omega t$:

$$\frac{dx}{dt} = -r\omega\sin\omega t, \qquad \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = -r\omega^2\cos\omega t$$

For $y(t) = r\sin\omega t$:

$$\frac{dy}{dt} = r\omega\cos\omega t, \qquad \frac{d^2y}{dt^2} = -r\omega^2\sin\omega t$$

> **Simple harmonic oscillation satisfies the equation of motion for an object under elastic force!!**

---

## Page 47 — General Solution of the Equation of Motion

**General Solution of the Equation of Motion**

$$\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + \omega^2 x = 0$$

**Try** $x(t) = e^{\alpha t}$:

$$\alpha^2 + \omega^2 = 0 \implies \alpha = \pm\,\omega i$$

**General solution:**

$$x(t) = Ae^{+i\omega t} + Be^{-i\omega t} \tag{1}$$

**Euler's formula:**

$$e^{\pm i\theta} = \cos\theta \pm i\sin\theta$$

$$x(t) = A(\cos\omega t + i\sin\omega t) + B(\cos\omega t - i\sin\omega t) = (A+B)\cos\omega t + i(A-B)\sin\omega t$$

$$\boxed{x(t) = C\cos\omega t + D\sin\omega t} \tag{2}$$

---

## Page 48 — General Solution (Amplitude-Phase Form)

**General Solution of the Equation of Motion (continued)**

From $x(t) = C\cos\omega t + D\sin\omega t$ with $E = \sqrt{C^2 + D^2}$:

$$\cos\varphi = \frac{C}{\sqrt{C^2+D^2}}, \qquad \sin\varphi = \frac{D}{\sqrt{C^2+D^2}}$$

Using sum formulas:
$$\cos\alpha\cos\beta \pm \sin\alpha\sin\beta = \cos(\alpha \mp \beta)$$
$$\cos\alpha\sin\beta \pm \sin\alpha\cos\beta = \sin(\alpha \pm \beta)$$

$$x(t) = E\!\left(\frac{C}{\sqrt{C^2+D^2}}\cos\omega t + \frac{D}{\sqrt{C^2+D^2}}\sin\omega t\right) = E(\cos\varphi\cos\omega t + \sin\varphi\sin\omega t)$$

$$\boxed{x(t) = E\cos(\omega t - \varphi)} \tag{3}$$

*(Diagram: right triangle with hypotenuse $\sqrt{C^2+D^2}$, adjacent side $C$, opposite side $D$, angle $\varphi$)*

---

## Page 49 — Solution Satisfying Initial Conditions

**Solution Satisfying Initial Conditions**

**General solution:**

$$x(t) = C\cos\omega t + D\sin\omega t \tag{1}$$

$$v(t) = \frac{dx}{dt} = -C\omega\sin\omega t + D\omega\cos\omega t \tag{2}$$

**Initial conditions:** at $t = 0$, $x(0) = x_0$, $v(0) = v_0$

From (1): $x(0) = C = x_0$

From (2): $v(0) = D\omega = v_0$

**Solution satisfying initial conditions:**

$$\boxed{x(t) = x_0\cos\omega t + \frac{v_0}{\omega}\sin\omega t}$$

## Page 50 — Simple Harmonic Oscillation: Example Problem (Setup)

**Simple Harmonic Oscillation — Example**

A log of mass $M$ rests on a frictionless horizontal surface connected to a spring with spring constant $k$. A bullet of mass $m$ traveling at speed $v$ embeds itself in the log. Find the angular frequency and amplitude of the resulting simple harmonic oscillation.

*(Diagram: spring-log-bullet system on frictionless surface)*

**Equation of motion:**

$$-kx = (M+m)\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} \implies \omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{M+m}}$$

**Conservation of linear momentum:**

$$mv = (M+m)v_0 \implies v_0 = \frac{mv}{M+m}$$

**Initial conditions:**

$$x_0 = 0, \qquad v_0 = \frac{mv}{M+m}$$

---

## Page 51 — Simple Harmonic Oscillation: Example Problem (Solution)

**Simple Harmonic Oscillation — Example (continued)**

**Initial conditions:** $x_0 = 0$, $v_0 = \dfrac{mv}{M+m}$

**Solution satisfying initial conditions:**

$$x(t) = x_0\cos\omega t + \frac{v_0}{\omega}\sin\omega t = \frac{m}{M+m}\frac{v}{\omega}\sin\omega t$$

**Amplitude:**

$$\boxed{\text{Amplitude} = \frac{m}{M+m}\frac{v}{\omega}}$$

## Page 55 — Simple Pendulum

**Simple Pendulum**

*(Diagram: pendulum of length $l$, mass $m$, displaced by angle $\theta$; axes $+x$ and $+y$ shown; tension $T$ along string, gravity $mg$ downward)*

**Rotational equation of motion:** $\tau = I\dfrac{d^2\theta}{dt^2}$

$$-mgl\sin\theta = ml^2\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} \implies \frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} + \frac{g}{l}\sin\theta = 0$$

**Small oscillation approximation:** $|\theta| \ll 1 \implies \sin\theta \approx \theta$

$$\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} + \frac{g}{l}\sin\theta = 0 \implies \frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} + \frac{g}{l}\theta = 0 \quad \text{(simple harmonic motion)}$$

$$\text{Angular frequency: } \omega = \sqrt{\frac{g}{l}} \qquad \text{Period: } T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$$

## Page 56 — Object Falling Through a Tunnel Through Earth's Center

**Object Dropped Through a Tunnel Drilled Through Earth's Center**

*(Diagram: Earth cross-section with tunnel; object of mass $m$ at distance $x$ from center)*

**Gravitational force on mass $m$:**

$$F = -G\frac{mm'}{x^2}, \qquad m' = M\frac{x^3}{R^3} \implies F = -G\frac{mM}{R^3}x$$

**Equation of motion:**

$$-G\frac{mM}{R^3}x = m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} \implies \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + \frac{GM}{R^3}x = 0$$

**Angular frequency:** $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{R^3}}$

**Period:**

$$T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{R^3}{GM}} \approx 5{,}060 \text{ s} \approx \mathbf{84 \text{ min}}$$

## Page 58 — Oscillation of a Partially Submerged Object

**Oscillation of an Object Partially Submerged in a Liquid**

*(Diagram: cylindrical object (density $\rho$, cross-section $S$, total length $l$) floating in liquid of density $\rho_0$; equilibrium submerged length $l_0$; displaced downward by $x$)*

**Equilibrium condition:** $\rho S l g - \rho_0 S l_0 g = 0$

**Net force when displaced by $x$:**

$$F = \rho S l g - \rho_0 S(l_0 + x)g = -\rho_0 S x g$$

**Equation of motion:**

$$-\rho_0 S x g = \rho S l\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} \implies \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + \frac{\rho_0 g}{\rho l}x = 0$$

**Angular frequency and period:**

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{\rho_0 g}{\rho l}} \qquad T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\rho l}{\rho_0 g}}$$

---

## Page 59 — Object Connected to Springs on Both Sides

**Object Connected to Springs on Both Sides**

*(Diagram: block of mass $m$ between two springs with constants $k_1$ and $k_2$, displaced by $x$)*

**Force on the block:**

$$F = -k_1 x - k_2 x$$

**Equation of motion:**

$$-(k_1 + k_2)x = m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} \implies \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + \frac{k_1+k_2}{m}x = 0$$

**Angular frequency and period:**

$$\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k_1+k_2}{m}} \qquad T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k_1+k_2}}$$

---

## Page 60 — Summary: Section 11F

**Summary**

**Verifying whether an object's equation of motion applies:**

If the equation of motion has the form $\dfrac{d^2x}{dt^2} + \omega^2 x = 0$, the object undergoes simple harmonic oscillation.

From this equation alone, the angular frequency of the simple harmonic oscillation can be read off directly.

Once the angular frequency is known, the period is $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$.

## Page 64 — Damped Oscillation: Equation of Motion

**Damped Oscillation $\implies$ Equation of Motion**

**Forces on the object:**
- Elastic force: $F_k = -kx$
- Friction force: $f = -b\dfrac{dx}{dt}$

**Equation of motion:** $F = m\dfrac{d^2x}{dt^2}$

$$-kx - b\frac{dx}{dt} = m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$$

$$\boxed{\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{dx}{dt} + \omega^2 x = 0}$$

where $\gamma = \dfrac{b}{2m}$, $\omega^2 = \dfrac{k}{m}$

---

## Page 65 — General Solution of the Constant-Coefficient 2nd-Order ODE

**General Solution of the Constant-Coefficient Second-Order Differential Equation**

$$\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{dx}{dt} + \omega^2 x = 0$$

**Try** $x(t) = e^{\alpha t}$: find $\alpha$ satisfying the equation.

$$\alpha^2 + 2\gamma\alpha + \omega^2 = 0 \implies \alpha_\pm = -\gamma \pm \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega^2}$$

**General solution:**

$$x(t) = Ae^{\alpha_+ t} + Be^{\alpha_- t}$$

$A$ and $B$ are determined by initial conditions.

---

## Page 66 — Types of Damped Oscillation

**Types of Damped Oscillation**

$$\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{dx}{dt} + \omega^2 x = 0, \qquad x(t) = Ae^{\alpha_+ t} + Be^{\alpha_- t}$$

$$\alpha_+ = -\gamma + \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega^2} \qquad \alpha_- = -\gamma - \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega^2}$$

- **(1) Underdamping (weak damping):** $\omega > \gamma$
- **(2) Critical damping:** $\omega = \gamma$
- **(3) Overdamping:** $\omega < \gamma$

---

## Page 67 — (1) Underdamping

**(1) Underdamped Oscillation**

$$x(t) = Ae^{\alpha_+ t} + Be^{\alpha_- t}, \qquad \alpha_\pm = -\gamma \pm \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega^2}$$

$\omega > \gamma \implies \gamma^2 - \omega^2 \equiv -\omega_1^2$

$$\sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega^2} = i\omega_1 \implies \alpha_+ = -\gamma + i\omega_1, \quad \alpha_- = -\gamma - i\omega_1$$

**General solution for underdamped oscillation:**

$$x(t) = Ae^{\alpha_+ t} + Be^{\alpha_- t} = e^{-\gamma t}\!\left(Ae^{+i\omega_1 t} + Be^{-i\omega_1 t}\right)$$

---

## Page 68 — Comparison: Simple Harmonic vs. Underdamped

**Simple Harmonic Oscillation vs. Underdamped Oscillation**

| **Simple harmonic** | **Underdamped** |
|---|---|
| $x(t) = Ae^{+i\omega t} + Be^{-i\omega t}$ | $x(t) = e^{-\gamma t}(Ae^{+i\omega_1 t} + Be^{-i\omega_1 t})$ |
| $x(t) = C\cos\omega t + D\sin\omega t$ | $x(t) = e^{-\gamma t}(C\cos\omega_1 t + D\sin\omega_1 t)$ |
| $x(t) = E\cos(\omega t - \varphi)$ | $x(t) = Ee^{-\gamma t}\cos(\omega_1 t - \varphi)$ |

*(Diagram: sinusoidal oscillation (simple harmonic) vs. decaying oscillation with exponential envelope $e^{-\gamma t}$ (underdamped))*

---

## Page 69 — (2) Critical Damping: Verification

**(2) Critical Damping — Verification**

$$x(t) = Ae^{\alpha_+ t} + Be^{\alpha_- t}, \qquad \alpha_\pm = -\gamma \pm \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega^2}$$

$\omega = \gamma \implies \gamma^2 - \omega^2 = 0 \implies \alpha_+ = \alpha_- = -\gamma$

Since $x(t) = e^{-\gamma t}$ is a solution from the repeated root, $x(t) = te^{-\gamma t}$ is also a solution.

**Verification:**

$$x(t) = te^{-\gamma t}, \quad \frac{dx}{dt} = (1-\gamma t)e^{-\gamma t}, \quad \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = -\gamma(2-\gamma t)e^{-\gamma t}$$

---

## Page 70 — (2) Critical Damping: General Solution

**(2) Critical Damping — General Solution**

$$x(t) = te^{-\gamma t}, \quad \frac{dx}{dt} = (1-\gamma t)e^{-\gamma t}, \quad \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = -\gamma(2-\gamma t)e^{-\gamma t}$$

**Substituting into the equation:**

$$\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{dx}{dt} + \omega^2 x = \left[-\gamma(2-\gamma t) + 2\gamma(1-\gamma t) + \omega^2 t\right]e^{-\gamma t} = -(\gamma^2-\omega^2)te^{-\gamma t} = 0 \checkmark$$

**General solution for critical damping:**

$$\boxed{x(t) = Ae^{-\gamma t} + Bte^{-\gamma t} = (A+Bt)e^{-\gamma t}}$$

---

## Page 71 — (3) Overdamping

**(3) Overdamped Oscillation**

$$x(t) = Ae^{\alpha_+ t} + Be^{\alpha_- t}, \qquad \alpha_\pm = -\gamma \pm \sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega^2}$$

$\omega < \gamma \implies \gamma^2 - \omega^2 \equiv \omega_2^2$

$$\sqrt{\gamma^2 - \omega^2} = \omega_2 \implies \alpha_+ = -\gamma + \omega_2, \quad \alpha_- = -\gamma - \omega_2$$

**General solution for overdamped oscillation:**

$$\boxed{x(t) = Ae^{\alpha_+ t} + Be^{\alpha_- t} = e^{-\gamma t}(Ae^{+\omega_2 t} + Be^{-\omega_2 t})}$$

---

## Page 72 — Three Types of Damped Oscillation

**Three Types of Damped Oscillation**

*(Diagram: time vs. displacement plot showing three curves — underdamped (oscillatory decay), critically damped (fastest non-oscillatory return to zero), overdamped (slow non-oscillatory return to zero))*

- Underdamped: oscillates with decaying amplitude
- Critically damped: fastest return to equilibrium without oscillation
- Overdamped: returns to equilibrium slowly without oscillation

> **Critical damping returns to equilibrium most quickly.**

## Page 75 — Forced Oscillation: Equation of Motion

**Forced Oscillation $\implies$ Equation of Motion**

**Forces on the object:**
- Elastic force: $F_k = -kx$
- Friction force: $f = -b\dfrac{dx}{dt}$
- **Driving force:** $F_d = F_0\cos\omega_0 t$

**Equation of motion:** $F = m\dfrac{d^2x}{dt^2}$

$$F = -kx - b\frac{dx}{dt} + F_0\cos\omega_0 t$$

$$\boxed{-kx - b\frac{dx}{dt} + F_0\cos\omega_0 t = m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}}$$

---

## Page 76 — Forced Oscillation: Inhomogeneous ODE

**Forced Oscillation $\implies$ Equation of Motion (continued)**

$$-kx - b\frac{dx}{dt} + F_0\cos\omega_0 t = m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$$

$$\boxed{\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{dx}{dt} + \omega^2 x = \frac{F_0}{m}\cos\omega_0 t}$$

where $\gamma = \dfrac{b}{2m}$, $\omega^2 = \dfrac{k}{m}$

This is an **inhomogeneous differential equation**.

$\implies$ Must find the solution of the inhomogeneous differential equation.

---

## Page 77 — Method for Solving the Inhomogeneous ODE

**Method for Solving the Inhomogeneous Differential Equation**

$$\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{dx}{dt} + \omega^2 x = \frac{F_0}{m}\cos\omega_0 t$$

Write the solution as $x(t) = x_h(t) + x_p(t)$:

**Homogeneous Solution $x_h(t)$:**

Solution satisfying $\dfrac{d^2x_h}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\dfrac{dx_h}{dt} + \omega^2 x_h = 0$

**Particular Solution $x_p(t)$:**

Solution satisfying $\dfrac{d^2x_p}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\dfrac{dx_p}{dt} + \omega^2 x_p = \dfrac{F_0}{m}\cos\omega_0 t$

---

## Page 78 — Homogeneous Solution (Already Solved)

**Homogeneous Solution**

$$\frac{d^2x_h}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{dx_h}{dt} + \omega^2 x_h = 0 \quad \implies \text{Already solved}$$

- For $\omega > \gamma$ (underdamped): $x_h(t) = e^{-\gamma t}(Ae^{+i\omega_1 t} + Be^{-i\omega_1 t})$, where $\omega_1 = \sqrt{\omega^2-\gamma^2}$
- For $\omega = \gamma$ (critically damped): $x_h(t) = (A+Bt)e^{-\gamma t}$
- For $\omega < \gamma$ (overdamped): $x_h(t) = e^{-\gamma t}(Ae^{+\omega_2 t} + Be^{-\omega_2 t})$, where $\omega_2 = \sqrt{\gamma^2-\omega^2}$

> The homogeneous solution is needed to match initial conditions!

---

## Page 79 — Particular Solution (Setup)

**Particular Solution**

$$\frac{d^2x_p}{dt^2} + 2\gamma\frac{dx_p}{dt} + \omega^2 x_p = \frac{F_0}{m}\cos\omega_0 t$$

**Try** $x_p(t) = a\cos\omega_0 t + b\sin\omega_0 t$ (where $a$ and $b$ are to be determined):

$$\frac{dx_p}{dt} = b\omega_0\cos\omega_0 t - a\omega_0\sin\omega_0 t$$

$$\frac{d^2x_p}{dt^2} = -a\omega_0^2\cos\omega_0 t - b\omega_0^2\sin\omega_0 t$$

---

## Page 80 — Particular Solution: Substitution

**Particular Solution (Substitution into equation)**

$$x_p(t) = a\cos\omega_0 t + b\sin\omega_0 t, \quad \frac{dx_p}{dt} = b\omega_0\cos\omega_0 t - a\omega_0\sin\omega_0 t, \quad \frac{d^2x_p}{dt^2} = -a\omega_0^2\cos\omega_0 t - b\omega_0^2\sin\omega_0 t$$

Substituting:

$$(-a\omega_0^2 + 2\gamma b\omega_0 + \omega^2 a)\cos\omega_0 t + (-b\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma a\omega_0 + \omega^2 b)\sin\omega_0 t = \frac{F_0}{m}\cos\omega_0 t$$

$$-a\omega_0^2 + 2\gamma b\omega_0 + \omega^2 a = \frac{F_0}{m}$$
$$-b\omega_0^2 - 2\gamma a\omega_0 + \omega^2 b = 0$$

Solve the two equations simultaneously for $a$ and $b$.

---

## Page 81 — Particular Solution: Solving for $a$ and $b$

**Particular Solution — Solving for $a$ and $b$**

$$(\omega^2 - \omega_0^2)a + 2\gamma\omega_0 b = \frac{F_0}{m} \tag{1}$$

$$2\gamma\omega_0 a - (\omega^2 - \omega_0^2)b = 0 \tag{2}$$

From (2): $b = \dfrac{2\gamma\omega_0}{\omega^2-\omega_0^2}a$ — substitute into (1):

$$(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)a + \frac{(2\gamma\omega_0)^2}{\omega^2-\omega_0^2}a = \frac{F_0}{m}$$

$$a = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{\omega^2-\omega_0^2}{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2 + (2\gamma\omega_0)^2}$$

Substituting $a$ back into the expression for $b$:

---

## Page 82 — Particular Solution: Result

**Particular Solution — Result**

$$a = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{\omega^2-\omega_0^2}{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2 + (2\gamma\omega_0)^2} \qquad b = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{2\gamma\omega_0}{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2 + (2\gamma\omega_0)^2}$$

$$x_p(t) = a\cos\omega_0 t + b\sin\omega_0 t$$

$$\boxed{x_p(t) = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)\cos\omega_0 t + (2\gamma\omega_0)\sin\omega_0 t}{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2 + (2\gamma\omega_0)^2}}$$

---

## Page 85 — Forced Oscillation: Complete Solution

**Forced Oscillation — Complete Solution**

$$x(t) = x_h(t) + x_p(t)$$

$$= \underbrace{\begin{cases} e^{-\gamma t}(Ae^{+i\omega_1 t} + Be^{-i\omega_1 t}) \\ e^{-\gamma t}(A+Bt) \\ e^{-\gamma t}(Ae^{+\omega_2 t}+Be^{-\omega_2 t}) \end{cases}}_{\textbf{Homogeneous solution}} + \underbrace{\frac{F_0}{m}\frac{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)\cos\omega_0 t + (2\gamma\omega_0)\sin\omega_0 t}{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2 + (2\gamma\omega_0)^2}}_{\textbf{Particular solution}}$$

## Page 86 — Steady State Solution: Phase Angle

**Steady State Solution**

$$x_p(t) = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)\cos\omega_0 t + (2\gamma\omega_0)\sin\omega_0 t}{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2 + (2\gamma\omega_0)^2}$$

$$\cos\varphi = \frac{\omega^2-\omega_0^2}{\sqrt{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2 + (2\gamma\omega_0)^2}} \qquad \sin\varphi = \frac{2\gamma\omega_0}{\sqrt{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2 + (2\gamma\omega_0)^2}}$$

---

## Page 87 — Steady State Solution: Amplitude-Phase Form

**Steady State Solution (continued)**

Using the definitions of $\cos\varphi$ and $\sin\varphi$:

$$x_p(t) = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega_0)^2}}\left(\cos\varphi\cos\omega_0 t + \sin\varphi\sin\omega_0 t\right)$$

$$= \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega_0)^2}}\cos(\omega_0 t - \varphi)$$

$$\boxed{x_p(t) = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega_0)^2}}\cos(\omega_0 t - \varphi)}$$

---

## Page 88 — Amplitude of the Steady State Solution

**Amplitude of the Steady State Solution**

*(Diagram: spring-mass-damper with driving motor rotating at $\omega_0$)*

$$x_p(t) = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega_0)^2}}\cos(\omega_0 t-\varphi) = A(\omega_0)\cos(\omega_0 t-\varphi)$$

The **amplitude** $A(\omega_0)$ depends on the driving frequency $\omega_0$.

- $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$: natural (free) angular frequency
- $\omega_0$: driving angular frequency

> **The amplitude of the steady state solution $A(\omega_0)$ changes with the driving frequency $\omega_0$.**

---

## Page 89 — Resonance of the Steady State Solution

**Resonance of the Steady State Solution**

**Steady state solution:** $x_p(t) = A(\omega_0)\cos(\omega_0 t - \varphi)$

**Amplitude:**

$$A(\omega_0) = \frac{F_0}{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega_0)^2}}$$

**Resonance:** The amplitude changes with driving frequency. When the driving frequency $\omega_0 = \omega_R$ gives maximum amplitude, $\omega_R$ is called the **resonance frequency**.

**Resonance condition:**

$$\frac{d}{d\omega_0}A(\omega_0) = 0$$

---

## Page 90 — Resonance Frequency

**Resonance Frequency**

**Amplitude:** $A(\omega_0) = \dfrac{F_0}{m}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega_0)^2}}$

**Resonance condition:** $\dfrac{d}{d\omega_0}A(\omega_0) = \dfrac{F_0}{m}\dfrac{d}{d\omega_0}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega_0)^2}} = 0$

$$= \frac{F_0}{m}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{-4\omega_0(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)+8\gamma^2\omega_0}{\left[(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2+(2\gamma\omega_0)^2\right]^{3/2}} = 0$$

$$\omega^2 - \omega_0^2 - 2\gamma^2 = 0$$

$$\boxed{\omega_R = \sqrt{\omega^2 - 2\gamma^2} < \omega}$$

> The resonance frequency is **slightly smaller** than the free oscillation frequency.

**Phase angle $\varphi$:**
