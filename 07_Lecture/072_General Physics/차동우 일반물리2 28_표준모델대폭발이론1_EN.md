## Page 3 — What Are Elementary Particles?

**What is an elementary particle?**

- A name given to particles that have no internal structure and cannot be broken down further
- The term "particle" (입자) is used here to emphasize that it is a particle, not a wave — it does not refer to a generic particle
- Elementary particles do not refer to any specific particle; the term is used as a general noun meaning the most fundamental constituents
- In English, they are called **Elementary particles**

> *(Note: For a long time the term was used as a proper noun.)*

---

## Page 4 — Dalton's Atomic Theory

**John Dalton**

**Dalton's Atomic Theory (1808)**

- Matter is composed of very small particles called atoms
- Atoms of the same element are identical in size, mass, and properties
- Atoms cannot be split, created, or destroyed
- Atoms of different elements combine to form compounds
- Atoms join or separate through chemical reactions

*(England, 1766–1844) — First to propose the atomic theory*

---

## Page 5 — Mendeleev's Periodic Table

**Dmitri Mendeleev**

**Mendeleev's Periodic Table of Elements (1869)**

- Elements known at the time were arranged in order of atomic mass
- Discovered that elements with similar properties repeat periodically
- Predicted the atomic masses and properties of undiscovered elements, leaving blank spaces, making it easier to find the corresponding elements

- Became the first evidence that atoms are not elementary particles

*(Russia, 1834–1907) — The first chemist to create the periodic table of elements*

---

## Page 6 — Thomson's Discovery of the Electron

**Joseph John Thomson**

**Thomson's Discovery of the Electron (1897)**

- Discovered the electron through cathode ray tube experiments
- The electron carries negative charge and has a mass $\frac{1}{2000}$ that of a hydrogen atom, suggesting the electron exists inside the atom
- Thomson proposed the **plum pudding model** of the atom (1904): electrons are embedded in a sphere of diffuse positive charge

*(England, 1856–1940) — Physicist, Cavendish Laboratory, discoverer of the electron*

## Page 12 — Summary: From Atoms to Elementary Particles

**Summary**

Starting from Dalton's atomic theory (1808), matter was thought to be made of atoms — the basic building blocks — and atoms could not be split, newly created, or destroyed.

However, through the discovery of the periodic table of elements (1869), the discovery of the electron (1897), the discovery of the atomic nucleus (1911), and the discovery of the neutron (1932), it became clear that atoms are composed of electrons, protons, and neutrons.

After confirming that protons and neutrons cannot be further split and that they cycle into each other, the name **elementary particle** was given to these three particles — the electron, proton, and neutron — at the end of the 20th century.

> *(Note: Used as a proper noun for a long time.)*

## Page 28 — Relativistic Quantum Mechanics: The Dirac Equation

**Relativistic Quantum Mechanics $\Rightarrow$ Dirac Equation**

**Non-relativistic Schrödinger equation:**

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi + V\psi = i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi$$

> *(Note: Time and space coordinates are not included in the equation in the same way — time appears as a first derivative but space appears as a second derivative.)*

**Relativistic Dirac equation:**

$$\left(i\gamma^0\frac{\partial}{\partial t} - i\vec{\gamma}\cdot\vec{\nabla} - m\right)\psi = 0$$

> *(Note: Both time and space coordinates are included as first-order derivatives.)*

---

## Page 29 — Energy Eigenvalues of the Dirac Equation

**Schrödinger equation:**

$$E = \frac{p^2}{2m} + V \geq 0$$

- In the ground state, electrons fill energy levels from the lowest upward.

**Dirac equation:**

$$E^2 = p^2c^2 + m^2c^4 \quad \Rightarrow \quad E = \pm\sqrt{p^2c^2 + m^2c^4}$$

- The energy eigenvalues of the Dirac equation include both positive and negative energies
- Dirac interpreted the vacuum state as analogous to a state where all negative energy levels are completely filled with electrons

---

## Page 30 — The Relationship Between Energy and Momentum: Holes and Antimatter

*(Diagram: Left — a lattice of negative-energy states (filled) and positive-energy states (empty), showing an electron being excited from a negative-energy state to a positive-energy state, leaving a "hole." Right — energy level diagram showing $E^+$ (positive) and $E^-$ (negative) regions with a particle and a hole.)*

- When an electron in a filled state receives energy and jumps out, an electron and a hole are created; that hole corresponds to the antiparticle of the electron
- This antiparticle is called the **positron**; the existence of the positron is predicted
- This shows that **special relativity** combined with **quantum mechanics** implies the existence of antimatter

## Page 34 — Electromagnetic Force and Mediator Particles

**Electromagnetic Force and Mediator Particles**

*(Diagram: A Feynman diagram showing two electrons $e^-$ exchanging a photon $\gamma$ (virtual photon), resulting in the electrons being deflected — representing electromagnetic repulsion.)*

- In the 1930s, physicists figured out the mechanism of electromagnetic force:
  - **A particle that mediates the force is exchanged between the two charges**
  - A **photon** mediates the electromagnetic force
- The mass of the mediator particle is inversely proportional to the range over which the force acts
  - The electromagnetic force is a long-range force, so the rest mass of the photon is **zero**

---

## Page 35 — Yukawa's Proposal for the Nuclear Force

**The nuclear force acts only inside the nucleus — a short-range force**

$$\Delta x \approx 2 \text{ fm} = 2 \times 10^{-15} \text{ m}$$

**Heisenberg uncertainty principle:** $\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \approx \hbar$, $\Delta E \cdot \Delta t \approx \hbar$ applied

**Time interval for the mediator to exist:**

$$\Delta t = \frac{\Delta x}{c} = 2 \text{ fm/c}$$

**Energy of the mediator** (using the uncertainty principle to find the rest mass energy):

$$\Delta E \approx \frac{\hbar}{\Delta t} = \frac{\hbar c}{2 \text{ fm}} = \frac{197.3 \text{ MeV·fm}}{2 \text{ fm}} \approx 100 \text{ MeV}$$

---

- From the condition that the nuclear force is limited to the range inside the nucleus, Yukawa used the uncertainty principle to propose that the mass of the nuclear force mediator is about **200 times the mass of the electron**

- The most fundamental theory describing the classification of fundamental particles that make up matter and the interactions between them is called the **Standard Model**
- The Standard Model is the result of the collective efforts of physicists throughout the 20th century

Today the Standard Model was finalized in the mid-1970s after the existence of quarks was confirmed, and has been further solidified by the discovery of the **top quark** in 1995, the **tau neutrino** in 2000, and the **Higgs boson** in 2013.
