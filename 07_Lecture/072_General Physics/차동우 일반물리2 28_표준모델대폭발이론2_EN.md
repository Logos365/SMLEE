## Page 12 — Special Relativity vs. General Relativity (Slide 77)

**Special Relativity vs. General Relativity**

**Special Relativity**

- Developed to resolve the strange phenomenon that the speed of light is constant.
- We came to learn that space and time cannot be separated, and that we live in a 4-dimensional spacetime.
- Electromagnetism was found to be consistent with special relativity from the outset.
- $E = mc^2$ — came to understand things such as high spin, antiparticles, etc., that had not been anticipated at all.
- When the speed is very small compared to the speed of light, the relativistic mechanics reduces to Newtonian mechanics.

---

## Page 13 — Special Relativity vs. General Relativity (Slide 78)

**General Relativity**

- Developed to generalize special relativity.
- Came to understand that gravity does not exist independently — mass curves the surrounding spacetime.
- For large masses, the effects of general relativity become significant.
- However, in the vicinity of objects with small mass, general relativity does not reduce to Newtonian gravity.

  $\Rightarrow$ Obtained the unexpected result known as the **Big Bang Theory** (cosmology).

## Page 14 — Einstein's Early Universe (Slide 79)

**Einstein's Field Equation**

$$R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} R g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}$$

*(Labels: left side = structure of spacetime; right side = distribution of mass–energy)*

**Introduction of the cosmological constant $\Lambda$ in Einstein's 1917 universe**

- At the time, it seemed natural to Einstein that the entire universe should remain unchanging and static.  $\Rightarrow$ **Static universe**
- However, if matter is uniformly distributed and gravity is the only force between mass elements, then the universe described by Einstein's field equations should contract.
- To ensure a non-contracting, static universe, an additional term was introduced into the field equations.

## Page 23 — The Doppler Effect for Sound (Slide 88)

**The Doppler Effect for Sound**

First described by the Austrian Doppler in 1842.

- Frequency emitted by the source: $f_s$
- Frequency observed by the observer: $f_o$

*(Diagram: Three cases of a train whistle — stationary locomotive (tone is normal): $f_o = f_s$; locomotive approaching (pitch is high): $f_o > f_s$; locomotive moving away (pitch is low): $f_o < f_s$)*

$$f_o = f_s \quad \text{(source stationary)}$$
$$f_o > f_s \quad \text{(source approaching)}$$
$$f_o < f_s \quad \text{(source receding)}$$

## Page 35 — Hubble's Law and the Age of the Universe (Slide 100)

**Hubble's Law $v = H_0 d$ and the Age of the Universe**

Time for the universe to expand to a radius $d$:

$$t = \frac{d}{v} = \frac{d}{H_0 d} = \frac{1}{H_0}$$

**Hubble constant**

$$H_0 = 67.6 \text{ (km/s)/Mpc} = \frac{67.6 \times 10^3 \text{ m/s}}{(3.086 \times 10^{16}) \times 10^6 \text{ m}}$$

$$= 2.191 \times 10^{-18} \text{ s}^{-1}$$

$$t = \frac{1}{H_0} = \frac{1}{2.191 \times 10^{-18}} \text{ s} = 4.564 \times 10^{17} \text{ s} = \frac{4.564 \times 10^{17}}{3.154 \times 10^7} \text{ yr}$$

$$= 1.447 \times 10^{10} \text{ yr} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \textbf{138.3 billion years}$$

## Page 45 — Blackbody (Slide 110)

**Blackbody**

A blackbody? $\Rightarrow$ **A body that does not reflect any incident light — it absorbs everything.**

*(Diagram: Same hollow-sphere cavity — light bouncing inside eventually escapes through the hole, so a blackbody also emits radiation)*

- Light moving inside the cavity will inevitably hit the hole and be emitted.
- $\Rightarrow$ In a blackbody, radiation of all wavelengths is emitted.
- The intensity of radiation emitted by a blackbody as a function of wavelength (frequency) is called the **blackbody radiation curve**.

---

## Page 46 — Blackbody Radiation Curve (Slide 111)

**Blackbody Radiation Curve**

**Blackbody radiation curve** $\Rightarrow$ The intensity spectrum of light emitted by a hot object; the curve depends **only on the temperature** of the object.

*(Diagram: Family of blackbody radiation curves (Planck curves) for different temperatures, showing the peak shifting to shorter wavelengths at higher temperatures — visible-light spectrum indicated)*

**Planck's blackbody radiation formula**

$$B_\lambda(T) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5} \frac{1}{e^{\frac{hc}{\lambda kT}} - 1}$$

**The cosmic microwave background corresponds to the blackbody radiation of a blackbody at $T = 2.73\text{ K}$ in Planck's blackbody radiation formula.**
