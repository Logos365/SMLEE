## Page 8 — What is Translational Motion?

A motion in which the **displacement** of every part of an extended object moves identically.

*(Diagram: A rectangular box (extended object) shown in two positions, with red arrows from each corner of the original position to the corresponding corner of the new position — all arrows are equal in length and direction, illustrating that every point of the object undergoes the same displacement.)*

In an extended object, **which part's displacement** represents the motion of the entire object?

➡ **The displacement of every part** can all represent the motion of the whole.

---

## Page 9 — Translational Motion and Total Mass

*(Diagram: Three masses $m_1$, $m_2$, $m_3$ connected by lines, each displaced by displacement vectors $\vec{\Delta r_1}$, $\vec{\Delta r_2}$, $\vec{\Delta r_3}$ pointing in the same direction, illustrating translational motion of a rigid body.)*

### Translational Motion

$$\Rightarrow \quad \vec{\Delta r_1} = \vec{\Delta r_2} = \vec{\Delta r_3} = \vec{\Delta r}$$

---

**Velocities of the three parts**

$$\Rightarrow \quad \vec{v_1} = \frac{\vec{\Delta r_1}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\vec{\Delta r}}{\Delta t}, \quad \vec{v_2} = \frac{\vec{\Delta r_2}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\vec{\Delta r}}{\Delta t}, \quad \vec{v_3} = \frac{\vec{\Delta r_3}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\vec{\Delta r}}{\Delta t}$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \vec{v_1} = \vec{v_2} = \vec{v_3} = \vec{v}$$

---

**Accelerations of the three parts**

$$\Rightarrow \quad \vec{a_1} = \frac{\vec{\Delta v_1}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\vec{\Delta v}}{\Delta t}, \quad \vec{a_2} = \frac{\vec{\Delta v_2}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\vec{\Delta v}}{\Delta t}, \quad \vec{a_3} = \frac{\vec{\Delta v_3}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\vec{\Delta v}}{\Delta t}$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \vec{a_1} = \vec{a_2} = \vec{a_3} = \vec{a}$$

---

## Page 10 — Translational Motion and Total Mass

*(Diagram: A rigid body composed of three masses $m_1$, $m_2$, $m_3$ connected together, each undergoing displacement vectors $\Delta\vec{r}_1$, $\Delta\vec{r}_2$, $\Delta\vec{r}_3$ respectively, all pointing in the same direction.)*

**Translational Motion**

$$\Delta\vec{r}_1 = \Delta\vec{r}_2 = \Delta\vec{r}_3 = \Delta\vec{r}$$

**Equation of motion for each part:**

$$\vec{F}_1 = m_1\vec{a}_1, \quad \vec{F}_2 = m_2\vec{a}_2, \quad \vec{F}_3 = m_3\vec{a}_3$$

**Adding all three equations together:**

$$\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_3 = m_1\vec{a}_1 + m_2\vec{a}_2 + m_3\vec{a}_3$$

$$= (m_1 + m_2 + m_3)\vec{a}$$

$$\boxed{\vec{F} = M\vec{a}}$$

**Where:**

$$\vec{F} = \vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_3 \qquad M = m_1 + m_2 + m_3$$

> **When an extended body undergoes translational motion, only the total mass is needed in the equation of motion.**

## Page 11 — What is Rotational Motion?

**A motion in which all parts of an extended object move with the same rotational angle**

*(Diagram: A green cylinder rotating about a fixed axis (rotation axis marked at the bottom with a black dot). A red line passes through the cylinder along its length, and arrows indicate that every part of the cylinder rotates by the same angular displacement $\Delta\theta$.)*

- **Rotation axis (회전축)**
- Angular displacement: $\Delta\theta$

**In an extended object, which part's displacement can represent the motion of the entire object?**

➡ **In rotational motion, the displacement of no single part can represent the motion of the whole.**

## Page 14 — Description of Rotational Motion

**Translational Motion** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **Rotational Motion**

*(Diagram: On the left, a cube with arrows showing all points moving in the same direction, illustrating translational motion. On the right, a cylinder rotating about a tilted axis with arrows indicating rotation.)*

Rotational motion is described using the **same equations** as those used to describe translational motion.

The **rotational quantities** that correspond to the physical quantities used to describe translational motion are used in their place.

**Time** and **energy** are used the same way in both translational and rotational motion.

## Page 15 — Translating from Translational Motion to Rotational Motion

**Translational Motion** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **Rotational Motion**

*(Diagram: On the left, a rectangular box (cube) with red arrows showing all points moving in the same direction — illustrating translational motion. On the right, a green cylinder rotating about a diagonal axis, with red arrows indicating rotational motion around the axis.)*

We find the rotational motion quantities that correspond to the physical quantities describing translational motion.

**If we replace the physical quantities in the formulas describing translational motion with rotational motion quantities, we obtain the formulas describing rotational motion.**

$$\text{Position} \quad x \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{Angle} \quad \theta$$

## Page 18 — The Radian as a Unit of Angle

*(Diagram: A circle centered at the origin with coordinate axes. A radius of length $r$ is drawn at angle $\theta$ from the positive $x$-axis, and the arc length $l$ is marked along the circumference.)*

**Definition of Angle**

$$\theta = \frac{l}{r} \ \text{rad}$$

An angle is a dimensionless quantity and does not require a unit, but the unit **rad** is used to indicate that the quantity represents an angle.

**Examples)**

$$l = r\theta = (2 \ \text{m})(3 \ \text{rad}) = 6 \ \text{m}$$

$$v = r\omega = (1 \ \text{m})(2 \ \text{rad/s}) = 2 \ \text{m/s}$$

## Page 20 — Angular Velocity

*(Diagram: A circle centered at the origin with x and y axes shown. A radius of length $l$ is drawn at angle $\theta$ from the $+x$ axis. A small angular displacement $\Delta\theta$ is shown, with the corresponding arc length $\Delta l$ indicated between two positions of the radius vector $r$.)*

**Angular velocity** $\quad \omega = \dfrac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta t}$

From $\quad \Delta\theta = \dfrac{\Delta l}{r}$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \omega = \frac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\Delta l}{\Delta t} = \frac{v}{r}$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \boldsymbol{v = r\omega}$$

---

## Page 21 — Velocity Vector Represented in 2D Polar Coordinates

**2D Polar Coordinate System** $\quad \vec{r} = r\hat{r}$

---

**Displacement vector** $\quad \vec{dr}$

$$\vec{dr} = d(r\hat{r}) = dr\,\hat{r} + r\,\overbrace{d\hat{r}}$$

$$= dr\,\hat{r} + r\,d\theta\,\hat{\theta}$$

---

**Velocity vector** $\quad \vec{v} = \dfrac{\vec{dr}}{dt}$

$$\vec{v} = v_r\hat{r} + v_\theta\hat{\theta} = \frac{dr}{dt}\hat{r} + r\frac{d\theta}{dt}\hat{\theta} \implies v_r = \frac{dr}{dt} \qquad v_\theta = r\frac{d\theta}{dt}$$

---

**Circular motion** $\implies$

$$v_r = \frac{dr}{dt} = 0, \quad v_\theta = r\frac{d\theta}{dt} = r\omega$$

## Page 22 — Summary

**Translational Motion** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Position &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Displacement &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Velocity &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Acceleration

**Rotational Motion** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Angle &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Angular Displacement &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Angular Velocity &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; Angular Acceleration

The formulas for rotational motion are conveniently obtained by translating (mapping) the formulas for translational motion.

Only **time** and **energy** are used identically in both translational and rotational motion.

## Page 43 — Angular Velocity and Angular Acceleration in Circular Motion

**Polar Coordinates**

$$\vec{r} = r\hat{r} \qquad \vec{v} = \frac{dr}{dt}\hat{r} + r\frac{d\theta}{dt}\hat{\theta}$$

$$\vec{a} = \left(\frac{d^2r}{dt^2} - r\left[\frac{d\theta}{dt}\right]^2\right)\hat{r} + \left(2\frac{dr}{dt}\frac{d\theta}{dt} + r\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2}\right)\hat{\theta}$$

**Circular Motion**

$r = \text{constant}$ $\qquad \dfrac{dr}{dt} = \dfrac{d^2r}{dt^2} = 0$

$$\vec{v} = r\omega\hat{\theta} \qquad \boxed{\vec{a} = -r\omega^2\hat{r} + r\alpha\hat{\theta}}$$

where $\quad$ **angular velocity** $\omega = \dfrac{d\theta}{dt}$ $\quad$ **angular acceleration** $\boxed{\alpha = \dfrac{d^2\theta}{dt^2}}$

---

## Page 44 — Tangential Velocity and Angular Velocity of Circular Motion

$$\vec{v} = \frac{dr}{dt}\hat{r} + r\frac{d\theta}{dt}\hat{\theta}$$

**Circular motion** $\quad \dfrac{dr}{dt} = 0$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \vec{v} = r\frac{d\theta}{dt}\hat{\theta} = v_\theta\hat{\theta}$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{d\theta}{dt} = \omega$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad v_\theta = r\frac{d\theta}{dt} = r\omega$$

**The velocity in circular motion is only the tangential velocity.**

➡ **The velocity in rotational motion is also only the tangential velocity.** $\quad v = r\omega$

## Page 45 — Acceleration and Angular Acceleration in Circular Motion

$$\vec{a} = \left(\frac{d^2r}{dt^2} - r\left[\frac{d\theta}{dt}\right]^2\right)\hat{r} + \left(2\frac{dr}{dt}\frac{d\theta}{dt} + r\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2}\right)\hat{\theta}$$

**Circular Motion** $\quad \dfrac{dr}{dt} = \dfrac{d^2r}{dt^2} = 0$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \vec{a} = -r\left(\frac{d\theta}{dt}\right)^2\hat{r} + r\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2}\hat{\theta} = a_r\hat{r} + a_\theta\hat{\theta}$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{d\theta}{dt} = \omega, \quad \frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} = \alpha$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad a_r = -r\omega^2, \quad a_\theta = r\alpha$$

*(Diagram: A circle centered at the origin with $+x$ and $+y$ axes. A red dot on the upper-right of the circle is at radius $r$ and angle $\theta$. ## Page 119 — One-Dimensional Problem

## Page 141 — Differential Equations

**Differential Equation** — An equation containing derivatives

**General form of a 2nd-order linear differential equation**

$$a(x)\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + b(x)\frac{dy}{dx} + c(x)y = f(x)$$

- **Independent variable** — The variable appearing in the denominator of the derivative $\Rightarrow$ $x$
- **Dependent variable** — The variable appearing in the numerator of the derivative $\Rightarrow$ $y$

- **Order of a differential equation** — The highest order derivative contained in the equation $\Rightarrow$ **2nd order**

- **Linear or Nonlinear** — If the degree of the dependent variable in each term on the left-hand side is 1, it is **linear**; otherwise, it is **nonlinear**

- **Homogeneous or Non-homogeneous** — If the right-hand side is 0, it is **homogeneous**; if not 0, it is **non-homogeneous**

## Page 143 — General Solution of a 2nd-Order ODE with Constant Coefficients

$$a\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + b\frac{dy}{dx} + cy = 0$$

---

**Try** $\quad y(x) = e^{\lambda x}$ $\Rightarrow$ **Find $\lambda$ that satisfies the original equation**

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \lambda e^{\lambda x}$$

$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \lambda^2 e^{\lambda x}$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad a\lambda^2 e^{\lambda x} + b\lambda e^{\lambda x} + ce^{\lambda x} = 0$$

Since $e^{\lambda x} \neq 0$:

$$\Rightarrow \quad a\lambda^2 + b\lambda + c = 0$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \lambda = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$
