## Page 17 — What is Force?

**What is Force?**

*(Diagram: two rocks (Object A and Object B) with red arrows pointing toward each other)*

- For a force to act, two objects must be present.
- Force is a quantity that describes how strongly two objects interact.
- We always say "A exerts force $F$ on B." — **Action**
- Then we can also say "B exerts force $F$ on A." — **Reaction**

---

## Page 18 — Newton's Third Law as stated in the Principia

**Third Law as stated in the Principia**

**LAW III.**

*To every action there is always opposed an equal reaction: or the mutual actions of two bodies upon each other are always equal, and directed to contrary parts.*

- For every action, there always exists an equal and opposite reaction. Or, the mutual forces that two bodies exert on each other are always equal in magnitude and directed toward each other.
- A force for which no reaction can be found is not a force.
- Newton used the action–reaction law to clarify that force is a measure of the mutual interaction between two bodies.

## Page 21 — Second Law vs. Third Law

**Second Law and Third Law — Comparison**

| **Second Law** | **Third Law** |
|---|---|
| Concerns the *motion* of an object | Concerns the *nature* of force |
| The vector sum of all forces acting on the object | Forces acting on the two mutually interacting objects |
| The acceleration of an object equals the net force on it divided by its mass. | Action and reaction are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. |

## Page 22 — Summary: Nature of Force

**Summary**

- Force does not exist on its own and then find an object to act on.
- There are only two interacting objects, A and B.
  - The degree to which the two objects interact **is** the force.
- If the force that A exerts on B is called the **action**, then the force that B exerts on A is the **reaction** of that action.

## Page 25 — Laws of Motion vs. Force Laws

**Laws of Motion vs. Force Laws**

- **Law of Motion** — the law that determines the motion of an object

$$F = ma$$

  The law of motion tells us only the magnitude and direction of the net force acting on an object.

- **Force Law** — the law that determines how the interaction (force) between two objects is determined.

  For every force there exists a force law that determines that force.

---

## Page 26 — Classification of Forces by Name

**Classification of Forces by Name**

**Forces determined by a force law:**
- Gravitational (universal), gravity (weight), electric force, elastic force, friction force, nuclear force, …

**Forces not determined by a force law — forces that arise because an object's motion is constrained (constraint forces):**
- Tension and normal force
- These two are called *constraint forces*; their magnitude and direction are determined by the law of motion.

**Names for groups of forces sharing a common property:**
- Centripetal force, conservative force, external force, internal force, central force

**Names that sound like forces but are not forces:**
- Electromotive force (EMF), electric power, centrifugal force, vitality (활력)

## Page 27 — Universal Gravitation and Gravity

**Universal Gravitation and Gravity**

**Universal Gravitation**

$$F = -G\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$$

*(Diagram: two masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ at positions $\vec{r}_1$ and $\vec{r}_2$; force vectors $\vec{F}_{12}$ and $\vec{F}_{21}$ pointing toward each other)*

$$\vec{F}_{12} = -G\frac{m_1 m_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|^2}\frac{\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|}$$

$$\vec{F}_{21} = -G\frac{m_2 m_1}{|\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1|^2}\frac{\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1}{|\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1|}$$

- The hallmark of universal gravitation is that the force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance!

---

## Page 28 — Universal Gravitation and Gravity (Gravity near Earth's Surface)

**Universal Gravitation and Gravity**

**Gravity (Weight)**

*(Diagram: object of mass $m$ at height $h$ above Earth of mass $M$ and radius $R$)*

$$F = G\frac{mM}{(R+h)^2} \approx m\frac{GM}{R^2} = mg$$

$$\frac{GM}{R^2} = \frac{(6.67\times10^{-11}\ \text{m}^3/\text{kg}\cdot\text{s}^2)(5.97\times10^{24}\ \text{kg})}{(6.37\times10^6\ \text{m})^2} = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2 = g$$

- The hallmark of gravity is that every object receives the same force regardless of where it is located.
- In Korea, the force inversely proportional to the square of the distance is called *universal gravitation* (만유인력); the force that is constant regardless of distance is called *gravity* (중력).

$$W_{DA} = mg$$

## Page 106 — Areal Velocity is Constant (Kepler's Second Law)

## Page 107 — Proof that Areal Velocity is Constant

$$\frac{d\Lambda}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}r^2\frac{d\theta}{dt}\right) = r\frac{dr}{dt}\frac{d\theta}{dt} + \frac{r^2}{2}\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} = \frac{r}{2}\left(2\frac{dr}{dt}\frac{d\theta}{dt} + r\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2}\right) = 0$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \Lambda = \text{constant} \quad \textbf{Law of Equal Areas (Kepler's Second Law)}$$

## Page 108 — $\hat{r}$ Component Differential Equation

**$\hat{r}$ Component Differential Equation**

$\hat{r}$ component:

$$-\frac{GM}{r^2} = \frac{d^2r}{dt^2} - r\left(\frac{d\theta}{dt}\right)^2 \tag{1}$$

Areal velocity: $\dfrac{dS}{dt} = \dfrac{1}{2}r^2\dfrac{d\theta}{dt} \equiv \Lambda \tag{3}$

From equation (3):

$$\frac{d\theta}{dt} = \frac{2\Lambda}{r^2} \tag{4}$$

Substitute (4) into (1):

$$\frac{d^2r}{dt^2} = -\frac{GM}{r^2} + \frac{4\Lambda^2}{r^3} \tag{5}$$

## Page 113 — Newton's First Law as Stated in the Principia

**First Law as Stated in the Principia**

**LAW I.**

*Every body perseveres in its state of rest, or of uniform motion in a right line, unless it is compelled to change that state by forces impressed thereon.*

- As long as no force acts on an object, it remains at rest or moves at constant velocity in a straight line. In other words, an object moving under no net force maintains its state.

- **An object on which the net force is zero undergoes uniform (constant-velocity) motion.**

**?** The First Law appears to be just a special case ($F = 0$) of the Second Law — why was it stated as a separate law?

**Why the First Law Was Stated Separately — Reason 1**

| **Aristotle's Terrestrial Law** | **Galileo's Law of Inertia** |
|---|---|
| *(Portrait)* | *(Portrait)* |
| Moving objects eventually come to rest if left alone. | Objects do not stop because of friction, not simply because they are left alone. |

## Page 115 — Why the First Law Was Stated Separately (Reason 2)

**Why the First Law Was Stated Separately — Reason 2**

1. To describe the motion of an object, one must define a **reference frame**.
2. The choice of reference frame is arbitrary.
3. There exist reference frames in which the First Law does **not** hold.
4. A reference frame in which the First Law holds is called an **inertial frame**.
5. The Second Law also holds only in inertial frames.

- The reference frame in which the equation of motion can be applied must be confirmed to be an inertial frame using the First Law.

---

## Page 116 — How to Identify an Inertial Frame

**How to Identify an Inertial Frame**

1. Choose an object on which the net force is clearly zero.
2. If that object undergoes **uniform motion (constant velocity)**, the frame is an inertial frame.
3. Any frame that moves at constant velocity relative to an inertial frame is also an inertial frame.
4. Any frame that is not an inertial frame is a **non-inertial frame**.

## Page 117 — Equation of Motion and Inertial Frames

**Equation of Motion and Inertial Frames**

Equation of motion: $F = ma$

- Force $F$: determined by the force law; **independent of the reference frame**.
- Mass $m$: an intrinsic property of the object; **independent of the reference frame**.
- Acceleration $a$: requires a reference frame to be a numerical value; **can have different values in different reference frames**.

**The equation of motion holds only in inertial frames.**

## Page 124 — Addition Law for Position

**Addition Law for Position**

*(Diagram: reference frames A (ground) and B (moving at velocity $v$); object C (airplane) at position $x$ in A and $x'$ in B)*

$$x = x_{CA},\quad x' = x_{CB},\quad vt = x_{BA} \quad \Rightarrow \quad x_{CA} = x_{CB} + x_{BA}$$

If the origins do not coincide ($x_{BA} \neq 0$):

$$x_{CB} \neq x_{CA}$$

## Page 125 — Addition Law for Velocity

**Addition Law for Velocity**

*(Diagram: frames A and B; object C at two positions separated by $\Delta x$ in A and $\Delta x'$ in B in time $\Delta t$)*

$$\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = v_{CA},\quad \frac{\Delta x'}{\Delta t} = v_{CB},\quad v = v_{BA} \quad \Rightarrow \quad v_{CA} = v_{CB} + v_{BA}$$

If frame B moves relative to frame A ($v_{BA} \neq 0$):

$$v_{CA} \neq v_{CB}$$

## Page 126 — Addition Law for Acceleration

**Addition Law for Acceleration**

The velocity addition law holds at every instant.

At time $t$: $v_{CA} = v_{CB} + v_{BA}$ **(1)**

At time $t'$: $v_{CA}' = v_{CB}' + v_{BA}'$ **(2)**

Subtracting (1) from (2): $\Delta v_{CA} = \Delta v_{CB} + \Delta v_{BA}$

Dividing both sides by $\Delta t$:

$$\frac{\Delta v_{CA}}{\Delta t} = \frac{\Delta v_{CB}}{\Delta t} + \frac{\Delta v_{BA}}{\Delta t} \quad \Rightarrow \quad a_{CA} = a_{CB} + a_{BA}$$

If frame B accelerates relative to frame A ($a_{BA} \neq 0$):

$$a_{CA} \neq a_{CB}$$

## Page 127 — Inertial Frame and Equation of Motion

**Inertial Frame and Equation of Motion**

Acceleration addition law: $a_{CA} = a_{CB} + a_{BA}$

If frame B is an inertial frame and $a_{BA} = 0$, then frame A is also an inertial frame.

- The acceleration of an object is the same in all different inertial frames.
- If the reference frame is an inertial frame, $F = ma$ always holds.
- If the reference frame is a non-inertial frame, $F = ma$ does not hold.

- Frame A is an inertial frame: $F = ma_{CA}$ holds.
- Frame B is a non-inertial frame: $F \neq ma_{CB}$ does not hold.

Using the acceleration addition law $a_{CA} = a_{CB} + a_{BA}$:

$$F = ma_{CA} = m(a_{CB} + a_{BA}) \quad \Rightarrow \quad F - ma_{BA} = ma_{CB}$$

Equation that holds in frame B:

$$F' = ma_{CB}$$

**Adding the fictitious force $-ma_{BA}$ allows use of the equation of motion!**

This fictitious force is the **inertial force**.

## Page 129 — Inertial Force

**Inertial Force**

**Inertial force:**
- A term added to the force side to make the equation of motion hold in a non-inertial frame.
- The negative of the mass of the object multiplied by the acceleration of the non-inertial frame relative to the inertial frame.

**Inertial force is not a true force:**
- It may feel like a force, but it is not a force arising from the mutual interaction between two objects.
- No reaction can be found for it.

## Page 134 — Artificial Satellite — Frame B (Non-Inertial) Analysis and Centrifugal Force

**Artificial Satellite**

$$F = ma_{CB} = 0 \quad \times \text{ (Does NOT hold)}$$
