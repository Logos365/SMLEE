## Page 4 — Linear Momentum and Newton's Equation of Motion

**Equation of Motion** $\quad \vec{F} = m\dfrac{d\vec{v}}{dt}$

➡ **If $m$ does not change:** $\quad \vec{F} = \dfrac{d(m\vec{v})}{dt} = \dfrac{d\vec{p}}{dt}$

➡ **If $m$ changes:** $\quad \vec{F} = \dfrac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = \dfrac{dm}{dt}\vec{v} + m\dfrac{d\vec{v}}{dt}$

---

$$\vec{F} = \dfrac{d\vec{p}}{dt} \begin{cases} \longrightarrow \quad \dfrac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = m\dfrac{d\vec{v}}{dt} \quad \textcolor{red}{\textbf{if } m \textbf{ does not change}} \\[10pt] \longrightarrow \quad \dfrac{d\vec{p}}{dt} \neq m\dfrac{d\vec{v}}{dt} \quad \textcolor{red}{\textbf{if } m \textbf{ changes}} \end{cases}$$

## Page 16 — Equation of Motion

**When a force acts on an object only during the interval $\Delta t$ between $t_1$ and $t_2 = t_1 + \Delta t$:**

- If $t < t_1$, then $\quad 0 = \dfrac{\vec{dp}}{dt} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \vec{p} = \vec{p}_1 = \text{constant}$

- If $t > t_2$, then $\quad 0 = \dfrac{\vec{dp}}{dt} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \vec{p} = \vec{p}_2 = \text{constant}$

- If $t_1 < t < t_2$, then $\quad \vec{F} = \dfrac{\Delta\vec{p}}{\Delta t}$

  $\Rightarrow \quad \Delta t = t_2 - t_1 \qquad \Delta\vec{p} = \vec{p}_2 - \vec{p}_1$

  $\Rightarrow \quad$ **Even if $\vec{F}$ is not well known, $\Delta\vec{p} = \vec{p}_2 - \vec{p}_1$ can be found without difficulty!**

---

## Page 17 — Impulse

When a force acts on an object only for the duration $\Delta t$ between $t_1$ and $t_2 = t_1 + \Delta t$

$$\vec{F} = \frac{\Delta \vec{p}}{\Delta t} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \boxed{\vec{F}\Delta t} = \Delta \vec{p} = \vec{p}_2 - \vec{p}_1$$

**Impulse**

$$\Longrightarrow \quad \textbf{Impulse equals the change in linear momentum.}$$

---

## Page 18 — Units and Dimensions of Impulse and Linear Momentum

**Units of Impulse and Linear Momentum**

- Impulse $\vec{F}\Delta t$ $\Longrightarrow$ unit: **N s**
- Linear momentum $m\vec{v}$ $\Longrightarrow$ unit: **kg m/s**

$$\text{N s} = (\text{kg})(\text{m/s}^2)(\text{s}) = \text{kg m/s}$$

**Dimensions of Impulse and Linear Momentum**

- Impulse $\vec{F}\Delta t$ $\Longrightarrow$ $[F\Delta t] = [ma][t]$

$$= \text{M L T}^{-2}\text{T} = \textbf{MLT}^{-1}$$

- Linear momentum $m\vec{v}$ $\Longrightarrow$ $[mv] = \textbf{MLT}^{-1}$

## Page 19 — Example Applying Impulse (1)

A ball of mass $m = 200\text{ g}$ flying at speed $v = 2\text{ m/s}$ collides with a horizontal surface at an angle of $\theta = 30°$ from the vertical, and then bounces up at the same angle $\theta = 30°$ from the vertical after $\Delta t = 0.2\text{ s}$. If the final speed is also $v' = 2\text{ m/s}$, find the magnitude of the average force exerted by the horizontal surface on the ball.

*(Diagram: A ball approaches the horizontal surface from the upper left at angle $\theta$ from the vertical (y-axis), strikes the surface, and rebounds at angle $\theta$ from the vertical toward the upper right. The incoming velocity is labeled $v$ and the outgoing velocity is labeled $v'$. A coordinate system shows $+x$ to the right and $+y$ upward.)*

**Initial momentum**

$$\vec{p}_1 = mv(\sin\theta\,\hat{x} - \cos\theta\,\hat{y})$$

**Final momentum**

$$\vec{p}_2 = mv(\sin\theta\,\hat{x} + \cos\theta\,\hat{y})$$

➡ **Change in momentum**

$$\Delta\vec{p} = \vec{p}_2 - \vec{p}_1 = 2mv\cos\theta\,\hat{y}$$

---

## Page 20 — Example of Applying Impulse (1)

A ball of mass $m = 200\text{ g}$ flying at speed $v = 2\text{ m/s}$ collides with a horizontal surface at an angle of $\theta = 30°$ from the vertical, and after $\Delta t = 0.2\text{ s}$, bounces up at an angle of $\theta = 30°$ from the vertical. If the final speed is also $v' = 2\text{ m/s}$, find the magnitude of the average force exerted by the horizontal surface on the ball.

*(Diagram: A ball approaches a horizontal surface from the upper left at angle $\theta$ from the vertical ($+y$ axis), bounces off, and leaves at angle $\theta$ from the vertical toward the upper right, with velocity $v'$. The coordinate axes show $+y$ upward and $+x$ to the right.)*

**Change in Linear Momentum**

$$\Delta\vec{p} = \vec{p}_2 - \vec{p}_1 = 2mv\cos\theta\,\hat{y}$$

**Magnitude of Average Force** — using $\vec{F} = \dfrac{\Delta\vec{p}}{\Delta t}$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \bar{F} = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \frac{2mv}{\Delta t}\cos\theta = \frac{2\times 0.2 \times 2}{0.2}\times\cos30°\ \text{N} = 3.46\text{ N}$$

---

## Page 21 — Example Applying Impulse (2)

A stationary billiard ball of mass $m = 160\text{ g}$ is struck with a cue stick for $\Delta t = 0.02\text{ s}$ with a force of $F = 24\text{ N}$. Find the speed $v$ of the billiard ball.

*(Diagram: Photo of a cue stick striking a white billiard ball on a green billiard table)*

- **Impulse** $\quad F\Delta t$
- **Initial momentum** $\quad p_1 = 0$
- **Final momentum** $\quad p_2 = mv$

⇒ **Change in momentum** $\quad \Delta p = p_2 - p_1 = mv$

⇒ Using $F\Delta t = \Delta p = mv$

$$v = \frac{F\Delta t}{m} = \frac{24 \times 0.02}{0.16} \text{ m/s} = \mathbf{3.0 \text{ m/s}}$$

---

## Page 22 — Summary

**When a force acts on an object only during the interval $\Delta t$ between $t_1$ and $t_2 = t_1 + \Delta t$:**

$$\vec{F} = \frac{\Delta \vec{p}}{\Delta t} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \vec{F}\Delta t = \Delta \vec{p} = \vec{p}_2 - \vec{p}_1$$

The **impulse** $F\Delta t$ is difficult to determine directly, but the **change in linear momentum** $\Delta \vec{p} = \vec{p}_2 - \vec{p}_1$ is not difficult to find.

## Page 32 — One-Body Potential Energy

**The external force depends only on the coordinates of the object receiving the force.**

*(Diagram: A system boundary (shaded polygon) contains three masses $m_1$, $m_2$, and $m_3$ inside, with red arrows pointing toward the left on each mass, representing external forces acting on them. Outside the system boundary, an external mass $m_4$ is shown to the left, acting as the source of the external force.)*

- **⟹** The potential energy representing the external force also depends only on the coordinates of the **target object**.

- **⟹** The potential energy representing the external force is called the **one-body potential energy**.

---

## Page 33 — Potential Energy → Conservative Force

**Conservative Force** ⟹ **Potential Energy**

$$V(\vec{r}) = -\int_{\vec{r}_s}^{\vec{r}} \vec{F}(\vec{r}) \cdot d\vec{r}$$

**Potential Energy** ⟹ **Conservative Force**

$$\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = -\nabla V(\vec{r})$$

> **Single-body potential energy**

$\vec{F}(\vec{r})$ ⟹ **Force Law**

$$\vec{F}(\vec{r}) = -\nabla \boxed{V(\vec{r})}$$ ⟹ **Potential energy is equivalent to the force law**

---

## Page 34 — Two-Body Potential Energy

**Internal forces depend on the coordinates of both interacting objects.**

*(Diagram: A system boundary (shaded polygon) contains three masses $m_1$, $m_2$, and $m_3$ with red double-headed arrows indicating mutual interactions between each pair. Outside the boundary, a separate mass $m_4$ is shown, representing an external body not interacting within the system.)*

- ⇒ **The potential energy representing internal forces also depends on the coordinates of both interacting objects.**

- ⇒ The potential energy representing internal forces is called **two-body potential energy**.

---

## Page 35 — Potential Energy and Newton's Third Law

**Interaction between two objects 1 and 2 located at position vectors $\vec{r}_1$ and $\vec{r}_2$, respectively**

$\Rightarrow$ **Potential energy** $V(|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|)$

**Force exerted by object 2 pulling object 1:**

$$\vec{F}_{12}(\vec{r}_1) = -\nabla_1\, V(|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|) = \vec{F} \quad \textbf{Action}$$

**Force exerted by object 1 pulling object 2:** &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; **Two-body potential energy**

$$\vec{F}_{21}(\vec{r}_2) = -\nabla_2\, V(|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|) = -\vec{F} \quad \textbf{Reaction}$$

$\Rightarrow$ **The reason Newton's Third Law holds becomes clear!**

## Page 36 — Summary

Internal forces and external forces are distinguished depending on how one defines the **system**, which is the collection of objects one wishes to describe.

An **external force** depends only on the coordinates of the single object in question, whereas an **internal force** depends on the coordinates of both interacting objects.

## Page 41 — Three-Body Motion → Equations of Motion

*(Diagram: Three masses $m_1$, $m_2$, $m_3$ shown in a coordinate system with position vectors $\vec{r}_1$, $\vec{r}_2$, $\vec{r}_3$ and velocity vectors $\vec{v}_1$, $\vec{v}_2$, $\vec{v}_3$ pointing in various directions from the origin.)*

**Equation of motion for $m_1$:**

$$\vec{F}_1 + \vec{f}_{12} + \vec{f}_{13} = m_1 \frac{d^2\vec{r}_1}{dt^2}$$

**Equation of motion for $m_2$:**

$$\vec{F}_2 + \vec{f}_{21} + \vec{f}_{23} = m_2 \frac{d^2\vec{r}_2}{dt^2}$$

**Equation of motion for $m_3$:**

$$\vec{F}_3 + \vec{f}_{31} + \vec{f}_{32} = m_3 \frac{d^2\vec{r}_3}{dt^2}$$

---

**Equation of motion for $m_i$ among $N$ bodies:**

$$\vec{F}_i + \sum_{j=1}^{N} \vec{f}_{ij} = m_i \frac{d^2\vec{r}_i}{dt^2}$$

| | |
|---|---|
| $\vec{F}_i(\vec{r}_i)$ | **⟹ External force** |
| $\vec{f}_{ij}(\vec{r}_i, \vec{r}_j)$ | **⟹ Internal force** |

---

## Page 42 — Three-Body Motion → Equations of Motion

**$m_1$** $\Rightarrow$

$$\vec{F}_1(\vec{r}_1) + \vec{f}_{12}(\vec{r}_1,\vec{r}_2) + \vec{f}_{13}(\vec{r}_1,\vec{r}_3) = m_1\frac{d^2\vec{r}_1}{dt^2} \quad \textbf{(1)}$$

**$m_2$** $\Rightarrow$

$$\vec{F}_2(\vec{r}_2) + \vec{f}_{21}(\vec{r}_1,\vec{r}_2) + \vec{f}_{23}(\vec{r}_2,\vec{r}_3) = m_2\frac{d^2\vec{r}_2}{dt^2} \quad \textbf{(2)}$$

**$m_3$** $\Rightarrow$

$$\vec{F}_3(\vec{r}_3) + \vec{f}_{31}(\vec{r}_1,\vec{r}_3) + \vec{f}_{32}(\vec{r}_2,\vec{r}_3) = m_3\frac{d^2\vec{r}_3}{dt^2} \quad \textbf{(3)}$$

**Coupled differential equations** $\Rightarrow$ To find $\vec{r}_1(t)$, equations **(1), (2), (3)** must be solved **simultaneously**

$\Rightarrow$ **Introduction of new physical quantities**

> **Center of Mass** $\quad$ **Linear Momentum**

## Page 43 — Three Bodies: Case Where Internal Forces Do Not Act

$m_1$ $\Longrightarrow$ $\vec{F}_1(\vec{r}_1)$ $= m_1 \dfrac{d^2\vec{r}_1}{dt^2}$ **(1)**

$m_2$ $\Longrightarrow$ $\vec{F}_2(\vec{r}_2)$ $= m_2 \dfrac{d^2\vec{r}_2}{dt^2}$ **(2)**

$m_3$ $\Longrightarrow$ $\vec{F}_3(\vec{r}_3)$ $= m_3 \dfrac{d^2\vec{r}_3}{dt^2}$ **(3)**

**Three differential equations** $\Longrightarrow$ To find $\vec{r}_1(t)$, it suffices to solve only equation **(1)**

$\Longrightarrow$ **Equations (1), (2), (3) are all one-body problems**

$\Longrightarrow$ **If internal forces do not act, it is three separate one-body problems**

## Page 49 — Summary

- Whether the motion is treated as **one object** or **multiple objects** is determined by whether **internal forces** act between the objects.

- If only **external forces** act on an object, the position vector of that object can be found using the **equation of motion for a single object**.

- If **internal forces** also act on the objects, the **equations of motion for multiple objects must be solved simultaneously as a system**.

## Page 60 — Example: Finding the Center of Mass of a Rod

**Find the center of mass coordinate $x_{cm}$ of a rod of length $L$ whose linear mass density is $\lambda = \lambda_0 + ax$.**

*(Diagram: A horizontal rod extending from position 0 to L along the +x axis, with a small element of width $dx$ marked at position $x$. The rod is shaded darker toward the right, indicating increasing density.)*

$$dm = \lambda(x)\,dx = (\lambda_0 + ax)\,dx$$

**Total mass**

$$M = \int_0^L (\lambda_0 + ax)\,dx = \left[\lambda_0 x + \frac{ax^2}{2}\right]_0^L = \left(\lambda_0 + \frac{aL}{2}\right)L$$

$$x_{cm} = \frac{1}{M}\int_0^L x\lambda(x)\,dx = \frac{\left(\dfrac{\lambda_0}{2} + \dfrac{aL}{3}\right)L^2}{\left(\lambda_0 + \dfrac{aL}{2}\right)L} = \boxed{\dfrac{\left(1 + \dfrac{2aL}{3\lambda_0}\right)}{\left(1 + \dfrac{aL}{2\lambda_0}\right)}\dfrac{L}{2}}$$

## Page 66 — Total Momentum → Equation of Motion

**$N$ objects moving under external force $\vec{F}_i$ and internal force $\vec{f}_{ij}$**

**Equation of motion applied to the $i$-th object $m_i$:**

$$\vec{F}_i + \sum_{j=1}^{N} \vec{f}_{ij} = m_i \frac{d\vec{v}_i}{dt}$$

**Summing all equations of motion:**

$$\Rightarrow \quad \sum_{i=1}^{N} \vec{F}_i + \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N} \vec{f}_{ij}}_{\displaystyle = \ 0} = \frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(m_i \vec{v}_i)$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \boxed{\vec{F} = \frac{d\vec{P}}{dt}} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \textbf{\color{red}{Exactly the same as the single-body equation of motion!}}$$

**Sum of external forces:** $\quad \vec{F} = \displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N} \vec{F}_i$ $\qquad$ **Total momentum:** $\quad \vec{P} = \displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N} \vec{p}_i = \sum_{i=1}^{N}(m_i \vec{v}_i)$

**Where:**

$$\vec{F} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \vec{F}_i \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \textcolor{red}{\textbf{Sum of external forces}}$$

$$\vec{P} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \vec{p}_i = \sum_{i=1}^{N} m_i \vec{v}_i \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \textcolor{red}{\textbf{Total linear momentum}}$$

**Conservation of Linear Momentum**

From $\vec{F} = \dfrac{d\vec{P}}{dt}$, if $\vec{F} = 0$, then the total linear momentum $\vec{P}$ remains constant.

$$\vec{v}' = \frac{m_1\vec{v}_1 + m_2\vec{v}_2}{m_1 + m_2}$$

**Kinetic energy before collision** $\Rightarrow$ $K = \dfrac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}m_2v_2^2$

**Kinetic energy after collision** $\Rightarrow$ $K' = \dfrac{1}{2}(m_1 + m_2)\vec{v}' \cdot \vec{v}'$

**Change in kinetic energy**

$$\Rightarrow \Delta K = K' - K = -\frac{1}{2}\frac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\left|\vec{v}_1 - \vec{v}_2\right|^2 \leq 0$$

---

## Page 86 — Summary

- **Collision problems** ⟹ Linear momentum is always conserved

  ⟹ Classified as elastic or inelastic collisions depending on whether kinetic energy is conserved

- **Elastic collision** ⟹ Both linear momentum and kinetic energy are conserved

- **Inelastic collision** ⟹ Kinetic energy is lost

- **Perfectly inelastic collision** ⟹ The two objects combine and move together after the collision

  ⟹ Kinetic energy loss is maximum

## Page 91 — Conservation of Linear Momentum and Conservation of Kinetic Energy

*(Diagram: Two objects of mass $m_1$ (orange) and $m_2$ (blue) moving with velocities $v_1$ and $v_2$ before collision, colliding, then moving with velocities $v_1'$ and $v_2'$ after collision. Before collision: $P = p_1 + p_2$; After collision: $P = p_1' + p_2'$)*

**Conservation of Linear Momentum** $\Rightarrow$ $m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = m_1v_1' + m_2v_2'$ **(1)**

**Conservation of Kinetic Energy** $\Rightarrow$ $\dfrac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}m_2v_2^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}m_1{v_1'}^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}m_2{v_2'}^2$ **(2)**

**Key Trick** $\Rightarrow$ **Rearrange so that the left side contains the first object's terms and the right side contains the second object's terms**

**(1)** $\Rightarrow$ $m_1(v_1 - v_1') = -m_2(v_2 - v_2')$ **(3)**

**(2)** $\Rightarrow$ $m_1(v_1^2 - {v_1'}^2) = -m_2(v_2^2 - {v_2'}^2)$ **(4)**

---

## Page 92 — Velocities of Two Objects After Collision

$$m_1(v_1 - v_1') = -m_2(v_2 - v_2') \quad \textbf{(3)}$$

$$m_1(v_1^2 - v_1'^2) = -m_2(v_2^2 - v_2'^2) \quad \textbf{(4)}$$

**Dividing equation (4) by equation (3)** $\Longrightarrow$ $v_1 + v_1' = v_2 + v_2'$ **(5)**

**Equation (3) + $m_1 \times$ equation (5)**

$$\Longrightarrow \quad 2m_1 v_1 = (m_1 - m_2)v_2 + (m_1 + m_2)v_2'$$

$$\Longrightarrow \quad v_2' = \frac{2m_1}{m_1 + m_2}v_1 - \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}v_2$$

## Page 93 — Velocities of Two Objects After Collision

$$m_1(v_1 - v_1') = -m_2(v_2 - v_2') \quad \textbf{(3)}$$

$$m_1(v_1^2 - v_1'^2) = -m_2(v_2^2 - v_2'^2) \quad \textbf{(4)}$$

$$v_1' = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}v_1 + \frac{2m_2}{m_1 + m_2}v_2$$

$$v_2' = \frac{2m_1}{m_1 + m_2}v_1 - \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}v_2$$

**Dividing equation (4) by equation (3):**

$$\Rightarrow \quad v_1 + v_1' = v_2 + v_2' \quad \textbf{(5)}$$

**Equation (3) $- m_2 \times$ Equation (5):**

$$\Rightarrow \quad (m_1 - m_2)v_1 - (m_1 + m_2)v_1' = -2m_2 v_2$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad v_1' = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}v_1 + \frac{2m_2}{m_1 + m_2}v_2$$

## Page 94 — Special Case: $m_1 \gg m_2$

*(Diagram: Two-object elastic collision shown in three stages — before collision (masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ moving with velocities $v_1$ and $v_2$), during collision (impact), and after collision (masses moving with velocities $v_1'$ and $v_2'$). ## Page 125 — 2D Elastic Collision → Laboratory Reference Frame

*(Diagram: Two-particle collision scenario. Before collision: mass $m_1$ moves to the right with velocity $\vec{v}_1$, mass $m_2$ is at rest ($\vec{v}_2 = 0$). **Laws to Apply**

- **Conservation of linear momentum — $x$-axis component:**

$$m_1 v_1 = m_1 v_1' \cos\phi_1 + m_2 v_2' \cos\phi_2 \quad \textbf{(1)}$$

- **Conservation of linear momentum — $y$-axis component:**

$$0 = m_1 v_1' \sin\phi_1 - m_2 v_2' \sin\phi_2 \quad \textbf{(2)}$$

- **Conservation of kinetic energy:**

$$\frac{1}{2}mv_1^2 = \frac{1}{2}m_1 v_1'^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2 v_2'^2 \quad \textbf{(3)}$$

## Page 128 — 2D Elastic Collision Example

*(Diagram: Before collision — orange ball moving right with velocity $v_1 = v$, gray ball at rest with $v_2 = 0$. After collision — orange ball deflects upward at angle $\phi_1 = 45°$ with speed $v_1'$, gray ball deflects downward at angle $\phi_2 = \phi$ with speed $v_2'$.)*

**Laws to Apply**

$$mv = mv_1'\cos45° + mv_2'\cos\phi$$

$$0 = mv_1'\sin45° - mv_2'\sin\phi$$

$$\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}m\!\left(v_1'^2 + v_2'^2\right)$$

$\Rightarrow$

$$v_1' + \sqrt{2}\,v_2'\cos\phi = \sqrt{2}\,v \quad \textbf{(1)}$$

$$v_1' - \sqrt{2}\,v_2'\sin\phi = 0 \quad \textbf{(2)}$$

$$v_1'^2 + v_2'^2 = v^2 \quad \textbf{(3)}$$

## Page 129 — 2D Elastic Collision Example

*(Diagram: Two balls shown before and after collision. Before collision: ball 1 moves with velocity $v_1 = v$ to the right, ball 2 is stationary with $v_2 = 0$. After collision: ball 1 deflects upward at angle $\phi_1 = 45°$ with speed $v_1'$, ball 2 deflects downward at angle $\phi_2 = \phi$ with speed $v_2'$.)*

$$v_1' + \sqrt{2}\, v_2'\cos\phi = \sqrt{2}\, v \quad \textbf{(1)}$$

$$v_1' - \sqrt{2}\, v_2'\sin\phi = 0 \quad \textbf{(2)}$$

$$v_1'^2 + v_2'^2 = v^2 \quad \textbf{(3)}$$

**Solving equations (1) and (2) simultaneously:**

$$\Rightarrow \quad v_1' = \frac{\sqrt{2}\, v\sin\phi}{\sin\phi + \cos\phi} \qquad v_2' = \frac{v}{\sin\phi + \cos\phi} \quad \textbf{(4)}$$

**Substituting result (4) into equation (3):**

$$\Rightarrow \quad 2v^2\sin^2\phi + v^2 = v^2(\sin\phi + \cos\phi)^2$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \sin\phi = \cos\phi \quad \therefore \quad \phi = 45° \quad \textbf{(5)}$$

## Page 130 — 2D Elastic Collision Example

*(Diagram: Two balls shown before and after collision. Before collision: ball 1 moves with $v_1 = v$ to the right, ball 2 is stationary with $v_2 = 0$. After collision: ball 1 deflects at angle $\phi_1 = 45°$ above the horizontal with speed $v_1'$, ball 2 moves at angle $\phi_2 = \phi$ below the horizontal with speed $v_2'$.)*

$$v_1' = \frac{\sqrt{2}\,v\sin\phi}{\sin\phi + \cos\phi} \qquad \textbf{(4)}$$

$$v_2' = \frac{v}{\sin\phi + \cos\phi}$$

$$\phi = 45° \qquad \textbf{(5)}$$

**Substituting result (5) into result (4):**

$$v_1' = \frac{\sqrt{2}\,v\sin45°}{\sin45° + \cos45°} = \frac{v}{\sqrt{2}} \qquad v_2' = \frac{v}{\sin45° + \cos45°} = \frac{v}{\sqrt{2}}$$

**Kinetic energy of the two balls after the collision:**

$$K_1' = \frac{1}{2}m{v_1'}^2 = \frac{1}{4}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}K \qquad K_2' = \frac{1}{2}m{v_2'}^2 = \frac{1}{4}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}K$$
