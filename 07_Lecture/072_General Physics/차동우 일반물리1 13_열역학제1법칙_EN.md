## Page 2 — Heat → Thermal Energy

**Heat → Thermal Energy**

A box composed of $N$ small spheres; box mass $M$, sphere mass $m$

$$M = Nm$$

**Just before the box hits the floor** — velocity of the box: $v$

- Box: $K_1 = \dfrac{1}{2}Mv^2$
- Spheres: $K_2 = N \times \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$

$$K_1 = K_2$$

**Just after the box hits the floor** — velocity of the box is 0

- Box: $K_1 = 0$
- Spheres: $K_2 = \dfrac{1}{2}\sum mv_i^2 \neq 0$

$$K_1 \neq K_2$$

The kinetic energy transforms into **kinetic energy of disordered (random) motion**.

## Page 40 — Heat Capacity of an Object

**Heat Capacity of an Object (Heat Capacity)**

If heat $Q$ is required to raise the temperature of an object by $\Delta T$, then the **heat capacity** of the object is:

$$C = \frac{Q}{\Delta T}$$

**Unit of heat capacity:**

- SI unit: **J/K**
- Also: J/°C, cal/K, cal/°C

## Page 61 — Boyle–Charles Law (Derivation)

**Boyle–Charles Law**

Consider four states: State 1 $(P_1, V_1, T_1)$, State 2 $(P_2, V_2, T_2)$, State 3 $(P_3, V_3, T_3)$, State 4 $(P_4, V_4, T_4)$

- If $T_1 = T_2$: Boyle's law gives $P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$
- If $P_2 = P_3$: Charles's law gives $\dfrac{V_2}{T_2} = \dfrac{V_3}{T_3}$
- If $V_3 = V_4$: Gay-Lussac's law gives $\dfrac{P_3}{T_3} = \dfrac{P_4}{T_4}$

Combining:

$$\frac{P_1V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2V_2}{T_2} = \frac{P_3V_3}{T_3} = \frac{P_4V_4}{T_4}$$

$$\boxed{\frac{PV}{T} = c} \quad \textbf{Boyle–Charles Law}$$

## Page 77 — Total Kinetic Energy of Gas Molecules in a Box

**Total Kinetic Energy of Gas Molecules in the Box**

## Page 81 — Derivation of the Ideal Gas Equation of State

**Derivation of the Ideal Gas Equation of State**

$$\boxed{PV = Nk_BT}$$

## Page 96 — First Law of Thermodynamics

**First Law of Thermodynamics**

Initial state of the thermodynamic system: pressure $P$, volume $V$, temperature $T$, internal energy $E$

Final state of the thermodynamic system: pressure $P'$, volume $V'$, temperature $T'$, internal energy $E'$

Change in internal energy:

$$\Delta E = E' - E = Q - W$$

## Page 115 — Isochoric Molar Heat Capacity of an Ideal Gas (Setup)

**Isochoric Molar Heat Capacity of an Ideal Gas**

Internal energy $E$ of $n$ mol of ideal gas at absolute temperature $T$, pressure $P$, volume $V$:

$$E = \frac{3}{2}nRT$$

**Heat $Q$ is added with volume fixed:**

Internal energy $E'$ of $n$ mol of ideal gas changed to absolute temperature $T'$, pressure $P'$, volume $V' = V$:

$$E' = \frac{3}{2}nRT'$$

First Law of Thermodynamics:

$$\Delta E = E' - E = Q - W = Q \quad (\text{since } \Delta V = V' - V = 0 \Rightarrow W = 0)$$

*(Section marker: 13J)*

## Page 116 — Isochoric Molar Heat Capacity of an Ideal Gas (Derivation)

**Isochoric Molar Heat Capacity of an Ideal Gas**

$$E = \frac{3}{2}nRT \qquad E' = \frac{3}{2}nRT' \qquad E' - E = Q$$

$$\frac{3}{2}nRT' - \frac{3}{2}nRT = \frac{3}{2}nR\Delta T = Q$$

Isochoric molar heat capacity:

$$c_V = \frac{Q}{n\Delta T} = \frac{3}{2}R$$

*(Section marker: 13J)*

## Page 117 — Isobaric Molar Heat Capacity of an Ideal Gas (Setup)

**Isobaric Molar Heat Capacity of an Ideal Gas**

Internal energy $E$ of $n$ mol of ideal gas at absolute temperature $T$, pressure $P$, volume $V$:

$$E = \frac{3}{2}nRT$$

**Heat $Q$ is added with pressure fixed:**

Internal energy $E'$ of $n$ mol of ideal gas changed to absolute temperature $T'$, pressure $P' = P$, volume $V'$:

$$E' = \frac{3}{2}nRT'$$

First Law of Thermodynamics:

$$\Delta E = E' - E = Q - W = Q - P\Delta V$$

Ideal gas equation of state: $PV = nRT \Rightarrow P\Delta V = nR\Delta T$

*(Section marker: 13J)*

## Page 118 — Isobaric Molar Heat Capacity of an Ideal Gas (Derivation)

**Isobaric Molar Heat Capacity of an Ideal Gas**

$$E = \frac{3}{2}nRT \qquad E' = \frac{3}{2}nRT' \qquad P\Delta V = nR\Delta T$$

$$E' - E = Q - P\Delta V$$

$$\frac{3}{2}nRT' - \frac{3}{2}nRT = \frac{3}{2}nR\Delta T = Q - nR\Delta T$$

$$\frac{5}{2}nR\Delta T = Q$$

Isobaric molar heat capacity:

$$c_P = \frac{Q}{n\Delta T} = \frac{5}{2}R$$

**Summary**

**Empirical thermophysics:**

- Difference between isobaric and isochoric molar heat capacities of gases:

$$c_P - c_V = 8.1 \sim 8.5 \text{ J/(mol·K)} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{gas constant}$$

**Fundamental law (statistical mechanics):**

- Isochoric molar heat capacity of an ideal gas: $c_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$
- Isobaric molar heat capacity of an ideal gas: $c_P = \dfrac{5}{2}R$

$$c_P - c_V = R = N_Ak_B = 8.31 \text{ J/(mol·K)}$$

The origin of the gas constant is now understood.
