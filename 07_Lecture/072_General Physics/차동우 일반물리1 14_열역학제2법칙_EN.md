## Page 3 — Classification of Thermodynamic Processes (Slide 2)

**Thermodynamic state of a system** — represented by the pressure, volume, and temperature of the system.

**Thermodynamic process** — a process in which the system changes from one state to another by exchanging heat and doing work with the surroundings.

| Process | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Isobaric process** | A thermodynamic process in which pressure $P$ is kept constant |
| **Isochoric process** | A thermodynamic process in which volume $V$ is kept constant |
| **Isothermal process** | A thermodynamic process in which temperature $T$ is kept constant |
| **Adiabatic process** | A thermodynamic process in which no heat $Q$ is exchanged with the surroundings |

---

## Page 4 — Isobaric Process (Slide 3)

A thermodynamic process in which pressure $P$ is kept constant while the volume changes from $V_i$ to $V_f$.

*(Diagram: P-V diagram showing a horizontal arrow at constant pressure $P$ from $V_i$ to $V_f$, with the rectangular area underneath shaded.)*

**Work done by the thermodynamic system on the surroundings:**

$$W = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} P\,dV = P \int_{V_i}^{V_f} dV = P(V_f - V_i) = P\Delta V$$

---

## Page 5 — Isochoric Process (Slide 4)

A thermodynamic process in which volume $V$ is kept constant while pressure changes from $P_i$ to $P_f$.

*(Diagram: P-V diagram showing a vertical downward arrow at constant volume $V$, from pressure $P_i$ to $P_f$.)*

**Work done by the thermodynamic system on the surroundings:**

$$W = \int_{V_i}^{V_f = V_i} P\,dV = 0$$

**First Law of Thermodynamics:**

$$\Delta E = Q - \underbrace{W}_{=0} = Q \implies \Delta E = Q$$

---

## Page 6 — Isochoric Process (continued) (Slide 5)

**Initial state** $\longrightarrow$ **Final state**

$n,\, V,\, P_i,\, T_i \quad \longrightarrow \quad n,\, V,\, P_f,\, T_f$

- **Isochoric process:** $\Delta E = Q$
- **Heat absorbed by the system:** $Q = nc_V \Delta T = nc_V(T_f - T_i)$
- **Change in internal energy:** $\Delta E = Q = nc_V(T_f - T_i)$
- **Ideal gas:** $\Delta E = \dfrac{3}{2}nR(T_f - T_i)$

---

## Page 7 — Isothermal Process (Slide 6)

A thermodynamic process in which temperature $T$ is kept constant while both pressure and volume change from $P_i,\,V_i$ to $P_f,\,V_f$.

*(Diagram: P-V diagram showing a hyperbolic curve labeled $T$ from $(V_i, P_i)$ to $(V_f, P_f)$.)*

**Work done by the thermodynamic system on the surroundings:**

$$W = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} P\,dV$$

For an **ideal gas**, using $E = \dfrac{3}{2}nRT$ and $PV = nRT$.

---

## Page 8 — Isothermal Process of an Ideal Gas (Slide 7)

**Work done by the thermodynamic system on the surroundings:**

From $PV = nRT \Rightarrow P = \dfrac{nRT}{V}$:

$$W = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} P\,dV = nRT \int_{V_i}^{V_f} \frac{dV}{V} = nRT \ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}$$

**First Law of Thermodynamics:** $E = \dfrac{3}{2}nRT \Rightarrow \Delta E = \dfrac{3}{2}nR\Delta T = 0$

$$\Delta E = Q - W = 0$$

$$\boxed{Q = W = nRT \ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}}$$

---

## Page 9 — Adiabatic Process (Slide 8)

A thermodynamic process in which the system is thermally isolated from the surroundings: $Q = 0$.

**First Law of Thermodynamics:**

$$\Delta E = Q - W = -W$$

| | Description | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Adiabatic expansion** | Volume increases; system does work on the surroundings; internal energy decreases | Temperature drops |
| **Adiabatic compression** | Volume decreases; surroundings do work on the system; internal energy increases | Temperature rises |

---

## Page 10 — Adiabatic Expansion or Compression of an Ideal Gas (Slide 9)

**Adiabatic expansion or compression:** $Q = 0$

**Ideal gas:** $E = \dfrac{3}{2}nRT$, $\ PV = nRT = n(c_P - c_V)T$

$$\Rightarrow dE = nc_V\,dT$$

$$PdV + VdP = n(c_P - c_V)dT \quad \cdots (1)$$

**First Law of Thermodynamics:** $\Delta E = Q - W = Q - P\Delta V$

$$\Rightarrow dE = -P\,dV = nc_V\,dT \quad \cdots (2)$$

Substituting (2) into (1):

$$\frac{PdV + VdP}{(c_P - c_V)} = -\frac{PdV}{c_V}$$

---

## Page 11 — Adiabatic Expansion or Compression of an Ideal Gas (continued) (Slide 10)

From the previous result:

$$c_V(PdV + VdP) = -(c_P - c_V)PdV$$
$$c_V VdP = -c_P PdV$$

$$\Rightarrow \frac{dP}{P} + \frac{c_P}{c_V}\frac{dV}{V} = 0 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \frac{dP}{P} + \gamma\frac{dV}{V} = 0 \qquad \boxed{\gamma = \frac{c_P}{c_V}}$$

Integrating:

$$\int_{P_i}^{P_f} \frac{dP}{P} + \gamma \int_{V_i}^{V_f} \frac{dV}{V} = 0 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \ln\frac{P_f}{P_i} + \gamma\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i} = 0$$

$$\left(\frac{P_f}{P_i}\right)\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right)^\gamma = 1 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad P_i V_i^\gamma = P_f V_f^\gamma \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \boxed{PV^\gamma = \text{const}}$$

---

## Page 12 — Free Expansion of an Ideal Gas (Slide 11)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Adiabatic expansion** | $Q = 0 \Rightarrow \Delta E = -W$, $\ PV^\gamma = \text{const}$ |
| **Free expansion** | $Q = 0,\ W = 0 \Rightarrow \Delta E = 0$, $\ PV = \text{const}$ |

*(Diagram: Two bulbs connected by a valve — one side contains gas (represented by dots), the other is a vacuum. After the valve opens, gas expands freely to fill both bulbs.)*

**Irreversible process:**

$$\Delta E = 0,\quad \Delta T = 0,\quad T_i = T_f = T$$

$$P_i V_i = nRT,\quad P_f V_f = nRT \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad PV = \text{const}$$

---

## Page 15 — Work and Energy (Slide 14)

**Work** — When A continuously exerts a force $\vec{F}$ on B and moves B by a displacement $\vec{d}$, the work done by A on B is $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d}$.

**Energy** — the ability to do work.

**Work and energy:**
- Work: We say that A does $W$ amount of work on B.
- Energy: We say A's energy is $E$.

**When A does work on B:**

A's energy decreases by the amount of work done, and B's energy increases by the amount of work received.

## Page 20 — Properties of Thermal Energy (Slide 19)

**Hot object:** When a hot object is placed in contact with another object, it only increases the disordered kinetic energy of the constituent particles of the other object and cannot move the object as a whole.

- A hot object with a large amount of thermal energy cannot do work by itself.
- When energy converts from one form to another and becomes thermal energy, it becomes useless energy that can no longer be utilized.

## Page 27 — Cyclic Process of a Heat Engine (Slide 26)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Heat engine** | A device that converts thermal energy into mechanical energy, making it available as work |
| **Working substance** | Receives heat from a hot reservoir and transfers it to a cold reservoir; part of the thermal energy is converted to work by exploiting the substance's thermodynamic properties |
| **Cyclic process** | A heat engine passes through several thermodynamic processes and returns to its original state; this return to the original thermodynamic state is called a cyclic process |
| **Internal energy change after one cycle** | $\Delta E = Q - W = 0 \Rightarrow W = Q$ |

## Page 30 — Operating Principles of a Heat Engine (Slide 29)

*(Diagram: Schematic showing hot reservoir at temperature $T_H$ at the top, cold reservoir at $T_C$ at the bottom. Heat $Q_H$ flows down from the hot reservoir into the engine; work $W$ flows out to the right; heat $Q_C$ flows down into the cold reservoir.)*

1. The heat engine absorbs heat $Q_H$ from the hot reservoir at high temperature $T_H$.
2. Part of the absorbed heat, $Q_C$, spontaneously flows out to the cold reservoir at low temperature $T_C$.
3. The work done by the heat engine on the surroundings during one cycle is $W = Q_H + Q_C$, where $Q_H > 0$, $Q_C < 0$, $W > 0$.

---

## Page 31 — Efficiency of a Heat Engine (Slide 30)

*(Diagram: Same engine schematic as above.)*

- **Energy input to the heat engine:** heat absorbed from the hot reservoir, $Q_H$
- **Work done on the surroundings:** $W = Q_H + Q_C$
- **Efficiency of the heat engine:**

$$\epsilon = \frac{W}{Q_H} = \frac{Q_H + Q_C}{Q_H} < 1$$

> **The efficiency of a heat engine is always less than 1.**
>
> **A heat engine with 100% efficiency does not exist.**

---

## Page 32 — Operating Principles of a Refrigerator (Slide 31)

*(Diagram: Schematic showing hot reservoir at $T_H$ at the top, cold reservoir at $T_C$ at the bottom. Heat $Q_H$ flows upward into the hot reservoir; work $W$ is input from the right; heat $Q_C$ flows upward from the cold reservoir into the device.)*

1. A refrigerator absorbs heat $Q_C$ from the cold reservoir at low temperature $T_C$.
2. The refrigerator receives work $W$ from the surroundings and releases heat $Q_H$ into the hot reservoir at high temperature $T_H$.
3. The work received by the refrigerator from the surroundings during one cycle is $W = Q_H + Q_C$, where $Q_H < 0$, $Q_C > 0$, $W < 0$.

---

## Page 33 — Coefficient of Performance of a Refrigerator (Slide 32)

*(Diagram: Same refrigerator schematic.)*

- **Heat removed by the refrigerator:** $Q_H$ released to the hot reservoir
- **Work received by the refrigerator from the surroundings:** $W = Q_H + Q_C$
- **Coefficient of performance (COP) of a refrigerator:**

$$\epsilon = \frac{|W|}{|Q_H|} = \frac{|Q_H + Q_C|}{|Q_H|} \neq 0$$

> The smaller $|W|$ is and the larger $|Q_H|$ is, i.e., the smaller $\epsilon$ is, the better the refrigerator.
>
> **A refrigerator with COP = 0% does not exist.**

## Page 38 — Efficiency of Heat Engines and Coefficient of Performance of Refrigerators (Slide 37)

**Efficiency of a heat engine:**

$$\epsilon = \frac{W}{Q_H} = \frac{Q_H + Q_C}{Q_H}$$

- If $Q_C = 0$ then $\epsilon = 1$, but such a heat engine can never be built.

**Coefficient of performance of a refrigerator:**

$$\epsilon = \frac{|W|}{|Q_H|} = \frac{|Q_H + Q_C|}{|Q_H|}$$

- If $|Q_C| = |Q_H|$ then $\epsilon = 0$, but such a refrigerator can never be built.

**Carnot engine** — the ideal heat engine that operates on a Carnot cycle using an ideal gas, achieving the maximum possible efficiency.

## Page 41 — Carnot Cycle and Carnot Engine (Slide 40)

*(Diagram: P-V diagram of the Carnot cycle — a closed loop with four labeled states (1, 2, 3, 4). The upper isothermal expansion at $T_H$ connects states 1 and 2; an adiabatic expansion connects 2 to 3 at $T_C$; isothermal compression at $T_C$ connects 3 to 4; adiabatic compression returns from 4 to 1. Heat $Q_H$ enters at the top, work $W$ is output, and heat $Q_C$ is rejected at the bottom.)*

**Carnot cycle:**
- Step 1 ($1 \to 2$): Isothermal expansion at $T_H$
- Step 2 ($2 \to 3$): Adiabatic expansion
- Step 3 ($3 \to 4$): Isothermal compression at $T_C$
- Step 4 ($4 \to 1$): Adiabatic compression

**Carnot engine** — a heat engine that operates on the Carnot cycle using an ideal gas as the working substance.

## Page 42 — Isothermal Process of an Ideal Gas (Slide 41)

**Work done by the thermodynamic system on the surroundings** (from $PV = nRT$, so $P = \dfrac{nRT}{V}$):

$$W = \int_{V_i}^{V_f} P\,dV = nRT \int_{V_i}^{V_f} \frac{dV}{V} = nRT \ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}$$

**First Law of Thermodynamics:** $E = \dfrac{3}{2}nRT \Rightarrow \Delta E = \dfrac{3}{2}nR\Delta T = 0$

$$\Delta E = Q - W = 0 \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad Q = W = nRT \ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}$$

*(This is the result for the isothermal process of an ideal gas, derived in Section 14A.)*

---

## Page 43 — Isothermal and Adiabatic Processes of an Ideal Gas (Slide 42)

**Isothermal process:** $PV = nRT \Rightarrow PV = \text{const}$

**Adiabatic process:** $PV^\gamma = \text{const}$

---

## Page 44 — Step 1: Isothermal Expansion (Slide 43)

*(Diagram: Carnot P-V diagram with states 1 and 2 on the $T_H$ isotherm highlighted.)*

Since this is an isothermal expansion, the change in internal energy is zero:

$$\Delta E_1 = Q_H - W_1 = 0$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad Q_H = W_1 = nRT_H \ln\frac{V_2}{V_1} > 0$$

$$P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2 = a$$

---

## Page 45 — Step 2: Adiabatic Expansion (Slide 44)

*(Diagram: Carnot P-V diagram with states 2 and 3 on the adiabatic expansion highlighted.)*

Since this is an adiabatic expansion, no heat is exchanged and internal energy decreases:

$$\Delta E_2 = -W_2 < 0$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad P_2 V_2^\gamma = P_3 V_3^\gamma$$

---

## Page 46 — Step 3: Isothermal Compression (Slide 45)

*(Diagram: Carnot P-V diagram with states 3 and 4 on the $T_C$ isotherm highlighted.)*

Since this is an isothermal compression, the change in internal energy is zero:

$$\Delta E_3 = Q_C - W_3 = 0$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad Q_C = W_3 = nRT_C \ln\frac{V_4}{V_3} < 0$$

$$P_3 V_3 = P_4 V_4 = b$$

---

## Page 47 — Step 4: Adiabatic Compression (Slide 46)

*(Diagram: Carnot P-V diagram with states 4 and 1 on the adiabatic compression highlighted.)*

Since this is an adiabatic compression, no heat is exchanged and internal energy increases:

$$\Delta E_4 = -W_4 > 0$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad P_4 V_4^\gamma = P_1 V_1^\gamma$$

---

## Page 48 — Efficiency of the Carnot Engine (Slide 47)

From the four steps:

$$\Delta E_1 = Q_H - W_1 = 0 \qquad P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2 = a \quad (1)$$
$$\Delta E_2 = -W_2 < 0 \qquad P_2 V_2^\gamma = P_3 V_3^\gamma \quad (2)$$
$$\Delta E_3 = Q_C - W_3 = 0 \qquad P_3 V_3 = P_4 V_4 = b \quad (3)$$
$$\Delta E_4 = -W_4 > 0 \qquad P_4 V_4^\gamma = P_1 V_1^\gamma \quad (4)$$

$$Q_H = W_1 = nRT_H \ln\frac{V_2}{V_1} \qquad Q_C = W_3 = nRT_C \ln\frac{V_4}{V_3}$$

From (2): $a V_2^{\gamma-1} = b V_3^{\gamma-1} \Rightarrow \dfrac{a}{b} = \left(\dfrac{V_3}{V_2}\right)^{\gamma-1}$

From (4): $b V_4^{\gamma-1} = a V_1^{\gamma-1} \Rightarrow \dfrac{a}{b} = \left(\dfrac{V_4}{V_1}\right)^{\gamma-1}$

$$\therefore \quad \frac{V_3}{V_2} = \frac{V_4}{V_1} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \frac{V_1}{V_2} = \frac{V_4}{V_3} = \left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^{-1} \cdot \frac{V_4}{V_3} \cdot \ldots$$

$$\boxed{\frac{V_3}{V_2} = \frac{V_4}{V_1}}$$

---

## Page 49 — Efficiency of the Carnot Engine (continued) (Slide 48)

Using $Q_H = nRT_H\ln\dfrac{V_2}{V_1}$ and $Q_C = nRT_C\ln\dfrac{V_4}{V_3}$, and the result $\dfrac{V_1}{V_2} = \dfrac{V_4}{V_3}$:

$$\text{Efficiency} \quad \epsilon = \frac{Q_H + Q_C}{Q_H} = 1 + \frac{Q_C}{Q_H} = 1 + \frac{T_C \ln\!\left(\dfrac{V_2}{V_1}\right)^{-1}}{T_H \ln\dfrac{V_2}{V_1}}$$

$$= 1 - \frac{T_C \ln\dfrac{V_2}{V_1}}{T_H \ln\dfrac{V_2}{V_1}} = \boxed{1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}}$$

---

## Page 54 — Second Law of Thermodynamics (Slide 53)

Thermal phenomena that cannot be explained by the First Law of Thermodynamics:

- Heat spontaneously flows from a high-temperature body to a low-temperature body.
- A heat engine with 100% efficiency cannot be made.

The **Second Law of Thermodynamics** is needed.

19th-century scientists worked to find a physical quantity that could concisely describe the Second Law.

The German physicist Clausius proposed **entropy** as the physical quantity to describe the Second Law of Thermodynamics.

## Page 58 — Clausius's Entropy (Slide 57)

**Carnot cycle steps:**
- Step 1 ($1 \to 2$): Isothermal expansion
- Step 2 ($2 \to 3$): Adiabatic expansion
- Step 3 ($3 \to 4$): Isothermal compression
- Step 4 ($4 \to 1$): Adiabatic compression

**Efficiency of the Carnot engine:**

$$\epsilon = 1 + \frac{Q_C}{Q_H} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$$

$$\frac{Q_C}{Q_H} = -\frac{T_C}{T_H} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad \frac{Q_C}{T_C} + \frac{Q_H}{T_H} = 0$$

**Change in entropy:**

$$\Delta S \equiv \frac{Q}{T}$$

## Page 59 — Clausius's Entropy (continued) (Slide 58)

**Carnot cycle — entropy change at each step:**

- Step 1 ($1 \to 2$, isothermal expansion): $\Delta S_1 = \dfrac{Q_H}{T_H} > 0$
- Step 2 ($2 \to 3$, adiabatic expansion): $\Delta S_2 = 0$
- Step 3 ($3 \to 4$, isothermal compression): $\Delta S_3 = \dfrac{Q_C}{T_C} < 0$
- Step 4 ($4 \to 1$, adiabatic compression): $\Delta S_4 = 0$

$$\Delta S = \Delta S_1 + \Delta S_2 + \Delta S_3 + \Delta S_4 = \frac{Q_H}{T_H} + \frac{Q_C}{T_C} = 0$$

> **After one complete cycle, the total entropy change is zero.**

## Page 60 — Clausius's Entropy (Slide 59)

Clausius's entropy is defined not by its absolute value, but by its **change**.

When a thermodynamic system at absolute temperature $T$ absorbs heat $Q$, the entropy of that system increases by $\Delta S = Q/T$.

After one complete cyclic process, the entropy change is $\Delta S = 0$.

In an **irreversible process in an isolated system**, the entropy change satisfies $\Delta S > 0$.

Heat flowing from high temperature to low temperature is the direction in which entropy does not decrease.

## Page 61 — Direction of Spontaneous Heat Flow (Slide 60)

**Problem:** If object A at absolute temperature $T_1$ and object B at absolute temperature $T_2$ are placed in contact, and $T_1 > T_2$, show that heat flows from A to B.

**Case 1 — Heat $Q$ flows from A to B:**

$$\Delta S_A = \frac{-Q}{T_1}, \qquad \Delta S_B = \frac{Q}{T_2}$$

$$\Delta S = \Delta S_A + \Delta S_B = -\frac{Q}{T_1} + \frac{Q}{T_2} = Q\,\frac{T_1 - T_2}{T_1 T_2} > 0 \quad \checkmark$$

**Case 2 — Heat $Q$ flows from B to A:**

$$\Delta S_A = \frac{Q}{T_1}, \qquad \Delta S_B = \frac{-Q}{T_2}$$

$$\Delta S = \Delta S_A + \Delta S_B = \frac{Q}{T_1} - \frac{Q}{T_2} = Q\,\frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1 T_2} < 0 \quad \times$$

Since entropy cannot decrease in an isolated system, only Case 1 is permitted — heat flows from high to low temperature.

---

## Page 62 — Summary: Second Law and Entropy (Slide 61)

**Second Law of Thermodynamics:**
- Heat that flows spontaneously always moves from a high-temperature body to a low-temperature body.
- A heat engine with 100% efficiency cannot be made.

**Clausius's Entropy:**
- When a thermodynamic system at absolute temperature $T$ absorbs heat $Q$, the entropy of that system increases by $\Delta S = Q/T$.

**Second Law of Thermodynamics expressed in terms of entropy:**
- The thermodynamic processes of an isolated system proceed in the direction in which entropy does not decrease.

## Page 109 — Volume of an $N$-Dimensional Sphere (Slide 109)

**Volume of an $N$-dimensional sphere of radius $R$** (using the Gamma function):

$$V_N(R) = \frac{\pi^{N/2}}{\Gamma\!\left(\dfrac{N}{2} + 1\right)} R^N$$

where $\Gamma(n) = (n-1)!$ for positive integers, and $\Gamma\!\left(n + \dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \left(n - \dfrac{1}{2}\right)\!\left(n - \dfrac{3}{2}\right)\cdots\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\pi^{1/2}$ for non-negative integers.

- **1D:** $V_1(R) = \dfrac{\pi^{1/2}}{\Gamma(3/2)} R = \dfrac{\pi^{1/2}}{(1/2)\pi^{1/2}} R = 2R$
- **2D:** $V_2(R) = \dfrac{\pi^{2/2}}{\Gamma(2)} R^2 = \dfrac{\pi}{1} R^2 = \pi R^2$

---

## Page 110 — Volume of an $N$-Dimensional Sphere (continued) (Slide 110)

- **3D:** $V_3(R) = \dfrac{\pi^{3/2}}{\Gamma(5/2)} R^3 = \dfrac{\pi\pi^{1/2}}{(3/2)(1/2)\pi^{1/2}} R^3 = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3$
- **4D:** $V_4(R) = \dfrac{\pi^{4/2}}{\Gamma(3)} R^4 = \dfrac{\pi^2}{2} R^4$

## Page 111 — Phase-Space Volume for $N$ Ideal-Gas Molecules with Energy $\leq E$ (Slide 111)

Internal energy of an ideal gas of $N$ molecules of mass $m$:

$$E = \frac{p_1^2}{2m} + \frac{p_2^2}{2m} + \cdots + \frac{p_N^2}{2m} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad 2mE = \sum_{i=1}^{N} p_i^2$$

Each $p_i^2 = p_{ix}^2 + p_{iy}^2 + p_{iz}^2$, so:

$$2mE = \sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(p_{ix}^2 + p_{iy}^2 + p_{iz}^2\right) \qquad \text{(total of } 3N \text{ terms)}$$

$$R = \sqrt{2mE} = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(p_{ix}^2 + p_{iy}^2 + p_{iz}^2\right)} \qquad \Rightarrow \quad \text{radius of sphere in } 3N\text{-dimensional momentum space}$$

## Page 112 — Phase-Space Volume for $N$ Ideal-Gas Molecules with Energy $\leq E$ (Slide 112)

The $6N$-dimensional phase-space volume $\tilde{\Omega}_{6N}$ representing $N$ ideal-gas molecules with volume $V$ and energy $\leq E$:

$$\tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E) = \frac{\pi^{3N/2}}{\left(\dfrac{3N}{2}\right)!}(2mE)^{3N/2}\, V^N$$

*(Volume of sphere of radius $\sqrt{2mE}$ in $3N$-dimensional momentum space) $\times$ ($3N$-dimensional position-space volume $V^N$)*

Using the $N$-dimensional sphere volume formula:

$$V_N(R) = \frac{\pi^{N/2}}{\Gamma\!\left(\dfrac{N}{2}+1\right)} R^N$$

## Page 115 — Number of States for $N$ Ideal-Gas Molecules with Energy $E$ (Slide 115)

Phase-space volume for energy $\leq E$:

$$\tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E) = \frac{\pi^{3N/2}}{\left(\dfrac{3N}{2}\right)!}(2mE)^{3N/2}\, V^N$$

Phase-space volume for energy between $E$ and $E + \Delta E$:

$$\tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E + \Delta E) - \tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E) = \frac{\partial \tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E)}{\partial E}\Delta E$$

Using the definition of derivative:

$$\frac{df(x)}{dx} \equiv \frac{f(x+dx)-f(x)}{dx} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad f(x+\Delta x) - f(x) \approx \frac{\partial f(x)}{\partial x}\Delta x$$

---

## Page 116 — Number of States for $N$ Ideal-Gas Molecules with Energy $E$ (Slide 116)

Phase-space volume for energy between $E$ and $E + \Delta E$:

$$\tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E + \Delta E) - \tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E) = \frac{\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E)}{\partial E}\Delta E$$

**Number of states** with energy between $E$ and $E + \Delta E$:

$$\Omega(E) = \frac{\tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E+\Delta E) - \tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E)}{N!\, h^{3N}} = \frac{1}{N!\, h^{3N}}\frac{\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E)}{\partial E}\Delta E$$

where $N!$ accounts for **identical particles** and $h^{3N}$ is the minimum phase-space volume per state.

---

## Page 117 — Effect of Identical Particles (Slide 117)

*(Diagram: Colored circles labeled a, b, c, d showing different arrangements:)*

- **1 particle (a):** $1! = 1$ state
- **2 particles (a,b):** $2! = 2$ states
- **3 particles (a,b,c):** $3! = 6$ states
- **4 particles (a,b,c,d):** $4! = 24$ states

Since identical particles are indistinguishable, swapping them does not produce a new state — so we divide by $N!$.

## Page 118 — From Boltzmann Entropy to Ideal-Gas Entropy (Slide 118)

**Boltzmann entropy:**

$$S(E) = k_B\ln\Omega(E) = k_B\ln\!\left(\frac{1}{N!\, h^{3N}}\frac{\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E)}{\partial E}\Delta E\right)$$

Computing $\dfrac{\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E)}{\partial E}$:

$$\tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E) = \frac{\pi^{3N/2}}{\left(\dfrac{3N}{2}\right)!}(2mE)^{3N/2} V^N = \frac{(2\pi m)^{3N/2}}{\left(\dfrac{3N}{2}\right)!} E^{3N/2} V^N$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E)}{\partial E} = \frac{(2\pi m)^{3N/2}}{\left(\dfrac{3N}{2} - 1\right)!} E^{3N/2 - 1} V^N \quad \text{(using factorial)}$$

## Page 119 — Stirling's Approximation (Slide 119)

$$\ln n! \approx n\ln n - n$$

This is an excellent approximation when $n$ is large. It is called **Stirling's approximation** or **Stirling's formula**.

**James Stirling** (Scotland, 1692–1770) — a Scottish mathematician who lived in the same era as Newton.

---

## Page 120 — Number of States $\Omega(E)$ and Its Logarithm $\ln\Omega(E)$ (Slide 120)

**Number of states $\Omega(E)$:**

$$\Omega(E) = \frac{1}{N!\, h^{3N}}\frac{\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{6N}(E)}{\partial E}\Delta E = \frac{1}{N!\, h^{3N}}\frac{(2\pi m)^{3N/2}}{\left(\dfrac{3N}{2}-1\right)!} E^{3N/2-1} V^N\,\Delta E$$

**Logarithm of the state count $\ln\Omega(E)$:**

$$\ln\Omega(E) = -\ln N! - 3N\ln h + \frac{3N}{2}\ln(2\pi m) - \ln\!\left(\frac{3N}{2}-1\right)! + \left(\frac{3N}{2}-1\right)\ln E + N\ln V + \ln\Delta E$$

Applying Stirling's approximation:

$$\approx -(N\ln N - N) - 3N\ln h + \frac{3N}{2}\ln(2\pi m) - \left(\frac{3N}{2}\ln\frac{3N}{2} - \frac{3N}{2}\right) + \left(\frac{3N}{2}\right)\ln E + N\ln V$$

---

## Page 121 — $\ln\Omega(E)$ (Slide 121)

Continuing the calculation:

$$\ln\Omega(E) \approx -(N\ln N - N) - 3N\ln h + \frac{3N}{2}\ln(2\pi m) - \left(\frac{3N}{2}\ln\frac{3N}{2} - \frac{3N}{2}\right) + \frac{3N}{2}\ln E + N\ln V$$

$$= N\!\left\{-\ln N + 1 - \ln h^3 + \ln(2\pi mE)^{3/2} - \ln\!\left(\frac{3N}{2}\right)^{3/2} + \frac{3}{2} + \ln V\right\}$$

$$= N\!\left\{\ln\frac{(2\pi mE)^{3/2} V}{Nh^3\!\left(\dfrac{3N}{2}\right)^{3/2}} + \frac{5}{2}\right\} = N\!\left\{\ln\frac{V}{N}\!\left(\frac{4\pi mE}{3Nh^2}\right)^{3/2} + \frac{5}{2}\right\}$$

$$= \ln\!\left[\frac{V}{N}\!\left(\frac{4\pi mE}{3Nh^2}\right)^{3/2}\right]^N + \frac{5}{2}N$$

---

## Page 122 — Sackur–Tetrode Equation (Slide 122)

$$\ln\Omega(E) = \ln\!\left[\frac{V}{N}\!\left(\frac{4\pi mE}{3Nh^2}\right)^{3/2}\right]^N + \frac{5}{2}N$$

From $S(E) = k_B\ln\Omega(E)$:

$$\boxed{S(N,V,E) = k_B\!\left\{\ln\!\left[\frac{V}{N}\!\left(\frac{4\pi mE}{3Nh^2}\right)^{3/2}\right]^N + \frac{5}{2}N\right\}}$$

**Sackur–Tetrode equation** — the entropy of an ideal gas consisting of $N$ molecules of mass $m$ in volume $V$ with internal energy $E$.

---

## Page 123 — Entropy and Absolute Temperature (Slide 123)

**Temperature definition using entropy:**

$$\frac{1}{T} = \frac{\partial S(E)}{\partial E}$$

**Entropy of an ideal gas (Sackur–Tetrode):**

$$S(E) = k_B\!\left\{\ln\!\left[\frac{V}{N}\!\left(\frac{4\pi mE}{3Nh^2}\right)^{3/2}\right]^N + \frac{5}{2}N\right\}$$

Computing $\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial E}$:

$$\frac{\partial S(E)}{\partial E} = Nk_B\,\frac{\dfrac{3}{2}\dfrac{V}{N}\!\left(\dfrac{4\pi m}{3Nh^2}\right)^{3/2} E^{1/2}}{\dfrac{V}{N}\!\left(\dfrac{4\pi mE}{3Nh^2}\right)^{3/2}} = \frac{3}{2}\frac{Nk_B}{E} = \frac{1}{T}$$

$$\therefore \quad \boxed{E = \frac{3}{2}Nk_B T} \qquad \text{(internal energy of an ideal gas)}$$

## Page 124 — Entropy Change in Free Expansion of an Ideal Gas (Slide 124)

*(Diagram: Two bulbs — gas on left, vacuum on right; then gas fills both.)*

**Problem:** 1 mol of an ideal gas undergoes free expansion, doubling its volume. Find the entropy change.

**Free expansion:** $Q = 0$, $W = 0$

**First Law:** $\Delta E = Q - W = 0 \Rightarrow T_i = T_f$

The entropy change equals that of an isothermal expansion (same initial and final states):

$$Q = W = nRT\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}$$

$$\Delta S = \frac{Q}{T} = nR\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i} = 1 \times 8.31 \times 0.693 \ \text{J/K} = 5.561 \ \text{J/K}$$

*(This is consistent with the result computed using the Sackur–Tetrode equation: $\Delta S = Nk_B\ln\dfrac{V_f}{V_i}$.)*

## Page 125 — Entropy Change in Free Expansion Using the Sackur–Tetrode Equation (Slide 125)

**Entropy of an ideal gas (Sackur–Tetrode):**

$$S(E) = k_B\!\left\{\ln\!\left[\frac{V}{N}\!\left(\frac{4\pi mE}{3Nh^2}\right)^{3/2}\right]^N + \frac{5}{2}N\right\}$$

**Initial entropy:**

$$S_i(E) = k_B\!\left\{\ln\!\left[\frac{V_i}{N}\!\left(\frac{4\pi mE}{3Nh^2}\right)^{3/2}\right]^N + \frac{5}{2}N\right\}$$

**Final entropy:**

$$S_f(E) = k_B\!\left\{\ln\!\left[\frac{V_f}{N}\!\left(\frac{4\pi mE}{3Nh^2}\right)^{3/2}\right]^N + \frac{5}{2}N\right\}$$

**Entropy change:**

$$\Delta S = S_f(E) - S_i(E) = Nk_B\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i}$$

**First Law of Thermodynamics:** The internal energy $E$ of an isolated system is constant.

**Second Law of Thermodynamics:** The entropy $S$ of an isolated system does not decrease.

**Clausius entropy:**

**Boltzmann entropy:**
