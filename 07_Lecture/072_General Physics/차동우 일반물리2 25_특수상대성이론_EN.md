### Slide 2 — Relative Motion (상대 운동)

**Is the Earth at rest?**

From everyday experience, we feel the Earth is stationary. But:

- The Earth rotates on its axis (~460 m/s at equator)
- The Earth orbits the Sun (~30 km/s)
- The Solar System orbits the Milky Way center (~220 km/s)
- The Milky Way moves relative to other galaxies

**Key insight**: There is no absolute rest — all motion is *relative*.

*Diagram: Concentric circles showing Earth's rotation, orbital motion around Sun, and galactic motion — illustrating multiple layers of relative motion.*

---

### Slide 3 — Inertial Reference Frames (관성 기준틀)

**Reference Frame (기준틀)**: A coordinate system with a clock, used to measure the position and time of events.

**Inertial Frame (관성 기준틀)**: A reference frame in which Newton's first law holds — an object at rest remains at rest, and an object in motion continues in a straight line at constant velocity, unless acted upon by a net force.

Examples of inertial frames:
- A train moving at constant velocity
- A spacecraft coasting in deep space

Non-inertial frames:
- A rotating merry-go-round
- An accelerating car

*Diagram: Two frames S and S', with S' moving at constant velocity v relative to S.*

---

### Slide 4 — Galilean Transformation (갈릴레이 변환)

Consider two inertial frames: **S** (stationary) and **S'** (moving at velocity $v$ along x-axis).

At $t = 0$, the origins coincide.

**Galilean Transformation:**

$$x' = x - vt$$

$$y' = y$$

$$z' = z$$

$$t' = t$$

**Key feature**: Time is *absolute* — the same in all frames.

*Diagram: S frame has observer A; S' frame has observer B moving at velocity v. An event P has coordinates (x, t) in S and (x', t') in S'.*

---

### Slide 5 — Galilean Velocity Addition (갈릴레이 속도 합성)

Differentiating the Galilean transformation with respect to time:

$$\frac{dx'}{dt} = \frac{dx}{dt} - v$$

$$u_x' = u_x - v$$

$$u_y' = u_y, \quad u_z' = u_z$$

**Example**: A ball thrown at $u_x = 20$ m/s in a train moving at $v = 30$ m/s.

- Ground frame: $u_x = 20 + 30 = 50$ m/s
- Train frame: $u_x' = 20$ m/s

This is **Galilean velocity addition** — velocities add linearly.

*Diagram: Person in train throwing ball; ground observer sees different speed.*

---

### Slide 6 — Newton's Laws and Galilean Relativity

Newton's laws of motion are **invariant** under Galilean transformation.

**Proof**: Since $t' = t$ and $m' = m$:

$$F' = ma' = m\frac{d^2x'}{dt'^2} = m\frac{d^2(x-vt)}{dt^2} = m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = ma = F$$

The force (and hence Newton's laws) looks the same in all inertial frames.

**Galilean Principle of Relativity**: The laws of mechanics are the same in all inertial frames.

*Diagram: Billiard balls colliding — same collision seen from moving train and from ground; physics equations identical in both frames.*

---

### Slide 7 — Maxwell's Equations and Light (맥스웰 방정식과 빛)

In the 1860s, James Clerk Maxwell unified electricity and magnetism into **Maxwell's equations**.

From Maxwell's equations, he derived that electromagnetic waves travel at:

$$c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0}} \approx 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$$

This is the **speed of light** — a constant that appears directly from the equations.

**Problem**: What frame is this speed measured in?

- If S' moves at $v$, Galilean addition gives $c' = c - v$ in S'.
- But Maxwell's equations give the same $c$ in every frame!

*Diagram: Maxwell's four equations; wave equation for E and B fields.*

---

### Slide 8 — The Contradiction (모순)

**Galilean mechanics** says: the laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames, but velocities add.

**Maxwell's electromagnetism** says: the speed of light is a fixed constant $c$.

These two cannot both be correct if Galilean velocity addition is true:

| Frame | Speed of light (Galilean) |
|-------|--------------------------|
| Stationary (S) | $c$ |
| Moving at $v$ (S') | $c - v$ |

If light travels at $c - v$ in a moving frame, then Maxwell's equations must look *different* in that frame — violating Galilean relativity!

**Late 19th century crisis**: Which is wrong — mechanics or electromagnetism?

---

### Slide 9 — The Ether Hypothesis (에테르 가설)

To resolve the contradiction, physicists proposed the **ether (에테르)**:

- A hypothetical medium that fills all of space
- Light propagates through the ether at speed $c$
- The ether defines the **absolute rest frame**
- Just as sound needs air, light needs ether

**Prediction**: The Earth moves through the ether at $v \approx 30$ km/s (orbital speed), so we should detect a difference in light speed depending on direction.

*Diagram: Earth moving through stationary ether medium; light traveling both with and against Earth's motion.*

## Section 25B: Michelson-Morley Experiment (마이컬슨-몰리 실험)

---

### Slide 11 — Section Title: 마이컬슨-몰리 실험

**25B: The Michelson-Morley Experiment**

*The most famous failed experiment in physics history.*

---

### Slide 12 — The Michelson Interferometer (마이컬슨 간섭계)

Albert Michelson (1852–1931) invented the **interferometer** — an instrument of extraordinary precision.

**Principle**: Split a light beam into two perpendicular paths, reflect off mirrors, recombine and look for interference.

- If the two arms have equal optical path lengths → constructive interference (bright)
- If paths differ by $\lambda/2$ → destructive interference (dark)

Sensitivity: can detect path length differences of a fraction of a wavelength (~$10^{-7}$ m).

*Diagram: Beam splitter splits light into arm 1 (horizontal) and arm 2 (vertical). Light reflects off mirrors M1 and M2 and recombines at detector.*

$$\Delta = \frac{2L v^2}{c^2 \lambda}$$

where $L$ = arm length, $v$ = Earth's speed through ether, $\lambda$ = wavelength.

---

### Slide 13 — Expected Result (예상 결과)

**If ether exists and Earth moves through it at $v$:**

- Arm parallel to motion: light goes "upstream" then "downstream"

$$t_\parallel = \frac{L}{c-v} + \frac{L}{c+v} = \frac{2Lc}{c^2-v^2} = \frac{2L/c}{1-\beta^2}$$

- Arm perpendicular to motion: light travels at angle

$$t_\perp = \frac{2L}{\sqrt{c^2-v^2}} = \frac{2L/c}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}}$$

**Time difference:**

$$\Delta t = t_\parallel - t_\perp = \frac{2L}{c}\left(\frac{1}{1-\beta^2} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}}\right) \approx \frac{Lv^2}{c^3}$$

With $L = 11$ m, $v = 30$ km/s: expected fringe shift $\approx 0.4$ fringes — easily detectable.

---

### Slide 14 — The 1887 Experiment (1887년 실험)

Michelson and Edward Morley conducted the definitive experiment in **1887**.

**Setup improvements:**
- Interferometer mounted on massive stone block
- Block floated on mercury to allow rotation
- Light reflected multiple times to increase effective arm length to $L = 11$ m

**Procedure**: Rotate the apparatus 90° — if ether wind exists, fringe pattern should shift.

**Result**:

> **No fringe shift was observed.**

The measured shift was less than 1/40 of the expected value — consistent with zero.

*Diagram: Circular stone slab on mercury bath; interferometer on top; rotation arrows.*

---

### Slide 15 — Implications (의미)

**The null result of Michelson-Morley:**

1. The ether does **not** exist (or cannot be detected)
2. The speed of light is the **same in all directions** regardless of Earth's motion
3. The concept of an absolute rest frame has **no experimental support**

**Attempted explanations (all failed):**
- Ether drag: Earth drags ether with it (contradicted by stellar aberration)
- Lorentz-FitzGerald contraction: arms contract in direction of motion (ad hoc)

**The real solution**: Einstein's Special Relativity (1905) — abandon absolute time and absolute space.

*Diagram: Timeline showing 1887 Michelson-Morley experiment, various failed explanations, and Einstein's 1905 resolution.*

---

### Slide 16 — Michelson's Achievement

Albert Michelson was awarded the **Nobel Prize in Physics in 1907** — the first American to win a Nobel Prize in science.

He received it for his "optical precision instruments and the spectroscopic and metrological investigations carried out with their aid."

The Michelson-Morley experiment, though a "failure" in its original goal, became one of the most important experiments in physics history by *disproving* the ether hypothesis.

*Portrait: Albert Michelson.*

## Section 25C: Ether and the Absolute Reference Frame Problem (에테르와 절대 기준틀)

---

### Slide 18 — Section Title: 에테르와 절대 기준틀

**25C: The Ether and the Absolute Reference Frame Problem**

*Why 19th century physicists were reluctant to abandon the ether.*

---

### Slide 19 — The Wave Medium Problem (파동 매질 문제)

**Classical wave mechanics** requires a medium:
- Sound waves → air (or other material)
- Water waves → water
- Seismic waves → rock

**Question**: What medium does light (electromagnetic wave) propagate through?

19th century answer: **Ether (에테르)**
- Fills all of space, including vacuum
- Extremely rigid (to support high-frequency light waves)
- Yet offers no resistance to planetary motion (planets move freely)
- Provides the absolute rest frame

These contradictory requirements made the ether physically implausible.

---

### Slide 20 — Properties Required of Ether (에테르의 필요 성질)

For ether to explain light propagation, it would need:

| Property | Requirement |
|----------|-------------|
| Density | Nearly zero (planets pass through unimpeded) |
| Rigidity | Extremely high (wave speed $c \propto \sqrt{E/\rho}$) |
| Permeability | Allows all matter to pass freely |
| Universality | Fills entire universe uniformly |

**Physical impossibility**: High rigidity requires high density. A material cannot be simultaneously extremely rigid and extremely tenuous.

*Diagram: Contradiction arrows between "rigid enough for light speed" and "transparent to all matter."*

---

### Slide 21 — Stellar Aberration Problem (별빛 수차 문제)

**Stellar aberration (별빛 수차)**: Stars appear to shift position slightly (~20 arcseconds) depending on Earth's orbital velocity direction.

This is like rain falling vertically — if you run, the rain appears to come from ahead of you.

**Ether drag hypothesis**: If Earth drags ether with it, there should be no aberration.

**Observation**: Aberration exists → Earth does NOT drag ether.

**But**: If Earth doesn't drag ether, the Michelson-Morley experiment should show ether wind → It didn't.

**Contradiction**: Aberration says Earth doesn't drag ether. M-M says there is no ether wind. Both cannot be true simultaneously with the ether hypothesis.

---

### Slide 22 — Lorentz-FitzGerald Contraction (로렌츠-피츠제럴드 수축)

**George FitzGerald (1889)** and **Hendrik Lorentz (1892)** independently proposed:

> Objects moving through the ether contract in the direction of motion by factor $\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}$.

This would explain why Michelson-Morley found no fringe shift — the parallel arm contracts, exactly compensating for the ether wind effect.

$$L_\parallel = L_0\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}$$

**Problem**: This is *ad hoc* — invented specifically to explain away the null result. Why would material objects contract when moving through ether?

*Diagram: Contracted ellipse in direction of motion vs. sphere at rest.*

---

### Slide 23 — Lorentz's Approach (로렌츠의 접근)

**Hendrik Lorentz** (1853–1928) went further and derived transformation equations that preserved Maxwell's equations:

$$x' = \gamma(x - vt), \quad t' = \gamma\left(t - \frac{vx}{c^2}\right)$$

where $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}$.

**Lorentz's interpretation** (incorrect):
- There is still an absolute ether frame
- These transformations are mathematical tricks
- "True" time is the ether-frame time
- Clocks moving through ether run slow due to interaction with ether

Lorentz had the right mathematics but the wrong physics.

*Portrait: Hendrik Lorentz.*

---

### Slide 24 — Poincaré's Contribution (푸앵카레의 기여)

**Henri Poincaré** (1854–1912) came very close to special relativity:

- Showed Lorentz transformations form a mathematical group
- Coined the term "Lorentz transformation"
- Proposed the principle of relativity as a general law
- Suggested that the speed of light is a universal constant

**Why not Poincaré's relativity?**
- He still retained the ether as a concept
- Did not fully abandon absolute time
- Did not derive $E = mc^2$

Einstein's 1905 paper was the decisive conceptual break.

---

### Slide 25 — The Need for a Conceptual Revolution (개념 혁명의 필요성)

**The crisis by 1905:**

- Michelson-Morley: No ether wind detected
- Stellar aberration: Earth doesn't drag ether
- Maxwell's equations: Speed of light constant in ether frame
- Galilean relativity: Speed of light should vary with frame

No piecemeal fix worked. What was needed was a fundamental revision of the concepts of **space** and **time**.

Einstein's insight: **Abandon** the concept of absolute time. Both space and time are relative — they depend on the observer's motion.

*Timeline diagram: 1887 M-M → 1889 FitzGerald contraction → 1892 Lorentz → 1895 Poincaré → **1905 Einstein**.*

---

### Slide 26 — Summary: 25C

| Concept | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Ether problem | Wave medium required but physically impossible |
| Stellar aberration | Earth doesn't drag ether |
| Lorentz-FitzGerald | Objects contract; ad hoc explanation |
| Lorentz transformation | Correct mathematics, wrong interpretation |
| Poincaré | Near miss — retained ether concept |
| Resolution | Einstein: abandon absolute time and absolute space |

## Section 25D: 2D/3D Space → Minkowski 4D Spacetime (민코프스키 4차원 시공간)

---

### Slide 27 — Section Title: 2D/3D 공간에서 4D 시공간으로

**25D: From 2D/3D Space to 4D Spacetime**

*How Hermann Minkowski gave Einstein's relativity its geometric home.*

---

### Slide 28 — Coordinate Systems (좌표계)

**1D space**: Position described by one number $x$

**2D space**: Position described by two numbers $(x, y)$

**3D space**: Position described by three numbers $(x, y, z)$

**Euclidean distance** (invariant under rotation):

$$L^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$$

This distance is the **same** regardless of which coordinate system (orientation) you use.

*Diagram: 1D number line, 2D coordinate plane, 3D coordinate system with axes.*

---

### Slide 29 — 2D Rotation Invariance (2D 회전 불변성)

A circle drawn on a 2D plane looks the same regardless of which direction you call "x."

**2D coordinate rotation** by angle $\theta$:

$$x' = x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta$$
$$y' = -x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta$$

**Distance invariant**:

$$L^2 = x^2 + y^2 = x'^2 + y'^2$$

The length of a vector does not change when you rotate the coordinate system — only the components change.

*Diagram: Same vector in two rotated coordinate systems; $x, y$ components differ but $L = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ is the same.*

---

### Slide 30 — Why Rotational Invariance? (왜 회전 불변?)

In 2D space, different people may use different coordinate axes (e.g., one uses magnetic north, another uses true north), but they can all agree on:

- **Distances** between points
- **Lengths** of objects
- **Physical laws**

The physical world does not depend on our choice of coordinate system. Nature is **rotationally symmetric** in space.

*Diagram: Two people at a table — one uses N/S/E/W compass, other uses a different reference direction. They measure the same distance between two chairs.*

---

### Slide 31 — 3D Euclidean Space (3차원 유클리드 공간)

Extending to 3D:

$$L^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$$

This is invariant under any **rotation in 3D space** (using rotation matrices — combinations of rotations about x, y, z axes).

**Scalars**: Quantities that don't change under rotation (e.g., temperature, mass, distance)

**Vectors**: Quantities with components that transform the same way as position $(x, y, z)$

**Tensors**: Higher-rank objects that transform accordingly

The invariance of $L^2$ defines the geometry of **Euclidean 3D space**.

---

### Slide 32 — Cheolsu and Younghi's Hospital Trip (철수와 영희의 병원 이야기)

**Example**: Cheolsu (철수) and Younghi (영희) need to find the distance to a hospital 50 km away.

- **Cheolsu** uses the **North Star** as his reference direction (x-axis points north)
- **Younghi** uses a **compass** (but her compass is slightly off — different x-axis direction)

They measure different x and y components, but both calculate the **same distance** $L = 50$ km.

$$L^2 = x_\text{Cheolsu}^2 + y_\text{Cheolsu}^2 = x_\text{Younghi}^2 + y_\text{Younghi}^2 = (50 \text{ km})^2$$

**Moral**: The choice of coordinate axes is arbitrary — physical distances are coordinate-independent.

*Diagram: Map with hospital, Cheolsu's north-pointing axes, Younghi's slightly rotated axes; both measure 50 km.*

---

### Slide 33 — 2D Rotation Equations

**Coordinate transformation** from Cheolsu's frame (unprimed) to Younghi's frame (primed), rotated by angle $\theta$:

$$x' = x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta$$
$$y' = -x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta$$

$$\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$$

**Invariant**:

$$x'^2 + y'^2 = x^2 + y^2$$

This is the **fundamental invariant** of 2D Euclidean space — preserved by all rotations.

---

### Slide 34 — What is a Scalar? What is a Vector? (스칼라? 벡터?)

**High school definition**: Scalars have magnitude only; vectors have magnitude and direction.

**University definition**: 
- **Scalar**: A quantity that is invariant under coordinate rotation
- **Vector**: A set of quantities that transform like $(x, y, z)$ under rotation

**Expert definition**:
- Scalars and vectors are defined by how they transform under the symmetry group of the space
- In 3D Euclidean space: rotation group SO(3)
- In 4D Minkowski spacetime: Lorentz group SO(3,1)

A "vector" in Newtonian mechanics (3D) is different from a "4-vector" in special relativity (4D).

---

### Slide 35 — 3D Space and Beyond (3D 공간과 그 너머)

**3D space**: $L^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$

Can we add a **4th spatial dimension**?

- **4D Euclidean space**: $L^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + w^2$
- Mathematically consistent, but we cannot draw or directly visualize it
- No evidence of a 4th spatial dimension at macroscopic scales

**But what about time?**

Time is different from space — you can move freely in any spatial direction, but you cannot move backward in time (in classical physics).

*Diagram: 1D line → 2D plane → 3D cube → 4D hypercube (can't draw fully, shown as projection).*

---

### Slide 36 — Euclidean Spaces 1D through 4D (1D~4D 유클리드 공간)

| Dimension | Coordinates | Distance invariant |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| 1D | $(x)$ | $L^2 = x^2$ |
| 2D | $(x, y)$ | $L^2 = x^2 + y^2$ |
| 3D | $(x, y, z)$ | $L^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$ |
| 4D Euclidean | $(x, y, z, w)$ | $L^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + w^2$ |
| **4D Minkowski** | $(x, y, z, ict)$ | $L^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - c^2t^2$ |

The last row is **Minkowski spacetime** — the 4th "direction" is imaginary time $ict$, giving a **minus sign** in the invariant.

This minus sign is the mathematical heart of special relativity.

---

### Slide 37 — 2D Space: Two Reference Frames (2D 공간: 두 기준틀)

**철수** (Cheolsu) is navigating by the North Star — his x-axis points toward it.

**영희** (Younghi) uses a magnetic compass — her x-axis points toward magnetic north (slightly different from true north).

Both are trying to find the distance to a hospital, 50 km away.

- Cheolsu gets: $(x, y) = (30, 40)$ km → $L = \sqrt{30^2+40^2} = 50$ km
- Younghi gets: $(x', y') = (35, 35.4)$ km → $L = \sqrt{35^2+35.4^2} \approx 50$ km

Same physical distance — different coordinate components.

*Diagram: Top-down map view with hospital. Cheolsu's N/S/E/W grid (solid lines) and Younghi's slightly rotated grid (dashed lines). Hospital marked at 50 km. Both observers at origin.*

---

### Slide 38 — 2D Rotation: x and y Components Change

When Younghi's coordinate system is rotated by angle $\theta$ relative to Cheolsu's:

$$x' = x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta$$
$$y' = -x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta$$

The **components** $(x, y)$ and $(x', y')$ are different, but:

$$L^2 = x^2 + y^2 = x'^2 + y'^2$$

is **invariant** — the same in both frames.

*Diagram: Vector arrow from origin to hospital; Cheolsu's axes (x, y) and Younghi's rotated axes (x', y'); components labeled.*

---

### Slide 39 — x' and y' Expressions

Detailed derivation of the rotation transformation:

If the primed frame is rotated by angle $\theta$ counterclockwise:

$$\begin{cases}
x' = x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta \\
y' = -x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta
\end{cases}$$

**Verification of invariance**:

$$x'^2 + y'^2 = (x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta)^2 + (-x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta)^2$$
$$= x^2\cos^2\theta + 2xy\cos\theta\sin\theta + y^2\sin^2\theta + x^2\sin^2\theta - 2xy\sin\theta\cos\theta + y^2\cos^2\theta$$
$$= x^2(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) + y^2(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta) = x^2 + y^2 \checkmark$$

---

### Slide 40 — Invariant Distance in 2D (2D 불변량)

The **invariant** of 2D Euclidean space:

$$L^2 = x^2 + y^2 = x'^2 + y'^2 = \text{invariant}$$

This is preserved under any rotation.

**Physical meaning**: The actual physical distance to the hospital is the same regardless of which direction Cheolsu or Younghi calls "north."

**General principle**: Physical quantities should be expressed as scalars (invariants) or vectors — not as raw coordinate components, which change with the choice of reference frame.

*Diagram: Two rotated coordinate systems, same vector, components different, length same.*

---

### Slide 41 — 2D Rotation Summary (2D 회전 요약)

$$L^2 = x^2 + y^2 \quad \text{(invariant)}$$

$$x' = x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta, \quad y' = -x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta$$

$$\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = R(\theta)\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}, \quad R(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}$$

The rotation matrix $R(\theta)$ is **orthogonal**: $R^T R = I$, $\det R = 1$.

*Diagram: Summary table of 2D rotation properties.*

---

### Slide 42 — Definitions of Scalar and Vector

| Level | Scalar | Vector |
|-------|--------|--------|
| High school | Magnitude only | Magnitude + direction |
| University | Invariant under rotation | Transforms like $(x,y,z)$ |
| Expert | Rank-0 tensor under symmetry group | Rank-1 tensor under symmetry group |

**Key point**: In relativity, we extend to 4D spacetime. A **4-vector** is a set of 4 quantities that transforms like $(x, y, z, ict)$ under Lorentz transformations — not the same as a 3-vector!

Examples:
- 3D vector: $\vec{p} = (p_x, p_y, p_z)$ (transforms under rotations)
- 4-vector: $p_4 = (p_x, p_y, p_z, iE/c)$ (transforms under Lorentz transformations)

---

### Slide 43 — 3D Space (3차원 공간)

Extending to 3 spatial dimensions:

$$L^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$$

This is invariant under **all 3D rotations** — rotations about x, y, z axes and combinations thereof.

**Examples of 3D scalars (invariants)**:
- Temperature $T$
- Mass $m$
- Distance $L$
- Dot product $\vec{A}\cdot\vec{B}$

**Examples of 3D vectors**:
- Position $\vec{r} = (x, y, z)$
- Velocity $\vec{v} = (v_x, v_y, v_z)$
- Force $\vec{F} = (F_x, F_y, F_z)$
- Momentum $\vec{p} = (p_x, p_y, p_z)$

---

### Slide 44 — 3D Invariant Distance

**In 3D space**:

$$L^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = x'^2 + y'^2 + z'^2$$

Invariant under any rotation in 3D.

Formally, the set of all 3D rotations is the **Lie group SO(3)** (Special Orthogonal group in 3 dimensions).

Under SO(3):
- Scalars: unchanged ($T' = T$)
- Vectors: transform as $\vec{v}' = R\vec{v}$
- Tensors: transform accordingly

*Diagram: 3D coordinate axes with rotation shown; distance arrow from origin to point P is same in both frames.*

### Slide 45 — 4D Euclidean Space (4차원 유클리드 공간)

$$L^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + w^2$$

- Cannot be directly visualized (3D beings can't see the 4th spatial direction)
- Mathematically well-defined
- Symmetry group: SO(4)

**This is NOT what special relativity uses.**

The 4th dimension in special relativity is **time**, not a 4th spatial direction.

### Slide 47 — Minkowski 4D Spacetime (민코프스키 4D 시공간)

**Hermann Minkowski** (1864–1909) — Einstein's former mathematics professor — recognized in 1907 that Einstein's relativity could be expressed as geometry in a 4D spacetime.

The **Minkowski 4-vector**:

$$r_4 = (x, y, z, x_4) \quad \text{where } x_4 = ict$$

**Minkowski invariant** (spacetime interval):

$$L^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + (ict)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - c^2t^2$$

The **minus sign** (from $i^2 = -1$) distinguishes Minkowski spacetime from Euclidean space.

This invariant is preserved under **Lorentz transformations** — the analog of rotations in spacetime.

*Portrait: Hermann Minkowski. Diagram: Minkowski spacetime diagram with $ct$ axis (vertical) and $x$ axis (horizontal).*

### Slide 48 — 3D Space vs. 4D Spacetime Terminology

| 3D Space | 4D Spacetime |
|----------|-------------|
| Point in space | **Event** (point in spacetime) |
| Coordinate axes: $x, y, z$ | Axes: $x, y, z, ct$ |
| Distance: $L^2=x^2+y^2+z^2$ | Interval: $s^2=x^2+y^2+z^2-c^2t^2$ |
| Rotation | Lorentz "boost" (rotation in spacetime) |
| Path of object in space | **World line** (path in spacetime) |
| Scalar (3D): invariant under rotation | **4-scalar**: invariant under Lorentz transformation |
| Vector (3D): 3 components | **4-vector**: 4 components |

*Diagram: Spacetime diagram showing world line of a moving object as a slanted line in the $x$-$ct$ plane.*

### Slide 49 — World Lines (세계선)

**World line**: The trajectory of a particle through 4D spacetime (path on a spacetime diagram).

**Spacetime diagram** (Minkowski diagram):
- Vertical axis: $ct$ (time × speed of light)
- Horizontal axis: $x$ (spatial position)

| Object | World line appearance |
|--------|----------------------|
| Object at rest | Vertical line ($x = $ const) |
| Object moving at constant $v$ | Straight slanted line |
| Object accelerating | Curved line |
| Light ray | Line at 45° ($x = ct$) |

**Important**: Nothing can travel faster than light → world line slope must be $\geq 45°$ from horizontal.

The 45° light cone divides spacetime into **timelike**, **spacelike**, and **lightlike** regions.

### Slide 50 — The Angle of World Lines (세계선의 기울기)

For an object moving at velocity $v$ along x:

$$x = vt$$

On the spacetime diagram ($ct$ vs $x$ axes):

$$\tan\phi = \frac{x}{ct} = \frac{vt}{ct} = \frac{v}{c} = \beta$$

where $\phi$ is the angle of the world line from the $ct$-axis.

- **At rest** ($v=0$): $\phi = 0°$, world line is vertical
- **At speed $v$**: $\phi = \arctan(\beta)$
- **At speed $c$** (light): $\phi = 45°$, world line at 45°

**No object can have $\phi > 45°$** (would require $v > c$).

## Section 25E: Lorentz Transformation (로렌츠 변환)

---

### Slide 51 — Section Title: 로렌츠 변환

**25E: Lorentz Transformation**

*The transformation between inertial frames that preserves the speed of light.*

---

### Slide 52 — The Problem at the Turn of the 20th Century (20세기 초의 문제)

**Two pillars of physics in 1900:**

1. **Newtonian Mechanics** (갈릴레이 변환)
   - $x' = x - vt$, $t' = t$
   - Galilean velocity addition: $u' = u - v$
   - Law of conservation of momentum: valid
   - Works perfectly for everyday speeds

2. **Maxwell's Electromagnetism** (맥스웰 방정식)
   - $c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0} \approx 3\times10^8$ m/s
   - Speed of light is a constant — in which frame?
   - Galilean transformation changes Maxwell's equations between frames

**These two pillars contradicted each other.**

Einstein solved this in 1905 by recognizing that **Newtonian mechanics must be modified** — not Maxwell's equations.

---

### Slide 53 — Albert Einstein (알베르트 아인슈타인)

**Albert Einstein** (1879–1955)

| Year | Achievement |
|------|-------------|
| 1905 | Photoelectric effect (Nobel 1921) |
| 1905 | Brownian motion (atomic theory confirmed) |
| 1905 | **Special Theory of Relativity** |
| 1905 | $E = mc^2$ |
| 1915 | **General Theory of Relativity** |

1905 is called **Einstein's miraculous year** (Annus Mirabilis) — he published four landmark papers while working as a patent clerk in Bern, Switzerland.

*Portrait: Young Einstein, ~1905.*

---

### Slide 54 — Two Postulates of Special Relativity (특수상대성이론의 두 공리)

Einstein built special relativity on two postulates (공리 — assumed as axioms without proof):

**Postulate 1 (상대성 원리)**: The laws of physics are the same in all inertial reference frames.

> There is no preferred inertial frame. No experiment can distinguish between two inertial frames.

**Postulate 2 (광속 불변의 원리)**: The speed of light in vacuum is the same in all inertial frames, independent of the motion of the source or observer.

> $c = 3\times10^8$ m/s in every inertial frame, regardless of how fast the source or observer moves.

These two postulates together force us to abandon the Galilean concepts of absolute time and absolute space.

---

### Slide 55 — From Galilean to Lorentz Transformation (갈릴레이에서 로렌츠로)

**S** frame: stationary. **S'** frame: moving at velocity $v$ in the x-direction.

**Galilean transformation** (incorrect at high speeds):
$$x' = x - vt, \quad t' = t$$

**Lorentz transformation** (correct):
$$\boxed{x' = \gamma(x - \beta ct)}$$
$$y' = y, \quad z' = z$$
$$\boxed{ct' = \gamma(ct - \beta x)}$$

where $\beta = v/c$ and $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1-\beta^2}$.

**Verification**: This preserves the Minkowski invariant:

$$x'^2 + y'^2 + z'^2 - c^2t'^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - c^2t^2 \checkmark$$

*Diagram: S frame with observer A at origin; S' frame with observer B moving at velocity v; event P at (x,t) in S and (x',t') in S'.*

---

### Slide 56 — Derivation of the Lorentz Factor (로렌츠 인수 유도)

**Finding $\gamma$:** The transformation must preserve $x^2 - c^2t^2$:

Assume $x' = \gamma(x - vt)$ and $ct' = \gamma(ct - \beta x)$.

Calculate $x'^2 - c^2t'^2$:

$$x'^2 - c^2t'^2 = \gamma^2(x-vt)^2 - \gamma^2(ct-\beta x)^2$$

$$= \gamma^2\left[(x^2 - 2xvt + v^2t^2) - (c^2t^2 - 2\beta xct + \beta^2x^2)\right]$$

$$= \gamma^2\left[x^2(1-\beta^2) - c^2t^2(1-\beta^2)\right]$$

$$= \gamma^2(1-\beta^2)(x^2 - c^2t^2)$$

For this to equal $x^2 - c^2t^2$:

$$\gamma^2(1-\beta^2) = 1 \implies \gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}}$$

---

### Slide 57 — Why "Lorentz Transformation"? (왜 '로렌츠 변환'?)

**Hendrik Antoon Lorentz** (1853–1928, Dutch physicist):

- 1892: Proposed the FitzGerald-Lorentz length contraction to explain Michelson-Morley
- 1904: Derived these exact transformation equations from the condition that Maxwell's equations have the same form in all frames
- Received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1902

**Lorentz's interpretation**: Incorrect — he thought these were mathematical tools within the absolute ether frame. "Local time" was not real time.

**Einstein's interpretation**: Correct — these are the actual coordinate transformations between inertial frames. There is no ether.

The transformation equations are called "Lorentz transformation" to honor Lorentz's mathematical contribution, even though Einstein gave them the correct physical interpretation.

*Portrait: Hendrik Lorentz.*

---

### Slide 58 — Relativistic Velocity Addition (상대론적 속도 합성)

**Setup**: Three frames — A (ground), B (rocket), C (object on rocket).

- B moves at $v_{BA}$ relative to A
- C moves at $v_{CB}$ relative to B
- What is $v_{CA}$ (C relative to A)?

**Galilean** (incorrect): $v_{CA} = v_{CB} + v_{BA}$

**Relativistic** (correct):

$$\boxed{v_{CA} = \frac{v_{CB} + v_{BA}}{1 + \dfrac{v_{CB}\cdot v_{BA}}{c^2}}}$$

The denominator $1 + v_{CB}v_{BA}/c^2$ prevents the sum from exceeding $c$.

*Diagram: Frame A (ground observer), Frame B (rocket at $v_{BA}$), object C moving at $v_{CB}$ inside rocket.*

---

### Slide 59 — Speed of Light Is Always c (광속은 항상 c)

**Test of relativistic velocity addition:**

Let C be a light beam: $v_{CB} = c$

B moves at $v_{BA} = 0.3c$ relative to A.

**Galilean**: $v_{CA} = c + 0.3c = 1.3c$ ← violates postulate 2!

**Relativistic**:

$$v_{CA} = \frac{c + 0.3c}{1 + \dfrac{c \cdot 0.3c}{c^2}} = \frac{1.3c}{1 + 0.3} = \frac{1.3c}{1.3} = c \checkmark$$

The speed of light remains $c$ in all frames — exactly as Einstein's second postulate requires.

---

### Slide 60 — Two Rockets, Proper Velocity Addition (두 로켓의 속도 합성)

**Problem**: Two rockets approach each other from opposite directions.

- Rocket 1 moves at $+0.5c$ relative to ground (frame A)
- Rocket 2 moves at $-0.5c$ relative to ground (frame A)

**Question**: At what speed does Rocket 2 approach Rocket 1?

Use relativistic velocity addition (in frame of Rocket 1):

$$v_{BA} = \frac{0.5c + 0.5c}{1 + \dfrac{(0.5c)(0.5c)}{c^2}} = \frac{c}{1 + 0.25} = \frac{c}{1.25} = 0.8c$$

The relative speed is **0.8c**, not $c$ or $1.0c$.

*Diagram: Two rockets facing each other, each at 0.5c from ground observer in center.*

---

## Section 25F: Time Dilation (시간 팽창)

---

### Slide 62 — Section Title: 시간 팽창

**25F: Time Dilation**

*Moving clocks run slower.*

---

### Slide 63 — Lorentz Transformation as a Rotation in Spacetime (시공간에서의 회전)

The Lorentz transformation has the same mathematical structure as a **rotation in 4D spacetime**.

Let $x_4 = ict$. Then the Minkowski invariant is:

$$L^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + x_4^2 \quad \text{(with imaginary 4th coordinate)}$$

The Lorentz transformation in the x-$x_4$ plane:

$$\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ x_4' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\phi & -\sin\phi \\ \sin\phi & \cos\phi \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ x_4 \end{pmatrix}$$

where the "rotation angle" $\phi$ is **imaginary**: $\tan\phi = iv/c = i\beta$.

$$\cos\phi = \gamma, \quad \sin\phi = i\beta\gamma$$

*Diagram: 2D rotation in x-$x_4$ plane (the spacetime "rotation").*

---

### Slide 64 — Rotation Angle in Spacetime (시공간의 회전각)

From $\tan\phi = i\beta$:

$$\cos\phi = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\tan^2\phi}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}} = \gamma$$

$$\sin\phi = \tan\phi \cdot \cos\phi = i\beta\gamma$$

**Lorentz transformation in matrix form**:

$$\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ ict' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \gamma & -i\beta\gamma \\ i\beta\gamma & \gamma \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ ict \end{pmatrix}$$

Expanding: $x' = \gamma x - i\beta\gamma \cdot ict = \gamma(x - \beta ct)$ ✓

$$ict' = i\beta\gamma x + \gamma \cdot ict = \gamma(ict - \beta x) \implies t' = \gamma(t - \beta x/c)$$ ✓

---

### Slide 65 — Spacetime Diagram: Two Frames (시공간 다이어그램: 두 기준틀)

On a spacetime diagram, the Lorentz transformation looks like a **rotation** of the coordinate axes.

**Frame A** (stationary): axes $x$ (horizontal) and $ct$ (vertical)

**Frame B** (moving at $v$): axes $x'$ (tilted toward ct-axis) and $ct'$ (tilted toward x-axis)

The angle of tilt: $\tan\phi = \beta = v/c$

Key features:
- Light world line ($x = ct$, slope 45°) is **the same in both frames**
- Events that are simultaneous in A may not be simultaneous in B (**relativity of simultaneity**)
- The $ct'$ axis = world line of origin of B frame

*Diagram: Spacetime diagram with A's orthogonal axes ($x$, $ct$) and B's oblique axes ($x'$, $ct'$) both drawn. Light cone at 45°.*

---

### Slide 66 — Reading Events in Frame A and Frame B

**Event P** has coordinates:
- In A: $(x_P, ct_P)$ — read by dropping perpendiculars to A's axes
- In B: $(x'_P, ct'_P)$ — read by dropping parallels to B's axes

**Key difference from 2D rotation**: In Euclidean space, perpendicular lines from a point to the axes are orthogonal. In Minkowski space, because the metric has a minus sign, the "perpendiculars" (parallels to the other axis) look oblique on the diagram.

*Diagram: Event P marked on spacetime diagram. Dashed lines parallel to $x'$ axis (to find $ct'_P$) and parallel to $ct'$ axis (to find $x'_P$).*

---

### Slide 67 — Simultaneous Events Are Not Universal (동시성은 절대적이지 않다)

**Relativity of simultaneity (동시성의 상대성):**

Two events that are simultaneous in frame A ($t_1 = t_2$, different $x$ positions) are **NOT** simultaneous in frame B.

From Lorentz transformation:
$$t'_1 - t'_2 = \gamma\left[(t_1 - t_2) - \frac{v}{c^2}(x_1 - x_2)\right]$$

If $t_1 = t_2$ but $x_1 \neq x_2$:

$$t'_1 - t'_2 = -\gamma\frac{v}{c^2}(x_1 - x_2) \neq 0$$

**Example**: Flash at $x_1 = 0$ and $x_2 = L$ at $t=0$ in A. In B moving at $v$, these flashes are NOT simultaneous.

*Diagram: Train with flashbulb at center. Ground observer sees simultaneous flashes at front and back. Train observer sees front flash first (since she's rushing toward it).*

---

### Slide 68 — Spacetime Diagram Reading Practice (시공간 다이어그램 읽기)

**Practice**: Given event P on spacetime diagram with both A and B axes drawn.

Finding P's coordinates in frame A:
1. Draw horizontal line from P to $ct$-axis → reads $ct_P$
2. Draw vertical line from P to $x$-axis → reads $x_P$

Finding P's coordinates in frame B:
1. Draw line parallel to $x'$-axis from P to $ct'$-axis → reads $ct'_P$
2. Draw line parallel to $ct'$-axis from P to $x'$-axis → reads $x'_P$

*Diagram: Fully labeled spacetime diagram with both sets of axes, event P, and dashed construction lines for reading coordinates in each frame.*

### Slide 69 — Time Dilation: Spaceship to a Star (시간 팽창: 우주선이 별로)

**Scenario**: A spaceship (frame B) travels from Earth to a distant star at speed $v$.

**Two events**:
- Event 1: Spaceship leaves Earth
- Event 2: Spaceship arrives at star

**In Earth frame A**:
- $x_1 = 0$, $t_1 = 0$ (departure from Earth)
- $x_2 = d$ (distance to star), $t_2 = d/v$ (travel time)

**In spaceship frame B**:
- Both events occur at the **same location** $x'_1 = x'_2 = 0$ (the spaceship's own position)
- Time measured: $t'_2 - t'_1 = ?$

---

### Slide 70 — Proper Time (고유 시간)

**Proper time (고유 시간, $\tau$)**: The time measured by a clock that is present at both events — i.e., a clock at rest in the frame where both events occur at the same place.

For the spaceship:

Using Minkowski invariant:

$$-c^2\Delta\tau^2 = \Delta x'^2 - c^2\Delta t'^2 = \Delta x^2 - c^2\Delta t^2$$

Since $\Delta x' = 0$ (both events at spaceship's location):

$$c^2\Delta\tau^2 = c^2\Delta t^2 - \Delta x^2 = c^2t^2 - v^2t^2 = c^2t^2(1-\beta^2)$$

$$\Delta\tau = \Delta t\sqrt{1-\beta^2} = \frac{\Delta t}{\gamma}$$

$$\boxed{\Delta t = \gamma\Delta\tau}$$

Since $\gamma \geq 1$: the Earth time $\Delta t \geq$ proper time $\Delta\tau$.

**Moving clocks run slow.**

---

### Slide 71 — Time Dilation Formula (시간 팽창 공식)

$$\boxed{\Delta t = \gamma \Delta\tau = \frac{\Delta\tau}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}}$$

Where:
- $\Delta\tau$ = **proper time** — measured by clock at rest relative to both events
- $\Delta t$ = **coordinate time** — measured by observer for whom the events occur at different locations
- $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1-\beta^2} \geq 1$

**Time dilation (시간 팽창)**: The proper time is **shorter** than the coordinate time.

> "Moving clocks run slow" from the perspective of the stationary observer.

Note: Both observers are in inertial frames — from the spaceship's perspective, the Earth's clocks run slow too. This symmetry is resolved by the **twin paradox** (which involves acceleration — a non-inertial frame).

---

### Slide 72 — Light Clock Derivation (빛시계를 이용한 유도)

**Light clock**: A clock that consists of a light pulse bouncing between two mirrors separated by distance $l$.

**At rest**: One tick = time for light to travel $2l$:

$$\Delta\tau = \frac{2l}{c}$$

**Moving at velocity $v$**: The light pulse follows a diagonal path. In the stationary frame:

- Horizontal distance per bounce: $\frac{v\Delta t}{2}$
- Diagonal path length per bounce: $\frac{c\Delta t}{2}$

Pythagorean theorem:

$$\left(\frac{c\Delta t}{2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{v\Delta t}{2}\right)^2 + l^2$$

$$c^2\Delta t^2 = v^2\Delta t^2 + 4l^2 = v^2\Delta t^2 + c^2\Delta\tau^2$$

$$\Delta t^2(c^2 - v^2) = c^2\Delta\tau^2 \implies \Delta t = \frac{\Delta\tau}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}} = \gamma\Delta\tau \checkmark$$

*Diagram: Light clock at rest (vertical bounce) and light clock moving horizontally (diagonal bounce forming triangle).*

---

### Slide 73 — Light Clock Diagram (빛시계 다이어그램)

*Detailed diagram:*

**At rest**:
- Mirror M1 at top, mirror M2 at bottom
- Light bounces vertically; period $\Delta\tau = 2l/c$

**Moving at $v$** (as seen from stationary frame):
- The clock moves horizontally while light bounces
- Light path forms a triangle (two diagonal legs)
- Right triangle: hypotenuse = $c\Delta t/2$, base = $v\Delta t/2$, height = $l$

$$\left(\frac{c\Delta t}{2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{v\Delta t}{2}\right)^2 + l^2$$

Result: $\Delta t = \gamma \cdot \frac{2l}{c} = \gamma\Delta\tau$

The stationary observer sees the moving clock tick more slowly by factor $\gamma$.

### Slide 74 — Time Dilation: Numerical Example

**Muon decay experiment** (verification of time dilation):

- Muons created in upper atmosphere at ~10 km altitude
- Muon lifetime (at rest): $\Delta\tau = 2.2$ μs
- Muon speed: $v \approx 0.998c$ → $\gamma \approx 15.8$
- Classical expected distance: $v \cdot \Delta\tau = 0.998c \times 2.2\mu s \approx 660$ m
- Should not reach sea level (10,000 m away)!

**With time dilation**:

$$\Delta t = \gamma \Delta\tau = 15.8 \times 2.2 \,\mu\text{s} = 34.8 \,\mu\text{s}$$

$$d = v \cdot \Delta t = 0.998c \times 34.8\,\mu\text{s} \approx 10{,}400 \text{ m}$$

**Muons DO reach sea level** — confirming time dilation experimentally.

*Diagram: Muon created at top of atmosphere; decays after traveling 10 km to sea level.*

## Section 25G: Length Contraction (길이 수축)

---

### Slide 78 — Section Title: 길이 수축

**25G: Length Contraction**

*Moving objects are shorter in the direction of motion.*

---

### Slide 79 — Length Contraction Derivation (길이 수축 유도)

**Setup**: A rod lies along the x-axis in frame B (moving at $v$). 

- In B frame: rod has length $L_0$ (proper length, 고유 길이)
- Left end at $x' = 0$, right end at $x' = L_0$, measured **simultaneously** in B.

**Question**: What is the rod's length in frame A (stationary)?

To measure length in A, both ends must be measured **simultaneously in A** (at the same time $t$).

Using Lorentz transformation with $t = $ const:

For the right end of the rod ($x' = L_0$):

$$x' = \gamma(x - \beta ct)$$

$$L_0 = \gamma(x_2 - \beta ct_1)$$

For the left end ($x' = 0$, at same $t_1$):

$$0 = \gamma(x_1 - \beta ct_1) \implies x_1 = \beta ct_1$$

Length in A: $L = x_2 - x_1$

$$L_0 = \gamma(x_2 - x_1) = \gamma L \implies L = \frac{L_0}{\gamma}$$

$$\boxed{L = \frac{L_0}{\gamma} = L_0\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}}$$

---

### Slide 80 — Length Contraction: Step by Step

Using the Lorentz transformation more carefully:

The two events (measuring the ends simultaneously in A at time $t_1$):
- Event 1: Left end at $(x_1, t_1)$ in A → maps to $(0, t'_1)$ in B
- Event 2: Right end at $(x_2, t_1)$ in A → maps to $(L_0, t'_2)$ in B

Note: Events simultaneous in A ($t_1 = t_1$) are NOT simultaneous in B ($t'_1 \neq t'_2$).

From $0 = \gamma(ct_1 - \beta x_1)$ → $ct_1 = \beta x_1$

Substituting into right-end equation:

$$L_0 = \gamma(x_2 - \beta ct_1) = \gamma(x_2 - \beta \cdot \beta x_1) = \gamma(x_2 - \beta^2 x_1)$$

$$L_0 = \gamma\left[(x_2-x_1) + x_1(1-\beta^2)\right]$$

Since $x_1 = \beta ct_1 = \beta^2 x_1$ ... working through gives $L_0 = \gamma L$, confirming:

$$L = L_0/\gamma \quad \checkmark$$

### Slide 81 — Geometric Proof from Spacetime Diagram (시공간 다이어그램으로 증명)

**From the spacetime diagram** (similar triangles):

The Lorentz factor $\gamma = \cos\phi$ relates the apparent time and length scales:

$$\frac{t}{t'} = \frac{x}{x'} = \frac{1}{\cos\phi} = \gamma$$

Therefore:
- $t' = t/\gamma$ (time dilation: B's clock runs slow relative to A)
- $x' = x/\gamma$ (length contraction: rod appears shorter in A)

This geometric approach directly shows that time dilation and length contraction are **dual aspects** of the same spacetime geometry.

## Section 25H: 4-Dimensional Vectors (4차원 벡터)

---

### Slide 87 — Section Title: 4차원 벡터

**25H: 4-Dimensional Vectors**

*Extending vectors to 4D spacetime.*

---

### Slide 88 — The 4-Position Vector (4차원 위치 벡터)

**3D position vector**: $\vec{r} = (x, y, z)$

**4D position vector (4-position)**:

$$r_4 = (x, y, z, ict) = (\vec{r}, ict)$$

The 4th component is $x_4 = ict$ (imaginary time scaled by $c$).

**Minkowski invariant**:

$$|r_4|^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + (ict)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - c^2t^2$$

This is preserved under Lorentz transformations — it is a **4-scalar** (Lorentz invariant).

The sign can be positive (spacelike), negative (timelike), or zero (lightlike):
- Timelike: $|r_4|^2 < 0$ → events causally connected
- Lightlike: $|r_4|^2 = 0$ → connected by light
- Spacelike: $|r_4|^2 > 0$ → no causal connection

---

### Slide 89 — Displacement 4-Vector (4차원 변위 벡터)

**4D displacement**:

$$\Delta r_4 = (\Delta\vec{r}, ic\Delta t) = (\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z, ic\Delta t)$$

**Invariant interval**:

$$|\Delta r_4|^2 = \Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2 + \Delta z^2 - c^2\Delta t^2 = \text{invariant}$$

**Proper time** from the invariant:

For a particle moving along its worldline:

$$|\Delta r_4|^2 = -c^2\Delta\tau^2$$

(The invariant is negative for timelike intervals, and $\Delta\tau$ is real.)

$$\Delta\tau = \frac{1}{c}\sqrt{c^2\Delta t^2 - \Delta\vec{r}^2} = \Delta t\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}} = \frac{\Delta t}{\gamma}$$

This recovers the time dilation result.

---

### Slide 90 — 4-Scalars, 4-Vectors (4차원 스칼라, 4차원 벡터)

**4-Scalar (Lorentz invariant)**: A quantity that is the same in all inertial frames.

Examples of 4-scalars:
| Quantity | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Proper time $\Delta\tau$ | Same in all frames |
| Proper length $L_0$ | Same in all frames |
| Rest mass $m_0$ | Same in all frames |
| Speed of light $c$ | Same in all frames |
| Spacetime interval $|\Delta r_4|^2$ | Same in all frames |

**4-Vector**: A set of 4 quantities that transforms like $(x, y, z, ict)$ under Lorentz transformations.

Examples: 4-position, 4-velocity, 4-momentum, 4-force, 4-current.

---

### Slide 91 — Operations with 4-Vectors (4차원 벡터 연산)

**Same rules as 3D vectors**, extended to 4D:

| Operation | 3D result | 4D result |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| scalar + scalar | scalar | 4-scalar |
| scalar × vector | vector | 4-vector |
| vector · vector (dot) | scalar | 4-scalar |
| vector × vector (cross) | vector | (tensor in 4D) |

**4D dot product**:

$$A_4 \cdot B_4 = A_xB_x + A_yB_y + A_zB_z + A_4B_4$$

where $A_4 = ica_t$, $B_4 = icb_t$, so:

$$A_4 \cdot B_4 = \vec{A}\cdot\vec{B} - c^2a_tb_t$$

This dot product is a **4-scalar** (Lorentz invariant).

---

### Slide 92 — 4-Velocity (4차원 속도)

**3D velocity**: $\vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt}$

**4-velocity**: $v_4 = \frac{dr_4}{d\tau}$ (differentiate with respect to **proper time** $\tau$)

$$v_4 = \frac{dr_4}{d\tau} = \frac{dr_4}{dt}\frac{dt}{d\tau} = \gamma\frac{dr_4}{dt}$$

$$\frac{dr_4}{dt} = \left(\frac{dx}{dt}, \frac{dy}{dt}, \frac{dz}{dt}, \frac{ic\,dt}{dt}\right) = (v_x, v_y, v_z, ic) = (\vec{v}, ic)$$

$$\boxed{v_4 = \gamma(\vec{v}, ic) = (\gamma\vec{v}, i\gamma c)}$$

The 4-velocity is a proper 4-vector (transforms like $r_4$ under Lorentz transformations).

---

### Slide 93 — Magnitude of 4-Velocity (4차원 속도의 크기)

**Magnitude of 4-velocity**:

$$|v_4|^2 = |\gamma\vec{v}|^2 + (i\gamma c)^2 = \gamma^2v^2 - \gamma^2c^2 = \gamma^2(v^2 - c^2)$$

$$= \gamma^2 c^2\left(\frac{v^2}{c^2} - 1\right) = \gamma^2 c^2\left(\beta^2 - 1\right) = \gamma^2 c^2 \cdot (-1)(1-\beta^2)$$

$$= -\gamma^2 c^2 (1-\beta^2) = -c^2 \quad \text{(since } \gamma^2(1-\beta^2) = 1\text{)}$$

$$\boxed{|v_4|^2 = -c^2}$$

**Remarkable result**: The magnitude of the 4-velocity is always $c$ — the same for all particles regardless of their speed!

This makes sense: all particles move through spacetime at "speed $c$" — they just mix their motion between spatial and temporal components differently.

---

### Slide 94 — 3D Scalars vs. 4D Scalars (3차원 스칼라와 4차원 스칼라)

**3D scalar**: Invariant under 3D rotations (SO(3))
- Temperature $T$, mass $m$, energy $E$, speed $|v|$ ...

**4D scalar (Lorentz invariant)**: Invariant under Lorentz transformations (SO(3,1))
- Proper time $\tau$, rest mass $m_0$, $c$, $|v_4|^2 = -c^2$

**Note**: A quantity that is a 3D scalar may NOT be a 4D scalar!

- Energy $E$: 3D scalar (invariant under 3D rotations), but **NOT** a 4D scalar
  - In different frames: $E' \neq E$ (energy depends on frame)
  - Energy is the time-component of the 4-momentum (a 4-vector)

- Rest mass $m_0$: Both 3D scalar AND 4D scalar
  - Same in all frames: $m_0' = m_0$

---

### Slide 95 — Invariant Magnitude of 4-Vectors

For any 4-vector $A_4 = (A_x, A_y, A_z, iA_t)$:

$$|A_4|^2 = A_x^2 + A_y^2 + A_z^2 - A_t^2 = |\vec{A}|^2 - A_t^2$$

This is a **4-scalar** — invariant under Lorentz transformations.

**4D dot product of two 4-vectors**:

$$A_4 \cdot B_4 = A_xB_x + A_yB_y + A_zB_z - A_tB_t = \vec{A}\cdot\vec{B} - A_tB_t$$

Also a **4-scalar**.

This is directly analogous to the 3D dot product being a 3D scalar (invariant under rotations).

---

### Slide 96 — Conditions for 4-Scalars and 4-Vectors

**How to check if a quantity is a 4-scalar**:
- Show it equals the invariant $|A_4|^2$ or $A_4 \cdot B_4$ for known 4-vectors
- Or directly verify it's the same in all frames using Lorentz transformation

**How to check if 4 quantities form a 4-vector**:
- Show they transform like $(x, y, z, ict)$ under Lorentz boosts
- Equivalently, show the magnitude $|\vec{A}|^2 - A_t^2$ is invariant

**Building 4-vectors**:
- $\frac{d}{d\tau}$ (differentiate with respect to proper time $\tau$) turns a 4-vector into another 4-vector
- $m_0 \times$ (4-vector) = 4-vector (rest mass is a 4-scalar)

---

### Slide 97 — 4-Vectors in Electromagnetism

Maxwell's equations naturally fit into 4D notation.

**4-current**: $J_4 = (J_x, J_y, J_z, ic\rho) = (\vec{J}, ic\rho)$

where $\vec{J}$ = current density, $\rho$ = charge density.

**4-potential**: $A_4 = (A_x, A_y, A_z, i\phi/c) = (\vec{A}, i\phi/c)$

where $\vec{A}$ = magnetic vector potential, $\phi$ = electric potential.

**Maxwell's equations** can be written as a single 4D equation:

$$\Box A_4 = \mu_0 J_4$$

where $\Box = \partial^2/\partial x^2 + \partial^2/\partial y^2 + \partial^2/\partial z^2 - (1/c^2)\partial^2/\partial t^2$ is the **d'Alembertian**.

This shows Maxwell's equations are **inherently relativistic** — they already embody special relativity.

## Section 25I: $E = mc^2$

### Slide 99 — Section Title: $E = mc^2$

**25I: $E = mc^2$**

*The most famous equation in physics.*

---

### Slide 100 — 4-Momentum (4차원 운동량)

**3D momentum**: $\vec{p} = m_0\vec{v}$ (non-relativistic)

**4-momentum**: $p_4 = m_0 v_4$

Since $m_0$ is a 4-scalar and $v_4$ is a 4-vector, $p_4$ is a 4-vector.

$$p_4 = m_0 v_4 = m_0\gamma(\vec{v}, ic) = (m_0\gamma\vec{v}, im_0\gamma c)$$

$$\boxed{p_4 = (\vec{p}, ip_0)}$$

where:
- $\vec{p} = m_0\gamma\vec{v}$ = **relativistic momentum**
- $p_0 = m_0\gamma c$ = the **time-component** of 4-momentum

**Relativistic mass**: $m = m_0\gamma$

$$\vec{p} = m\vec{v} = m_0\gamma\vec{v}, \quad p_0 = mc$$

---

### Slide 101 — 4-Momentum Components (4차원 운동량 성분)

$$p_4 = (p_x, p_y, p_z, ip_0) = (\vec{p}, ip_0)$$

where $p_0 = m_0\gamma c$.

**Relativistic momentum**: $\vec{p} = m_0\gamma\vec{v}$

For $v \ll c$: $\gamma \approx 1$, so $\vec{p} \approx m_0\vec{v}$ ← Newtonian limit ✓

**Conjugate pairs**: Notice the structural similarity:

$$r_4 = (x, y, z, ict)$$
$$p_4 = (p_x, p_y, p_z, ip_0)$$

The spatial parts are conjugate ($x \leftrightarrow p_x$, etc.), and the time-like parts are conjugate: $ict \leftrightarrow ip_0$.

This suggests: $p_0 \propto E/c$ — the energy is the time-component of 4-momentum.

---

### Slide 102 — Energy as the Time-Component (에너지는 시간 성분)

**The conjugate structure**:

$$r_4 = (x, y, z, ict) \quad \xleftrightarrow{\text{conjugate}} \quad p_4 = (p_x, p_y, p_z, iE/c)$$

Comparing $ip_0 = im_0\gamma c$ with $iE/c$:

$$E = m_0\gamma c^2$$

**This identifies $p_0 = E/c$**, so:

$$p_4 = \left(\vec{p}, \frac{iE}{c}\right)$$

The 4-momentum contains both the **3D momentum** (spatial components) and the **energy** (time component).

This is a deep result: **energy and momentum are components of a single 4-vector**, just as time and space are components of the position 4-vector.

---

### Slide 103 — Energy-Momentum Relation (에너지-운동량 관계)

The 4-momentum conjugate relationship:

| 4-vector | Components |
|----------|-----------|
| $r_4 = (\vec{r}, ict)$ | Position + time |
| $p_4 = (\vec{p}, iE/c)$ | Momentum + energy |

In quantum mechanics, this conjugation is fundamental:
- Momentum $\hat{p} = -i\hbar\nabla$ is the conjugate to position $\hat{x}$
- Energy $\hat{E} = i\hbar\partial/\partial t$ is the conjugate to time $t$

In relativity, $\vec{p}$ and $E/c$ transform together just like $\vec{r}$ and $ct$.

---

### Slide 104 — Total Energy: $E = m_0\gamma c^2$

**From the time-component of 4-momentum**:

$$p_0 = m_0\gamma c = \frac{E}{c} \implies E = m_0\gamma c^2$$

**Total relativistic energy**:

$$\boxed{E = m_0\gamma c^2 = \frac{m_0c^2}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}}$$

At rest ($v = 0$, $\gamma = 1$):

$$E_0 = m_0c^2$$

**Rest energy** — energy stored in mass even when completely at rest.

This is Einstein's famous equation: **a stationary object with mass $m_0$ has energy $E_0 = m_0c^2$**.

---

### Slide 105 — Invariant Mass from 4-Momentum

**Magnitude of 4-momentum** (a 4-scalar = invariant):

$$|p_4|^2 = |\vec{p}|^2 + \left(\frac{iE}{c}\right)^2 = p^2 - \frac{E^2}{c^2}$$

From $p_4 = m_0 v_4$ and $|v_4|^2 = -c^2$:

$$|p_4|^2 = m_0^2|v_4|^2 = -m_0^2c^2$$

Therefore:

$$p^2 - \frac{E^2}{c^2} = -m_0^2c^2$$

$$\boxed{E^2 = p^2c^2 + m_0^2c^4}$$

This is the **relativistic energy-momentum relation** — one of the most important equations in physics.

---

### Slide 106 — Energy-Momentum Diagram (에너지-운동량 다이어그램)

$$E^2 = p^2c^2 + m_0^2c^4$$

**Special cases**:

| Case | Result |
|------|--------|
| At rest ($p = 0$) | $E = m_0c^2$ (rest energy) |
| Massless ($m_0 = 0$) | $E = pc$ (e.g., photon) |
| $p \ll m_0c$ | $E \approx m_0c^2 + \frac{p^2}{2m_0}$ (Newtonian limit) |

**For the photon** ($m_0 = 0$): $E = pc = hf$ and $p = hf/c = h/\lambda$ ← consistent with quantum mechanics.

*Diagram: Hyperbola $E^2 = p^2c^2 + m_0^2c^4$ in E-p plane. Massless particles lie on the diagonal $E = pc$.*

---

### Slide 107 — At Rest: $E_0 = m_0c^2$

**Setting $\vec{p} = 0$** (particle at rest):

$$E_0 = m_0c^2$$

**What does this mean?**

Mass is a form of energy. A 1 kg object at rest contains:

$$E_0 = 1 \times (3\times10^8)^2 = 9\times10^{16} \text{ J}$$

This is equivalent to ~21 megatons of TNT (the yield of a large thermonuclear weapon).

**Nuclear reactions** convert a small fraction of rest mass to energy — that's why they release enormous power.

$$\Delta E = \Delta m \cdot c^2$$

In fission: $\Delta m / m \approx 0.1\%$. This 0.1% yields the energy of a nuclear bomb.

*Diagram: Mass ↔ Energy conversion. Fission: heavy nucleus splits; fusion: light nuclei combine. Both release energy.*

## Section 25J: Relativistic Mechanics (상대론적 역학)

---

### Slide 108 — Section Title: 상대론적 역학

**25J: Relativistic Mechanics**

*Newton's laws, extended to high speeds.*

---

### Slide 109 — Newton's Equation in 4D Form (4차원 뉴턴 방정식)

**Newton's second law** in 3D:

$$\vec{F} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}$$

**4D extension** — differentiate 4-momentum with respect to **proper time** $\tau$:

$$F_4 = \frac{dp_4}{d\tau}$$

This is the **4-force** (or Minkowski force). Since $d/d\tau = \gamma \cdot d/dt$:

$$F_4 = \gamma\frac{dp_4}{dt} = \gamma\left(\frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}, \frac{i}{c}\frac{dE}{dt}\right) = \gamma\left(\vec{F}, \frac{i}{c}P\right)$$

where $P = dE/dt$ is the power (rate of energy input).

$$F_4 = \left(\gamma\vec{F}, \frac{i\gamma P}{c}\right)$$

---

### Slide 110 — Which Forces Have 4D Forms?

**Electromagnetic force**: Has a natural 4D form.

The electromagnetic 4-force:
$$F_4^{em} = q \cdot \mathcal{F} \cdot v_4$$

where $\mathcal{F}$ is the **electromagnetic field tensor** (4×4 antisymmetric matrix containing $\vec{E}$ and $\vec{B}$ fields).

This gives both the electric force $q\vec{E}$ and magnetic force $q\vec{v}\times\vec{B}$ from a single 4D equation.

**Gravitational force**: Does NOT fit as a 4-force in flat spacetime.
- Gravity requires **General Relativity** — curved spacetime.

**Nuclear/Strong force**: Very short range; governed by quantum chromodynamics (QCD).

---

### Slide 111 — Non-Relativistic vs. Relativistic Mechanics

| Quantity | Non-relativistic | Relativistic |
|----------|-----------------|--------------|
| Momentum | $\vec{p} = m_0\vec{v}$ | $\vec{p} = m_0\gamma\vec{v}$ |
| Energy | $K = \frac{1}{2}m_0v^2$ | $E = m_0\gamma c^2$ |
| Force law | $\vec{F} = m_0\vec{a}$ | $\vec{F} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt}$ |
| Velocity addition | $u' = u - v$ | $u' = \frac{u-v}{1-uv/c^2}$ |
| Mass | Constant $m_0$ | $m = \gamma m_0$ |

**Key difference**: In relativistic mechanics, $\vec{F} = m_0\vec{a}$ is **wrong** (unless the force is perpendicular to velocity). The correct law is $\vec{F} = d\vec{p}/dt$.

$$\vec{F} = \frac{d}{dt}(m_0\gamma\vec{v}) = m_0\frac{d(\gamma\vec{v})}{dt}$$

---

### Slide 112 — Relativistic Force in 1D (1차원 상대론적 힘)

For motion in one dimension:

$$F = \frac{dp}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}(m_0\gamma v) = \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{m_0 v}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}\right)$$

This can be expanded:

$$F = m_0\gamma^3\dot{v} = m_0\gamma^3 a$$

Note: the effective "mass" for longitudinal acceleration is $m_0\gamma^3$ (much larger than $m_0\gamma$).

**Implication**: As $v \to c$, $\gamma \to \infty$, so $F \to \infty$ for any nonzero acceleration.

**No finite force can accelerate a massive object to $c$.**

The speed of light is an absolute speed limit for massive objects.

---

### Slide 113 — Total Energy and Kinetic Energy

**Total relativistic energy**:

$$E = \frac{m_0c^2}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}} = m_0\gamma c^2$$

**Rest energy**: $E_0 = m_0c^2$

**Kinetic energy** = Total energy - Rest energy:

$$K = E - E_0 = m_0\gamma c^2 - m_0c^2 = m_0c^2(\gamma - 1)$$

**Non-relativistic limit** ($v \ll c$, $\beta \ll 1$):

$$\gamma = (1-\beta^2)^{-1/2} \approx 1 + \frac{1}{2}\beta^2 + \frac{3}{8}\beta^4 + \cdots$$

$$K = m_0c^2\left(\gamma - 1\right) \approx m_0c^2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\beta^2 = \frac{1}{2}m_0v^2 \checkmark$$

Newtonian kinetic energy is recovered in the low-speed limit.

### Slide 114 — Pop Quiz: Inelastic Collision (퀴즈: 완전 비탄성 충돌)

**Problem**: Two objects, each with rest mass $m_0$, collide head-on at equal and opposite speeds and stick together. What is the rest mass $M$ of the combined object?

**Naïve guess**: $M = 2m_0$ (conservation of mass)

**Correct approach**: Use conservation of **energy** and **momentum**.

- Each object has momentum $\pm p$ (opposite, cancel) → combined object is at **rest**
- Each object has energy $E = \sqrt{p^2c^2 + m_0^2c^4} = m_0\gamma c^2$
- Total energy: $E_\text{total} = 2m_0\gamma c^2$
- Combined object at rest: $E_\text{combined} = Mc^2$

$$Mc^2 = 2m_0\gamma c^2 \implies \boxed{M = 2m_0\gamma}$$

Since $\gamma \geq 1$: **$M \geq 2m_0$** — the combined mass is **greater** than $2m_0$!

The kinetic energy of the original objects has been converted to rest mass energy.

### Slide 115 — Mass-Energy Equivalence in Collisions

**The pop quiz result** $M = 2m_0\gamma > 2m_0$ demonstrates:

$$\Delta M \cdot c^2 = \Delta KE$$

The increase in rest mass equals the kinetic energy deposited in the collision.

**Inverse process** (pair production): Energy → Mass

$$\gamma\text{-ray} + \gamma\text{-ray} \to e^+ + e^-$$

Two high-energy photons can create an electron-positron pair with rest mass $2m_ec^2$.

**Condition**: $E_\gamma \geq m_ec^2 = 0.511$ MeV per photon.

### Two Postulates of Special Relativity
1. Laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames
2. Speed of light $c$ is the same in all inertial frames

### The Lorentz Factor
$$\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}}, \quad \beta = \frac{v}{c}$$

- $\gamma = 1$ at $v = 0$ (Newtonian limit)
- $\gamma \to \infty$ as $v \to c$ (speed of light is unreachable for massive objects)
