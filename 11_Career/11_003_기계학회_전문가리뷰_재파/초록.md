

**국문:** 원자층증착의 외도(外道): 반도체는 두고, 배터리와 자연으로
**영문:** *Atomic Layer Deposition's Incurable Wanderlust: Leaving Semiconductors for Batteries and Nature*


- 국문: 원자층증착(ALD), 기상 침투(VPI), 등각 나노코팅, 이차전지 전극, 화학–기계적 응력, 유기–무기 하이브리드, 자연 모사, 파괴 인성
- English: atomic layer deposition (ALD), vapor phase infiltration (VPI), conformal nanocoating, battery electrodes, chemo-mechanical stress, organic–inorganic hybrids, biomimicry, fracture toughness


Atomic layer deposition, or ALD, grows pinhole free films. It controls thickness at the subnanometer scale. These strengths made ALD essential in semiconductor manufacturing. The same self limiting surface chemistry now works far outside the cleanroom. In this review, we follow ALD into three fields. The first field is rechargeable batteries. Conformal oxide nanolayers coat electrode particles and porous electrodes. They suppress interfacial side reactions. They buffer chemomechanical stress. They slow the microcracking that drives capacity fade. The second field is vapor phase infiltration, or VPI. VPI is derived from ALD. Inorganic precursors diffuse into a polymer and react inside it. The product is an organic and inorganic hybrid. Its stiffness, toughness, and stability rise sharply. Commodity plastics gain properties they never had. The third field is biomimicry. Nacre uses a brick and mortar structure. It reaches high strength and fracture toughness at the same time. It does so through inorganic reinforcement at the molecular scale. This is exactly what VPI aims to reproduce. These cases share one message. ALD is not only a niche semiconductor tool. It is a general platform for tuning mechanical behavior at the molecular scale
